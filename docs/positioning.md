# Aligned — Positioning Foundation

Developed using April Dunford's 5 Components of Positioning framework. This document is the strategic foundation — README copy, landing pages, and messaging should all trace back to this.

**Scope:** This positioning is for the open-source GitHub repo README specifically. It targets people who have already landed on the repo — they know what Claude Code is and they know what skills/plugins are (or were referred by someone who does). A separate positioning document covers the broader consulting offering.

## Component 1: Competitive Alternatives

What the README reader would do if aligned didn't exist:

1. **Other skill repos and plugins** — hundreds of them, generic prompt collections and workflow tools
2. **Building their own skills** — rolling their own prompts and frameworks from scratch
3. **Using Claude Code without skills** — they know about skills but haven't committed to one
4. **Inertia** — clicking away and not adopting skills at all

Key insight: The dominant alternative is not manual ChatGPT prompting (that's the broader market). For the README audience, the alternative is other skill repos that use generic "act as a marketing expert" prompting rather than codified expert personas and published frameworks.

## Component 2: Unique Attributes

Specific, provable things aligned has that alternatives don't:

1. **Named expert personas with codified voices** — calibrated from real content, quotes, and speech patterns. Not "act as a positioning expert" but April Dunford speaking like April Dunford.
2. **Exact published frameworks with phase gating** — sequential phases, enforced order, quality gates. The 5 Components framework runs the way April Dunford actually runs it with clients, not a hallucinated approximation.
3. **Document intake before starting** — asks for relevant context and supporting documents up front.
4. **Gap reporting at the end** — tells you what information it didn't have and how that affected the output.
5. **Auto-selection of the right advisor** — system matches the domain to the right expert. User doesn't need to know who to ask.
6. **Multi-advisor panels** — multiple experts read, critique, debate, and synthesize. Not one perspective but three or four arguing with each other.
7. **65 advisors, 138 frameworks** — breadth across business, technical, and creative domains.
8. **Plugin architecture** — runs in Claude Code, Claude co-work, and potentially other AI tools.

### Why frameworks matter (validated through testing)

Without codified frameworks, the LLM fails in three specific ways:
- **Asks all questions at once** instead of gating phase by phase — collapses a sequential process into a single prompt, producing shallow answers
- **Gets the framework wrong** — typically 4 out of 5 components correct, with one contaminated from another methodology. Probabilistic contamination that *looks* right but isn't.
- **No intake or output discipline** — no request for supporting documents up front, no summary of gaps at the end

The Notion/Lenny's Newsletter example validates market demand: someone codified ~150 expert voices without frameworks and got massive LinkedIn traction. The concept resonates. Aligned adds the frameworks, phase gating, and execution environment that make the output actually reliable.

## Component 3: Value

**Primary value:** Your work gets held to expert standards you didn't know existed. The gap between "AI said it's fine" and "an expert who's done this 200 times would tear this apart" is invisible to the person in the gap. Generic LLMs confirm your blind spots by giving you the average. This system exposes them by applying a specific expert's standard.

**Supporting value:** This happens through a virtual board of advisors that's impossible to assemble in real life — April Dunford, Richard Rumelt, Steve Jobs, and Jeff Bezos all reading your document simultaneously, each through their own lens, debating each other. CEOs pay $50K for one of those perspectives.

**Tertiary value:** You get specific, actionable feedback — not "this could be better" but "here are the four things wrong and why."

### The demo that lands

The most effective demonstration: ask someone to bring a document they created with ChatGPT that they think is good. Load it into aligned and have the right advisor evaluate it. It consistently comes back C-minus or D-plus — with specific, compelling feedback on what to fix. The "oh shit" moment is seeing that a document you thought was good is actually bad relative to expert standards, combined with actionable information on how to make it better.

The explanation has proven harder than the demonstration. People struggle to understand the value until they see the output quality difference. Consider building a lightweight web app or interactive demo for this purpose.

## Component 4: Target Customers

**For the README audience:** Professionals already using Claude Code or Claude co-work who create business deliverables — positioning documents, strategy docs, blog posts, sales materials — and want expert-level output quality. Early adopters comfortable with cutting-edge AI tools.

**Two segments within this audience:**
1. People new to skills — referred by Substack, colleagues, or friends. They need to understand why this approach matters at all.
2. People who already know skills — shopping across repos. They need to understand why the advisor/framework approach produces better results.

The README must serve both. The core argument is the same for each: codified experts with real frameworks produce measurably better output than generic AI prompting or generic skills.

Note: Deeper ICP work (role, company stage, psychographics) is deferred to the consulting positioning document. For an open-source adoption play, the above is sufficient.

## Component 5: Market Category

**Virtual Board of Advisors**

Why this works:
- Triggers an aspirational mental model everyone understands — boards of advisors are expensive, exclusive, and something successful companies have
- Elevates out of the "plugin" comparison set — reader evaluates against "hiring actual advisors," not against other plugin repos
- Makes the differentiated value feel obvious — "your work gets held to expert standards" is exactly what a board of advisors does
- The assumptions work in our favor — no need to explain what a board of advisors does

Category type: **Create new category** (within the plugin/skills space). Low risk in this context because the audience is narrow and technical enough to grasp it quickly.

## Broader Framing: Using vs. Building

The positioning above is scoped to the README audience. For broader messaging (landing pages, consulting positioning, Substack content), there's a complementary frame that operates at a higher level.

Most people are *using* AI — typing into ChatGPT, getting output that's fine but not great, and moving on. Very few are *building* with AI — creating persistent systems with codified expertise that compound over time. "Using vs. building" describes the **behavior**. "Average vs. expert-level" describes the **outcome**. They're complementary layers, not competing frames:

- **Using** → average results (the LLM gives you the mean of all knowledge)
- **Building** → *can* get expert-level results, but only with the right foundation
- **Aligned** → the foundation that makes expert-level results achievable

The full argument for broader audiences: if you're using, you're stuck at average and you can't tell. If you're building, you need a system that encodes real expertise, not just better prompts. Aligned is that system.

This framing is useful for content marketing, Substack, and the consulting positioning document. It's less useful for the README, where the reader has already self-selected toward building. For the README, skip the behavior diagnosis and go straight to the outcome: why this approach produces expert-level results when others don't.[^1]

[^1]: The "using vs. building" distinction is explored in Alex McFarland's Substack series ["Everyone's using AI. Almost nobody's building with it."](https://alexmcfarland.substack.com/p/everyones-using-ai-almost-nobodys) — a rising-tides framing that drives people toward exactly the kind of tool aligned provides.

## What This Means for the README

1. **Lead with the gap, not the feature list.** Don't open with "65 advisors, 138 frameworks." Open with the problem — you're getting average AI output and you don't even know it.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Concrete before/after or a demo that lets people feel the difference. The explanation is hard; the demonstration is easy.
3. **"Virtual Board of Advisors" should be the framing** — not "Claude Code plugin" or "skills collection."
4. **Every claim must trace back to an attribute, every attribute back to a competitive alternative.** No orphan claims.
