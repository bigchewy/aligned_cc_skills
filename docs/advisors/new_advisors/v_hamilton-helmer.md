## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **Power = Benefit + Barrier.** "Power requires a benefit and a barrier, you have to have something that you
  do that gives you a better outcome than your competitors, lower cost or higher price, and then something
  that makes it impossible for somebody else to mimic that." ✅ "So benefit and a barrier, we call it the to
  be or not to be test." ✅ The benefit: "there's something in what you do that gives you either a cost or
  price advantage over others." ✅ The barrier: "there's something that is durable about that that makes it
  over time that you can't, the competitors can't take away from you." ✅ [LENNY-T] Book definition, "the set
  of conditions creating the potential for persistent differential returns" 🧠.
- **Moat is only the barrier.** "So moat is the second. So it's not synonymous with power because you can have
  a moat around a very undesirable piece of property" ✅ "you have to have a pretty good understanding of why
  it's a castle and not a shack." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Strategy is about value, so it is long-run.** "it's very useful for a business to focus on the
  fundamental determinants of business value" ✅ "if you do the math of it right, it's NPV of cash flow" ✅
  "strategy is a long time concept." ✅ Pearl Harbor: "tactical short, strategy long." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Three things create value.** Lenny puts it as "power, market size and operational excellence" (host's
  words). Helmer: "all those things fall into those three categories. So it's an exhaustive set and it simply
  comes out of the math." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **The Value equation.** Book form, "Value = M₀ g s̄ m̄": M₀ is today's market size, g a discounted market
  growth factor, s̄ long-term market share, m̄ long-term differential margin (margin above the cost of
  capital). 🧠 Exact symbols unchecked. Power is what drives m̄; market share comes from takeoff. 🧠
- **Materiality.** "The key thing here is materiality, not whether the flywheel exists, but whether the
  effect is strong enough to really tilt returns." ✅ "it's not an impact, it's a material impact. So it
  could have... If it's a penny to your bottom line, that's one thing if it's a billion dollars or something
  else." ✅ "there are lots of things that I would say have network effects but not network economies." ✅
  Uber and Lyft: "they probably have network effects involved but not network economies." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Scale economies, Netflix.** "The cost of their content is a very large fixed cost, about 50% of their cost
  structure every year. They can take that fixed cost and spread it over more subscribers" ✅ Data scale is
  usually not material: "The curve flattens in other words" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Operational excellence is a treadmill, not power.** "You're on a treadmill and if you stop running on
  that treadmill, you get creamed, but it's not power. The things that drive operational excellence can be
  mimicked." ✅ The Blockbuster case: "If you look at the Blockbuster site, their UI site, you couldn't tell it
  different from Netflix. They just copied it." ✅ But in takeoff "operational excellence is everything." ✅
  He credits Michael Porter: "operational excellence is not strategy." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Process power is rare.** "process power is really operational excellence on steroids and usually is
  imitable" ✅ It must be material and opaque; TSMC "maybe they have it" and "In my book, I use the example of
  Toyota" ✅ Test (Lenny's heuristic, Helmer agrees): "And there isn't a consulting firm that offers to bring
  you up to speed on that." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Branding cannot be bought.** "you can get brand recognition by buying an ad in the Super Bowl. That's not
  power, that you paid for it." ✅ [LENNY-T] Book example Tiffany, and the "uncertainty reduction" and
  "affective valence" benefits. 🧠
- **Cornered resource.** "you have something that is of value that if you transferred it to somebody else it
  would be of value to them, but you own the rights. So the barrier is law for example, or lack of knowledge
  from others." ✅ Example: "if you are the first person to come up with Viagra" ✅ [LENNY-T] Book example
  Pixar's Brain Trust; also Dolby? 🧠 De Beers was not checked as one of his examples. 🧠
- **Power Progression (Origination / Takeoff / Stability).** "let's separate a business into three phases,
  origination, takeoff, and stability." ✅ Branding and process power come late: "there's some that only are
  really available in when you reach a stability phase" "So those two are branding and process power." ✅
  Start-ups begin with counter-positioning: "almost every startup that you want to deal with starts with
  counter positioning because remember what product market fit is primarily is a substitution." ✅ Then "scale
  economies, switching costs and network economies, and those depend on your scale relative to competitors."
  ✅ [LENNY-T] Book chart: Origination = Counter-Positioning and Cornered Resource; Takeoff = Scale, Network,
  Switching Costs; Stability = Branding and Process Power. 🧠 (a third-party skill page says the same 🟡)
- **Counter-positioning.** Spoken: "if you don't have counter positioning at that point you're pretty high
  risk from an incumbent who already has the capabilities necessary" "counter positioning is the refuge from
  that." ✅ [LENNY-T] Book definition: "A newcomer adopts a new, superior business model which the incumbent
  does not mimic due to anticipated damage to their existing business." 🟡 (quoted in a search snippet of
  [S-CP]; matches memory 🧠). Barrier is the incumbent's "collateral damage"; incumbents rationally keep
  "milking" the old model rather than "investing" in the new one; examples Vanguard vs. active fund
  managers, Netflix streaming vs. Blockbuster, Dell's direct model. 🧠 Some readers call it a "partial" power
  that needs a second one later 🟡 [S-CP]; Helmer's own sequencing ✅ implies the same.
- **Think about power always, even before product-market fit.** "before I thought it was you do product
  market fit and then you do strategy" "That's wrong." ✅ "when should you thinking about this? The answer is
  always." ✅ "You're tilting probabilities, you're not creating a determinative things." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Second acts restart the cycle.** "think of AWS or Intel going into CPUs or Apple going into iPhones, all not
  the origin of business. And that's actually common" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Seven is empirical.** "So far I'm pretty satisfied that seven is an exhaustive set, but never say never.
  That's an empirical seven, not a theoretical seven." ✅ No eighth power from AI: "currently I don't see any
  change in seven powers from it" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Action first.** "remember, action is the first principle of business." ✅ "The idea was not to tell you
  what to do, but to give you guideposts" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Invention is the mother of Power.** Book phrase, from the "Path to Power" chapter. 🧠 Not read. Spoken
  neighbor: "remember that underneath all of this is a change in technology. That's what made the product
  market fit possible in the first place." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **"Power intensity."** Not found on any page read. 🧠 Do not use the term.

### What he does NOT publish (the prompt must not invent these)

- Go-to-market, pricing tactics, copy, growth loops, or how to get the first users. He says the opposite of a
  plan matters early: "Not like that at all. At the earlier stage, you can imagine wildly more degrees of
  freedom" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- Small businesses with no real competitors. His math is market share and margins over years.
- Pharma-style cornered resources: "it's usually not the types of things that I'm dealing with" ✅ [LENNY-T]
- Fast answers. "it might take us weeks to answer that question for a single company." ✅ [LENNY-T]

## Known critiques (for the Failure Modes section)

- **Hard to apply quickly.** His own warning: "despite the name 7 Powers, which makes it sound like, oh, you
  can sort this out. Actually understanding whether or not there is a type of power in place is hard." ✅
  [LENNY-T] A 15-minute conversation must not pretend to settle it; it can only rule things out.
- **Built for scale and public equities.** The frame needs competitors, market share and margins. For a tiny
  or pre-market product the honest answer is often "no Power yet," which he says is fine early ("tilting
  probabilities") ✅. The persona must not invent a moat to be helpful.
- **Static vs dynamic.** He concedes operational excellence is "everything" in takeoff ✅; a persona that
  dismisses execution would misquote him.
- **Outside critics.** None read this session. 🧠
