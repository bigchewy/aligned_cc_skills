## Selected Slot

Runs `/aligned:create-design-principles`. He interviews a solo app builder, often building with AI coding
agents, and writes `design-principles.md`: tokens and rules an agent can follow without guessing. His
published method sets most of the file with numbers: colour roles with contrast ratios, a type scale from a
base size and ratio, line height on a 4pt grid, two font weights, t-shirt spacing in 8pt steps, three radii,
two shadows, 48pt targets, a button hierarchy, and lighter surfaces for elevation in dark mode. His stated
reason for the whole thing: "Without a logical system, you're just using gut feeling to move stuff around
until it looks pretty." ✅ [T16]

Steve Jobs stays as critique voice 1 (feeling, point of view, distinctiveness). Brad Frost is critique voice
2 (can the screens be built from the system). Steve Krug is critique voice 3 (usability). Dannaway does not
critique his own file.

Where the slot outruns him: the feeling, the anti-feeling, the reference, and the personality. He treats
brand as a layer added after the basics: "You could further customise the decorative visual styles to match
a specific brand vibe if needed." "For this example though, we were focused on getting the basics right." ✅
[T16] The prompt must take the Design Direction answers from the user and not invent a Dannaway method for
them. See "What he does NOT publish."

## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **Logic over opinion.** "most of my visual and interaction design decisions are governed by a system of
  logical guidelines." ✅ [T16] "Having a system of logical UI design guidelines helps you efficiently make
  informed design decisions." ✅ [T16] "Relying on objective guidelines, rather than subjective opinion, makes
  it far easier (and faster) to design interfaces that are intuitive, accessible, and visually polished." ✅
  [T14] "you really only need a small handful of UI design guidelines to solve most interface design
  problems" ✅ [ABOUT] Book lede: "Learn a logic-driven approach to design intuitive, accessible, and
  beautiful interfaces using quick and practical guidelines." ✅ [PUI]
- **Limit the options.** "Limiting your options in this way helps improve consistency and speeds up
  decision making." ✅ [BDS] "These predefined reusable options are often referred to as "tokens"." ✅ [BDS]
  "The hardest part of creating a design system is establishing the foundations for colour, typography, and
  spacing." ✅ [PUI]
- **Colour by role, made in HSB from the brand colour.** "It's made up of 5 variations of the brand colour."
  ✅ [BDS] (The post then names six roles.) "Use the main brand colour hue as a base and change the saturation
  and brightness to create the other variations." ✅ [BDS] The roles: ✅ [BDS]
  - Brand: "used to indicate interactive elements like text links and buttons."
  - Text strong: "used for primary text, like headings, body content, and form labels to ensure they're
    prominent and legible."
  - Text weak: "used for supporting text to make it less prominent."
  - Stroke strong: "used for non-decorative borders on interface elements like form input fields. Also used
    for icons."
  - Stroke weak: "used for decorative borders, like dividing lines, that aren't critical to identifying
    interface elements."
  - Fill: "used as a secondary background to help differentiate elements"
  - The Figma system keeps the same idea: "A concise set of colour variables named and organised based on how
    they're used." ✅ [PUI-DS]
- **Contrast numbers.** "Make sure that colours used for text ("Brand", "Text strong", and "Text weak") have
  a 4.5:1 contrast ratio." "Colours used for interface elements, like "Stroke strong", need at least a 3:1
  contrast ratio." ✅ [BDS] "Small text (18px and under) needs a minimum contrast of 4.5:1." "Large text
  (above 18px with bold weight or above 24px with regular weight) needs a minimum contrast of 3:1." ✅ [T14],
  [T16] "user interface elements, like form fields, icons, and buttons, need to have at least a 3:1 contrast
  ratio." ✅ [T16] Floor: "aim to at least meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 level AA
  colour contrast requirements." ✅ [BDS]
- **Colour for meaning, brand colour for what you can click.** "Start with black and white and introduce
  colour where it conveys meaning." ✅ [T16] "Use colour sparingly and with purpose." "Try to avoid using
  colour purely for decoration, as it can be confusing and distracting." ✅ [T16] "A simple and effective
  approach is to apply the brand colour to interactive elements like text links and buttons." "This helps
  teach people what's interactive and what's not." "Try to avoid using the brand colour on non-interactive
  elements." ✅ [T16] With several brand colours: "try using the highest contrast colour exclusively for
  interactive elements" "and use the others sparingly for decorative elements." ✅ [LI-COL]
- **Never colour alone.** "Don't rely on colour alone to convey meaning or distinguish visual elements," "as
  those with low vision or colour blindness might not be able to see the indicator." ✅ [T14] Fixes: "Adding
  an underline to the selected tab helps to make it clearer." "To make it more obvious, the selected icon is
  filled." ✅ [T14] "Underlining the link text clearly differentiates it from other text in the absence of
  colour." ✅ [T16]
- **No pure black.** "For UI design, it's generally safest to avoid pure black, as it has a very high
  contrast against white." "This high contrast can cause eye strain and fatigue when reading text." "It's
  safest to avoid pure black against white and opt for a dark grey instead." ✅ [T16]
- **Type scale from a base and a ratio.** "start with a base font size of 16px and use a "1.200 – Minor
  Third" scale." "Multiply 16px by 1.2 to get the next font size of 19.2px. Round it to 20px for simplicity."
  "Round font sizes to the nearest whole number for simplicity." ✅ [BDS] The post lists ratios from 1.067
  (Minor Second) to 1.618 (Golden Ratio). ✅ [BDS] The full list of sizes is in an image and was not read. 🟡
- **Line height on a 4pt grid, at least 1.5 for body.** "It's not crucial that font sizes are divisible by 4,
  but try to ensure that their line height is" ✅ [BDS] "This will align your text neatly to a 4pt vertical
  layout grid." ✅ [BDS] "For accessibility and readability, especially for long body text, ensure that line
  height is at least 1.5 (150%)." "Keeping line height between 1.5 and 2 generally works well." ✅ [T16]
- **Two weights, one sans serif.** "Keep your design system simple and concise by using regular and bold font
  weights only." ✅ [T14], [T16] "It's safest to use a single sans serif typeface for interface design" ✅
  [T16] "Look for typefaces with taller lower case letters and greater letter spacing" ✅ [T16] His example
  swaps League Spartan for Inter. ✅ [T16] "Unless you're shouting at people, there aren't many valid reasons
  to use uppercase." ✅ [T16] "A small trick to make large headings look better is to decrease their letter
  spacing" ✅ [T14] "For long body text, it's safest to avoid centre aligned or justified text." ✅ [T16]
- **Line length.** "Ensure text is 40 – 80 characters per line (including spaces) to improve readability." ✅
  [PUI] (free sample guideline "Ensure ideal line length")
- **Spacing in t-shirt sizes on 8pt.** "Create simple t-shirt sized spacing options based on increments of 8
  points." "Why 8? Many popular screen sizes are divisible by 8 and it provides a bit more spacing
  flexibility than using 10 points." "For more detailed interfaces, you could use 4 point increments for a bit
  more control." "Much like a typography scale, your spacing options should grow by larger amounts as they
  get bigger." ✅ [BDS], [T14] Two named values seen: "extra small (8pt) and small (16pt)" ✅ [T14]; the rest
  of the scale was in an image. 🟡 The Figma kit uses "4px grid spacing variables." ✅ [PUI]
- **Spacing shows relationship.** "The amount of spacing between interface elements should depend on how
  closely related the elements are." ✅ [T14] "Using containers is the strongest visual cue to group
  interface elements, but it can add unnecessary clutter." ✅ [T14]
- **Radius and shadows.** "Create 3 border radius options (8pt, 16pt, and 32pt) to use on small, medium, and
  large interface elements respectively." "You'll generally need 2 shadow options (raised and overlay) to
  indicate the depth of interface elements." ✅ [BDS] Shadow values were not given in text. 🟡
- **Dark mode: lighter surfaces, not shadows.** "Shadows work well to indicate different levels of elevation
  in light mode, but they're difficult to see in dark mode." "So, progressively lighter background colours
  are used to help indicate elevation in dark mode." ✅ [PUI-DS] "Predefined colour variables allow you to
  automatically switch between light and dark mode instantly." ✅ [PUI-DS] Dark mode is in the Figma kits,
  not a book chapter. ✅ [PUI]
- **States by overlay.** "Hover and press states are indicated using a transparent overlay that sits on top
  of interactive elements." ✅ [PUI-DS] He likes transparent colours in token sets: "I think transparency will
  become more prevalent once more design teams realise the advantages." ✅ [DSX]
- **Three button weights, one primary.** "For most website or app projects, you'll need 3 button weights to
  indicate the importance of actions:" "primary, secondary, and tertiary." ✅ [T14] "Avoid using multiple
  primary buttons on a screen." "They can compete for attention and cause confusion around what to do next."
  ✅ [T14] "If there isn't a single most important action on an interface, use secondary or tertiary buttons
  for those actions." ✅ [T14] "The primary action should generally be the most prominent element on an
  interface." "Giving it a high contrast background colour and bold font weight helps achieve this." ✅ [T16]
  "ensure buttons are left aligned and avoid disabled buttons." ✅ [BDS] On X: "Always keep buttons enabled by
  default" 🟡 [X-MIRROR]
- **Targets.** "Make buttons at least 48pt by 48pt in size." "Separate buttons by at least 8pt to help
  prevent people mistakenly pressing the wrong one." ✅ [T14]
- **Hierarchy and the Squint Test.** "Aim to present information in order of importance by making more
  important elements look more prominent." "You can create a clear visual hierarchy using variations in
  size, colour, contrast, spacing, position, and depth." "Simply squint your eyes and look at your design."
  ✅ [T16]
- **Icons.** "Outlining all icons with a 2pt stroke weight and rounded corners improves consistency" ✅ [T16]
  Filled versus outlined matters: "filled icons often indicate that an element is selected." ✅ [T16] "Text
  labels are also added to the icons to help ensure people can understand what they mean" ✅ [T16] "When
  pairing icons with text, try to ensure they have a similar visual prominence" ✅ [T14] Icons are "typically
  24px" and one style holds throughout. 🟡 [ICONS] (paraphrase) Favourite set: "Phosphor is my favourite free
  icon set for many reasons." ✅ [ICONS]
- **Consistency, and "minimal" is not "simple."** "Consistency in UI design means that similar elements look
  and work in a similar way." ✅ [T14] "Rounding the corners of the photos helps to create a more consistent
  visual language." ✅ [T14] "Minimal doesn't mean simple." "Removing or hiding too much can harm usability."
  ✅ [T14]
- **Audit before you build, for an existing app.** "Initial audit – identify and document all components and
  styles present in your website or app." "Merge similar items – try to cut down the number of different
  components and styles in your system." ✅ [BDS] "Keep guidance concise so that it's easy to consume and
  remember." ✅ [BDS]
- **Developers need guidance too.** "it's important to remember that developers also require guidance on how
  to use the system." ✅ [DSX] Of Vercel: "There aren't many usage guidelines, which opens up the door to
  inconsistency." ✅ [DSX] The book is for "Developers who no longer want to rely on a designer." ✅ [PUI]

## Known critiques (for the Failure Modes section)

- His own: rules taken too far. "Minimal doesn't mean simple." "Removing or hiding too much can harm
  usability." ✅ [T14] A persona built on "fewer options" must not strip needed labels or states.
- His own: the guidelines are a base, not the end. "Think of them as a solid base that you can build on as you
  explore new ideas" ✅ [T16]. A file that is only his defaults will look like every other Practical UI app.
  This is the gap Steve Jobs's critique covers.
- For this slot: "regular and bold only" and "a single sans serif" conflict with the skill's question 8 (serif
  or sans headlines, what weight) and with brands that choose a serif at 400. The skill's rule "The
  principles file wins" must hold over his defaults, and he must say so when he gives way.
- For this slot: his contrast threshold for large text ("above 18px with bold weight or above 24px with
  regular weight") ✅ [T14] is a bit stricter than the skill's Quality Floor ("3:1 for text 24px and up, or
  19px bold"). His target size (48pt) is larger than the floor (44px). Pick one set of numbers before the
  prompt is written.
- For this slot: no material on motion, states, or the focus ring, which are four of the skill's REQUIRED
  sections. The prompt must take those from the skill's Quality Floor and Starting Defaults, not from him.
- For this slot: his sizes use "pt" in Figma terms. The output file is CSS, so the prompt must write px and
  rem and say that his 8pt is 8px on the web. 🧠 (standard 1pt = 1px in Figma web frames; not checked this
  session)
- Others: no published critique of *Practical UI* was found. Reviews on the sales page appear only as image
  alt text. ✅ [PUI] The common complaint about rule-based UI books (they produce safe, samey interfaces) is
