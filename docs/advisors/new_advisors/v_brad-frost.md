## Selected Slot

Turning a structural plan of a small app into exact static screens built only from the project's design
system, and checking that the screens and the system agree. He starts from the structure (Ryan Singer's
breadboard), places system parts into a layout per place (template), pours in real content (page), then
pushes each page through the content and user cases that break it (variations). Second job: review UI for
consistency with the design system and catch one-offs that should be system parts.

The slot excludes usability and whether a user understands a screen (Steve Krug), taste and choosing the
tokens themselves (Steve Jobs, through `/aligned:create-design-principles`), breadboarding (Ryan Singer),
and story maps (Jeff Patton).

How the breadboard maps onto his stages is our inference, not his words: a place becomes a template, an
affordance becomes a molecule or organism, a connection becomes a link between pages. 🧠 (Our mapping. He
never mentions breadboards.)

## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **The five stages.** ✅ [AD2]
  - Atoms: "the atoms of our interfaces serve as the foundational building blocks that comprise all our user
    interfaces." In 2013 these included "abstract items like color palettes, fonts, and animations." 🟡
    [AWD] (paraphrase returned, not exact)
  - Molecules: "relatively simple groups of UI elements functioning together as a unit."
  - Organisms: "relatively complex UI components composed of groups of molecules and/or atoms and/or other
    organisms."
  - Templates: "page-level objects that place components into a layout."
  - Pages: "specific instances of templates that show what a UI looks like with real representative content
    in place."
- **Templates show content structure, not final content.** Templates "focus on the page's underlying
  content structure rather than the page's final content." ✅ [AD2] "What is your content _made_ from, not
  what your content _is_." ✅ [AD2] "templates begin their life as HTML wireframes" ✅ [AWD]
- **Character lengths and image sizes.** Templates "articulate important properties of components like image
  sizes and character lengths for headings and text passages." ✅ [AD2] "One article headline might be 40
  characters long, while another article headline might be 340 characters long." ✅ [AD2]
- **Pages test the system.** "pages are essential for testing the effectiveness of the underlying design
  system" ✅ [AD2]. "The page stage is essential as it's where we test the effectiveness of the design
  system." ✅ [AWD] "placeholder content is replaced with real representative content" ✅ [AWD]
- **Variations.** "Pages also provide a place to articulate variations in templates" ✅ [AD2]. "Pages are
  also the place to test variations in templates." ✅ [AWD] Examples: "A user has one item in their shopping
  cart and another user has ten items in their cart." "Users with administrative privileges might see
  additional buttons and options on their dashboard." ✅ [AD2] "the underlying templates are the same, but
  the user interfaces change to reflect the dynamic nature of the content." ✅ [AD2]
- **Best case and worst case.** "To create more robust and resilient designs, we need to concurrently account
  for the best situations and the worst" ✅ [AD3]. Pattern Lab's pseudo-patterns (marked with `~`)
  "articulate sometimes wildly different scenarios with just a few changes to our data." ✅ [AD3]
- **Structure and content kept apart.** Pattern Lab "Creates a clear separation between structure and
  content." ✅ [AD3] An advantage of the method: "Clean separation between structure and content" (section
  title). ✅ [AD2]
- **Not a linear process.** "Atomic design is not a linear process, but rather a mental model." ✅ [AD2]
  "Atomic design provides us a structure to navigate between the parts and the whole of our UIs." ✅ [AD2]
  "One of the biggest advantages atomic design provides is the ability to quickly shift between abstract
  and concrete." ✅ [AD2]
- **Systems, not pages.** Chapter 1 subtitle "Create design systems, not pages." ✅ [AD-TOC] He quotes
  Stephen Hay: "We're not designing pages, we're designing systems of components." ✅ [AD1], [AWD]
- **Small screen first, for priority.** "using the constraints of small screens to force the team to focus
  on the core content and hierarchy." ✅ [AD4] The question to ask of each page: "What content and display
  patterns go on this page? And in what general order?" ✅ [AD4]
- **Whole width range, not device sizes.** "better consider the entire resolution spectrum rather than a
  handful of popular device dimensions." ✅ [AD3] "The more fluid a UI component is, the more resilient and
  versatile it becomes." ✅ [AD3] Responsive design "involves a lot more than creating squishy pages." ✅
  [AD1] Design "around dynamic data (such as variable character lengths, image sizes, and other dynamic
  content)" ✅ [AD4]
- **Get into the browser.** "getting the design into the browser as fast as possible" ✅ [AD4]. "Once the
  designs are in the browser, they should stay in the browser." ✅ [AD4] He quotes Dan Mall: change
  "designing in the browser" to "deciding in the browser." ✅ [AD4] "Pixels are cheap." ✅ [AD4]
- **Interface inventory.** "An interface inventory is a comprehensive collection of the bits and pieces
  that make up your user interface." ✅ [AD4] Steps: "Round up the troops," "Prepare for screenshotting,"
  "Screenshot exercise," "Present Findings," "Regroup and Establish Next Steps." ✅ [CII] Categories: Global,
  Navigation, Image types, Icons, Forms, Buttons, Headings, Blocks, Lists, Interactive Components, Media,
  3rd Party, Advertising, Messaging, Colors, Animation. ✅ [CII] "this exercise doesn't mean capturing every
  instance of a particular UI pattern" ✅ [AD4]. "between 30 and 90 minutes" for a first pass ✅ [AD4]. About
  "10-15 minutes per category presenting the findings" ✅ [CII]. "Naming things is hard." ✅ [AD4]
- **Components, recipes, snowflakes.** "Design system components are shared, content-agnostic, and
  context-agnostic built for maximal reuse." "Recipe components are specific compositions of design system
  components." Snowflakes are "one-off components that are needed to build a product." ✅ [SNOW] "Polluting a
  design system library with a bunch of product-specific components gets noisy and messy." ✅ [SNOW] "It's
  worth asking if this is a one-off situation or something that can be leveraged in other applications."
  ✅ [AD5] "If every whim results in a brand new pattern, the design system will become a bloated and
  unwieldy Wild West." ✅ [AD5]
- **The system is a product.** "a design system shouldn't be a project with a finite scope, but rather a
  product meant to grow and evolve over time" ✅ [AD5]. "a design system is the official story of how an
  organization designs and builds user interfaces." ✅ [SYN] "the coded design system library truly is the
  source of truth" ✅ [SYN]
- **Design tokens in three tiers.** "Tier 1 tokens define all of the ingredients available to the design
  token system." "Tier 2 tokens map Tier 1 tokens to specific roles in a user interface." "Tier 3 tokens are
  reserved for component-specific and other special use cases." ✅ [SUB] Spoken: tokens are "really low
  level brand attributes." ✅ [SYN]
- **AI limited to the system.** "the AI is deliberately constrained to using the high-quality design system
  materials" and "This is what distinguishes DS+AI from vibe coding." ✅ [ADS26] Design systems "can help
  keep AI on the rails"; generative AI "can make a huge mess." ✅ [COURSE] Prototypes should follow the
  organization's own practices, not "whatever AI decides to randomly generate." ✅ [RTUI] "Any AI tool needs
  to produce predictable, reliable results." ✅ [AIDS] "We like to think of AI as a smart-but-sometimes-
  unsophisticated junior developer." ✅ [AIDS] His rebuild goal: "how to get AI to _only_ use our design
  system infrastructure," for a "genuine connection between system & product." ✅ [REBUILD]
- **Quality still needs people.** Professional designers and developers are needed "now more than ever!"
  ✅ [RTUI] "We need to care about quality." ✅ [MOMENT]

### What he does NOT publish (the prompt must not invent these)

- No type scale, ratio, or rules for font sizes, line height, or line length. 🟡 (none found in the book
  or posts read; chapter 4 only says style tiles "explore color, typography, texture, icons" ✅ [AD4])
- No color rules, palette method, or contrast thresholds. Accessibility comes up as a value ("our collective
  accessibility game is abysmal" ✅ [GDS]), not as numbers.
- No spacing scale or grid. The token tiers say how tokens are organized, not what values to pick. ✅ [SUB]
- No visual hierarchy rules for a single screen (emphasis, weight, grouping). Element collages and style
  tiles are tools for exploring "design atmosphere" ✅ [AD4], not rules.
- No named design critique method. A search found nothing on design critique. 🟡 His review lens is system
  consistency: does this come from the system, is it a recipe, or is it a snowflake.
- No breakpoint values. He argues against designing to device sizes. ✅ [AD3]
- Pages in his book are a stage of design, not specified pixel mockups. The "exact static screen" in our
  slot is our use of his page stage. The values come from `design-principles.md`, not from him.

## Known critiques (for the Failure Modes section)

- His own: "Atomic design is not a linear process." ✅ [AD2] Teams that run the five stages in strict order
  miss the point.
- His own: the hard part is people, not tools. Smashing Magazine summary: the hard part "isn't specific
  design tools or code frameworks; it's wrangling people and all their quirks." 🟡 (2019 interview, not
  opened) "Naming things is hard." ✅ [AD4]
- His own: one-offs pile up. "noisy and messy" ✅ [SNOW]; "Wild West" ✅ [AD5].
- Others: the chemistry names confuse teams, and the line between molecule and organism is arbitrary, so
  teams argue about labels instead of building. 🧠 (common complaint; no source read this session)
- Others: Chris Coyier questioned who would run a global design system and why earlier attempts failed. 🟡
  (search summary of "Thoughts on a Global Design System")
- For this slot: he gives no visual rules, so with a weak `design-principles.md` the screens will be
  consistent but not good-looking. The advisor must say when the tokens are missing a value rather than
  inventing one.
