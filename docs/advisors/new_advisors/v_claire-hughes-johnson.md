Now an advisor and corporate director at multiple tech companies including Cloudflare and Ameritas. Previously VP at Google (AdWords, Google X, various operations roles).

Known for building Stripe's management infrastructure from scratch during hyper-growth: performance frameworks, operating cadences, hiring systems, written communication culture. Has published extensively through Stripe Press, First Round Review, and her own writing.

## Frameworks (Curation Candidates)

### 1. Operating Review Design (TOP CANDIDATE)

Formal review meetings (monthly business reviews, quarterly operating reviews) designed around four elements:
1. **Pre-read** — written memo circulated 24–48 hours in advance; attendees read before the meeting
2. **Discussion, not presentation** — the meeting is for debate and decisions on pre-identified issues, not slide readouts
3. **Decision and action log** — every meeting ends with a written list of decisions made and actions owned
4. **Connected to strategy** — each review tied to objectives/KRs; metrics presented in context of what they mean for strategic priorities

Distinct from Mochary's weekly check-in: this is for the *formal* review rhythm (monthly/quarterly), not the weekly pulse. They complement each other.

**Selection score:** Directly applicable to the Headspace CPO use case (operating committee); most distinctive contribution; repeatable.

### 2. Working-With-Me Document

A personal operating manual that every manager writes and shares with their team. Covers: communication style preferences, decision-making approach, feedback preferences, what energizes/drains them, known failure modes, best ways to escalate or disagree. Creates alignment at the start of a working relationship instead of discovering mismatches over months.

**Selection score:** Highly distinctive, widely imitated; directly applicable to leadership team alignment.

### 3. DACI Decision Framework

Decision Accountability: Driver (proposes and owns), Approver (final decision authority), Contributors (provide input), Informed (kept in the loop). For every significant decision, document who plays each role. Prevents unclear ownership and decisions that get relitigated.

**Selection score:** Useful; but broadly known (not exclusive to CHJ). Best embedded as a tool within Operating Review Design rather than standalone.

### 4. Management Writing Discipline

The doctrine that good managers write: strategy memos, team updates, decision rationale, performance feedback — not as bureaucracy but as a thinking-clarification and alignment tool. The act of writing forces clarity the meeting never would. Covers: how to write a strategy memo, how to give written feedback, how to document a decision so it can be revisited.

**Selection score:** Distinctive, especially for leaders moving from verbal to written culture; pairs with Operating Review Design.

### 5. Organizational Operating System

How mission, vision, values, and strategy connect to quarterly priorities, hiring decisions, and day-to-day tradeoffs. Not the platitudes — the mechanics: how to write a real strategy doc, how to cascade priorities through the org, how to use review meetings to close the loop on execution vs. strategy.

**Selection score:** Important but broad; best as context within the other frameworks rather than standalone.
