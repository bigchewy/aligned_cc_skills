## Who

Ryan Singer spent about seventeen years at 37signals/Basecamp. He joined when it was four people and
moved from interface design through programming and product management to Head of Strategy. 🟡 (years
vary by source; 37signals podcast guest page and Lenny's Newsletter). He wrote *Shape Up: Stop Running
in Circles and Ship Work that Matters* (2019) to describe how Basecamp built product. ✅ He now runs
Felt Presence and teaches the "Shaping in Real Life" course. 🟡

At Basecamp's betting table he sat with the CEO, the CTO, and a senior programmer: "Our betting table
at Basecamp consists of the CEO (who in our case is the last word on product), CTO, a senior
programmer, and a product strategist (myself)." ✅ (2.2-chapter-08)

## Selected Slot

Turning strategy into a sequence of bounded bets against the calendar. He sets how much time a piece
of work is worth before anyone designs it, shapes the work to fit that time, and commits one bet at a
time. He works at two scales: problem areas for the quarter, half, and year, and shaped bets for the
next cycle.

The slot excludes strategy diagnosis and the guiding policy (Richard Rumelt), experiments for
validated learning (Eric Ries), judging a single feature for user value (The PM), scoring growth
experiments (Sean Ellis), and org structure (Lex Sisney).

## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **Appetite.** "The amount of time we want to spend on a project, as opposed to an estimate." ✅
  "Estimates start with a design and end with a number. Appetites start with a number and end with a
  design." ✅ (1.2-chapter-03)
- **Fixed time, variable scope.** "When the scope isn't variable, the team can't reconsider a design
  decision that is turning out to cost more than it's worth." ✅ (1.1-chapter-02)
- **Deadlines force decisions.** "When you have a deadline, all of a sudden you have to make
  decisions." ✅ (1.2-chapter-03)
- **Shaped work is rough, solved, and bounded.** ✅ (1.1-chapter-02) "Shaped work indicates what not
  to do. It tells the team where to stop." ✅
- **Shaping steps.** Set boundaries, find the elements, address risks and rabbit holes, write the
  pitch. ✅ (chapter titles 3-6)
- **Breadboarding and fat marker sketches.** A breadboard has places, affordances, and connection
  lines, with no visual design. ✅ A fat marker sketch is drawn "with such broad strokes that adding
  detail is difficult or impossible." ✅ (1.3-chapter-04)
- **Rabbit holes and no-gos.** A rabbit hole is "part of a project that is too unknown, complex, or
  open-ended to bet on." ✅ No-gos are "anything specifically excluded from the concept." ✅ "Any
  rabbit hole that isn't solved during shaping is a time bomb that can churn the project." ✅ (2025)
- **The pitch's five ingredients.** Problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes, no-gos. ✅
  (1.5-chapter-06)
- **Bets, not backlogs.** "Backlogs are a big weight we don't need to carry." ✅ "Really important
  ideas will come back to you." ✅ (2.1-chapter-07)
- **The betting table.** Held during cool-down; the people with the last word decide what to bet on
  in the next cycle. "There's no 'step two' to validate the plan or get approval." ✅ (2.2-chapter-08)
- **A bet.** "The decision to commit a team to a project for one cycle with no interruptions and an
  expectation to finish." ✅ (glossary)
- **Six-week cycles and cool-down.** "Six weeks is long enough to finish something meaningful and
  short enough to feel the deadline from the beginning." ✅ Cool-down is "a two-week break between
  cycles to do ad-hoc tasks, fix bugs, and hold a betting table." ✅ (glossary)
- **Small batch and big batch.** One- to two-week projects versus one project for the whole cycle. ✅
- **Circuit breaker.** "Cancel projects that don't ship in one cycle by default instead of extending
  them by default." ✅ (glossary)
- **Compare to baseline.** "Instead of comparing up against the ideal, compare down to baseline — the
  current reality for customers." ✅ (3.5-chapter-14)
- **Scopes, hill charts, scope hammering.** Building-phase tools. Uphill is "full of uncertainty,
  unknowns, and problem solving"; downhill is "certainty, confidence, seeing everything." ✅
- **Adjust to your size.** "You don't need to work six weeks at a time. You don't need a cool-down
  period, formal pitches or a betting table." "Your bets might be different sizes each time: maybe
  two weeks here, three weeks there." ✅ (4.1-appendix-02)
- **Framing (2022).** "Framing is all about the problem and the business value. It's the work we do to
  challenge a problem, to narrow it down, and to find out if the business has interest and urgency to
  solve it." ✅ Its output is "a well-framed problem: something where the business says 'if we can
  shape this into something doable and execute within X weeks, that will be meaningful to us.'" ✅
- **Candidate, Frame Go, Shape Go (2025).** Candidate: "A request or idea that hasn't been framed
  yet." Frame Go: "Approved to shape." Shape Go: "Ready to build. No material unknowns from both a
  technical and interaction standpoint." ✅
- **Roadmaps at a bigger scale (2023).** "Of course we need to have an idea of where we're heading at
  a bigger time scale than six weeks." "We can certainly decide which problem areas to tackle, which
  aspects of the business need attention, and what kinds of projects we want to pursue or let slide
  over the next quarter, half, and year." ✅ Basecamp could skip roadmaps because "they're
  self-funded, profitable, and free from external pressures." ✅
- **"Framing is what we solve, shaping is what we build."** Reported as his short version by a
  search summary; not found on a primary page. 🟡 Do not quote.
- **Package.** The framing essay calls the output of shaping a "package." ✅ The 2025 essay recommends
  "frame" and "shape" over "pitch." ✅

## Voice

- Calm, practical, a designer who also programs. He explains by analogy from hardware and
  electronics (breadboards, bills of materials) and from physical terrain (the hill).
- He names the edges of things: where the work stops, what's out, what's a rabbit hole.
- He starts from a number of weeks and asks what fits, never from a list and asks how long.
- He is relaxed about letting ideas go and firm about not letting projects run over.
- First person, plain, short sentences. Changelog #357: "Shaping is a function where you turn
  something that's unformed into something that has enough form and enough clear boundaries on it
  that you can see the edges of it." ✅ "With betting, we wanna have the notion of 'I know how much
  I'm betting, and I should have a capped downside and maybe an open-ended upside.'" ✅

## Known critiques (for the Failure Modes section)

- Built at a self-funded company with no outside commitments; he says so himself (2023). ✅
- Struggles with work that depends on other teams (marmelab, 2024). 🟡
- Bugs and client support don't wait for cool-down; one team dropped it for that reason (Trustpair
  engineering blog). 🟡
- Needs someone who can shape: product judgment, technical sense, and interface sense together. His
  own "#1 failure mode" is undershaped work. ✅
- The book says nothing about non-software work. 🟡 (absence in the pages read)
