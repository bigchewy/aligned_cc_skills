## Selected Slot

Getting a team to agree on what a user actually does, start to finish, before anyone designs screens or
cuts scope. He builds the whole story as a map of user activities, walks it aloud with others to find the
holes, and slices it into releases named by the outcome each one delivers.

The slot excludes the usability of a single screen (Steve Krug), visual craft and design-system work (The
Designer), technical architecture and data modeling (The Architect), roadmap timing against fixed dates
(Ryan Singer), and experiment design for validated learning (Eric Ries).

## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **Backbone.** "I refer to them as the 'backbone' of the software." ✅ [NB] "When it comes time to
  prioritize stories, I don't prioritize the backbone. It just 'is.'" ✅ [NB] "The backbone is arranged in
  a narrative flow. Smaller sub-tasks, details and variations hang down to form the ribs." ✅ [QR]
- **Activities and tasks.** "An activity is sort of a big thing that people do – something that has lots
  of steps." ✅ [NB] "User's tasks are short verb phrases that are the basic building block of a map." ✅
  [QR] "Activities organize tasks done by similar people at similar times to reach a goal." ✅ [QR]
  Examples: "Going through my inbox", "Read an email message". Check that backbone tasks share a goal
  level. ✅ [QR]
- **Left to right, top to bottom.** "The left to right axis in a story map is organized in the order you'd
  tell the story about your user to someone else." ✅ [QR] "Place them high to indicate they're absolutely
  necessary, lower to indicate they're less necessary." ✅ [NB]
- **Mile wide, inch deep.** "Focus on getting the whole story. Think 'mile-wide, inch deep'." ✅ [QR]
- **Frame first.** "Before mapping, create a short product or feature brief to frame and constrain what
  you map." ✅ [QR] What, who (users and "choosers" who buy), and why (the benefit to the organization). ✅
- **Explore.** Sub-tasks, alternative tasks, exceptions, details. "Look for exceptions: What could go
  wrong, and what would the user have to do to recover?" ✅ [QR] "Down in the details of the map, it's OK
  to include details about what UI might look like." ✅ [QR]
- **Walk the map.** "I can walk the map from beginning to end with a user, stakeholder, or developer." ✅
  [NB] "Tell your product's story to others that understand users and use. They'll find holes in your
  story." ✅ [QR]
- **Shared understanding.** "The most retweeted phrase from the story mapping book is that shared
  documents aren't shared understanding." ✅ [MTP] "Effective teams know how to collaborate for shared
  understanding." ✅ [DOCS] "Product design documents are like vacation photos." ✅ [DOCS]
- **Stories are told.** Book sections "Telling Stories, Not Writing Stories" and "Talk and Doc" ✅ [TOC].
  "Stories get their name from how they should be used, not what should be written." 🟡G
- **Slicing.** "The smallest number of tasks that allow your specific target users to reach their goal
  compose a viable product release." ✅ [QR] "Identify the target outcomes of your slice in a sticky note
  or card to the left of the slice." ✅ [QR] "Ruthlessly remove anything they won't need to get those
  outcomes." ✅ [5M]
- **Walking skeleton.** Credited to Alistair Cockburn. ✅ [NB] The 2005 form: "the bare bones minimum
  anyone could legitimately do and still use the system." ✅ [SLICE]
- **Outcomes over output.** "Outcome is what your customers and users do, say, and feel." ✅ [GN] Book
  section "Don't Prioritize Features—Prioritize Outcomes" ✅ [TOC]. "Minimize output, and maximize outcome
  and impact." 🟡G
- **There's always too much.** Book section title ✅ [TOC]. Exact sentence 🟡G.
- **Other users.** "As you follow the typical use of your product, you may find other types of users
  enter your story. Continue modeling their story left to right." ✅ [QR] "This map crosses many types of
  users" and "The system can be a user too!" ✅ [QR] In 2005, role changes split the model into
  "something that looks a bit like swim lanes." ✅ [SLICE] He does not give each persona its own row.
- **Now map.** "Story maps are for understanding now, and imagining later." ✅ [QR]
- **Group size.** "building a map from scratch with a big group does not work." ✅ [MTP] 2005: "Four to
  eight people total is a good number." ✅ [SLICE]
- **The flat backlog.** "A bag of context-free mulch." ✅ [NB] "I hate the backlog concept." ✅ [MTP]
- **Dual track.** "Discovery work happens concurrently and continuously with development work." "The most
  expensive way to test your idea is to build production quality software." ✅ [DT]
- **Not found:** "Goldilocks" story size, the Luke Barrett cartoon, "a story map is a backlog" as a
  phrase, "talk and doodle" (the section is "Talk and Doc"). Do not use.

## Known critiques (for the Failure Modes section)

- His own: "If you're looking for the precision of a workflow model, flow chart, or UML model, then a story
  map isn't your best choice." ✅ [QR]
- His own: technical or back-office items like "migrate to an Oracle database" "don't work well in this
  type of model." ✅ [SLICE]
- His own: a map of nouns is "not a story map" ✅ [5M]; too much detail causes "map shock" ✅ [5M]; big
  groups can't build a map from scratch ✅ [MTP].
- Others: maps built alone feel decided already; maps turn into a work breakdown; teams skip the release
  cuts; maps and the tracker drift apart. 🟡 (Zarar Siddiqi, 2022; tool vendor tutorials)
