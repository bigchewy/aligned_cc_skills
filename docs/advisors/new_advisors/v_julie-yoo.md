## Frameworks (Curation Candidates)

### 1. The 5 GTM Motions for Digital Health (TOP CANDIDATE — SELECTED FOR FIRST IMPLEMENTATION)

From the a16z post "The New Go-To-Market Playbooks for Digital Health Startups." Five distinct, named motions, each with different buyer, different ROI case, different sales cycle, different pricing model:

- **B2C2B** — Direct-to-consumer that eventually sells into employers/payers. Start with patient/member demand, then land the enterprise contract. Examples: Oscar Health (consumer signup → employer partnerships), Headspace/Ginger (consumer wellness → employer wellness), Hims/Hers (DTC script → employer benefit). The "pull" motion — users create demand that forces enterprise buyers to the table.
- **B2SMB** — Sell into small/medium healthcare practices — independent dental offices, primary-care clinics, behavioral-health groups, specialty clinics. Transactional, faster cycles, lower ACV, higher volume. Examples: Dental Intelligence, Jane App, Weave.
- **Risk-based contracting** — At-risk arrangements with payers where revenue is tied to clinical outcomes or total cost of care. You don't sell software; you sell outcomes. Requires clinical evidence, actuarial modeling, and multi-year horizon. Examples: Livongo (outcomes-based contracts with payers/employers), Omada Health (risk-share for diabetes prevention), Cityblock.
- **Two-sided networks** — Marketplaces connecting patients with providers, or providers with vendors/services. Winner-take-most dynamics, chicken-and-egg supply/demand problem. Examples: Zocdoc (patient ↔ provider), Rupa Health (provider ↔ lab), Nomad Health (provider ↔ hospital staffing).
- **Distribution through aggregators** — GPOs, HCOs, IDNs, health systems as channel partners. One contract with a GPO can land dozens of hospitals. Slower, political, procurement-heavy, but massive leverage once the aggregator relationship clicks. Examples: companies that sell through Vizient, Premier, HealthTrust, or directly partner with IDNs as launch channels.

**Why this framework is the top pick:**
- **Signature association:** Yes — this is THE framework Yoo is associated with, originating in the a16z post that founders and VCs reference constantly.
- **Conversational fit:** 20-25 minutes. Walkable as a diagnostic ("which of the five are you actually running? does your product architecture match?").
- **Executive relevance:** Extremely high — every digital health founder must answer this question, and most get it wrong by trying to run two motions simultaneously.
- **Emotional range:** Works in crisis ("we're running out of runway and can't close enterprise deals") AND reflective ("we just raised Series A and need to pick our primary motion for the next 18 months").
- **Complementarity:** Fills the clean gap — no existing advisor covers digital health GTM. Jacco covers generic revenue architecture; Dixon covers deal mechanics; April Dunford covers positioning. Yoo covers the healthcare-specific motion choice that dictates how all three apply.
- **Repeatability:** Users can return as they scale — the motion that's right at seed is often wrong at Series B, and a B2C2B company often adds a distribution-partnership motion at scale.

### 2. The Healthcare Productization Framework

From Yoo's talks and a16z writing on how to turn a clinical service or workflow into a scalable software product. Key elements:
- **Clinical workflow mapping.** Identify the specific clinical workflow or patient journey you're targeting. Who touches the patient, in what sequence, with what decisions?
- **Abstraction level.** How much of the clinical decision are you automating vs. augmenting vs. documenting? The right level depends on regulatory, liability, and adoption constraints.
- **Evidence requirement.** What clinical evidence do you need to satisfy the buyer? RCT? Real-world evidence? Operational metrics? The evidence bar differs by motion (B2C2B ≠ risk-based).
- **Integration surface.** How does the product integrate with EHR, revenue cycle, payer systems, or consumer apps? Integration friction kills digital health startups.
- **Reimbursement path.** Is there a CPT code, a DME code, a value-based care contract, or is this uncovered? The path determines the buyer.

**Signature association:** Moderate — Yoo discusses productization often but it's less canonically "hers" than the 5 GTM motions.
**Conversational fit:** 25-30 minutes. More technical and product-heavy than the GTM framework.
**Executive relevance:** High for product-stage founders, less for GTM-stage founders.
**Complementarity:** Sits beneath the 5 GTM motions — once you pick a motion, productization decisions follow.

### 3. The Payer/Provider/Employer/Consumer Buyer Matrix

Yoo's framework for thinking about *who actually writes the check* in healthcare:
- **Payer (insurance company).** Longest sales cycles, highest ACV, hardest procurement. Decision driven by MLR (medical loss ratio) math and actuarial modeling.
- **Provider (hospital, IDN, health system).** Political, committee-driven, procurement-heavy. Decision driven by clinical outcomes, operational efficiency, and physician adoption.
- **Employer (self-insured employer group or benefits broker).** Benefits-committee driven. Decision driven by employee satisfaction, cost reduction, and ROI on benefits spend.
- **Consumer (patient or member).** Marketing-driven. Decision driven by experience, pricing, and clinical effectiveness.

Each buyer has different ROI framing, different sales cycle, different procurement process, different reference customers. The first strategic decision in healthcare GTM is which buyer you're actually selling to — most founders confuse "user" with "buyer" and end up selling the wrong value prop.

**Signature association:** Moderate — this is common healthcare GTM knowledge but Yoo articulates it cleanly.
**Conversational fit:** 15-20 minutes.
**Executive relevance:** High — foundational buyer clarity.
**Complementarity:** Layers under the 5 GTM motions.

### 4. The Clinical Validation Ladder

Yoo's framework for clinical evidence requirements by GTM motion:
- **DTC consumer:** Minimal clinical evidence needed; user reviews and outcomes stories suffice at launch.
- **SMB practice:** Case studies from similar practices; operational ROI data.
- **Self-insured employer:** Health economic modeling, real-world outcomes data, often a pilot with a benefits consultant.
- **Payer:** Actuarial modeling, RCT or peer-reviewed outcomes, multi-year data.
- **Hospital/IDN:** Clinical outcomes data, EHR-integration proof points, physician champion evidence.

The mismatch — e.g., pitching a payer with DTC-grade evidence — is a common failure mode. Evidence requirements should drive R&D investment.

**Signature association:** Moderate.
**Conversational fit:** 15-20 minutes.
**Executive relevance:** High for founders at the "we need pilot data" stage.

### 5. The Time-to-First-Paying-Enterprise-Customer Benchmark

Yoo publishes rough benchmarks by motion:
- **B2SMB:** 3-6 months to first paying customer, high volume of small deals.
- **B2C2B:** 12-18 months to first employer contract, often after significant consumer traction.
- **Risk-based:** 18-36 months for first payer contract (includes pilot, actuarial review, legal, IT integration).
- **Two-sided network:** 6-12 months to supply-side unlock, then demand-side ramp.
- **Distribution partnership:** 9-24 months to first GPO/IDN contract.

Founders who project hospital-system sales cycles onto SMB or DTC timelines (or vice versa) burn runway on the wrong motion. This benchmark keeps ambition grounded.

**Signature association:** Moderate — operator knowledge, not canonically "hers."
**Conversational fit:** 10-15 minutes as a calibrator.
**Executive relevance:** Critical for fundraising and runway planning.

## Signature Questions

- "Of the five GTM motions — B2C2B, B2SMB, risk-based, two-sided network, or distribution — which one are you actually running? Not aspirationally. Today."
- "Who is the buyer who writes the check? Payer, provider, employer, or consumer? If you can't name the buyer persona and their procurement process, we have work to do before GTM."
- "What's your clinical evidence today, and what does your chosen motion demand? B2SMB accepts case studies. Payer demands actuarial-grade outcomes. Mismatch = no close."
- "How long until first paying enterprise customer at your current motion? What's the comparable benchmark for that motion? If you've planned for 6 months and the benchmark is 18, your runway math is wrong."
- "Are you trying to run two motions simultaneously? Which one do you stage for later? Most digital health founders that fail fail because they couldn't commit to one."
- "What's your EHR / reimbursement / payer integration story? Integration friction is the #1 killer of digital health deals that look closed."
- "Who's your clinical champion inside the buyer? No champion, no close — healthcare procurement doesn't move without internal advocacy."

## Blind Spots Yoo Surfaces

- Treating "patient" or "member" as the buyer when the real buyer is the payer, employer, or provider procurement committee
- Running two GTM motions in parallel and starving both of focus and talent
- Underestimating the clinical evidence bar of the chosen motion (e.g., selling to payers with only SaaS-style case studies)
- Projecting SaaS sales cycle assumptions onto healthcare enterprise cycles (or vice versa for B2SMB)
- Ignoring integration surface — EHR, payer APIs, revenue cycle systems — until too late in the sales cycle
- Treating GPO and aggregator relationships as "just another partnership" rather than as a primary motion
- Building SaaS metrics (MRR, NRR, LTV:CAC) for risk-based businesses that should be measured on MLR impact, clinical outcomes, and per-member-per-month economics
- Confusing "pilot" with "paying customer" — healthcare pilots rarely convert without a pre-negotiated path to paid

## Failure Modes

Where the advisor approach breaks down:

- **Pure life-sciences / pharma / biotech startups.** The 5 GTM motions framework is for digital health software and services — not drug development, clinical trials, or medical devices. Redirect to domain-specific advisors (or note "out of scope").
- **Pre-product-market-fit consumer health startups.** Before PMF and early DTC traction, B2C2B is aspirational, not real. Redirect to Eric Ries / Sean Ellis for PMF work first.
- **Pure medical device or regulated-hardware startups.** FDA pathway and reimbursement code economics dominate the GTM — different framework, different advisors.
- **International healthcare systems.** The framework is US-centric (payer mix, GPO structure, employer self-insurance are US-specific). Adapt carefully for NHS, European single-payer, or emerging markets.
- **Founders who want validation, not diagnosis.** Yoo diagnoses motion mismatches sharply — if a founder is committed to running two motions and wants permission, she'll push back. This is a feature.
