# Michael Bierut — Research

The board's first visual identity advisor. Nobody else in the registry makes logos or decides how
a set of sibling brands look next to each other.

Research started from a brief written in another project on 2026-10-21. Four agents read mostly
interviews and summaries there, not his books. The facts below were checked again on 2026-10-21,
before the prompts were written. Each item says what was confirmed and where.

## Who he is

- Graphic designer. Worked for Massimo Vignelli before Pentagram. Pentagram partner in New York
  from 1990. In 2025 he stepped away from the partner role into what he calls "semi-retirement,"
  and took an advisory role at Pentagram.
  ([99% Invisible, Exit Interview](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/655-exit-interview-with-michael-bierut/),
  [Pentagram bio](https://pentagram.com/about/michael-bierut))
- Co-founder of Design Observer.

## Books (checked)

- *Seventy-nine Short Essays on Design*, Princeton Architectural Press, 2007.
- *How to use graphic design to sell things, explain things, make things look better, make people
  laugh, make people cry, and (every once in a while) change the world*, Harper Design (US) and
  Thames & Hudson (UK), November 2015. A career monograph of about 35 projects, each with sketches
  and rejected options. A revised edition later added Mastercard and Verizon.
- *Now You See It and Other Essays on Design*, Princeton Architectural Press. Released November
  2017; library records give 2018.
- Co-editor of the *Looking Closer: Critical Writings on Graphic Design* series (Allworth Press)
  with William Drenttel and Steven Heller, and D. K. Holland on early volumes. **The brief was
  wrong here in spirit: Jessica Helfand was a contributor, not an editor.** She is his Design
  Observer co-host.

## Work (checked)

- **MIT Media Lab, 2014**, with Aron Fay. Kept the seven-by-seven grid from Richard The's 2011
  logo, drew an "ML" monogram on it, then drew a mark for each of the 23 research groups on the
  same grid. Helvetica came back as the typeface. A fixed parent mark with related marks for each
  group. ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=579622),
  [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/pentagram-mit),
  [Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/mit-media-lab/story)) **Confirmed.**
- **Mastercard, 2016.** Two circles and the overlap, three flat colors.
  ([AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/pentagrams-michael-bierut-geeks-out-over-the-color-theory-behind-the-new-mastercard-logo/))
- **Museum of Arts and Design, 2008.** **The brief had this story wrong.** Bierut drew a logo made
  of one continuous line, after the building's architect. It didn't work with the letters M-A-D.
  He proposed changing the name to "A+D" so the idea would work. The client said no. He dropped
  his own idea and drew a monogram from squares and circles, taken from the building and from
  Columbus Circle. That one was approved unanimously. The lesson he draws is that an idea that
  can't be made to work isn't a good idea, not that a name should change to suit a logo.
  ([excerpt from *How to*](https://thecreativestore.co.nz/creative-talk/design/how-to-think-like-michael-bierut/),
  [Azure, DesignThinkers 2018](https://www.azuremagazine.com/article/michael-bierut-designthinkers-2018/),
  [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/museum-of-arts-and-design/story))
- **Hillary Clinton "H," 2015.** He wanted it "so simple, a kindergartener could make it with a
  pair of scissors." ([Azure](https://www.azuremagazine.com/article/michael-bierut-designthinkers-2018/))
- **Syracuse University.** "We were able to create a spine for all the materials they have." (Azure)
- **The New York Times building sign.** (Azure, 99% Invisible)
- Saks Fifth Avenue was in the brief and not checked this session. Kept out of the prompts.

## Borrowed sources for the frameworks (credit by name)

- **Sagi Haviv** (Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv). A mark should be **appropriate**, **distinctive
  and memorable**, and **simple**. Appropriate means it fits the organization's character, not
  that it explains the business. The "doodle test": can someone see it once or twice and draw it
  from memory. Simple enough to work at any size, on any surface. He judges marks on what works,
  not on taste. Confirmed in several summaries of his talks and his Skillshare class
  ("3 Criteria of Timeless Symbols"); his own wording of the second test is "distinctive and
  memorable." ([Logo Geek](https://logogeek.uk/podcast/sagi-haviv/),
  [Skillshare](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/designing-brand-symbols-the-principles-and-process-of-making-logos-that-last/404415631))
- **Wally Olins.** Three types of brand family: **monolithic** (one name and one look everywhere),
  **endorsed** (the sibling has its own name and look, and the parent shows behind it), **branded**
  (the sibling stands alone). ([Grapheine](https://www.grapheine.com/en/history-of-graphic-design/wally-olins-father-of-territoty-branding))
- **David Aaker.** One brand by default. A second brand needs a reason: does the parent add
  anything to the new product, and does the new product need its own name?
  ([Branding Strategy Insider](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/brand-architecture-for-competitive-advantage/))
- **Josef Albers**, *Interaction of Color*: "color deceives continually." A color is judged next to
  the colors around it. ([Albers Foundation](https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color))
  Bierut says the same thing in his own words about Mastercard, above.
- **Penguin Books.** Kept the frame (bands, spine, title panel) and changed the color by series.
- **Contrast numbers.** WCAG 2.x: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for graphics a
  person needs to see.

## Voice notes

- Self-deprecating story first, then the rule. He calls his own early work "so inept."
- Plain words: "hot mess," "deadeningly literal," "jazzed."
- Gives the client credit. Learned to "shut up and listen."
- Prefers plain over clever; enduring marks are "a lot of the time ... not clever."
- His modesty can read as not choosing. The persona commits to one mark and one direction, and says
  so.
