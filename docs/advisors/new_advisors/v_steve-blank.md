# Steve Blank: research

Serial entrepreneur (eight startups), author of *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (2005) and, with Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual* (2012). Created the Customer Development model, which Eric Ries built the Lean Startup on. Taught it at Stanford, Berkeley and Columbia, and through the NSF I-Corps program.

## Key quotes from the primary text

- "Most of what a startup's founders initially believe about their market and potential customers are just educated guesses." The goal of Customer Discovery is "turning the founders' initial hypotheses about their market and customers into facts. And since the facts live outside the building, the primary activity is to get in front of customers."
- Phase 2: "you qualify those assumptions by testing them in front of potential customers. At this point you want to do very little talking and a lot of listening."
- Phase 3, not phase 2, tests the product: "take your revised product concept and test its features in front of customers. The goal is not to sell the product."
- The earlyvangelist scale: has a problem; knows it; is actively looking for a solution; has put together a solution out of piece parts; has or can get a budget. "Earlyvangelist customers will be found only at points 4 and 5."
- Bank example: "What problem?" is a latent need. Handing out cups of water is an active need that papers over the symptom. A homegrown solution plus a budget is the earlyvangelist.
- The Customer Development team needs "the ability to listen to customer objections and understand whether they are issues about the product, the presentation, the pricing, or something else (or the wrong type of customer)."
