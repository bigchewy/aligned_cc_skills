## Selected Slot

Launch advisor for phases 1 and 8 of the launch-a-site playbook, running a `launch-bet` framework (see
`docs/plans/2026-10-20-find-advisor-launch-brief.md`). In phase 1 she sets the bet, runs the premortem, and
writes the pass line and quit line as a state and a date before the build. In phase 8 she reads one
customer's result against those lines and separates the decision from the luck.

Why her and not Eric Ries: Ries reads progress over time. She sets the quit line in advance and asks how much
of one result was luck. A single couple with fifty guests is what she calls a sample of one: "luck and/ or
incomplete information (and a sample size of one) intervened." ✅ [TIB]

Where the slot outruns her: picking and recruiting the first customer. She has nothing published on it. See
"What she does NOT publish."

## Master Concepts (encode these)

- **Two things decide how things turn out.** "Thinking in bets starts with recognizing that there are
  exactly two things that determine how our lives turn out:" "the quality of our decisions and luck." ✅
  [TIB] "Learning to recognize the difference between the two is what thinking in bets is all about." ✅ [TIB]
  Spoken form: "There's only two things that determine how our lives turn out." ✅ [CT]
- **Resulting.** Book: "Pete Carroll was a victim of our tendency to equate the quality of a decision with
  the quality of its outcome." "Poker players have a word for this: "resulting."" ✅ [TIB] Her spoken
  definition: "There's this word that we use in poker: "resulting." It's a really important word." Resulting
  is "creating too tight a relationship between the quality of the outcome and the quality of the
  decision." ✅ [NAUT] "And we tie the quality of the result back to the quality of the decision." ✅ [CT]
  "The problem is that we're all resulters at heart." ✅ [NAUT] "So the thing that we need to remember is
  that resulting is not a small mistake." ✅ [CT] Warning she got as a young player: "resist the temptation to
  change my strategy just because a few hands didn't turn out well" "in the short run." ✅ [TIB]
- **One result is a tiny sample.** "You can't use outcome quality as a perfect signal of decision quality,
  not with a small sample size anyway." ✅ [NAUT] "But one accident doesn't tell me much." ✅ [NAUT] "When we
  think probabilistically, we are less likely to use adverse results alone as proof" "that we made a decision
  error, because we recognize the possibility that the decision might have been good" "but luck and/ or
  incomplete information (and a sample size of one) intervened." ✅ [TIB] "But we don't know after one flip."
  ✅ [CT] "I have to play many, many, many hands before I actually know, do I actually have an edge?" ✅
  [LENNY-T] Skill changes how much a result tells you: "The outcome certainly tells you more and more and
  more the more skill there is in what you're doing." ✅ [CT]
- **Outcomes don't say what was your fault.** "Outcomes don't tell us what's our fault and what isn't, what we
  should take credit for and what we shouldn't." "A negative outcome could be a signal to go in and examine
  our decision-making." "That outcome could also be due to bad luck, unrelated to our decision, in which case"
  "treating that outcome as a signal to change future decisions would be a mistake." "It could also mean that
  we got lucky" ✅ [TIB]
- **The four-box grid (decision quality × outcome quality).** From *How to Decide*, via a summary only. 🟡
  [SF] Good decision, good outcome: "earned reward." Good decision, bad outcome: "bad luck." Bad decision, good
  outcome: "dumb luck." Bad decision, bad outcome: "just deserts." Her exact framing of the grid was not read.
  The launch brief's fourth label ("bad decision and bad result") should become "just deserts." Her own
  spoken line closest to the grid: "That doesn't make it a bad decision." ✅ [BSCI] (on losing an even-money
  bet at 75 percent)
- **Hindsight bias.** "Hindsight bias is the tendency, after an outcome is known, to see the outcome as having
  been inevitable." "When we say, "I should have known that would happen," or, "I should have seen it
  coming,"" "we are succumbing to hindsight bias." ✅ [TIB] "Any prediction that is not 0% or 100% can't be
  wrong solely because the most likely future doesn't unfold." "Long shots hit some of the time." ✅ [TIB]
- **Pete Carroll, Super Bowl XLIX.** Book: "Carroll got unlucky." "He had control over the quality of the
  play-call decision, but not over how it turned out." ✅ [TIB] Interview: "Think about the 2015 Super Bowl."
  "Pete Carroll has Russell Wilson throw and it's intercepted." "Do you remember what the headlines looked like
  the next day?" ✅ [NAUT] "The outcome was irrelevant to the decision quality." ✅ [NAUT] Her test of it:
  "Imagine that Pete Carroll called the exact same play." ✅ [CT] (and it scores). The interception odds she
  uses (about 2 percent, from NFL data) are in secondary summaries only. 🟡
- **Poker, not chess.** "They act like we're playing a game of chess when we're dealing with life's outcomes
  as opposed to playing a game of poker." ✅ [HARB] "Chess, for all its strategic complexity, isn't a great
  model for decision-making in life," "where most of our decisions involve hidden information and a much
  greater influence of luck." ✅ [TIB] "poker is much noisier than you think because when I win a hand, I have
  no idea why." ✅ [LENNY-T] "Did I play poorly and get lucky?" "Or did I play well?" ✅ [TIB]
- **"Wanna bet?" and confidence as a percentage.** "a lot of good can result from someone saying, "Wanna
  bet?"" "Offering a wager brings the risk out in the open, making explicit what is already implicit (and
  frequently overlooked)." ✅ [TIB] "By saying, "I'm 80%" and thereby communicating we aren't sure," "we open
  the door for others to tell us what they know." ✅ [TIB] "When confronted with new evidence, it is a very
  different narrative to say, "I was 58% but now I'm 46%."" "That doesn't feel nearly as bad as "I thought I
  was right but now I'm wrong."" ✅ [TIB] Spoken form: "It's so incredibly necessary in improving decision
  quality to take what's implicit and make it explicit." "If you don't make it explicit, then you don't get to
  find out when it's wrong." ✅ [LENNY-T]
- **Expected value.** "It's probability times payoff." ✅ [CT] "What matters is, is the next dollar that you
  put in the pot going to be positive expected value or not?" ✅ [LUX] "when the expected value is negative,
  we ought to walk away" ✅ [FR-POD] For founders: "'Should we pivot?' is essentially an expected value
  question." ✅ [FR-GUIDE] *Quit* teaches "how to think in expected value" 🟡 [QUIT-PRH] (publisher copy).
- **Kill criteria, set in advance.** "we write down a list of those signals and we call those 'kill
  criteria'." ✅ [PIMA] "So kill criteria are just a set of signals that you might see that would tell you
  that it's time to pivot or stop" ✅ [LENNY-T] "And now you have benchmarks, and those benchmarks now become
  what I call kill criteria." ✅ [FR-POD] Why in advance: "One of the best ways to become better at quitting is
  to think in advance." "don't trust yourself to be good at making decisions in the moment." ✅ [PIMA] "The
  worst time to make a decision is when you're in it." ✅ [LUX] (she credits Daniel Kahneman). Show-notes
  form: "Determine kill criteria in advance when you're not as likely to be swayed by the emotions of the
  moment." 🟡 [CFL] (host's notes)
- **States and dates.** Her words: "It's what I refer to as 'states and dates'." "So the best kill criteria
  contain a state and a date." "If I am not in this state by this date, I must quit." "If I am in this state by
  this date, then I can continue." ✅ [PIMA] On why a date: "that's why I think getting them to some date in
  the future works better." ✅ [FR-POD] On a deadline as the date: "So let's say that you set a deadline of
  say, three months." ✅ [GRANT]
- **A premortem only helps with a precommitment.** "So a pre-mortem is great, but only if you attach a
  pre-commitment with it." ✅ [LENNY-T] "Use the pre-mortem to now create structure around those signals that
  you've spotted and commit to actions" ✅ [LENNY-T] Her sales example: "Another one was the customer didn't
  want to demo, they only wanted to talk about price." ✅ [LENNY-T] That signal became a rule to drop the deal.
  🟡 [LENNY-T] (the fetch tool's summary)
- **Premortem and backcasting, from Gary Klein.** She credits Klein in the book: "In a Harvard Business Review
  article, decision scientist Gary Klein summarized the results of a 1989 experiment" "by Deborah Mitchell, J.
  Edward Russo, and Nancy Pennington." They "found that prospective hindsight—imagining that an event has
  already occurred—increases the ability" "to correctly identify reasons for future outcomes by 30%." ✅ [TIB]
  Backcasting: "Each person engaged in the planning imagines holding up a newspaper whose headline reads"
  (success) ✅ [TIB]; "Backcasting makes it possible to identify when there are low-probability events that
  must occur to reach the goal." ✅ [TIB] Premortem: "A premortem is an investigation into something awful,
  but before it happens." ✅ [TIB] "Once we frame the exercise as "Okay, we failed. Why did we fail?" that
  frees everyone to identify" "potential points of failure" ✅ [TIB] "Being a team player in a premortem isn't
  about being the most enthusiastic cheerleader;" "it's about being the most productive heckler." ✅ [TIB] Why
  both: "Without a premortem, we don't see as many paths to the future in which we don't reach our goals." ✅
  [TIB] Spoken form: "If I imagine it's six months from now and this project has failed" ✅ [FR-POD]; "Imagine
  it's a year from now, and you're really unhappy with this hire" ✅ [FR-GUIDE]. Her step of sorting premortem
  reasons into "my decisions" versus luck appears only in a library summary. 🟡
- **Monkeys and pedestals (Astro Teller, X).** "So he has a mental model, which I think is really useful,
  called monkeys and pedestals, and it goes like this." ✅ [CT] "The monkey is the unknown. It's the
  bottleneck." "Reason No. 1 is if you can't train the monkey, what's the point?" "Reason No. 2 is that the
  pedestal actually represents false progress." "building the pedestal first is going to stop you from
  quitting the project" ✅ [DELOITTE] "So his whole thing at X is you first have to identify: What are the
  monkeys?" ✅ [DELOITTE] "You should never start by seeing if you can build the pedestal." ✅ [CT] She calls
  Teller "Captain of Moonshots over at x at Google." ✅ [FR-POD]
- **By the time you think of quitting, you're late.** Her words: "by the time you're actually even thinking
  about quitting, it's probably already past the time that you ought to have quit." ✅ [LENNY-T] "usually past
  the time that you should have quit." ✅ [GRANT] "as Richard Thaler put it, most people won't quit until it
  actually isn't a decision." ✅ [LENNY-T] "Generally we won't quit until it's no longer a decision." ✅ [PIMA]
  "So we have this broad issue, which is that we tend to quit things too late." ✅ [CT] The Lenny takeaway
  "Know that if you're considering quitting, it's likely overdue" is Lenny's team's summary, not her words. 🟡
  [LENNY]
- **Quitting on time feels early.** Her words: "If you quit on time, meaning at the objectively right moment, it
  will feel like you quit too early — usually." ✅ [PIMA] Publisher copy: "If you quit on time, you will feel
  you quit early" ✅ [QUIT-PRH], [QUIT-HBS] (publisher description, so likely approved by her, but not her
  spoken words). The wording "Quitting on time will usually feel like quitting too early" was not found on
  any page.
- **Would you start this today?** Her words: "And what should matter is, knowing what you know today, would you
  start this today?" ✅ [CT] On Butterfield: "Would I start this today? That's a forecast of the future,
  right?" ✅ [LENNY-T] "if you wouldn't start this today, then that means that everything that you're putting
  into this going forward" (is the waste) ✅ [LENNY-T] To founders: "Imagine we were starting the company today
  — what would we do?" ✅ [FR-GUIDE] The Lenny takeaway "examine if you'd start the project today" is a
  summary. 🟡 [LENNY]
- **Sunk cost; waste looks forward.** "waste is a prospective problem, not a retrospective one" ✅ [LENNY-T]
  "But I can't quit now because I put in all this effort, and look at this pedestal I built." ✅ [DELOITTE]
  "Well, I couldn't fold 'cause I had too much money in the pot." ✅ [PIMA] "And that's the tragedy of sunk
  cost." ✅ [LUX] "we'll escalate our commitment to the losing cause." ✅ [PIMA]
- **Pivoting is quitting.** "I prefer the term quit because I don't think we should hide behind the term
  pivoting." "Pivoting is still quitting." ✅ [FR-POD] "If you were developing game never ending and then you
  pivot to Slack, that's quitting." ✅ [FR-POD] Quitting is not a locked door: "we think that quitting means

- **Identity.** "the hardest thing to walk away from is who you are." ✅ [PIMA]
- **Shorten the feedback loop.** "There is no such thing as a long feedback loop." "That is your choice to
  live in a long feedback loop, and you can choose to shorten the feedback loop." ✅ [LENNY-T] Her VC example

- **Keep a record of decisions.** "We need to create an evidentiary record, a way to Google our own

- **The paradox of experience.** "And that sets up the paradox of experience, which is that experience is
