# Aligned Plugin — Positioning Foundation

*Developed through April Dunford's 5 Components of Positioning framework, April 2026*

---

## Context

This positioning was developed for the open-sourcing of the aligned plugin repo. It serves as the strategic foundation for the README, onboarding docs, consulting pitch, and any public-facing content about the plugin. It complements (but is distinct from) Eric's personal brand positioning for coaching and AI enablement at ewp-site.

The key difference: Eric's consulting positioning leads with credibility through demonstration for warm referrals. This plugin positioning needs to work *cold* — someone lands on the GitHub repo with zero context and needs to immediately understand what they're looking at and why they should care.

## The Positioning

### Component 1: Competitive Alternatives

What would people do if this plugin didn't exist?

| Alternative | What happens | Why it's incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic LLM personas** | Ask ChatGPT to "be Steve Jobs." Get a costume, not a consultant. | No real methodology. No sequencing. No quality gates. No company context. Template-level output. |
| **DIY with Claude Code** | Know the tool exists, spend weeks tinkering without a system. | No encoded methodology. Trial and error. No compounding. |
| **Hire human consultants** | Get real expertise, but expensive, slow, and not always available. | Can't call April Dunford at 2 AM. Can't scale expert access across the whole team. |
| **Do nothing / status quo** | Manual knowledge work, tribal knowledge, inconsistent decisions. | Quality degrades as the company grows and the founder can't be in every room. |

### Component 2: Unique Attributes

What does this plugin have that the alternatives don't?

| Attribute | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **62+ expert advisor personas** | Not generic LLM roleplay. Real experts with their actual voice, frameworks, and methodology encoded. April Dunford pushes back on vague positioning. Steve Jobs demands simplicity. The personas have opinions and enforce them. |
| **135 encoded methodologies with quality gates** | Real frameworks (5 Components of Positioning, Jobs to Be Done, Leveraged Learning, etc.) made interactive and sequential. The system enforces the order, doesn't let you skip steps, and pushes back on vague answers. |
| **Institutional knowledge that compounds** | When a company layers in their own strategy, competitors, personas, and context, the advisors don't just know methodology — they know *your company*. This compounds over time. |
| **Opinionated workflow with quality gates** | TDD, root cause analysis, verification discipline, critique panels. The plugin has a point of view about how AI-assisted work should be done. |
| **Knowledge work, not just code** | Positioning, sales decks, brainstorming, business strategy, content generation, coaching frameworks. More than half the skills are knowledge work, not development. |

### Component 3: Value

**The headline value:**

> World-class advisors at your fingertips, available instantly 24/7, possessing intimate knowledge of your company, your role, and what you're trying to do.

**What that means concretely:**

- **For the leader:** Consistent, high-quality decisions at scale — your team gets world-class thinking without hiring world-class consultants for every function. The quality of decisions doesn't degrade as the company grows.
- **For the team:** Access to expert methodology on demand. A VP of Marketing can run a positioning exercise at 2 AM without the CEO or a $500/hour consultant in the room.
- **For the organization:** Institutional knowledge becomes executable rather than tribal. The system compounds — it gets more valuable as more company context is layered in.

**The hook and the close:**
- The **hook** is the advisor personas. "I get Steve Jobs and April Dunford on speed dial" — people immediately get it and want it.
- The **close** is the institutional knowledge compounding. That's what makes a CEO write a check for consulting — "my team makes better decisions even when I'm not in the room."

### Component 4: Target Customers

**Primary:** Leaders of growing companies who are making too many decisions themselves and want their teams to have access to world-class thinking without hiring world-class consultants for every function.

**Characteristics:**
- Growth mindset — believes there's a better way
- Feels the pain of inconsistent decisions as the company scales
- Open to AI-augmented work, not just AI-as-chatbot
- Currently accessed through technical founders (because Claude Code is the delivery mechanism), but the deeper characteristic is "scaling decision-maker," not "technical person"

**The trigger:** When a leader sees the advisor personas in action — particularly someone walking through a real framework like April Dunford's positioning or Clayton Christensen's Jobs to Be Done — that's the moment that flips them from "interesting tool" to "I need this."

**Anti-patterns (not for):**
- People looking for a prompt library or template collection
- Anyone who thinks the value is "better prompts for ChatGPT"
- Organizations not willing to invest in layering in their own institutional knowledge

### Component 5: Market Category

**Virtual Board of Advisors** (new category)

This is not an "AI dev tool" or a "prompt library" or a "Claude Code plugin." It's a virtual board of advisors — world-class experts with real methodologies, available 24/7, with intimate knowledge of your business.

**Category strategy:** New category creation. There's nothing to go head-to-head with, and "big fish, small pond" within "Claude Code plugins" undersells the value. "Virtual Board of Advisors" names what people already react to — it's the phrase that makes eyes light up.

**Why this works:** People already understand the concept of a board of advisors. The "virtual" modifier immediately communicates AI-powered. The frame triggers the right expectations: expert guidance, strategic thinking, always available. It differentiates from every other Claude Code plugin or AI tool.

## Advisory Panel Insights

Four advisors analyzed the repo during this exercise. Key insights worth preserving:

**Seth Godin:** The remarkable thing isn't the feature count — it's the *opinion*. The plugin has a spine, a worldview about how AI-assisted work should be done. That's what makes people talk about it. The tribe is leaders who believe AI should be rigorous, not just convenient. Open-sourcing is the smartest permission play — every install is a raised hand.

**Clayton Christensen:** The real job to be done is "close the gap between raw AI capability and reliable, repeatable output." This is disruptive in the precise sense — making structured thinking that used to cost $500K from McKinsey available at the marginal cost of an API call. Risk: breadth without a wedge. Narrow to the ten things the first five paying clients need.

**Rob Walling:** Open-source + consulting is viable but fragile if you become an "installation technician." Validate with three organizations measuring before-and-after. The SaaS play hiding in here is a managed platform for institutional knowledge as AI-consumable skill packs. Pick one wedge skill that delivers obvious ROI.

**Joe Pulizzi:** This is a content engine disguised as a GitHub repo. The content tilt — nobody else packages recognized methodologies into AI-native executable workflows — is real and defensible. But a GitHub star is not a subscriber. Build an audience (newsletter) alongside the repo. The repo is the proof, not the platform.

## How to Use This Document

- **README rewrite:** Lead with the one-paragraph positioning and the tagline. Replace "opinionated development stack" with "virtual board of advisors."
- **Onboarding docs:** Frame the getting-started experience around "meeting your advisors" rather than "installing a plugin."
- **Consulting pitch:** The repo demonstrates the hook (advisors). The consulting engagement delivers the close (institutional knowledge + cultural transformation).
- **Content/blog:** Each advisor + framework combination is a natural content piece. "How to run a positioning exercise with April Dunford at 2 AM" writes itself.
- **ewp-site integration:** Update the AI enablement positioning to reference this plugin positioning. They share the same thesis (systems + mindset) but speak to different audiences.
