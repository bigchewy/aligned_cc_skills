# [Company] Leadership Meeting

90 minutes, [day and time].

## Purpose

Drive the short-range actions that move [Company] toward its strategy, and decide what gets done this week and by whom.

## Ten rules for highly effective meetings

1. Respect the time.
2. Defreeze the group to start and reinforce at the end.
3. State the meeting purpose up front.
4. Call out who is the decision maker and who is the facilitator.
5. Gather data from the team before generating insights and making a decision.
6. Assign the who, what and when.
7. Attendees should reach the minimum critical mass.
8. Avoid meetings just to have a meeting.
9. Record meetings and use them to build shared consciousness.
10. Instill a bias for action. It's fine to disagree and commit.

## Roles

- Decision maker: [name]. Ranks the questions to answer in step 3.
- Facilitator: rotates each week. The facilitator prepares the agenda, runs it and keeps time. The CEO does not facilitate.
- Note taker: last week's facilitator. Writes down action items, questions and decisions during the meeting.

## A day before the meeting

- Each key action owner posts a written update.
- The decision maker posts the overall update. It is the narrative for the dashboard review.
- The facilitator copies this agenda, moves the questions from the updates into Questions to answer, and lists the action items that are due.
- Everyone reads the updates before the meeting.

## Agenda

### 1. Set the stage

- Facilitator this week: [name]
- Read the meeting purpose.
- Each person says, in 1 minute or less: one fear or frustration, and one win or gratitude.

### 2. Progress review

A. Dashboard review. Open the dashboard for each key action. A green key action with a date gets credit and no discussion. Yellow, red and moved dates are already in Questions to answer. Capture anything new there. Don't discuss it yet.

B. Review due action items, including the changes from last meeting's team page. Done or not done, with brief context from the implementer. Anything not done goes into Questions to answer.

C. Anything unspoken. Anything not yet discussed, anything that seems high priority, and any new big decision. Capture each one in Questions to answer.

### 3. Prioritize

The decision maker puts Questions to answer in order, most important first.

### 4. Work the questions to answer

Work them in that order. Name the obstacle a fix answers before agreeing on the fix. Each question ends as a new action item, a recorded decision, or a Parking lot topic.

### 5. Close

Read back the decisions and action items. Then appreciations and gratitude.
