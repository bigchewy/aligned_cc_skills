# Examples: Find Potential Advisors Done Right

These come from a real run on 2026-10-28, a search for consumer-virality advisors, after it was
redone properly. Use them to see how much evidence each part needs.

## An evaluation that counts what it saw

The evaluator was asked about Jonah Berger and Andrew Chen. It opened each episode page and
copied the length shown there.

Sum: 66 + 44 + 48 + 23 = 181 minutes, or 3.0 hours.

**Not found:** Talks at Google "Contagious" (2013), Maximize Your Influence ep. 163 and HBR
IdeaCast 354 were checked, but their pages show no length, so they're left out of the sum. The
Talks at Google talk was re-released as a podcast; that copy is a duplicate and isn't counted.
Curious Minds CM 017 returned 410.

Why this is right:
- Every length comes from a page that was opened, with its URL.
- Both snippets were wrong, one by 20 minutes and one by 15. That's why snippets don't count.
- Duplicates and pages with no length are named, not quietly dropped or guessed.
- The total is shown as a sum, so anyone can check it.

## A profile that keeps memory out of the facts

From the same run, Andrew Chen:

> **Credentials.** General partner at a16z, investing through a16z Speedrun
> (https://andrewchen.com). The page doesn't mention Uber, but the "growth team lessons from
> Uber" essay is listed there. I did not verify the Uber employment itself.

Why this is right: the widely repeated fact ("ran growth at Uber") wasn't on a page that was
opened, so it's flagged and kept out of the credentials. In the template, it goes in "What was
skipped or not verified."

## A "What was skipped or not verified" section with something in it

From the corrected brief:

- Jonah Berger's audio is 3.0 hours on checked pages. Several of his big talks show no length, so
  3.0 is a floor, not the real total.
- Andrew Chen's Tim Ferriss episode is counted at 2h05m, the last timestamp in its transcript. The
  episode page shows no length.
- Chen's time at Uber is from memory, not checked.
- No gift company has published how many writers or recipients go on to buy one.

Why this is right: the user can see exactly how sure each rating is, without asking.

## The checkpoint when there are too few strong candidates

From a pediatric eczema search on 2026-10-07: the top experts write guidelines and trial papers,
not books for the public, and their talks are for doctors. Nobody reached High. The right move is
to say so and give the user the choices: widen the search, lower the bar, or go ahead with the
best candidate. Don't lift someone's rating to make the list look stronger.
