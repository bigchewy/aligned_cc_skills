Jobs

  1. Test your riskiest guess before you build

    Leap of Faith Assumptions · Eric Ries

    Identifying and testing the riskiest assumptions underlying a business before building anything.

    /aligned:use-framework leap-of-faith-assumptions
    Other ways to do this
    • Build-Measure-Learn · Eric Ries

      Designing a learning cycle that starts with what you need to learn, then works backward to what to measure and build.

      /aligned:use-framework build-measure-learn
    • Launch Bet · Annie Duke

      Treating a launch with one real customer as one bet, writing the success line and the quit line as a state and a date before the build, and judging that customer's result apart from the luck.

      /aligned:use-framework launch-bet

  2. Find out which problems customers will pay to solve

    Customer Problem Presentation · Steve Blank

    Building a short presentation of the problems they think a customer has, so a customer meeting draws answers out of the customer instead of pitching to them.

    /aligned:use-framework customer-problem-presentation
    Other ways to do this
    • Earlyvangelists · Eric Ries

      Specifically, whether they've identified customers who will actually buy versus those who are simply being polite.

      /aligned:use-framework earlyvangelists
    • Four Market Types · Eric Ries

      Diagnosing which market type you're entering and aligning your tactics to match.

      /aligned:use-framework four-market-types

  3. Map what the user does start to finish, and slice the first release

    User Story Map · Jeff Patton

    Building a shared, visual map of what a user does start to finish on one timeline, walking it aloud to find the holes, slicing it into releases by outcome, then breadboarding Slice 1 with Ryan Singer and handing it to Brad Frost for screens, in one session.

    /aligned:use-framework user-story-map

  4. Check whether customers would miss the product if it went away

    Product-Market Fit Test · Sean Ellis

    A diagnostic to determine if you should scale or iterate based on user love, not vanity metrics.

    /aligned:use-framework product-market-fit-test

  5. Find out why users don't do what the product asks

    3B Framework (Behavior, Barriers, Benefits) · Kristen Berman

    A behavioral-science diagnostic for product behavior problems that names the specific target behavior, audits five categories of barriers, and tests benefit salience to surface the single highest-leverage experiment.

    /aligned:use-framework 3b-behavioral-design
