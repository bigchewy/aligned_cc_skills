Sets the strategy, builds the leadership team, and makes the calls nobody else can make.

Jobs

  1. Write a strategy that names what is hard and what you will do about it

    Kernel of Good Strategy · Richard Rumelt

    The irreducible core that distinguishes actual strategy from goal-setting dressed up as strategy.

    /aligned:use-framework kernel-of-good-strategy
    Other ways to do this
    • Finding the Crux · Richard Rumelt

      Identifying the single most critical challenge that unlocks progress.

      /aligned:use-framework finding-the-crux
    • Proximate Objectives · Richard Rumelt

      Setting objectives that are achievable and create momentum for harder goals.

      /aligned:use-framework proximate-objectives
    • Lifecycle Strategy · Lex Sisney

      Finding out where each product, its market, and the company's ability to execute actually sit on their lifecycles, and whether the three are aligned.

      /aligned:use-framework lifecycle-strategy
    • Blue Ocean Value Props · Kim & Mauborgne

      Deciding what one offering will eliminate, reduce, raise, and create so that its value curve stands apart from every alternative buyers have today.

      /aligned:use-framework blue-ocean-value-props
    • Working Backwards · Jeff Bezos

      Starting from the customer experience and writing the press release before building anything.

      /aligned:use-framework working-backwards

  2. Make the big calls, and know which ones you can undo

    Type 1 vs Type 2 Decisions · Jeff Bezos

    Classifying decisions as irreversible or reversible and matching the appropriate speed and rigor to each.

    /aligned:use-framework type-1-type-2-decisions
    Other ways to do this
    • Believability-Weighted Decision Making · Ray Dalio

      Weighting opinions by track record and explanatory logic to improve decision quality.

      /aligned:use-framework believability-weighted-decision-making
    • First Principles Thinking · Elon Musk

      Breaking a problem down to fundamental truths and reasoning up from there instead of by analogy.

      /aligned:use-framework first-principles-thinking
    • Fear-Setting · Tim Ferriss

      The Stoic exercise of premeditatio malorum that you call "the most valuable exercise I do every month."

      /aligned:use-framework fear-setting
    • Regret Minimization Framework · Jeff Bezos

      Making major life decisions by projecting to age 80 and asking which choice you would regret not taking.

      /aligned:use-framework regret-minimization

  3. Decide whether to change course or keep going

    Pivot or Persevere · Eric Ries

    A structured decision process for determining whether to change strategy, stay the course, or stop entirely.

    /aligned:use-framework pivot-or-persevere
    Other ways to do this
    • Do Things That Don't Scale · Paul Graham

      Identifying the unscalable work a founder should be doing right now to learn and recruit users by hand.

      /aligned:use-framework do-things-that-dont-scale
    • Earnestness Filter · Paul Graham

      Pressure-testing whether a founder is genuinely earnest about their startup or just performing.

      /aligned:use-framework earnestness-filter

  4. Find why a problem keeps coming back

    Root Cause Analysis · Coach Eric

    A four-phase diagnostic process, Gap → Obstacles → Root Causes → Solutions, that produces a defensible diagnostic document.

    /aligned:use-framework root-cause-analysis
    Other ways to do this
    • 6-Step Process · Coach Eric

      A structured approach to solving operational problems that starts with a mindset check before goal-setting, diagnosis, and planning.

      /aligned:use-framework 6-step-process

  5. Look at your own part in what keeps going wrong

    100% Responsibility · Diana Chapman

    Taking full ownership of your contribution to a situation without blame or shame.

    /aligned:use-framework 100-percent-responsibility
    Other ways to do this
    • Above/Below the Line · Diana Chapman

      Locating yourself on the line, working the Drama Triangle, and shifting through tailored willingness questions.

      /aligned:use-framework above-below-the-line
    • Four Pillars of Integrity · Jim Dethmer

      Helping them identify where their integrity is fractured and restore full energetic wholeness.

      /aligned:use-framework pillars-of-integrity

  6. Spend your week on the work only you can do

    Zone of Genius discovery process · Coach Eric

    Identifying your unique genius and distinguishing it from the Zone of Excellence trap where most high achievers get stuck.

    /aligned:use-framework zone-of-genius
    Other ways to do this
    • 80/20 Audit · Tim Ferriss

      A ruthless Pareto analysis of their work and life to identify what to double down on and what to eliminate.

      /aligned:use-framework 80-20-audit
    • Personal Resource Allocation · Clayton Christensen

      Exposing the gap between where you say your priorities are and where your time and energy actually go.

      /aligned:use-framework personal-resource-allocation

  7. Look back on the past year and decide what to keep doing

    Past Year Review (PYR) · Tim Ferriss

    A data-driven alternative to vague New Year's resolutions.

    /aligned:use-framework past-year-review
    Other ways to do this
    • The Story So Far · Coach Eric

      A reflective tour through a user's session history that surfaces breakthrough moments, patterns, and growth.

      /aligned:use-framework the-story-so-far

  8. Give your coach the full picture of you and your company

    Professional Context Intake · Coach Eric

    A structured intake that maps business context, expertise, challenges, and decision style to build a professional brief.

    /aligned:use-framework professional-context-intake
    Other ways to do this
    • Personal Context Intake · Coach Eric

      A structured intake that maps health, emotional patterns, growth background, relationships, and values to build a personal client file.

      /aligned:use-framework personal-context-intake
