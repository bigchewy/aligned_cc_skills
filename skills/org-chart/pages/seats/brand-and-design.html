Brand and design

MarketingB2B: 1 personB2C: 2 peopleCovered

Owns the logo, the visual identity, and the voice.

Jobs

  1. Find a logo in something the product already has

    Boil It Down · Michael Bierut

    Finding a mark in something the product already has, throwing out the category cliché, and cutting it down until it looks almost unfinished, so the result is one mark described well enough to draw.

    /aligned:use-framework boil-it-down
    Other ways to do this
    • The Small-Size and Print Test · Michael Bierut

      Running a mark through Sagi Haviv's three tests, the draw-it-from-memory test, the smallest and hardest places it has to live, one-color print, and Josef Albers's rule for color, and ending with pass, fix, or redraw for each.

      /aligned:use-framework small-size-and-print-test

  2. Decide how a new product looks next to the old one

    How Much the Siblings Share · Michael Bierut

    Deciding how a new product looks next to the product it came from, by picking one of three types of brand family and then listing exactly which of name, mark, type, and color the sibling shares.

    /aligned:use-framework how-much-siblings-share

  3. Write down how the company sounds

    Brand Voice · Steve Krug

    Extracting a brand voice file from 3 to 10 samples of existing copy: tone principles, a register table, dos and don'ts, banned phrases and terms, a glossary, and how to name competitors.

    /aligned:use-framework brand-voice

  4. Find the feeling that makes people lean in

    The Carousel · Don Draper

    Finding the feeling behind a product: one real person, what they feel, a true moment from their story, and a one-sentence idea an ad or page can carry.

    /aligned:use-framework the-carousel
