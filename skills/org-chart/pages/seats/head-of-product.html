Head of product

Product and designB2B: 1 personB2C: 1 personCovered

Decides what the product does, what it stops doing, and what gets built this quarter.

Jobs

  1. Decide what the team builds next quarter

    Frame, Shape, Bet · Ryan Singer

    Turning a list of planned work into a dated roadmap of problem areas and one shaped, committed bet, by setting how much time each piece of work is worth before anyone designs it.

    /aligned:use-framework frame-shape-bet

  2. Cut a feature list down to the half that matters

    Getting Real Cut · Jason Fried

    Taking a feature list, a spec, or a design and cutting it to the half that kicks ass, built from its epicenter outward, before anyone builds it.

    /aligned:use-framework getting-real-cut
    Other ways to do this
    • Focus Through Saying No · Steve Jobs

      A ruthless focus exercise that forces a not-doing list by cutting everything that isn't the single top priority.

      /aligned:use-framework focus-through-saying-no
    • Four-Quadrant Focus · Steve Jobs

      Clarifying product portfolio using a 2x2 grid of your key dimensions.

      /aligned:use-framework four-quadrant-focus

  3. Find out what customers hire the product to do

    Jobs to Be Done · Clayton Christensen

    Uncovering the functional, emotional, and social progress a customer is hiring a product to make.

    /aligned:use-framework jobs-to-be-done

  4. Decide what the product does that no rival does

    Blue Ocean Value Props · Kim & Mauborgne

    Deciding what one offering will eliminate, reduce, raise, and create so that its value curve stands apart from every alternative buyers have today.

    /aligned:use-framework blue-ocean-value-props

  5. Start from what the customer sees and work back to the build

    Start With Experience · Steve Jobs

    Designing products by starting with customer experience and working backwards.

    /aligned:use-framework start-with-experience
