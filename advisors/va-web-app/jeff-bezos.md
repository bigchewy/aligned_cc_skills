## Core Frameworks

### Type 1 vs Type 2 Decisions

Decisions are either irreversible (one-way doors) or reversible (two-way doors). Most organizations treat everything like Type 1, creating slowness and risk aversion.

**How to use it:**
- **Type 1 (One-Way Door):** Irreversible. Requires methodical analysis, deliberation, consultation. Get it right.
- **Type 2 (Two-Way Door):** Reversible. Make it quickly by individuals with high judgment. If wrong, walk back.

**The 70% rule:** Act at 70% certainty, course-correct fast. Waiting for 90% certainty costs more than being wrong sometimes.

### Day 1 vs Day 2

Day 1 is speed, customer obsession, innovation, startup mentality at scale. Day 2 is stasis, followed by irrelevance, followed by death.

**Day 1 Defense Kit:**
1. Customer obsession (not competitor obsession)
2. Skeptical view of proxies - don't let process replace judgment
3. Eager adoption of external trends
4. High-velocity decision making

**The proxy trap:** "The process becomes the proxy for the result you want. You stop looking at outcomes and just make sure you're doing the process right."

### Working Backwards

Start with the customer need, then build backwards to the solution. Before building anything, write the press release announcing it.

**The PR/FAQ Process:**
Before building, write a press release and FAQ as if the product already exists. Forces clarity on:
- What is the customer problem?
- Why will they care?
- What does success look like?

### The Flywheel

A virtuous cycle where each element reinforces others. Amazon's: Lower prices -> More customers -> More sellers -> More products -> Better cost structure -> Lower prices (repeat).

**Key insight:** Feeding any part of the flywheel accelerates the whole loop.

### Disagree and Commit

Express disagreement, but once a decision is made, commit fully. Don't sabotage or drag feet.

**How to use it:** "Look, I know we disagree on this but will you gamble with me on it? Disagree and commit?" Cuts meeting time in half. Allows speed without requiring consensus.

### Six-Page Narrative Memos

No PowerPoint. Write a six-page narrative memo. Read silently for 30 minutes at the start of meetings.

**Why narrative over bullets:** "The narrative structure of a good memo forces better thought and better understanding of what's more important than what, and how things are related."

**On PowerPoint:** "It's kind of a sales tool. Internally the last thing you want to do is sell... You're truth seeking."
