## Core Frameworks

### Radical Truth and Radical Transparency

Everything that matters is put on the table - mistakes, problems, weaknesses. No hiding, no spin, no politics. This isn't niceness - it's effectiveness. You can't fix what you can't see.

**How to use it:**
- State problems and mistakes directly without softening
- Record decisions for later review
- Expect 18 months for new people to adapt - "the two yous" fight this
- Distinguish what must be transparent from what stays private

**The paradox:** "Like going to a nudist camp" - uncomfortable at first, then liberating.

### Believability-Weighted Decision Making

Not all opinions are equal. Weight input by track record AND ability to explain the cause-effect logic. Not democracy, not autocracy - meritocracy weighted by believability.

**How to use it:**
1. Who has actually accomplished this before? (not adjacent experience - THIS thing)
2. Can they explain their reasoning? (success without logic might be luck)
3. Weight input accordingly
4. When believability-weighted and equal-weighted votes conflict, go with believability

### Pain + Reflection = Progress

Pain is a signal, not something to avoid. AFTER the pain, reflect: What caused this? What principle emerges?

**How to use it:**
1. Experience failure or pain - don't suppress it
2. After the acute moment, ask: "What happened? Why?"
3. Trace the cause-effect chain
4. Extract a principle: "In situations like X, I should do Y because of Z"
5. Write it down. Test it. Refine it.

**The trap:** Most people wish they didn't have problems. That's wrong-headed. Problems are opportunities for principles.

### The Principles Stack

Values -> Principles -> Algorithms -> Decisions. Each level translates the one above into something more operational.

**How to use it:**
1. Clarify the value at stake
2. Articulate principles that serve that value
3. Translate to operational rules
4. Test the algorithm against past decisions
5. Run alongside your judgment, compare, refine

### The Machine Mental Model

Everything operates like a machine with cause-effect relationships. You can be a cog OR zoom out and design the machine.

**How to use it:**
1. Describe your situation as a machine: inputs, processes, outputs
2. Compare outputs to goals - where's the gap?
3. Diagnose: problem in the design or the people running it?
4. Adjust the machine rather than just working harder
5. Build feedback loops to detect drift
