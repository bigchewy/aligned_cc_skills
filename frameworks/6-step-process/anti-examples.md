# 6-Step Process Anti-Examples

### Sharing your list before the person writes

**User:** "80% in-office attendance by the end of Q2, up from 40%."

**Wrong:** "From your documents, I'd expect commuting and unclear expectations to be big ones. Write your own list, then say done."

**Right:** "Write your own list of what stands between you and this goal. Use paper or a note, not here, and don't tell me what's on it. If I share first, you'll gravitate toward what I said. Say "done" when you have it."

> Once the person hears your list, their list drifts toward it. Your list comes after "done," for obstacles, solutions and both sets of picks.

### Offering hints before "done"

**User:** "What kinds of things should I be writing down?"

**Wrong:** "Think about people, process, resources and incentives."

**Right:** "A hint would pull your list toward mine. Write whatever comes first, and say "done" when you have it."

> Categories are hints. They shape the list as much as examples do.

### Treating a typed list as if you never saw it

**User:** "My list is commuting, noise and managers not enforcing the policy."

**Wrong:** "Thanks. Those cover it, so let's pick the ones that matter most."

**Right:** "I'll take those as yours, and I've now heard them. The documents and the goal suggest these as well: expectations are unclear, and there's no reason to come in since meetings are still remote. What would you change, and what's missing?"

> Say you heard it, and still show your own.

### Leaving a solution untied

**User:** "Let's also run a monthly pulse survey. I don't know which obstacle it goes with."

**Wrong:** "Fine, I'll leave it in the tray and we can move on to picking."

**Right:** "Which obstacle does it get past? One on the map is that some people feel less productive in the office, and the survey would show how many. If none fits, we may have missed an obstacle."

> Solutions can come in any order, but every one is tied by the end of 4a.

### Pushing back on someone who chose to continue

**User:** "I'm below the line, and I want to continue anyway."

**Wrong:** "I'd push back once. You said you're frustrated, and a plan made from there will be compromised. Are you sure?"

**Right:** "What outcome do you want, with a number if you can? From what, to what, by when?"

> The person decides. You offer `above-below-the-line` once, and you don't argue.

### Testing someone who says they're above the line

**User:** "Above."

**Wrong:** "You sounded a little frustrated earlier. Are you sure?"

**Right:** "What outcome do you want, with a number if you can? From what, to what, by when?"

> If they say above, trust them.

### Presenting your list as Eric's view

**Wrong:** "Eric would say the real obstacle is that you haven't defined what the office is for."

**Right:** "The documents and the goal suggest this one: you haven't defined what the office is for."

> Coach Eric runs the process. The list comes from the documents and the goal.

### Root-causing every obstacle, or a fixed number of rounds

**Wrong:** "We have six obstacles, so let's take each in turn and ask why five times."

**Right:** "You and I both picked commuting and the lack of a reason to come in. Why don't people want to commute?"

> Ask why on the picks only, and stop when the person can act on the answer.

### Rewriting the map without reading it first

**Wrong:** Writing the Step 5 rows over the file from what you remember, which wipes out the obstacle the person renamed on the page.

**Right:** Reading the JSON in the file first, seeing it differs from `baseline`, building on their version, and saying in one line what you kept.

> The file is the source of truth. The person can edit it between any two of your writes.

### Publishing the map

**User:** "Can you make this look nice?"

**Wrong:** Building a page from the map and publishing it to claude.ai.

**Right:** "The map stays a file on your machine, and it's yours to show to whoever you want. Tell me what you'd change on it."

> The map is a local file and is never published.

### Ending without a feedback check

**User:** "I have my first moves, so I'll go do them."

**Wrong:** "You've got a goal, root causes and first moves. Go make it happen."

**Right:** "Which number will you check, when, and what result sends you back to redraw this map?"

> Step 6 is part of the map. Without it, nobody knows when to come back.
