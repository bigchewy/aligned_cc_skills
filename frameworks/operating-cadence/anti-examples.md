# Operating Cadence Anti-Examples

### Inventing a key action

**User:** "We don't have a strategy. What should our key actions be?"

**Wrong:** "A company at your stage usually needs revenue growth, a hiring plan and a product launch. Let's use those as 1, 2 and 3."

**Right:** "I won't write them for you. They're your strategy. What must get done this quarter? Name up to four, and one owner for each. I'll mark them draft."

> Every action item ties to a key action. If Claude guesses them, the whole team works on a guess, and the meeting looks like it is on strategy when it is not.

### Inventing a workstream

**User:** "I don't have a list of workstreams. Just make one up."

**Wrong:** "Sure. I'll set up Sales, Product, Engineering and Support, and give each an owner."

**Right:** "I won't write your workstreams for you. Tell me who writes a WBR each week and what they own. On a small team, each key action owner can be a workstream."

> A guessed workstream gets a WBR from nobody, and the page shows a hole that was never real.

### Running past a stop

**User:** "Northwind Supply. Five of us attend."

**Wrong:** "Great. Now the key actions, the owners, the dashboards, the day and time, and the tool. Tell me all of those."

**Right:** "Five people. Who makes the final call when the group can't agree?" Then stop.

> The setup runs one question at a time. Each answer shapes the next, and a person who answers five questions in one message skips the ones that needed thought.

### Writing to the tool before approval

**User:** "We use Asana. Key actions are 1 and 2."

**Wrong:** "I've created two projects and an agenda in Asana."

**Right:** "Here's what I plan to create: [list]. I write nothing until you say so. Do you approve this?"

> The framework writes only after a person approves, and it never sets an owner or assignee. Undoing a wrong write in someone's tool costs more than one more question.

### Retyping the template from memory

**User:** "Show me the template."

**Wrong:** A rewritten agenda with six steps and a round where everyone talks.

**Right:** Read `skills/_shared/operating-cadence/meeting-template.md` and show it with the brackets filled in. If the person wants a change, make that change and leave the rest as written.

> The template has five steps, 90 minutes and ten rules. A copy from memory drifts, and the feedback job grades against the real one.

### Editing the team page template

**User:** "Can you add a column for budget to the team page?"

**Wrong:** Editing `team-page-template.html` before publishing it.

**Right:** "The page is published as it is, so every team's page works the same way and the skills can read and write it. Keep the budget in your own dashboard and put the link on the key action."

> The skills read and write fixed rows. An edited page breaks them without a warning.

### Publishing before the person approves

**User:** "Yes, the template looks right."

**Wrong:** Publishing the team page and writing its rows right away.

**Right:** Show the rows that go on the page and ask for approval. Then publish and write.

> Approving the template is not approving the page. The page is shared with the whole team.

### Letting status back into the meeting

**User:** "Can we add a round where everyone gives a quick status?"

**Wrong:** "Sure, I'll add a step 2D for verbal status."

**Right:** "Status is why the WBRs come a day before. A green key action with a date gets credit and no discussion in step 2A. What are you hoping the round would catch? We can check whether the WBRs or Questions to answer already cover it."

> Verbal status takes back the time the template frees for decisions.
