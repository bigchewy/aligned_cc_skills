# Power Progression Check — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Walking all seven powers before placing the business

**User:** "We run a subscription box for specialty teas, about 900 subscribers after a year. Which of the seven powers do we have?"

**Wrong:**
"Let's go through all seven. Scale economies: possibly, as volume grows. Network economies: maybe through referrals. Counter-positioning: perhaps. Switching costs: some. Branding: your packaging is strong. Cornered resource: your supplier relationships. Process power: your curation process."

**Right:**
"Let's not do all seven; that conversation always goes too long. First, where are you? A year in, 900 subscribers, still learning who stays: that's origination. So branding and process power come off the table now; they only come at stability. The first question for you is what your subscribers stopped doing to buy from you, and whether whoever they used before could copy you without hurting their own business."

**What to do instead:** Place the business in one phase first. Then strike what that phase can't offer, and spend the time on the one or two questions that phase opens.

---

## 2. Accepting a brand claim at origination

**User:** "Our moat is our brand. We've built a loyal community on social media for our running shoes. We're eight months old."

**Wrong:**
"That's great. A loyal community is a powerful brand moat, and it will be hard for competitors to replicate."

**Right:**
"A loyal community is worth a lot, and I don't want to be unkind. But at eight months, branding isn't available yet; it only comes at stability, after many years of consistent behavior. Recognition you paid for isn't Power. I'll put branding under 'struck,' and we'll look at what could protect you sooner. If you're sure customers pay you more than they'd pay for an identical shoe, we can test that claim with `benefit-and-barrier-test`."

**What to do instead:** Strike branding and process power before stability, say why in one line, and offer the hand-off if the user wants to defend the claim.

---

## 3. Calling growth a power

**User:** "We're growing 20% a month. Doesn't that mean we have scale economies?"

**Wrong:**
"Yes, at that growth rate you're clearly building scale economies, and that will become your moat."

**Right:**
"Growth is what takeoff looks like, and in takeoff, operational excellence is everything. But scale economies don't come from growing; they come from a big fixed cost spread over more customers than your rival has. What's the large fixed cost in your business, and are you ahead of the next competitor on it? If there isn't one, scale economies aren't plausible, however fast you grow."

**What to do instead:** Tie scale economies, switching costs and network economies to scale relative to competitors, and ask what the mechanism is, not how fast the line goes up.

---

## 4. Taking "they're too slow" as counter-positioning

**User:** "We're a digital-only insurance broker. The big brokers won't copy us because they're dinosaurs."

**Wrong:**
"Exactly, that's counter-positioning. Incumbents are too slow to respond, so you're protected."

**Right:**
"They may be slow today. But counter-positioning isn't about speed; it's about what copying you would cost them. Would a digital-only offer cannibalize commission they earn through their branch network now? If yes, that's plausible counter-positioning. If they'd lose nothing, you're pretty high risk from an incumbent who already has the capabilities. Let's write it as 'not shown yet' and, if you want, test it with `counter-positioning-test`."

**What to do instead:** Ask what the incumbent would lose by copying. Mark counter-positioning plausible only when copying hurts the incumbent's existing business.

---

## 5. Ending with a list instead of one power and a sign

**User:** "So what should we focus on?"

**Wrong:**
"Focus on building network effects, increasing switching costs, achieving scale, and investing in your brand over time. All of these will strengthen your position."

**Right:**
"One: switching costs. The sign it's forming: customers with a year of history in your system cancel at a quarter of the rate of new ones. We look again in six months, or when you pass 200 customers, whichever comes first. Everything else stays struck or waiting."

**What to do instead:** Name one power to aim for (two at most), a condition someone can count, and a date or trigger for the next check.
