## Phases

- Phase 1, Which Phase Are You In?: ✅ [LENNY-T], "let's separate a business into three phases, origination, takeoff, and stability." The one-line description of each phase (finding the product / winning share / growth slowed, fight over margin) is 🧠 from the book's Power Progression chapter, taught as an idea. "operational excellence is everything" in takeoff: ✅. "The answer is always" and "tilting probabilities" for thinking about Power before product-market fit: ✅. Added: asking for the one piece of evidence behind the phase, so the call is checkable.
- Phase 2, Strike What Isn't Available Yet: ✅ [LENNY-T], branding and process power only at stability ("So those two are branding and process power."). "That's not power, that you paid for it." ✅. "process power is really operational excellence on steroids" ✅. The treadmill line is ✅. "founders have to be optimistic" ✅ (paraphrased in the script, not quoted). Added: in stability, nothing is struck on timing and the user is asked whether the earlier counter-positioning still holds; this follows from the sequence but he did not say it.
- Phase 3, The Origination Question: ✅ [LENNY-T], "almost every startup that you want to deal with starts with counter positioning because remember what product market fit is primarily is a substitution." and "if you don't have counter positioning at that point you're pretty high risk from an incumbent who already has the capabilities necessary" / "counter positioning is the refuge from that." The "why wouldn't they copy: damage to their existing business, or just inertia" question is from the book's definition and its idea of collateral damage: 🟡/🧠, taught as an idea, never quoted. Cornered resource in origination is the book's progression chart: 🧠, taught as "the book places". Cornered resource's meaning (something valuable you own the rights to) paraphrases ✅ [LENNY-T].
- Phase 4, The Takeoff Question: ✅ [LENNY-T], "scale economies, switching costs and network economies, and those depend on your scale relative to competitors." Materiality ✅: "The key thing here is materiality, not whether the flywheel exists", "it's not an impact, it's a material impact.", "there are lots of things that I would say have network effects but not network economies." The Netflix fixed-content-cost example of scale economies and "the curve flattens" on data are ✅ [LENNY-T], paraphrased in the script.
- Phase 5, Name the One and What Would Show It's Forming: Added. Helmer does not publish a "sign to watch" step. It is here because the user asked for a deliverable with the plausible powers, the evidence to watch for, and the next check. It rests on ✅ "Power requires a benefit and a barrier" and his warning that confirming Power is hard ("Actually understanding whether or not there is a type of power in place is hard." ✅), so the check names what to watch for rather than declaring Power. "castle and not a shack" ✅.
- Phase 6, The Page: Added: assembling the locked lines into the artifact, as other frameworks in this plugin do.
- Closing line on second acts restarting the cycle: ✅ [LENNY-T], "think of AWS or Intel going into CPUs or Apple going into iPhones, all not the origin of business." (paraphrased, not quoted).

## Book ideas taught, never quoted (🧠 / 🟡)

- The progression chart: Origination = counter-positioning and cornered resource; Takeoff = scale, network, switching costs; Stability = branding and process power. 🧠 (a third-party page says the same 🟡). The spoken ✅ version covers counter-positioning first, the three scale-relative powers, and branding/process power at stability.
- Counter-positioning's barrier as damage to the incumbent's existing business ("collateral damage"). 🧠 / 🟡 [S-CP]. The phrase "collateral damage" is not used in the prompt.
- Branding needing a long history of consistent behavior. 🧠
- Process power needing a long history and being hard to copy even when watched (Toyota). Toyota ✅ spoken; the "long history" part is 🧠.
