# Getting Real Cut — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. A solo founder is building a site where friends and family write illustrated memories of one person, delivered a few a day for weeks. Version one has twelve items: invite people, write a memory, AI illustration, delivery schedule, recipient inbox, reactions, comments, an admin analytics dashboard, theme and font settings, multi-language support, scale work for 10,000 campaigns, and a mobile app.

---

## Phase 1: The Big Idea and the Enemy

### Golden path

**User:** "Big idea: the people who love someone tell them why, in a way they'll keep. The enemy is the group card everyone signs in thirty seconds. It's not my itch exactly. I made one for my mom."

**Say something like:**
"Good. 'Not the thirty-second group card' tells you a lot about what to leave out. You've been the person organizing it once, so you know some of the answers. For the writers and the person receiving it, we're guessing a little."

**Why it works:** The enemy gives the cut a direction, and the itch question is honest about where the guesses are.

### Struggle: a big idea that's three ideas

**User:** "It's a memory book, a social network for families, and a gift marketplace."

**Say something like:**
"That's three ideas. Which one is it? Pick one and the other two get easy to cut."

**Why it works:** One idea makes every later cut a yes or no instead of a debate.

---

## Phase 3: Find the Epicenter

### Golden path

**User:** "People writing a memory, and the person getting it."

**Say something like:**
"That's the epicenter. Without those two, it isn't the product. Everything else is around it. Build those first, alone, and make them great."

**Why it works:** Two items, both things the product can't live without.

---

## Phase 4: Cut It in Half, Then Again

### Golden path

**User:** "Cutting the mobile app, multi-language, theme settings, the analytics dashboard, and the scale work. Then comments and reactions."

**Say something like:**
"Good. That leaves invite, write, illustration, schedule, and inbox. Everything else is 'not now.' Don't write it down. If people want reactions, they'll tell you, again and again."

**Why it works:** Two rounds of halving, and nothing cut becomes a list to manage.

### Struggle: wants to save the cut list

**User:** "Let me put the cut stuff in Linear so I don't lose it."

**Say something like:**
"The backlog way is based on what you thought then. Now is a far more accurate version of next. Let your customers be your memory."

**Why it works:** It keeps old guesses from turning into commitments.

---

## Phase 5: Make Each Survivor Earn It

### Golden path

**User:** "AI illustration. It's slow and costs money per picture."

**Say something like:**
"Count it. Screens, code, testing, a cost on every memory, a failure state when the picture is wrong, and a promise to someone's grandmother that her memory gets a picture. Does it wait on the porch three days? If the writing is the product, maybe illustrations come in version two. Your call."

**Why it works:** The hidden costs are named before the yes, and the decision stays with the founder.

---

## Phase 6: Strip What Doesn't Matter Yet

### Edge case: a setting that hides a decision

**User:** "I kept a setting for how many memories get delivered per day."

**Say something like:**
"That's a decision you're handing to the customer. Make the call. Three a day? Pick one. Change it if it's wrong. This isn't brain surgery, it's a web app."

**Why it works:** "Make simple decisions on behalf of your customers."

---

## Phase 7: Race to Running Software

### Golden path

**User:** "Invite plus write plus inbox, working end to end, in two weeks. My sister's birthday is the first one."

**Say something like:**
"That's running software in front of a real person, in the real product. Real things lead to real reactions. Build the epicenter first, then see what comes back."

**Why it works:** A real date, a real person, and the smallest working whole.
