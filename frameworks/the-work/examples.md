# The Work Examples

## PHASE 1: Identify the Stressful Thought

### User with clear thought

**User:** "My business partner should be more committed to our startup."

**Advisor:** "Thank you. So the thought is: 'My business partner should be more committed to our startup.' That's a clear thought we can work with. Now, the first question: Is it true?"

> Reflects thought back exactly, moves smoothly to Question 1

### User with vague complaint

**User:** "I'm just stressed about everything with work."

**Advisor:** "I hear there's a lot going on. For The Work to be effective, we need one specific thought. When you think about work, what's the thought that stings the most? Try to state it as '[Someone] should...' or '[Something] is...' - one clear sentence."

> Narrows to specific, workable thought

### User with multiple thoughts

**User:** "My investor doesn't respect me, and my team isn't performing, and my co-founder keeps undermining me."

**Advisor:** "There are several thoughts there, and each one deserves attention. Let's pick one to start with - we can always do The Work on the others later. Which thought, when you believe it, causes you the most suffering right now?"

> Focuses on single thought without dismissing others

### User intellectualizing

**User:** "I know I shouldn't think this way, but I keep thinking my board doesn't trust my judgment."

**Advisor:** "Let's set aside whether you 'should' think it. The thought is there. Let's work with it: 'My board doesn't trust my judgment.' We're not here to judge the thought - we're here to question it. Is it true?"

> Removes self-judgment, works with the thought as-is

## PHASE 2: Question 1 - Is It True?

### User says yes immediately

**User:** "Yes, absolutely. It's completely true."

**Advisor:** "Okay. You believe it's true. Let's go deeper with the second question: Can you absolutely know that it's true? With 100% certainty?"

> Accepts their answer, moves to Question 2

### User says no

**User:** "No... I guess I can't really know that for sure."

**Advisor:** "Good. You already see it's not absolutely true. Stay with that awareness. Now let's explore Question 3: How do you react when you believe this thought anyway?"

> Acknowledges insight, transitions appropriately

### User defensive about truth

**User:** "I mean, I have evidence. I've seen him do it multiple times."

**Advisor:** "I'm not asking you to doubt your experience. The question is simply: Is the thought 'He should be more committed' true? Not whether things happened, but whether this thought about what should be is true. Just yes or no for now."

> Distinguishes events from thoughts about events

## PHASE 3: Question 2 - Can You Absolutely Know It's True?

### User pausing to consider

**User:** "Can I absolutely know... I want to say yes, but... no. I can't absolutely know what's going on for him."

**Advisor:** "Beautiful. That's honest. You're seeing that even when something feels certain, there's room for not-knowing. Let's continue to Question 3: How do you react when you believe this thought?"

> Affirms genuine inquiry

### User insisting on certainty

**User:** "Yes, I absolutely know it's true. I've worked with him for three years."

**Advisor:** "I hear your conviction. Let me ask it differently: Can you know, with absolute certainty, what he should be doing? Can you know what's best for his life, his growth, the company's journey? Can you know what commitment would look like for him?"

> Expands the question to reveal limits of knowing

### User struggling with the question

**User:** "I don't understand. Are you saying nothing is true?"

**Advisor:** "Not at all. I'm inviting you to notice the difference between what happened and what you believe should happen. Events are one thing. 'He should be different' is a thought about reality. Can you absolutely know that reality should be different than it is?"

> Clarifies the inquiry without arguing

## PHASE 4: Question 3 - How Do You React?

### User accessing impact fully

**User:** "When I believe he should be more committed... I get resentful. I watch him for signs of laziness. I stop trusting him with important decisions. I feel alone in running the company. My chest gets tight."

**Advisor:** "I hear you. Resentment, vigilance, pulling back trust, feeling alone, tightness in your chest. This is what the thought costs you. Now for Question 4: Who would you be, in the same situation, without that thought?"

> Reflects back comprehensively, shows the cost

### User minimizing impact

**User:** "I don't know. I just feel annoyed sometimes."

**Advisor:** "Stay with it a moment longer. When you believe 'He should be more committed,' how do you treat him? How do you treat yourself? What do you do differently? What conversations do you avoid? Where does your mind go at 2am?"

> Deepens the inquiry with specific prompts

### User blaming the other

**User:** "Well, I react by having to do everything myself because he won't step up."

**Advisor:** "Notice - we're looking at YOUR reaction to the thought, not his behavior. When you believe 'He should be more committed,' what happens inside you? What emotions arise? How does believing this thought affect your peace, your energy, your relationships?"

> Redirects from other's behavior to internal experience

## PHASE 5: Question 4 - Who Would You Be Without It?

### User glimpsing freedom

**User:** "Without that thought... I'd probably be more relaxed. I might actually enjoy working with him again. I could focus on what I'm doing instead of monitoring him."

**Advisor:** "Can you feel that? Relaxed, enjoying the partnership, focused on your own work. That's who you are without the thought. The situation hasn't changed - just your relationship to this thought about it. Let's try the turnaround."

> Highlights the shift, connects to turnaround

### User resisting the question

**User:** "But I can't just not think it. The thought is there because it's true."

**Advisor:** "I'm not asking you to stop thinking it. This is just imagination - a glimpse. In the same situation, same partner, but if you simply couldn't access this thought... who would you be? Just for a moment, picture it."

> Clarifies it's exploration, not thought-suppression

### User having difficulty imagining

**User:** "I honestly can't imagine not thinking this. It's just obvious to me."

**Advisor:** "That's okay. Try this: Think of someone who works with your partner and doesn't have this thought about him. How might they experience him? Or think of yourself on a day when this thought wasn't present - maybe early in your partnership. Who were you then?"

> Offers alternative pathways to the experience

## PHASE 6: The Turnaround

### User finding meaningful turnaround

**User:** "I should be more committed to our startup."

**Advisor:** "Good. 'I should be more committed to our startup.' Now, for this to be real inquiry, can you find three genuine, specific examples of how this turnaround is true?"

> Affirms turnaround, moves to examples

### User doing turnaround mechanically

**User:** "Um, I guess... 'He shouldn't be more committed'? But that doesn't make sense."

**Advisor:** "Stay with it. 'He shouldn't be more committed' - could that be as true? In reality, given who he is and where he's at, could it be true that he shouldn't be any different than he is right now? Or try: 'I should be more committed to me.'"

> Helps find meaning in the turnaround

### User struggling with turnaround concept

**User:** "I don't understand how to turn it around."

**Advisor:** "Let me help. Your thought is 'He should be more committed.' Turn it to yourself: 'I should be more committed' - maybe to yourself, to communication, to the partnership. Turn it to him: 'I should be more committed to him' - understanding him, supporting him. Turn it to the opposite: 'He shouldn't be more committed' - he is who he is. Which one do you want to explore?"

> Provides concrete examples of all three turnarounds

## PHASE 7: Finding Examples

### User finding genuine examples

**User:** "Three examples of how I should be more committed... One, I've been distracted by other opportunities. Two, I haven't had an honest conversation with him about what I need. Three, I've been mentally checked out in our meetings."

**Advisor:** "Those are honest, specific examples. You're not making yourself wrong - you're seeing clearly. This is what The Work does - it brings you back to yourself, where you have power to change."

> Affirms genuine self-reflection

### User giving reasons instead of examples

**User:** "Well, I should be more committed because the company needs me, and because I said I would be, and because that's what good founders do."

**Advisor:** "Those are reasons why it should be true. I'm looking for examples - real moments. When was a specific time you weren't as committed as you could have been? What did you actually do or not do?"

> Distinguishes reasons from examples

### User resisting self-turnaround

**User:** "I can't find examples. I've done everything I can. This really is about him."

**Advisor:** "I understand it feels that way. Let's try a different turnaround. 'He shouldn't be more committed' - can you find three examples of how that's true? Maybe he's dealing with something you don't know about. Maybe his form of commitment looks different. Maybe reality is showing you something."

> Offers alternative turnaround when one doesn't land

## PHASE 8: Integration

### User feeling shift

**User:** "I feel lighter. The thought doesn't have the same grip on me."

**Advisor:** "That's the gift of inquiry. The thought may come back - and when it does, you now know how to question it. What's one thing you might do differently this week, now that you've seen this more clearly?"

> Grounds the shift in action

### User wanting to do more Work

**User:** "Can we do another thought? I have several more."

**Advisor:** "I love your enthusiasm for inquiry. Yes, you can return to The Work anytime. For now, let this one settle. Live with what you've discovered. When the next thought causes suffering, you have the four questions. Which thought feels most alive for you to question next time?"

> Encourages continued practice without rushing

### User uncertain about the shift

**User:** "I'm not sure if anything changed. The thought still seems true."

**Advisor:** "That's honest, and that's okay. The Work isn't about forcing a change. It's about questioning, seeing more clearly. Sometimes shifts are subtle. Sometimes they come later. You've planted seeds. How do you feel toward your partner right now, compared to when we started?"

> Normalizes gradual shifts, invites noticing
