# Leadership Meeting Anti-Examples

### Inventing a key action

**User:** "We don't have a strategy. What should our key actions be?"

**Wrong:** "A company at your stage usually needs revenue growth, a hiring plan and a product launch. Let's use those as 1, 2 and 3."

**Right:** "I won't write them for you. They're your strategy. What must get done this quarter? Name up to four, and one owner for each. I'll mark them draft."

> Every action item ties to a key action. If Claude guesses them, the whole team works on a guess, and the meeting looks like it is on strategy when it is not.

### Running past a stop

**User:** "[EXAMPLE NEEDED: a company name]. Five of us attend."

**Wrong:** "Great. Now the key actions, the owners, the dashboards, the day and time, and the tool. Tell me all of those."

**Right:** "Five people. Who makes the final call when the group can't agree?" Then stop.

> The setup runs one question at a time. Each answer shapes the next, and a person who answers five questions in one message skips the ones that needed thought.

### Writing to the tool before approval

**User:** "We use Asana. Key actions are 1 and 2."

**Wrong:** "I've created two projects and an agenda in Asana."

**Right:** "Here's what I plan to create: [list]. I write nothing until you say so. Do you approve this?"

> The skill writes only after a person approves, and it never sets an owner or assignee. Undoing a wrong write in someone's tool costs more than one more question.

### Retyping the template from memory

**User:** "Show me the template."

**Wrong:** A rewritten agenda with six steps and a round where everyone talks.

**Right:** Read `skills/leadership-meeting/template.md` and show it with the brackets filled in. If the person wants a change, make that change and leave the rest as written.

> The template has five steps, 90 minutes and ten rules. A copy from memory drifts, and the feedback job grades against the real one.

### Letting status back into the meeting

**User:** "Can we add a round where everyone gives a quick status?"

**Wrong:** "Sure, I'll add a step 2D for verbal status."

**Right:** "Status is why the updates come a day before. A green key action with a date gets credit and no discussion in step 2A. What are you hoping the round would catch? We can check whether the updates or Questions to answer already cover it."

> Verbal status takes back the time the template frees for decisions.
