# Leadership Meeting Examples

Company names, people and key actions below use the placeholder form. Fill them from the person's own answers.

## PHASE 1: Who Attends

### User names the group and the decision maker

**User:** "[EXAMPLE NEEDED: a company name]. Four of us attend: me, [name], [name] and [name]. I make the final call."

**Advisor:** "Four people and one decision maker. That's the right size. The decision maker ranks the questions in step 3, so that's you. Who facilitates? It rotates, and the CEO doesn't facilitate. We'll set the first one at the end."

> Fills the Roles section from the answer and flags the CEO rule early.

### User lists too many people

**User:** "Everyone on the management team, so about twenty."

**Advisor:** "Twenty is too many for a decision meeting. It works at 3 to 15. Who has to be in the room to decide? The rest read the updates and the team page."

> Holds the 3 to 15 range without refusing the meeting.

## PHASE 2: Key Actions

### User has a strategy

**User:** "Here's our strategy doc." [pastes a kernel with three key actions]

**Advisor:** "Three key actions, so codes 1, 2 and 3. The goals under each become the letters. Here's how I read it: 1 is [the first key action], 1A is [its first goal]. Is that how you'd name them?"

> Maps the kernel straight onto codes and asks for confirmation instead of rewording it.

### User has no strategy

**User:** "We don't have a strategy written down. Can you suggest key actions?"

**Advisor:** "I won't write them for you. They're your strategy, and a guess from me would send the team after the wrong thing. What are the things that must get done this quarter? Name up to four, and one owner for each. I'll mark them draft and point you to the strategy framework at the end."

> Declines to invent, offers the draft path, and sets up the end recommendation.

### User names six

**User:** "Revenue, hiring, product launch, fundraising, support quality, and the new office."

**Advisor:** "Four is the limit. Which four move the company most this quarter? The rest become letters under those four, or they wait."

> Holds the cap and asks the person to rank.

## PHASE 3: Codes, Owners and Dashboard Links

### User gives owners and links

**User:** "1 is mine. 2 is [name]. The dashboard for 1 is [link]. Two has no dashboard yet."

**Advisor:** "Got it. 1 has an owner and a link. 2 has an owner, and the dashboard stays open in the table. You can add the link the day it exists."

> Leaves the gap open rather than filling it.

### User gives a code that goes too deep

**User:** "Put the pricing work under 1C3."

**Advisor:** "The codes stop at 4D. Anything under 1C is part of 1C. Does pricing belong under 1C, or under a different letter?"

> Corrects the code without arguing about structure.

## PHASE 5: The Team's Tool

### User names a tool the session can write to

**User:** "We use Linear."

**Advisor:** "I'll look for it in this session. I can see it. I'll show you everything I plan to create before I create any of it."

> Searches the session's tools and does not assume a name.

### User names a tool the session can't reach

**User:** "We keep it all in a spreadsheet."

**Advisor:** "I can't write to that from here. I'll give you the steps, and anything to post comes back as a block you paste."

> Uses the no-connector path from the tool file.

## PHASE 7: Approval

### User asks for a change instead of approving

**User:** "Don't create a project for key action 4, it's just one task."

**Advisor:** "Key action 4 keeps its own project, even with one task in it, or it stays on the agenda with no task. Which do you want? I'll show you the list again with your pick and ask you to approve it."

> Holds one project per key action, then redraws the plan and asks again before writing.

## PHASE 8: First Meeting Date

### User picks a date that is too close

**User:** "Let's start tomorrow."

**Advisor:** "Updates are due a day before. Can everyone write one by tonight? If not, move it out a week. You can also go first for two weeks while the others say their status in step 2A."

> Protects the day-before rule and gives the leader-first option.
