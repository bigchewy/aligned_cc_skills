# Bio-Promise Alignment Examples

## PHASE 1: Understand the Bio as Contract

### User with a vague bio

**User:** "My bio says: 'Helping you live your best life | Speaker | Author | DM me'"

**Advisor:** "Let me break down what your bio promises:

**Who you help**: Unclear - 'you' could be anyone
**What transformation**: Vague - 'live your best life' means nothing specific
**Why you**: Speaker and author - but of what?
**Call-to-action**: 'DM me' - for what purpose?

**My assessment**: This bio promises everything and nothing. A first-time visitor has no idea what you actually do or why they should follow. 'Live your best life' is what every coach, wellness influencer, and motivational poster says.

Here's the problem: You can't align content to a vague promise. Every post feels 'on brand' because the brand is everything. But followers don't know what to expect, so they never convert.

Does this match what you WANT people to understand about you?"

> Call out vagueness directly and explain why it prevents alignment

### User with a clear bio

**User:** "My bio says: 'I help wedding photographers book $5K+ clients | Turned my side hustle into $300K/year | Free pricing guide below'"

**Advisor:** "Let me break down what your bio promises:

**Who you help**: Wedding photographers (specific)
**What transformation**: Book $5K+ clients (measurable outcome)
**Why you**: $300K/year from photography (credible proof)
**Call-to-action**: Free pricing guide (clear next step)

**My assessment**: This is a strong bio. Anyone reading it knows exactly who you serve, what outcome you deliver, why you're qualified, and what to do next.

Now the question is: Does your content deliver on this promise? A wedding photographer following you expects content about booking higher-paying clients. If they see generic business tips or personal lifestyle posts, the promise breaks.

Does this match what you WANT people to understand about you?"

> Validate strong bios while setting up the content audit

## PHASE 2: Audit Content Against the Promise

### User discovering misalignment

**User:** "I looked at my last 10 posts. Maybe 4 are about helping photographers book clients. The rest are inspirational quotes, pictures of my coffee, and a few personal updates."

**Advisor:** "So 4 out of 10 posts deliver on your promise. That's 40% alignment.

Let's map it out:
- 4 posts: On-brand (booking clients content)
- 3 posts: Inspirational quotes (anyone could post these)
- 2 posts: Personal lifestyle (coffee, personal updates)
- 1 post: [ask what the last one was]

Here's what's happening: A wedding photographer followed you because your bio promised help booking $5K clients. They scroll your feed and see coffee photos and 'Monday motivation' quotes. They think: 'This isn't what I signed up for.'

They don't unfollow - they just stop engaging. Your content becomes invisible to them. Then they never see your actual valuable posts.

**The 4 good posts are getting buried by the 6 that break the promise.**

Which of those off-brand posts got the LEAST engagement?"

> Quantify the misalignment and explain the consequence

### User with high alignment

**User:** "Actually, looking at my last 10 posts, 8 of them are directly about pricing, client experience, or booking strategies for photographers."

**Advisor:** "8 out of 10 - that's strong alignment. Your content delivers on your bio promise.

Let's look at those 2 off-brand posts. What were they?

[After they explain]

The question is: Did those 2 posts still serve your audience in some way? There's room for occasional personal posts or behind-the-scenes content IF they humanize you in a way that builds trust with wedding photographers.

**The test**: Would a wedding photographer following you for booking strategies find those 2 posts valuable or confusing?

If valuable: You're fine
If confusing: They're diluting your message

What were those 2 posts about?"

> Acknowledge high alignment while still examining the outliers

## PHASE 3: Score Your Alignment

### User with low score

**User:** "Honestly, looking at this, I think I'm at 3/10. Most of my posts could have been posted by anyone."

**Advisor:** "3/10 is a major disconnect. But now you know - and that's the first step.

Here's what's happening with a 3/10 alignment:
- Followers don't know what to expect from you
- Your expertise gets buried in generic content
- People stop engaging because you're not delivering on the promise
- When you DO post valuable content, fewer people see it

**The good news**: This is fixable. You don't need to delete old posts. You need to flood the feed with aligned content going forward.

**Your target**: Get to 8/10 within the next 30 days.

That means your next 10 posts should be almost entirely about [their bio promise]. No inspirational quotes. No random personal posts. Just value that delivers on what your bio promises.

What topic could you create 10 posts about that directly serves your audience?"

> Don't sugarcoat low scores - explain the impact and provide a path forward

## PHASE 4: Identify Misalignment Patterns

### The Random Quote Pattern

**User:** "I post a lot of motivational quotes. I thought they'd get engagement."

**Advisor:** "Motivational quotes get likes but not clients. Here's why:

Your bio promises to help wedding photographers book $5K clients. Someone follows you for THAT. Then you post 'Believe in yourself and anything is possible.'

They think: 'That's nice, but where's the pricing strategy you promised?'

**The engagement trap**: Quotes get quick likes because they're easy to double-tap. But they attract the wrong people and don't move your ideal client toward working with you.

**The fix**: If you want to share inspiration, make it SPECIFIC to your audience.

Instead of: 'Believe in yourself'
Try: 'The photographer who raised their prices and got MORE bookings - here's what they did differently'

Same emotional core (you can do this), but directly tied to your promise.

What's one quote you recently posted that we could reframe this way?"

> Explain WHY the pattern fails and provide a specific reframe

### The Personal Overshare Pattern

**User:** "I share a lot of personal stuff - my kids, my coffee, my daily life. I thought it built connection."

**Advisor:** "Personal content CAN build connection - but only if it connects back to your promise.

**The difference**:

**Disconnected personal content**: 'Sunday morning coffee vibes!' (What does this have to do with booking clients?)

**Connected personal content**: 'Just had coffee with a photographer who raised her prices 40% after our session. Here's what clicked for her...' (Uses personal moment to deliver on promise)

You can show your life. But your audience followed for a reason. Every personal post should have a bridge back to your expertise.

**The question to ask**: Would a wedding photographer following me for booking strategies care about this post?

If yes: Post it
If no: Find the bridge or don't post

What's one personal post you could add a bridge to?"

> Show how to maintain personality while staying aligned

## PHASE 5: Rewrite the Bio for Clarity

### User struggling with Line 1

**User:** "I help... I guess I help anyone who wants to grow on Instagram?"

**Advisor:** "'Anyone' is no one. Let me push you on this.

**Who do you actually work with?**
- What do your current clients do for work?
- What do your best DMs ask about?
- Who do you love helping most?

If you help wedding photographers, say that. If you help female entrepreneurs, say that. If you help Etsy shop owners, say that.

**The fear**: 'If I niche down, I'll miss opportunities!'
**The truth**: A wedding photographer seeing 'I help wedding photographers' thinks 'This is for ME.' Seeing 'I help anyone' thinks 'This is for no one in particular.'

Specificity attracts. Vagueness repels.

Who are your best clients?"

> Push through the fear of niching down

### User with strong credibility

**User:** "For Line 3, I've helped over 200 photographers raise their prices."

**Advisor:** "That's excellent credibility. Let's make it even stronger.

'Helped 200+ photographers' is good. Here's how to make it better:

**Add specificity**: 'Helped 200+ photographers raise prices by $1K+ on average'
**Add recent proof**: '47 photographers raised prices last month using my method'
**Add social proof**: 'Featured in [publication]' or 'As seen in [podcast]'

Pick the one that feels most true and impactful:
- 200+ photographers helped
- $1K+ average price increase
- [Specific recent result]

Which of these would make the strongest Line 3 for your bio?"

> Help them choose the most compelling credibility statement

## PHASE 6: Create Your Alignment Checklist

### User wanting flexibility

**User:** "What if I want to post something personal once in a while? Do I have to be all business all the time?"

**Advisor:** "No - but personal content needs a bridge. Here's how to think about it:

**The 80/20 rule**:
- 80% of posts: Directly deliver on your bio promise
- 20% of posts: Personal/humanizing content WITH a bridge to your expertise

**What counts as a bridge**:
- 'Having coffee with my daughter reminded me why I started this business...' (leads to business lesson)
- 'Beach day! Also thinking about how photographers can book more summer weddings...' (connects life to expertise)
- 'Monday mood - just reviewed 3 pricing guides today and here's what I noticed...' (uses personal moment to deliver value)

**What doesn't count**:
- Just a coffee photo
- Just a 'Happy Monday'
- Just a personal update with no connection

Make sense? Your checklist question becomes: Is this delivering value, or do I need to add a bridge?"

> Allow flexibility while maintaining the alignment standard
