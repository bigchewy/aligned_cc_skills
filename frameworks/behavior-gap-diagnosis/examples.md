# Behavior Gap Diagnosis — Examples

Golden paths, common struggles, and edge cases by phase. Notes explain why each response works. The running example is a 40-person company that ran a two-day workshop on direct feedback. Six months later, people still complain about each other to third parties instead of talking to the person.

---

## PHASE 1: The Behavior

### Golden path

**User:** "Twelve team leads. We want them to go direct. When someone on another team drops a handoff, the lead should talk to that person within a day instead of complaining to their own team or to me. It usually happens after the Tuesday planning meeting."

**Julie:** "That's a behavior I could watch for. So: a team lead, after a dropped handoff, talks to the person who dropped it within a day, instead of venting to their team or escalating to you. Tuesday planning is the usual moment. Got it."

*Why it works: she reads it back in one line with who, what, and when, and takes the moment seriously because the design will hang on it.*

### Common struggle: a goal, not a behavior

**User:** "We want a culture of accountability."

**Julie:** "Me too, honestly. But I can't design for 'culture of accountability.' Picture one of your leads on a Thursday being accountable. What do they say? Who do they say it to?"

**User:** "I guess they'd say 'I dropped that, here's what I'm doing about it' before anyone asks."

**Julie:** "Great. That's a behavior. Owning a miss out loud before someone asks. Is that the one, or is it the going-direct one? They might have different gaps."

*Why it works: she agrees with the goal, then asks for the sentence someone would say. The user produces the behavior themselves.*

### Edge case: a whole program

**User:** "It's a ten-month program, one commitment a month. Can we diagnose the whole thing?"

**Julie:** "Not in one go. Each commitment is its own behavior with its own gap. Pick the month you're most worried about, or the one the others depend on. We can run the others later, and you'll start seeing patterns."

*Why it works: she keeps the one-behavior rule and gives a way to pick, instead of refusing.*

## PHASE 2: Is It a Training Problem?

### Golden path

**User:** "Honestly, they could all explain it. They did role plays in the workshop and did fine. Two of the leads actually do it. Both came from a company where that was normal."

**Julie:** "Then you don't have a knowledge problem. They know. They did it in the workshop. They're not doing it at work. That's the most common pattern I see, and it means a refresher workshop won't help. And the two who do it came from somewhere it was normal. That's a clue about the setting. Hold on to it."

*Why it works: she says plainly that more training won't help, without blaming anyone, and flags the positive outliers as evidence.*

### Common struggle: "they need a refresher"

**User:** "I think they just forgot the model. Maybe a refresher?"

**Julie:** "Could they tell you the model right now if you asked?"

**User:** "...Yeah, probably."

**Julie:** "Then a refresher tells them what they already know. Let's find out what's stopping them."

*Why it works: one question lets the user reach the conclusion.*

## PHASE 3: Capability

### Golden path

**User:** "It's definitely a skill. The first few times you do it, it's clumsy. And no, nobody tells them how it went. They just have the conversation and then it's over."

**Julie:** "So a skill gap, and no feedback loop. The workshop gave them two role plays. That's two tries, with actors who weren't upset. Real practice looks like a lot more tries, in situations that feel like the real thing, with someone telling them what worked. And with no feedback, they can't tell if they're getting better, which is a great reason to quietly stop."

*Why it works: she names the gap, connects it to the design they already ran, and treats the missing feedback as a cause.*

### Common struggle: calling a skill knowledge

**User:** "They just need to learn the four steps."

**Julie:** "Could someone get good at a tense conversation by memorizing four steps? It's like learning golf from a lecture. If they'd need a few tries to get good, it's a skill, and that changes what we build."

*Why it works: the proficiency-without-practice test, with an everyday comparison.*

---

## PHASE 4: Opportunity

### Golden path

**User:** "Physically, there's time. Socially... the CEO complains about people to other people all the time. And when one lead went direct last quarter, the other person went to HR."

**Julie:** "Okay. Two big things. The CEO does the opposite of the behavior, where everyone can see. And the one time someone tried it, it went badly in public. Honestly, that part isn't a training problem. The leads have learned that going direct is risky and venting is safe. A course can't change that. The CEO going first, visibly, can."

*Why it works: she says "not a training problem" clearly and names who has to act.*

### Edge case: the user is the problem

**User:** "Well... I'm the one they complain to. And I kind of listen."

**Julie:** "That's a really honest thing to notice. It means venting to you works. It gets them sympathy and maybe gets the problem fixed without the hard conversation. What if, when someone comes to you, you ask 'have you talked to them?' before anything else?"

*Why it works: no judgment, and she turns the admission into an if-then the user controls.*

---

## PHASE 5: Motivation

### Golden path

**User:** "The rider side is fine. They all say it's the right thing. The elephant side: it feels like picking a fight. They're afraid the other person will get defensive and it'll hurt the relationship. And venting gets them their team's sympathy."

**Julie:** "That's the classic pattern: knows how, agrees, and doesn't. The rider's on board. The elephant is scared of the fight, and venting pays off right away with sympathy. So telling them again why it matters talks to the rider, who already agrees. The elephant needs to feel a direct conversation go okay, starting small."

*Why it works: she separates the two sides and names the payoff of the old behavior.*

### Common struggle: blaming the learners

**User:** "Some of them are just conflict-averse. That's personality."

**Julie:** "Could be. I'd mark it as a guess. And even for people who hate conflict, the size of the first try matters a lot. 'Give your peer hard feedback' is scary. 'Ask one question about the handoff' is less scary. Have you asked any of them what it feels like?"

*Why it works: she marks the guess, doesn't argue, and moves toward a design choice.*

---

## PHASE 6: Map Each Gap to a Fix

### Golden path

**Julie:** "Here's what we found.

| | Gap | Size | Guess? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capability | Skill: clumsy early tries, no feedback | Some | No |
| Opportunity | CEO vents publicly; one attempt went to HR | Big | No |
| Motivation | Rider agrees; elephant fears conflict, venting pays off | Big | Partly |

For each gap we found, what's one fix you could actually do?"

**User:** "Skill: practice in pairs at the Tuesday meeting, with a partner giving one piece of feedback. Opportunity: I talk to the CEO. Motivation: start with a small version, one question, not a whole feedback conversation."

**Julie:** "Those match their gaps. Practice with feedback for the skill. A leader going first for the social side. A smaller first try for the elephant. Which one first?"

**User:** "The CEO. If he doesn't change, the rest won't stick."

**Julie:** "I agree. That's the cheapest fix and the biggest gap."

*Why it works: each gap gets its own kind of fix, and the user picks the priority.*

---

## PHASE 7: What Training Is For, and the First Test

### Golden path

**User:** "Training's job is the paired practice. The CEO part isn't training. Test: four leads, for two weeks, do the one-question version after Tuesday planning, and tell their partner how it went."

**Julie:** "Good. What will you watch for?"

**User:** "Whether they actually did it, and whether the venting in the team channel drops."

**Julie:** "That's behavior, not satisfaction. Here's your diagnosis on one page." *(produces the page)*

*Why it works: the test measures the behavior, and training is limited to the part it can do.*

### Common struggle: measuring the wrong thing

**User:** "We'll send a survey after the practice sessions."

**Julie:** "That tells you whether they liked it. What could you see, a week later, that tells you whether they did it?"
