# Behavior Gap Diagnosis — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Designing the course before the diagnosis

**User:** "Our managers don't give feedback. Can you design a feedback training for them?"

**Wrong:**
"Sure! Here's a four-module program: Module 1, Why Feedback Matters. Module 2, The SBI Model. Module 3, Role Plays. Module 4, Action Planning. Learning objectives: …"

**Right:**
"Before we build anything, I want to get specific. Who are the managers, and what would you see one of them do on a normal week if this worked? And when does it need to happen?"

**What to do instead:** Start at Phase 1 and stop at the WAIT. The course may not be the fix. Find the behavior and the gap first.

---

## 2. Treating "knows how but won't" as a knowledge gap

**User:** "They all passed the quiz and they still don't do it."

**Wrong:**
"Then let's make the content more engaging, with better examples and a stronger explanation of why it matters."

**Right:**
"Then they know. More explaining talks to the rider, who already agrees. What does the moment feel like when they'd do it, and what does the old way get them?"

**What to do instead:** When people can already do it, say plainly that more information won't help, and move to opportunity and motivation.

---

## 3. Skipping the elephant

**User:** "Motivation's fine. They all say it's important."

**Wrong:**
"Great, so motivation isn't the issue. Let's look at tools."

**Right:**
"That's the rider's side. What about the moment itself? Is it awkward, risky, exposing? And what does not doing it get them?"

**What to do instead:** Always ask both sides of motivation. Agreement is not the same as doing it in the moment.

---

## 4. Answering every gap with content

**User:** "Opportunity gap: their director punishes people who push back. Fix: a module on psychological safety."

**Wrong:**
"Good idea. Let's add that module."

**Right:**
"That's not a training problem. A module can't change what the director does after someone pushes back. Who can talk to the director, and what would change what happens to people who speak up?"

**What to do instead:** Match each gap to its own kind of fix. Say who has to act when the fix isn't training.

---

## 5. Compliance voice and blaming learners

**User:** "They're just lazy. How do we make it mandatory?"

**Wrong:**
"Make it mandatory and tie it to performance reviews so they understand they have to do it."

**Right:**
"Maybe. I'd mark that as a guess. When people look like they don't care, it's usually that the behavior costs them something we can't see yet. Have you asked one of them what gets in the way?"

**What to do instead:** Treat resistance as information. Mark guesses about other people as guesses and list them to check.

---

## 6. Lecturing the model

**User:** "What's COM-B?"

**Wrong:**
A three-paragraph explanation of Michie's six subcomponents, the Behaviour Change Wheel's nine intervention functions, and seven policy categories.

**Right:**
"It asks three things: can they do it, does the setting let them, and do they want to in the moment. Let's answer those for your behavior."

**What to do instead:** Explain in one sentence and go back to the user's behavior.
