# The Counter-Positioning Test: Anti-Examples

## 1. Accepting "they're slow" as the barrier

**User:** "The incumbents are dinosaurs. It would take them three years to build what we have."

**Wrong:** "That's a real advantage. Big companies struggle to innovate, and your speed is a moat. Let's think about how to make the most of that head start."

**Right:** "I don't want to be unkind, but slowness isn't a barrier. Large companies are slow right up until they're scared. Suppose they decided tomorrow to copy you. What would it damage in their existing business? Finish the sentence: 'If they copied us, they would lose ___.'"

**What to do instead:** Set inertia, ignorance and slowness aside every time they come up, and ask what copying would cost the incumbent's existing business. Only that cost counts as the barrier.

## 2. Testing features instead of business models

**User:** "Our dashboard is far cleaner than the market leader's, and setup takes ten minutes instead of two weeks."

**Wrong:** "That's strong counter-positioning. Your user experience is fundamentally different from theirs, and they'd have to rebuild their product to match it."

**Right:** "Those are real strengths, and their engineers can build them. Blockbuster's site ended up looking just like Netflix's. I'm asking about the model underneath: how do you charge, who pays, and how does it reach the customer? Is there something there they'd find painful to copy?"

**What to do instead:** Keep both columns about how the money comes in and how the product is delivered. A better product is the benefit at best; the barrier has to come from the incumbent's model.

## 3. Calling it a permanent moat

**User:** "So we're counter-positioned. That means we're safe from them, right?"

**Wrong:** "Yes. Because copying you would hurt their core business, they'll never follow. You have a durable moat."

**Right:** "Safe for a stage. Collateral damage holds while their old business is worth more to them than following you. As you grow and their model shrinks, there's a point where following becomes rational, and then they come with everything they have. Where is that point? Whatever it is, that's how long you have to build a second power, probably one that comes with scale."

**What to do instead:** Always find the crossing point and name a second power to build before it. Hand off to `power-progression-check` to choose it.

## 4. Treating it as disruption

**User:** "Isn't this just disruption? We're the cheap, worse version that'll get better."

**Wrong:** "Exactly. You're a classic disruptor, and the incumbent will ignore you until it's too late."

**Right:** "It's a neighbor of Clayton Christensen's idea, and people blur them. Disruption is usually about a technology that starts worse and improves. This test doesn't care whether you started worse. It asks one thing: would copying your model hurt the incumbent's existing business? If yes, they stay out on purpose, not because they underestimate you."

**What to do instead:** Credit Christensen by name, then draw the line: the barrier here is the incumbent's own business model, not a technology curve or their failure to notice.

## 5. Putting book language in quotation marks

**User:** "How does Helmer actually define counter-positioning?"

**Wrong:** Reciting a book definition from memory inside quotation marks, as his exact words, and adding his supposed terms for the incumbent's choice in quotation marks too. The book wasn't checked; those words may not be his.

**Right:** "In my own words: a newcomer adopts a better business model, and the incumbent, who could copy it, chooses not to because copying would damage the business it already has. What I've said out loud is that almost every startup that you want to deal with starts with counter positioning, and that counter positioning is the refuge from an incumbent who already has the capabilities necessary."

**What to do instead:** Explain the formal definition, collateral damage, and harvesting the old model as concepts in your own words. Use quotation marks only for lines he has said on the record.
