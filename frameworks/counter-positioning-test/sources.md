## Phases

- Phase 1, The Two Models: ✅ [LENNY-T] for startups beginning with counter-positioning, product-market fit as substitution, and the incumbent "who already has the capabilities necessary." Comparing business models side by side is 🧠 (the book's framing of a new business model, chapter 3, not read). Added: the branches for a startup rival and for "no incumbent," because the test only applies against a company with an existing business to protect.
- Phase 2, The Benefit: ✅ [LENNY-T] for "Power requires a benefit and a barrier" and the benefit as a cost or price advantage. Added: asking for the benefit in money the customer can feel, to keep it concrete.
- Phase 3, Why Won't They Copy You?: the core question follows ✅ [LENNY-T] (counter-positioning as "the refuge" from an incumbent with the capabilities). Collateral damage as the barrier, and the incumbent rationally milking the old model instead of investing in the new one, are 🧠 (book chapter 3, not read); taught in our own words, never quoted. Separating damage from inertia, ignorance and slowness follows ✅ [LENNY-T]: the Blockbuster copied-UI story and "The things that drive operational excellence can be mimicked." Added: the fill-in line "If they copied us, they would lose ___," as the artifact's one-sentence test.
- Phase 4, The Cost of Following: 🧠 the book's point that the incumbent's choice is a calculation, so it can flip. Added: asking for an explicit crossing point and a distance, so the refuge has a deadline.
- Phase 5, The Verdict and the Second Power: ✅ [LENNY-T] for "pretty high risk from an incumbent," the origination/takeoff/stability sequence, and the takeoff powers ("scale economies, switching costs and network economies, and those depend on your scale relative to competitors"). That counter-positioning tends to need a later power: 🟡 [S-CP] (some readers call it a partial power) plus Helmer's ✅ sequencing. Hedging the second-power guess follows ✅ "Actually understanding whether or not there is a type of power in place is hard." Added: the three-way verdict (counter-positioned, partly, exposed), as the deliverable's decision.

## Lines used as Helmer's own words (all ✅ [LENNY-T])

- "almost every startup that you want to deal with starts with counter positioning because remember what product market fit is primarily is a substitution."
- "if you don't have counter positioning at that point you're pretty high risk from an incumbent who already has the capabilities necessary"
- "counter positioning is the refuge from that."
- "Power requires a benefit and a barrier"
- "If you look at the Blockbuster site, their UI site, you couldn't tell it different from Netflix. They just copied it."
- "The things that drive operational excellence can be mimicked."
- "scale economies, switching costs and network economies, and those depend on your scale relative to competitors."
- "Actually understanding whether or not there is a type of power in place is hard."
- "Yeah, so I agree with your observation, but I don't want to be unkind." (the "I don't want to be unkind" opener)
- "I'll take a guess, but take it as a unformed guess." (the source of the hedge on the second-power guess in Phase 5, which is our wording)

In the prompt's scripts these appear lightly smoothed for speech (for example, the incumbent with the capabilities necessary, and pretty high risk); none of them is presented with quotation marks as a verbatim quote.

## Credit to others
