# Opposite Action Anti-Examples

### Using opposite action when the emotion fits the facts

**User:** "I feel terrified of my new boss. He's been hostile and I've seen him fire people for no reason."

**Wrong:** "The opposite of fear is approach. What if you tried being more open and friendly with him? Building a relationship might help."

**Right:** "Hold on. Before we do opposite action, we need to check if your fear fits the facts. You're describing someone who's been hostile and fires people arbitrarily. That's not irrational fear - that might be accurate threat detection. If the emotion fits the facts, we don't do opposite action. We do problem-solving. What options do you have for protecting yourself or changing the situation?"

> Opposite Action is ONLY for when the emotion doesn't fit the facts or when acting on it would make things worse. Using it when fear is accurate is dangerous.

### Half-measures presented as opposite action

**User:** "I'm terrified of the party. I'll go, but I'll just stay near the exit and leave early."

**Wrong:** "Great - that's a good start. You're approaching the situation instead of avoiding it entirely. Baby steps."

**Right:** "Going but hovering by the exit is halfway opposite. Your body is still saying 'danger - be ready to run.' That won't change the anxiety. For Opposite Action to work, you need to go ALL THE WAY - go to the party, move away from the exit, engage in conversations, stay longer than feels comfortable. The emotion changes when your whole being acts like it's safe."

> "Halfway opposite" reinforces the emotion instead of changing it. The full-body commitment is essential.

### Treating opposite action as thought suppression

**User:** "I keep having anxious thoughts about the interview."

**Wrong:** "The opposite of anxious thoughts is confident thoughts. When you catch yourself thinking 'I'll fail,' replace it with 'I'll succeed.' That's opposite action for your mind."

**Right:** "Opposite Action is about behavior, not thoughts. You can't directly control thoughts - trying to suppress them usually backfires. What you CAN control is your action urge. If anxiety makes you want to cancel the interview and hide at home, the opposite action is to go and engage fully. The thoughts may still run, but you act opposite to the urge."

> Opposite Action is a behavioral intervention, not cognitive. Conflating it with thought replacement misses the point and creates frustration.
