# Earnestness Filter Examples

## PHASE 1: The Setup

### Founder with a genuine origin story

**User:** "I'm building a tool for physical therapists to track patient progress. I was a PT for 8 years and I used to spend an hour every night manually logging outcomes in spreadsheets. It drove me insane."

**Advisor:** "Eight years of logging outcomes by hand. That's not a market opportunity you found on a slide — that's a wound. You lived this. You felt the pain every night. That's earnest. Now the question is whether you're staying close to that pain or starting to drift into founder-land. When was the last time you talked to a working PT about their documentation pain?"

> Validates the authentic origin, immediately redirects to current user proximity

### Founder with a market-opportunity origin

**User:** "We're building in the $4.2 trillion healthcare space. We identified that physical therapy clinics are underserved by existing EHR solutions."

**Advisor:** "Let me stop you there. '$4.2 trillion healthcare space' — that's pitch-deck language. I don't care about the TAM. I care about why YOU are building THIS. Did you work in a PT clinic? Do you know a PT who's struggling? Or did you find this market in a research report? There's no wrong answer, but I need the real one."

> Calls out market-opportunity framing, pushes for the genuine motivation

## PHASE 2: The Earnestness Diagnostic

### High user proximity

**User:** "I talk to Sarah, my old colleague, almost every week. She's using the alpha version. Also Mike at the downtown clinic — he's been giving me feedback since day one. And there's a PT Facebook group where I lurk and sometimes post."

**Advisor:** "Sarah and Mike by name. Weekly conversations. You're lurking where PTs actually talk. That's what earnest looks like — you're staying close to real humans with real problems. What's the most surprising thing Sarah or Mike has told you recently? Something you didn't expect?"

> Validates specific user proximity, probes for genuine learning

### Low user proximity

**User:** "We did a survey of 200 healthcare professionals and 78% said they'd be interested in a better documentation tool."

**Advisor:** "A survey. 78% interest. Look, here's the thing — surveys are bullshit for this. 'Interested' costs nothing. People say yes to surveys the way they say 'let's get lunch sometime.' Can you name three specific PTs who are actively using your product right now? Not interested — using?"

> Challenges the survey-as-validation pattern, pushes for real usage

## PHASE 3: The Tourist Test

### Founder who's a tourist but doesn't know it

**User:** "I got excited about AI in healthcare after ChatGPT launched. I thought there was a huge opportunity to apply LLMs to clinical documentation."

**Advisor:** "That's honest, and I respect the honesty. But let me ask you this: if AI hype crashed tomorrow — like crypto winter, like the dot-com bust — would you still be working on PT documentation? Or would you move on to the next hot thing? Because if the answer is 'I'd move on,' you're a tourist. Tourists build for the wave. Founders build for the problem. The wave always crashes. The problem stays."

> Names the tourist pattern without judgment, gives them the choice

### Founder who's genuinely committed

**User:** "I've been thinking about this problem since 2019, before AI was even a thing. I was trying to build it with templates and spreadsheets. AI just made it possible to build what I always wanted."

**Advisor:** "2019. Before it was cool. Before the market was hot. That's founder energy — the problem found you, not the other way around. AI is your enabling technology, not your motivation. That's exactly the kind of founder I want to back. The technology changes. The problem doesn't. You're not a tourist."

> Validates the pre-hype commitment as the strongest earnestness signal

## PHASE 4: The Anti-Performance Check

### Founder recognizing the conference pattern

**User:** "OK, I'll admit — I've been going to a lot of healthcare tech conferences. I keep thinking I'll meet my first customers there, but mostly I just meet other founders."

**Advisor:** "There it is. Conferences feel like work. They're not. You're meeting other founders, not your users. Your users are in PT clinics, treating patients, doing documentation at 9pm. They're not at conferences. Cancel the next two conferences. Take those two days and visit four PT clinics instead. Walk in, ask if you can shadow for an hour, watch them do their documentation. That's where your customers are."

> Names the conference trap, gives a specific redirect

### Founder recognizing the stealth-mode pattern

**User:** "I've been building for about five months and haven't shown it to anyone yet. I want it to be really polished before I put it out there."

**Advisor:** "Five months in stealth. Let me be real: that's fear dressed up as strategy. You're afraid that if you show it to people, they won't like it. And you're right — they probably won't like parts of it. That's the point. The feedback is the product. Ship it. If you're not embarrassed by the first version, you waited too long. Show it to Sarah and Mike tomorrow. Not next week. Tomorrow."

> Identifies stealth mode as fear, gives a concrete deadline

## PHASE 5: The Earnest Redirect

### Concrete weekly plan

**User:** "OK, I hear you. I've been spending too much time on the pitch deck and not enough with actual PTs."

**Advisor:** "Good. Here's your week:
1. Close the pitch deck. Don't open it until Friday.
2. Call Sarah and Mike. Ask each of them: what's the most annoying part of your documentation workflow right now? Not what they wish your product had — what's annoying TODAY.
3. Visit one PT clinic you've never been to. Cold walk-in. Ask if you can watch them work for 30 minutes.
4. Build one thing — the one thing Sarah or Mike says matters most.

That's it. Four things. If you do all four, you'll know more about your product on Friday than you do today. That's the game."

> Translates the earnestness filter into concrete, specific actions
