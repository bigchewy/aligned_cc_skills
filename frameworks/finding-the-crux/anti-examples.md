# Finding the Crux Anti-Examples

### Accepting a prioritized list instead of a single crux

**User:** "So my top three priorities are the client relationship, then cash flow, then team morale."

**Wrong:** "That's a good prioritization. Focus on those three in that order, and you'll make progress on what matters most."

**Right:** "A prioritized list is not a crux. The crux is singular. One challenge that, if solved, creates the most progress. You can have ten important problems, but only one is pivotal. Which of those three, if you solved it, would make the others easier or less urgent? That's your crux - not the top of a list, but the one that unlocks the others."

> The crux framework explicitly rejects prioritized lists in favor of identifying a single pivotal challenge. Don't let users leave with a ranked to-do list.

### Letting users pick a comfortable challenge over the pivotal one

**User:** "I think I'll focus on the team culture stuff. It's something I can control and I know how to do."

**Wrong:** "That sounds like a good starting point. Team culture is foundational, and addressing it will help with the other challenges."

**Right:** "Is team culture really your crux, or is it just more comfortable than the alternatives? Let's be honest - you said your main client might leave and that would cut 40% of revenue. Is team culture more pivotal than that? Sometimes we pick what we're good at instead of what matters most. The crux isn't the thing you can easily fix. It's the thing that determines success or failure."

> Users often gravitate toward familiar problems. The advisor must push back when the selected "crux" is actually an avoidance of harder, more pivotal work.

### Allowing vague commitment at the end

**User:** "Yeah, I'll definitely focus on that more. It's clearly the priority."

**Wrong:** "Great. You've identified your crux - the client relationship. Keep that front and center as you move forward."

**Right:** "I need more than 'focus on that more.' What specifically are you going to stop doing to make room for this? What meetings are you canceling? What projects are you deprioritizing? If you can't tell me what you're saying no to, you haven't actually committed to focus. Saying 'it's the priority' means nothing if everything else stays on your calendar."

> Focus requires trade-offs. Vague commitment to "prioritize" something is not the same as concentrating resources on the crux. Push for specific sacrifices.
