# How Much the Siblings Share — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Not picking a type

**User:** "Should the new product be part of our brand or its own thing?"

**Wrong:**
"There are pros and cons either way. A shared brand builds equity; a separate brand gives flexibility. It really depends on your long-term goals."

**Right:**
"From what you said, the buyers are different people and nobody knows your company name yet. I'd go endorsed: its own name and look, with a small 'from' line where there's room. If your name becomes known to these buyers, look at monolithic."

**What to do instead:** Run Aaker's two questions, pick one of Olins's three types, and give the reason.

---

## 2. A second brand because it's exciting

**User:** "We want a fresh new brand for the new product. It'll be fun."

**Wrong:**
"Great, a fresh brand lets you start clean. Let's pick a name and a new look."

**Right:**
"One brand is the default. A second one needs a reason. Does the parent add anything here? Does the new product need its own name? If both answers are no, the fun part is a product line, not a brand."

**What to do instead:** Make the second brand earn its place with Aaker's test before anything is designed.

---

## 3. Forcing a sibling under an unknown parent

**User:** "We'll put our main logo big on everything so people learn our name."

**Wrong:**
"Smart. Consistent use of the parent mark builds recognition fast."

**Right:**
"Who's buying this one? If they've never heard of the parent, a big parent logo tells them nothing and crowds out the thing they came for. Recognition comes from use, not from size."

**What to do instead:** Ask whether the parent adds anything for this buyer before deciding how big it shows.

---

## 4. Skipping parts of the share list

**User:** "So it's endorsed. We're done, right?"

**Wrong:**
"Yes, endorsed is the answer. You can figure out the rest as you design."

**Right:**
"The type is half of it. Now each part: name, mark, type, color. Endorsed can still share the same typefaces and paper, or nothing at all. Let's fill each one in."

**What to do instead:** Fill in name, mark, type, and color, each with a pick and a reason.

---

## 5. Changing everything to look different

**User:** "To make them distinct, should we change the font, the colors, the logo style, and the background?"

**Wrong:**
"Yes, changing all four makes them clearly separate products."

**Right:**
"Look at Penguin. It kept the frame and changed only the color, and you could tell every series apart. Change the one thing buyers see first, and keep the rest. If they're branded, change more. If they're endorsed, one or two parts is usually enough."

**What to do instead:** Change as little as the type needs. Keep the frame, change the one part that tells them apart.
