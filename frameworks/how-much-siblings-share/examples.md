# How Much the Siblings Share — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. A two-person studio runs Ledgerly, bookkeeping software for freelancers. It launched a year ago and has about 2,000 paying users. They're adding Ledgerly Payroll, for small shops with two to ten employees. The two share a login and a billing page.

---

## Phase 1: The Family

### Golden path

**User:** "Ledgerly is bookkeeping for freelancers. The freelancer buys and uses it. Payroll is for shop owners with a few employees; the owner buys it, and employees see their pay stubs. About 2,000 people know Ledgerly. Same login and same billing page."

**Say something like:**
"Good. Two different buyers, a freelancer and a shop owner, and a third person who only sees the stub. And they meet at login and billing, so whatever we pick has to survive sitting next to each other there."

**Why it works:** Buyers, users, how known the parent is, and where the two meet are all laid out before any choice is made.

### Struggle: "same buyers"

**User:** "It's basically the same people. Small businesses."

**Say something like:**
"Think of the moment they buy. A freelancer buys bookkeeping when tax season scares them. A shop owner buys payroll when they hire their second person. Same reason? Same price?"

**User:** "No. Different moment, and payroll costs more."

**Why it works:** The question makes the user test their own claim instead of being told it's wrong.

---

## Phase 2: Aaker's Two Questions

### Golden path

**User:** "Does the parent add anything? Some. Freelancers who grow into shops already trust us. Does Payroll need its own name? No, it's the same promise: money stuff, done right."

**Say something like:**
"Then the parent adds something to some buyers, and the sibling doesn't need its own name. Both point toward sharing a lot."

**Why it works:** Each answer has a reason, and the advisor reads them together.

### Edge case: the parent is unknown

**User:** "Honestly, nobody outside our users has heard of Ledgerly."

**Say something like:**
"That's the common case for a new company. A name nobody knows can't lend trust to strangers. But your growing freelancers know it, and they're some of your payroll buyers. So it adds something to them, and nothing to everyone else."

**Why it works:** The answer is split by who's buying, not given as a flat yes or no.

---

## Phase 3: Pick One Type

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"I'd go with monolithic. Same promise, a name your growing users already trust, and they share a login. 'Ledgerly Payroll' says exactly what it is. Next closest is endorsed, a new name 'from Ledgerly,' and I'd pass because a new name costs you the trust you already have, and you'd have two names to grow with one team."

**Why it works:** One type, a reason, and a runner-up with its reason.

### Struggle: wants a bit of each

**User:** "Can't it be monolithic but feel a bit different?"

**Say something like:**
"Every family ends up between two types in practice. Pick the one you'd defend, monolithic. The 'feel a bit different' goes in the share list."

**Why it works:** The choice stays clear and the user's wish has a place.

---

## Phase 4: The Share List

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"For monolithic:
- **Name:** shared. 'Ledgerly Payroll.'
- **Mark:** the same mark. No separate payroll symbol.
- **Type:** the same typefaces.
- **Color:** same ground and neutrals, its own accent, so a shop owner can tell at a glance which screen they're on.
- **Ground:** same.
Tell me where you disagree."

**User:** "The accent makes sense. We've had people mix up screens already."

**Why it works:** Every part has a pick. The one change is the one that solves a real problem, the Penguin move.

### Edge case: shares nothing but a footer

**User:** "What if Payroll had a whole new look and just a 'Ledgerly' line in the footer?"

**Say something like:**
"That's branded with a footnote. It's a real option, but it throws away the trust your Phase 2 answer said you have."

**Why it works:** The alternative is named by type and checked against the earlier answers.

---

## Phase 5: Where the Parent Shows

### Golden path

**User:** "Everywhere. It's monolithic."

**Say something like:**
"Then let's name the places. On the icon, 'Ledgerly' with the payroll accent color. In emails, the same header with 'Payroll' after it. On pay stubs, the employee sees 'Ledgerly Payroll' in the same type as the rest."

**Why it works:** The places are named one by one, including the third person who only sees the stub.

---

## Phase 6: The Family Decision

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Here's the family.
- **Type:** monolithic. Same promise, shared login, trust from growing users.
- **Aaker's test:** parent adds something for growing freelancers; no new name needed.
- **Share list:** name shared, mark shared, type shared, own accent color, same ground.
- **Where the parent shows:** everywhere, as the main name.
- **What would change this:** if payroll buyers turn out to be mostly strangers to Ledgerly, look at endorsed.
Next, check the new accent with The Small-Size and Print Test."

**Why it works:** One page with every decision, the reasons, and what would reopen it.
