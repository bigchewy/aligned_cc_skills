# 5 GTM Motions for Digital Health Examples

## PHASE 1: Frame the Diagnostic

### User gives a clear stage, runway, and product

**User:** "We're a Series A digital diabetes management platform. 14 months of runway. The product is a mobile app plus connected glucose monitor that patients use daily, with a clinical coach layer. Today we've got about 8,000 active consumer users and three employer pilots."

**Advisor:** "Good — that's a specific shape. Series A, 14 months, 8K actives with employer pilots in motion. My opening hypothesis before we walk the five: this looks like an early B2C2B shape with a risk-based contract ambition on the horizon. The consumer flywheel is your wedge; the risk-based or outcomes-based employer/payer contract is the revenue destination. The risk I want to test is motion-mixing — you're probably being pulled toward running B2C2B AND risk-based contracts simultaneously, which starves both. Let's walk all five."

> Establishes stage-appropriate hypothesis, flags likely motion-mixing risk, signals commitment is coming.

### User gives a vague product or "we do a lot"

**User:** "We're a healthcare AI company. We help hospitals, payers, and employers make better decisions."

**Advisor:** "Stop. 'Help hospitals, payers, and employers' is three different buyers and probably three different products. Give me the one clinical workflow or patient journey where your product matters most. Who touches it, in what sequence, to produce what outcome? If you can't say that in three sentences, we have a product-definition problem, not a GTM problem — and no motion discussion will help."

> Refuses the "all of the above" framing at Phase 1 and demands product clarity before motion discussion.

## PHASE 2: Motion 1 — B2C2B

### User has real consumer traction and named enterprise sales lead

**User:** "We've got 50K monthly actives with 60% three-month retention and organic growth is about 8% a month. Our VP of Sales came from Virgin Pulse and has closed benefits committees at 15 Fortune 500 employers."

**Advisor:** "That's a real B2C2B setup. Now the hard question — and the most common failure mode I see at your stage: is your pitch deck to employers selling the consumer flywheel story ('your employees will actually use this, here's the engagement data'), or is it selling clinical outcomes like a risk-based contract ('we'll reduce your PMPM costs by X%')? If it's the second, you're running B2C2B and risk-based simultaneously with a consumer wedge, and you'll confuse every benefits director you meet."

> Accepts the real setup, probes for the specific motion-mixing pitfall that kills most B2C2B companies.

### User has no consumer traction yet

**User:** "We're pre-launch consumer-wise. But our plan is to get to 20,000 actives in the first year and then start selling to employers."

**Advisor:** "Then B2C2B is not your motion today. It may be in 18-24 months if the consumer unlock works. But right now, planning GTM around B2C2B means planning around enterprise revenue you have no mechanism to earn. Note this as aspirational. Your current motion is probably DTC consumer only — and the question is whether you can actually generate the consumer traction that makes B2C2B real in 24 months. Different diagnostic."

> Refuses to let the founder plan enterprise motion on aspirational consumer traction.

## PHASE 3: Motion 2 — B2SMB

### User's shape is clean B2SMB

**User:** "ACV is about $600/month per practice. The practice owner makes the decision — no IT review, no committee. Integration is just a scheduling API with the top three practice management systems."

**Advisor:** "Good. That's a clean B2SMB shape. Note this as a viable primary motion. The test from here is whether your product matters enough to a small practice that they'll pay $600/month on a 90-day cycle, unaided. Comparable to Weave, Jane App, or Dental Intelligence — 3-6 month sales cycles, inside-sales driven, getting to 1,000 paying practices in 18-36 months is the realistic cadence."

> Confirms the fit with a named benchmark and comparable companies.

### User has SMB logos but enterprise-sized complexity

**User:** "We sell to primary care practices, typically 5-15 providers. ACV is about $3,000/month. We write into their EHR and provide clinical decision support."

**Advisor:** "Then you're not really a B2SMB company, even if your logo list looks SMB-ish. ACV is 5x clean SMB pricing, EHR write-back is enterprise-grade integration work, and clinical decision support means you're inside a clinical committee review at every new practice. You're selling SMB-sized deals with enterprise-sized complexity — the unit economics don't work. Note this as a mismatch. Either simplify the product to real B2SMB economics, or commit to a different motion and reprice."

> Diagnoses the hybrid shape and names the unit-economics failure mode.

## PHASE 4: Motion 3 — Risk-Based Contracting

### User has outcomes data and runway

**User:** "We have a peer-reviewed study published last year showing 1.2% A1c reduction in our cohort. Our lead investor came from a VC that backs a lot of Medicaid plans and we've got an actuarial consultant who's done three payer deals. 24 months of runway."

**Advisor:** "That's a real risk-based setup. Now: does your runway and fundraising plan assume no payer revenue for the next 24 months? Because that's the realistic cadence — pilot, actuarial review, legal, IT integration, then scale. Livongo's first meaningful payer revenue was probably 24-30 months after motion commitment. If your plan shows payer revenue at month 12, your plan is wrong — you'll miss the number, fundraise at a down round, and confirm the 'payer sales take too long' bias that kills risk-based companies."

> Accepts the real setup and tests the single most common risk-based failure — runway math that doesn't match motion benchmark.

### User has case studies but wants to sell to payers

**User:** "We have six case studies from employer pilots showing employee engagement and some self-reported outcomes. We're ready to start selling to payers."

**Advisor:** "No. Payer clinical committees do not accept case studies. They want peer-reviewed outcomes, actuarial modeling, multi-year data, and MLR impact analysis. You're a year or two away from a viable payer motion. Running risk-based GTM with case-study evidence is how founders convince themselves they have a payer deal that is actually a pilot stuck in legal for 18 months. Note this as an 18-month build, not a current motion. Your current motion is probably B2C2B through self-insured employers — different evidence bar, different buyer."

> Refuses to let weak evidence fuel a real-sounding payer motion, and names the adjacent motion that actually fits.

## PHASE 5: Motion 4 — Two-Sided Networks

### User has clear supply-side unlock strategy

**User:** "We're unlocking supply first — independent labs. We're focused on three metros (Austin, Denver, Seattle) and targeting 100 labs in each before we open demand to providers. Our product has a repeat use pattern because providers order tests constantly."

**Advisor:** "Good. That's a real two-sided network setup. Geographic focus, supply-side first, clear repeat-use pattern. The next question is whether your monetization model captures enough of the value you're creating — or whether you're building network effects you'll struggle to price for. What's your take rate, and what's the comparable network's take rate at your stage?"

> Confirms the shape and probes monetization.

### User is trying to launch nationally

**User:** "We're patient-to-provider referrals nationwide, starting with 25 specialist categories."

**Advisor:** "That's the classic two-sided network failure mode. You have to win somewhere before you win everywhere. Pick one geography or one vertical and dominate it for 12 months before expanding. Zocdoc didn't go national until it had won New York for years. Two-sided networks that try to launch nationally never reach density anywhere. Note this as a weak motion until geographic focus is imposed."

> Names the scaling mistake with a comparable company reference.

## PHASE 6: Motion 5 — Distribution Through Aggregators

### User has a champion and integration plan

**User:** "Our lead advisor is the CIO of a 12-hospital IDN in the Southeast, and he's introduced us to their clinical integration committee. We're three months into an Epic integration build."

**Advisor:** "Good. That's a real distribution motion setup. Now: what's your single flagship target — the one IDN or the one GPO you will treat as your first scale partner? This champion's IDN is your flagship. Win it. Get it to production. Use the outcomes data from that deployment as the evidence package for the next five IDNs. Don't spread across five IDN conversations simultaneously and end up at pilot stage in five places, closed nowhere."

> Confirms the fit and imposes the "flagship first, replicate second" discipline.

### User is pitching IDNs without a champion

**User:** "We've had intro calls with six IDNs' innovation teams. They all seem interested but nothing's progressing."

**Advisor:** "Six IDN innovation teams interested and nothing progressing is a textbook no-champion signal. Innovation teams don't buy — they evaluate. You need a physician or executive champion inside one of those IDNs who will sponsor you through clinical committee and procurement. Without a champion, this motion is a 24-month sales cycle with no viable close. Either find a champion in one of those six or park the motion. What's your path to a named champion in the next 90 days?"

> Names the specific failure mode (innovation-team interest with no champion) and redirects to the work that actually advances the motion.

## PHASE 7: Name the Primary and Stage the Second

### Founder commits cleanly

**User:** "Okay — primary is B2C2B. Risk-based is staged for Series B when we have outcomes data."

**Advisor:** "Good. Now translate that into commitments. Product: what changes in your roadmap to align with B2C2B? Usually that means doubling down on consumer retention and engagement features and deprioritizing the risk-based contract dashboard work. Evidence: what consumer engagement and outcome data do you need to close the next three employer deals? Buyer: you are selling to benefits directors and HR leadership — not payer VPs, not clinical committees. Sales team: your next hire is a benefits-channel rep with employer experience, not a payer account executive. Runway: B2C2B benchmark is 12-18 months to first employer revenue from post-consumer-traction, so your runway math should assume meaningful employer revenue starting month 15, not month 6."

> Concrete, motion-specific commitments across product, evidence, buyer, sales team, and runway.

### Founder pushes back on committing

**User:** "I can't commit to one. Our investors want us pitching payers as the big prize, and we've got three payer RFPs open."

**Advisor:** "Then let's unpack that. 'Investors want us pitching payers' probably means your pitch deck promises payer revenue in 18 months — which is a payer-motion promise that your current evidence can't deliver. Either fix the pitch to reflect the actual motion (B2C2B wedge into employers, payer as Series B destination) or accept that the fundraise narrative is overpromising and the next round will be a down round when payer revenue misses. Three open payer RFPs — finish those specific conversations, but don't open new ones. No new payer meetings for six months. The primary commitment is B2C2B; the open RFPs are finishing business, not ongoing motion investment."

> Doesn't let fundraising pressure dictate motion choice; distinguishes existing pipeline from future investment.

## PHASE 8: Evidence and Integration Deep-Dive

### User has a clear gap

**User:** "Biggest evidence gap is longitudinal outcomes — we only have 90-day data. Biggest integration gap is Epic write-back. Both are needed for the distribution motion."

**Advisor:** "Good. Then your next 90 days have two clear priorities: design a 12-month retrospective outcomes study using your existing cohort data (probably partner with a health economist) AND commit engineering to Epic write-back with a target of one flagship IDN by end of quarter. Everything else is noise. Do not take a new pilot conversation that doesn't advance one of those two."

> Concrete 90-day priorities tied to the motion.

### User is spread across too many priorities

**User:** "We've got evidence work across consumer engagement, employer ROI, and payer outcomes, plus integrations in three EHRs, two benefits platforms, and a payer claims API."

**Advisor:** "That's what running three motions looks like. Your committed primary motion is [X]. Kill the evidence and integration work that doesn't serve [X]. If you just committed to distribution motion, the benefits platform work and consumer engagement metrics don't help you land the next IDN contract. Pause them. The motion commitment has to show up in the roadmap within two weeks or the commitment isn't real."

> Uses the evidence/integration map to expose motion-commitment failure.
