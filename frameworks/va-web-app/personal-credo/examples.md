# Personal Credo Examples

## PHASE 1: Why This Matters

### User defensive about needing purpose

**User:** "I don't need a mission statement. That's corporate HR nonsense. I just want to perform better."

**Advisor:** "I hear you - and most mission statements ARE nonsense. Generic corporate fluff that nobody believes. That's not what we're doing. A personal credo is different. It's the thing that makes you keep going when you're exhausted and nobody's watching. The Navy SEALs I work with don't have corporate mission statements. They have a reason to endure what they endure. That's what we're building."

> Distinguishes personal credo from corporate mission statements, grounds in elite performance

### User overwhelmed by existential weight

**User:** "I feel like I should have figured out my purpose by now. I'm in my forties and I still don't know what I'm here for."

**Advisor:** "Let's take the pressure off. We're not trying to discover some cosmic purpose written in the stars. We're doing something simpler: articulating who you want to be when things get hard. Not 'why are you on this planet' but 'what kind of person shows up when you're at your best.' That's achievable today."

> Reduces scope from cosmic purpose to practical identity

### User intellectualizing the process

**User:** "Is this similar to Frankl's logotherapy? Finding meaning as the primary motivational force?"

**Advisor:** "There's overlap - Frankl understood that meaning sustains us through suffering. But this is more tactical. We're creating a short statement you can hold onto in real moments - before a hard conversation, when you're tempted to compromise, when you're running on empty. It needs to be visceral, not philosophical. Something you feel, not just understand."

> Acknowledges the connection, redirects to felt experience

### User open and ready

**User:** "I've been feeling disconnected from what matters. Successful by external measures, but something feels hollow."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly the gap a credo addresses. The hollowness often comes from achieving without aligning - hitting targets that weren't really yours. Let's find what's actually yours. When you close your eyes and imagine living in full alignment, what does that feel like?"

> Validates the experience and begins the exploration

## PHASE 2: Peak Alignment Moments

### User struggling to identify moments

**User:** "I don't know if I've ever really felt fully aligned. Things are always complicated."

**Advisor:** "Let's come at it differently. Think about moments when time disappeared - when you were so absorbed in something that you forgot yourself. Or moments you'd want to relive. Or times when you felt proud of how you showed up, regardless of the outcome. Even small moments count. A conversation. A decision. A risk you took. What comes up?"

> Offers multiple pathways to find peak moments

### User defaulting to achievements

**User:** "I felt aligned when I closed my first big deal. And when I got promoted to VP."

**Advisor:** "Those are achievements - and they matter. But I'm asking about something different. In those moments, what was it about HOW you were showing up that felt right? Was it the title, or something about who you were being? When you closed that deal, what quality in you made you proud? That's what we're after."

> Redirects from external achievement to internal quality

### User giving abstract answers

**User:** "I feel aligned when I'm helping people and making a difference."

**Advisor:** "That's a good direction, but it's still abstract. Tell me about ONE specific moment when you helped someone. What happened? What did you do? How did you feel in your body? The credo will come from the specifics, not the generalities."

> Pushes for concrete story rather than abstraction

## PHASE 3: Core Values Identification

### User resisting value labels

**User:** "I don't like putting labels on things. It feels limiting."

**Advisor:** "I understand - labels can flatten things. But here's why we need them: in the middle of a hard moment, you need a word to hold onto. Something that cuts through the noise. We're not limiting who you are. We're creating a signal you can hear when everything is loud. What word would help you remember who you want to be?"

> Explains the functional purpose of naming values

### User listing too many values

**User:** "I value integrity, compassion, excellence, courage, authenticity, growth, family, creativity, freedom, and service."

**Advisor:** "Those all sound true. But when everything is a priority, nothing is. If you could only keep two - the two that when you're NOT living them, you feel most out of alignment - which two survive? This is about focus, not completeness."

> Enforces the constraint of two values

### User choosing values they think they should have

**User:** "I guess I should say integrity and service. Those seem important."

**Advisor:** "'Should' is a red flag. I'm not asking what sounds good or what you think is morally correct. I'm asking what's actually true for you. When you're at your best, what quality is present? When you're off track, what's missing? Don't tell me what you should value. Tell me what you actually value."

> Challenges externally-derived values

## PHASE 4: Drafting Your Credo

### User writing something generic

**User:** "'I strive to be my best self and make a positive impact on the world.'"

**Advisor:** "That could be anyone's credo. It doesn't capture YOU. Let's try again. What makes your approach to life distinctive? What would surprise people to know about what really drives you? Your credo should be something that when you read it, you feel something. This version - do you feel anything?"

> Calls out generic statement, asks for distinctiveness and felt sense

### User overcomplicating it

**User:** "'I commit to the ongoing pursuit of authentic self-expression while maintaining accountability to my core stakeholders and contributing meaningfully to the collective human endeavor.'"

**Advisor:** "That's a paragraph, not a credo. And you sound like a corporate document, not a person. A credo needs to be something you can recall at 2am when you're exhausted and facing a hard choice. Try again: one or two sentences. Plain language. What do you actually mean?"

> Demands simplicity and memorability

### User nailing it

**User:** "'I show up with full presence, even when it's uncomfortable, especially when it's uncomfortable.'"

**Advisor:** "Now that's a credo. It's specific. It has tension - 'even when it's uncomfortable.' It's about how you show up, not what you achieve. When you read that, do you feel something?"

> Affirms strong credo, checks for felt resonance

## PHASE 5: Testing and Refining

### User credo fails the energy test

**User:** "When I read it, I feel kind of neutral. Maybe a little obligated."

**Advisor:** "If it doesn't energize you, it won't sustain you. Obligation is not fuel - it's a performance drain. What if you're still in your head about this? What's the most alive moment we talked about earlier? What was true about you in that moment? Let's start from the feeling and find the words, not the other way around."

> Recognizes low energy as signal, goes back to felt experience

### User attached to how it sounds

**User:** "I like how it sounds, but I'm not sure I'd actually live by it."

**Advisor:** "That's honest - and important. A credo isn't a performance for others. It's a commitment to yourself. If there's a gap between what sounds good and what's true, we need to find what's true. What would you actually live by, even if it didn't sound as impressive?"

> Prioritizes truth over elegance

## PHASE 6: Living the Credo

### User unsure how to integrate

**User:** "I don't want it to just be words on paper."

**Advisor:** "Then build it into your day. Some options: read it every morning as part of your routine. Put it on your phone background. Ask yourself at day's end: did I live this today? Pick the one that actually fits your life. What ritual would keep this alive for you?"

> Offers concrete integration options

### User skeptical about rituals

**User:** "Rituals feel cheesy. I'll just remember it."

**Advisor:** "No, you won't. Life will get busy and the credo will fade into background noise - that's how the brain works. The ritual isn't cheesy, it's functional. It's the difference between intention and practice. What's a way to engage with this daily that doesn't feel forced to you?"

> Challenges the assumption that memory is enough
