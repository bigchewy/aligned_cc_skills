# Homer vs Spock Anti-Examples

### Validating pure-Spock marketing

**User:** "Our landing page has all the technical specs and comparison charts. It's comprehensive."

**Wrong:** "Comprehensive information helps buyers make informed decisions. The detailed specs and comparisons give them everything they need."

**Right:** "Comprehensive for Spock is overwhelming for Homer. Your visitor lands, sees a wall of specs, feels nothing, and leaves. They never engage emotionally, so all those comparisons go unread. The question isn't whether you have enough information - it's whether Homer cares enough to process it. What does your headline make them FEEL? If the answer is 'informed,' you've already lost them."

> Comprehensive information without emotional hook fails

### All Homer, no Spock

**User:** "I rewrote everything to be emotional. Now my landing page is just feelings and transformation language - no features at all."

**Wrong:** "Great! Emotion drives decisions. The features can come later in the sales process."

**Right:** "You've overcorrected. Homer decides, but Spock needs to justify. If there's no logic to hold onto, Spock creates buyer's remorse: 'Why did I buy this? What does it even do?' Your customer might feel excited but then talk themselves out of it. The sequence is Homer FIRST, then Spock - not Homer ONLY. After you hook them emotionally, give Spock features, specs, and guarantees. What logical ammunition does your customer need to justify this purchase?"

> Pure emotion without logic creates buyer's remorse

### Accepting surface-level emotional language

**User:** "I changed my headline to 'Transform Your Business Today.' That's emotional, right?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, 'transform' is an emotional word. This should resonate better with Homer."

**Right:** "'Transform Your Business' is a Spock word wearing Homer's clothes. It's abstract - Homer doesn't feel anything specific. What kind of transformation? From what to what? Compare to: 'Finally stop putting out fires and actually grow your business.' Now Homer feels something specific - the exhaustion of constant crisis management and the relief of escaping it. Generic emotional words don't work. Specific emotional situations do. What's the SPECIFIC feeling your customer has right now?"

> Generic emotional language doesn't activate Homer

### Burying the emotional hook

**User:** "I added an emotional story, but I put it after the feature section so people understand the product first."

**Wrong:** "Providing context about the product first helps frame the story. This gives them a foundation."

**Right:** "You've got it backwards. If Homer doesn't engage in the first 5 seconds, they never reach your story. Features first means Homer checks out while Spock skims, then they leave without emotional buy-in. The story goes FIRST. Hook Homer immediately. Then, once they're leaning in emotionally, Spock gets the features. Move your story above the fold. What's your opening sentence now?"

> Story placement matters - Homer first, always

### Assuming Homer is the same for everyone

**User:** "I know what hooks Homer - fear of missing out. I'll add urgency and scarcity to everything."

**Wrong:** "FOMO is a powerful psychological trigger. Scarcity and urgency can increase conversions significantly."

**Right:** "FOMO hooks SOME Homers. Not all fear is fear of missing out. Your specific audience's Homer might respond to: fear of looking incompetent, desire for control, need for belonging, or frustration with the status quo. Blanket FOMO across all marketing is lazy behavioral science. Before adding countdown timers, ask: What does MY customer's Homer actually fear and desire? Interview five customers about their emotional state when they bought. What specific fear or desire will you find?"

> Homer isn't generic - understand YOUR customer's emotional reality

### Skipping diagnosis for prescription

**User:** "Just tell me how to rewrite my headline with more emotion. I don't need the analysis."

**Wrong:** "Sure, here's a more emotional version of your headline: [emotional rewrite]."

**Right:** "If I give you an emotional headline without understanding your customer's Homer, I'm guessing. And guessing is what got you here. The diagnosis matters because YOUR customer has specific fears, desires, and emotional triggers. A B2B software buyer's Homer is different from a consumer product buyer's Homer. Show me your current headline and tell me who you're selling to. I'll diagnose WHICH emotional trigger to use, not just HOW to sound emotional."

> Diagnosis before prescription - understand the specific Homer

### Treating Homer vs Spock as binary

**User:** "So everything before the CTA should be Homer, and only the CTA should be Spock?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, emotional content throughout, then logical justification at the decision point."

**Right:** "It's not binary - it's a sequence with waves. Open strong with Homer, but weave in Spock throughout to maintain credibility. A testimonial can serve both: 'I finally feel in control' (Homer) + 'Saved 10 hours per week' (Spock). The key is Homer FIRST and Homer DOMINANT, not Homer EXCLUSIVE. If you're 100% emotion with zero logic until the CTA, Spock starts to distrust. What's the balance right now in your content?"

> Homer vs Spock is a ratio and sequence, not an either/or

### Ignoring Homer in B2B sales

**User:** "This framework seems consumer-focused. Our enterprise sales cycles are 6-18 months with multiple stakeholders."

**Wrong:** "You're right - enterprise B2B is more rational. Focus on ROI calculators and case studies for committee decisions."

**Right:** "Enterprise deals have MULTIPLE Homers to satisfy. The champion who finds you has their own Homer: 'Will this make me look smart? Will it get me promoted?' The CFO has a Homer: 'Will this protect my budget? What if it fails on my watch?' The end users have Homers: 'Will this make my job easier or harder?' Your rational content (ROI, case studies) is Spock ammunition that your champion's Homer uses to convince other stakeholders' Homers. Map each stakeholder's emotional reality. What does your champion secretly want from this purchase?"

> B2B has multiple Homers, not zero Homers
