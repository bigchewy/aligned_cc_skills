# Homer vs Spock Examples

## Phase 1: Understand the Brain Hierarchy

### User doesn't believe B2B is emotional

**User:** "This doesn't apply to B2B. Our buyers are procurement teams making rational decisions based on specifications."

**Advisor:** "That's the myth that keeps B2B marketing boring and ineffective.

Think about what a procurement manager is actually thinking:
- 'Will this make me look competent to my boss?' (Status - Homer)
- 'What if this vendor fails and I get blamed?' (Fear - Homer)
- 'Is this company trustworthy enough to stake my reputation on?' (Trust - Homer)

The RFP spreadsheet they fill out is Spock rationalizing what Homer already decided. If Homer doesn't feel safe and excited about your company, they'll find rational reasons to reject even the best specs.

What does your ideal buyer secretly fear about making this purchase?"

> Challenge the 'B2B is rational' assumption

### User already understands but wants confirmation

**User:** "I think I get it - emotion first, then logic. Like how car ads show people looking cool, then mention horsepower at the end."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. Car advertisers have known this for decades. Homer sees the lifestyle, the freedom, the status. Homer decides 'I want that feeling.' Then Spock gets 0-60 times and safety ratings to justify the purchase to the spouse.

Now apply it to your situation. What are you working on - a landing page, email sequence, sales pitch? Let me diagnose your Homer vs Spock ratio."

> Validate understanding and move to application

## Phase 2: Diagnose Current Homer vs Spock Ratio

### User with feature-heavy landing page

**User:** "Here's my headline: 'AI-Powered Project Management with Gantt Charts, Resource Allocation, and Real-Time Collaboration.' Then we list 12 features with screenshots."

**Advisor:** "You're 100% Spock. Zero Homer.

**The problem:** A visitor lands, sees 'AI-Powered Project Management,' feels nothing, and thinks 'I already have a project management tool.' There's no emotional hook.

**What Homer actually cares about:**
- 'I'm drowning in chaos and deadlines'
- 'My team keeps missing things'
- 'I look disorganized to leadership'

**Homer-first version:** 'Finally feel in control of your projects (instead of controlled BY them).'

Now Homer feels something. Then Spock gets Gantt charts and AI-powered features as justification.

What emotional state are your customers in when they search for a solution like yours?"

> Diagnose pure-Spock content and redirect to Homer

### User with some emotional content

**User:** "Our homepage says 'Stop wasting time on manual processes' then shows how our automation works."

**Advisor:** "'Stop wasting time' is touching Homer - frustration is an emotion. Good instinct.

But 'how our automation works' immediately pivots to Spock. You hooked Homer, then lost them.

**Your ratio:** About 20% Homer, 80% Spock

**The fix:** Stay with Homer longer. After 'Stop wasting time,' agitate:
- 'You know the feeling - it's Friday, you're still copying data between spreadsheets, and everyone else has gone home.'
- Paint that picture. Let Homer FEEL it.

THEN show the automation. Now Spock has something to justify.

What does 'wasting time' actually feel like for your customers? Get specific."

> Identify partial Homer and show how to deepen it

## Phase 3: Identify What Hooks Homer

### User struggling to find emotional angle

**User:** "We sell accounting software. There's nothing emotional about accounting."

**Advisor:** "There's nothing emotional about FEATURES. There's everything emotional about the OUTCOME.

Think about who buys accounting software:
- The small business owner who lies awake worrying about cash flow
- The bookkeeper terrified of making an error that triggers an audit
- The founder who feels stupid because they don't understand their own numbers

Those are emotional realities.

**Homer hooks:**
- 'Finally understand your business finances (without an accounting degree)'
- 'Sleep better knowing your books are always audit-ready'
- 'Feel confident when investors ask about your numbers'

Now we're talking to Homer. Which emotional reality fits your customer best?"

> Find emotion behind seemingly boring products

### User with clear emotional understanding

**User:** "Our customers are exhausted small business owners. They feel overwhelmed, behind, and like they're failing. They want to feel competent and in control."

**Advisor:** "That's the Homer hook right there.

**Before:** They feel overwhelmed, behind, failing
**After:** They feel competent, in control, confident

Your marketing should make them FEEL that transformation before you explain HOW you deliver it.

**Homer-first opening:**
'Remember when running your business felt exciting instead of exhausting? That feeling isn't gone - it's buried under chaos. Let's dig it out.'

Now Homer is leaning in. They feel seen. THEN you can explain features.

What's the ONE transformation that would make your customers feel like they're winning again?"

> Build on clear emotional insight

## Phase 4: Redesign with Homer First

### User wants landing page restructured

**User:** "Can you show me exactly how to restructure my landing page? Here's what I have: Headline about features, then feature list, then pricing, then FAQ."

**Advisor:** "Classic Spock structure. Here's the Homer-first rebuild:

**1. Hero Section (Homer)**
- Headline: Emotional outcome, not features
- Subhead: 'For [specific person] who [emotional pain]'
- Image: Show the transformation, not the product

**2. Agitation Section (Homer)**
- 'You know the feeling when [their daily frustration]...'
- 2-3 specific pain points they'll recognize
- Make them nod and think 'yes, that's me'

**3. Transformation Section (Homer)**
- 'Imagine instead [their desired emotional state]...'
- Paint the picture of life after
- Testimonial with emotional quote, not feature praise

**4. How It Works (Spock)**
- NOW show features - Homer is hooked
- 3-5 key capabilities, not 12
- Connect each feature to an emotional benefit

**5. Social Proof (Homer + Spock)**
- Testimonials: emotional transformation + specific results
- Logos for credibility (Spock)

**6. Pricing (Spock)**
- Move after they want it, not before

**7. CTA (Both)**
- Emotional urgency: 'Stop [pain], start [transformation]'
- Logical safety: 'Risk-free trial / Money-back guarantee'

Want me to draft the hero section first?"

> Provide specific structural template

### User wants email sequence help

**User:** "I need to rewrite a sales email. Currently it lists features and ends with 'Schedule a demo.'"

**Advisor:** "Feature-list emails get deleted. Here's the Homer-first email structure:

**Subject line (Homer):** Make them feel curious, not informed
- Not: 'New Features in Our Platform'
- Try: 'The 3am email I wish I'd never sent'

**Opening (Homer):** Start with a story or emotional hook
- 'Last week, I talked to a [their role] who was [their pain]...'
- Or: 'Remember the last time you [frustrating moment]?'

**Agitate (Homer):** Make them feel the problem
- 2-3 sentences deepening the pain
- 'And the worst part is...'

**Bridge (Homer to Spock):** Transition with hope
- 'But what if you could [emotional outcome]?'

**Solution (Spock):** Now features, briefly
- 3 bullet points max
- Each tied to an outcome, not just a function

**Close (Both):**
- Homer: 'Stop [pain] this week'
- Spock: 'See it in action - free 15-minute demo'

What's the story you could open with?"

> Apply framework to email format

## Phase 5: Apply the Homer Test

### User's revised content passes

**User:** "Here's my new headline: 'Finally feel organized (not overwhelmed) running your business.' Does it pass the Homer test?"

**Advisor:** "Let's check:

1. **Does it make someone FEEL something?** Yes - relief. The contrast between organized and overwhelmed creates emotional resonance.

2. **Would someone share it because of how it made them feel?** Possibly - it's relatable. Any overwhelmed business owner would nod.

3. **Remove features - does it still resonate?** Yes - there are no features in it, and it still works.

**Verdict:** Passes the Homer Test.

**One refinement:** 'Finally' is doing heavy lifting - it implies they've been trying and failing. Consider amplifying that:
'Finally feel organized running your business (yes, it's actually possible).'

The parenthetical reinforces Homer's doubt and addresses it.

What comes after this headline?"

> Validate passing content and offer refinement

### User's content still needs work

**User:** "New version: 'Get organized with our powerful project management features.'"

**Advisor:** "Let's run the Homer Test:

1. **Does it make someone FEEL something?** Barely. 'Get organized' is a weak emotional pull. 'Powerful features' is Spock.

2. **Would someone share this?** No - it's generic. Every project management tool says this.

3. **Remove features - does it work?** 'Get organized' is too vague without 'how.'

**Verdict:** Doesn't pass. You've softened the Spock but not strengthened Homer.

**The issue:** 'Get organized' tells them what to do, not how they'll feel.

**Try:** 'Stop dreading Monday mornings' - now Homer feels something specific.

Or: 'Finally know what your team is actually working on' - addresses a specific emotional pain.

What's the emotional pain your customers feel MOST acutely?"

> Show why content fails the test and redirect

## Phase 6: Implementation Roadmap

### User ready to implement

**User:** "This makes sense. I'm going to rewrite my homepage this weekend. Any final advice?"

**Advisor:** "Three things:

**1. Start with just the headline**
Get that right before touching anything else. If the headline doesn't hook Homer, nothing else matters.

**2. Read it out loud**
Homer responds to how things sound, not just what they say. If it sounds like a robot wrote it, Homer tunes out.

**3. Test one change**
Don't rewrite everything at once. Change the headline, measure results, then move to the next section.

**The checklist to tape to your monitor:**
- Does the headline make someone FEEL something?
- Is there a story or emotional hook in the first 3 sentences?
- Are features coming AFTER emotional buy-in?
- Does the CTA have both urgency (Homer) and safety (Spock)?

What's the ONE thing you'll change first?"

> Provide actionable implementation guidance

### User overwhelmed by the framework

**User:** "This is a lot to remember. How do I apply this in the moment when I'm writing?"

**Advisor:** "One question. That's all you need:

**'Am I talking to Homer or Spock right now?'**

If you're writing about features, capabilities, specifications, or ROI - you're talking to Spock.

If you're writing about feelings, fears, desires, or transformations - you're talking to Homer.

**The simple rule:** Open with Homer. Close with Spock.

Before hitting publish on ANYTHING - email, landing page, social post - ask: 'Would someone FEEL something reading the first sentence?'

If no, rewrite until yes.

What are you writing next that we can apply this to?"

> Simplify to one actionable question
