# ABCDE Reframe Anti-Examples

### Doing positive thinking instead of accurate thinking

**User:** "The project failed and I'm worried about my job security."

**Wrong:** "Let's reframe that negatively. What if the project failure is actually a gift? An opportunity to learn and grow? Try to see the positive!"

**Right:** "Let's examine this accurately. First, the adversity: what specifically failed, and what's the actual impact? Then we'll look at the beliefs your mind generated. Some might be accurate, some might be distortions. We're after truth, not positivity."

> ABCDE is cognitive realism, not optimism theater. The goal is accurate thinking, which may include acknowledging real problems.

### Rushing through without getting specific beliefs

**User:** "I feel terrible about how the meeting went."

**Wrong:** "Okay, so something went wrong in the meeting (A), you had some negative thoughts (B), and now you feel bad (C). Let's skip to disputation - what's a more positive way to think about it?"

**Right:** "Let's slow down. What specifically happened in the meeting? That's your adversity. Now, what thoughts fired in your mind when that happened? I want the exact words - 'I always...' or 'They think...' or 'This means...' The specific beliefs are where the distortion lives."

> The power is in naming specific beliefs. Rushing past them to "reframe" doesn't work because you haven't identified what to reframe.

### Disputing valid concerns

**User:** "I'm worried because I've missed three deadlines and my manager seemed frustrated."

**Wrong:** "Let's dispute that worry. Maybe your manager isn't really frustrated. Maybe missing deadlines isn't that big a deal. What evidence do you have that anything is actually wrong?"

**Right:** "Three missed deadlines and visible frustration - those are real data points, not distortions. The question is: What is your mind telling you those facts MEAN? Are you going from 'I've struggled recently' to 'I'm incompetent and will be fired'? That jump is where the distortion might live."

> Don't dispute facts - dispute interpretations. If there's a real problem, the ABCDE helps separate the problem from the catastrophizing about the problem.
