# 80/20 Audit Examples

## PHASE 1: The Setup

### User choosing work/business focus

**User:** "I want to audit my business. I feel like I'm working constantly but the results don't match the effort."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly when you need this audit. Working constantly, results don't match. You're spending time on the wrong 80%. Let's find the 20% that actually produces results, and the 20% that produces most of your problems. We'll need your calendar, customer list, and product breakdown."

> Validates the symptom and sets up what data they'll need

### User choosing time/energy focus

**User:** "I'm exhausted. I have no time for myself. I want to audit where my time goes."

**Advisor:** "When you're exhausted and time-poor, there's a Pareto distribution hiding in your schedule. Some activities give energy, most drain it. Some produce fulfillment, most are just... filler. Let's find what's actually worth your time and what's secretly stealing it."

> Frames time/energy audit through the 80/20 lens

## PHASE 2: Identify the Positive 20%

### User with clear positive patterns

**User:** "Looking at my calendar... the things that made me feel most alive were: the two strategic planning sessions with my leadership team, the product design reviews, and the dinner I had with that key customer. Everything else was just... meetings."

**Advisor:** "Strategy sessions, design reviews, customer dinners. That's your positive 20%. Notice the pattern: high-impact, creative, relationship-building. You probably spent 10% of your time on those three things, but they produced almost all your satisfaction. What else shows up that way?"

> Identifies the pattern across positive experiences

### User struggling to identify positives

**User:** "I don't remember many positive peaks. Most of my weeks kind of blur together."

**Advisor:** "That's a signal itself. If you can't remember positive peaks, either you're not having them, or they're crowded out by noise. Let me ask differently: if you could only work 2 hours a day and had to choose, what would you do? That's usually where the positive 20% lives."

> Uses the heart attack question as a forcing function

## PHASE 3: Identify the Negative 20%

### User being honest about drains

**User:** "The negative is clear: the weekly ops review that goes two hours and never produces action, the customer who calls constantly with complaints but doesn't pay much, and the spreadsheet reporting I do every Friday that nobody reads."

**Advisor:** "Three specific things: useless meeting, problem customer, unread reports. That's maybe 20% of your time but probably 80% of your frustration. The ops review drains two hours of energy. The customer drains you emotionally. The reporting drains you for literally no value. These are targets."

> Names specific negative items and their costs

### User defending their negatives

**User:** "But the ops review is important for accountability. The customer has been with us for years. The reporting is required by finance."

**Advisor:** "You just defended all three with vague justifications. 'Important for accountability' - has it ever produced actual accountability? 'Been with us for years' - does history justify current drain? 'Required by finance' - have you asked finance if they read it? These are the things you keep doing because stopping feels scary. That's exactly why they persist."

> Challenges automatic defense of negative activities

## PHASE 4: The Heart Attack Test

### User getting real

**User:** "If I could only work 2 hours a day... I'd probably do: one strategic decision per day, one customer conversation, and one team check-in. That's it. Everything else would have to... just not happen."

**Advisor:** "One decision, one customer, one team check-in. Two hours. And you know what? You'd probably produce 80% of your current results. Because that's where the leverage is. The other six hours of your day aren't zero value - but they're a fraction of the value of those two. Now the question is: why aren't you doing that already?"

> Reflects the clarity of the 2-hour answer

### User resisting the question

**User:** "That's not realistic. I can't actually only work 2 hours. I have obligations."

**Advisor:** "I'm not asking what's realistic. I'm asking what's essential. If a heart attack forced you to choose, what would you choose? This isn't about working less - it's about seeing clearly what actually matters versus what just fills time. What would make the cut?"

> Refocuses on the purpose of the question

## PHASE 5: Double Down Strategy

### User making concrete plans

**User:** "I'm going to protect Tuesday and Thursday mornings for strategic work. No meetings, no emails. I'm also scheduling monthly dinners with my top 5 customers."

**Advisor:** "Good - but protecting time isn't enough. You have to fill it with the high-leverage work. Tuesday and Thursday mornings for strategy - what specifically will you do? Block it for 'strategic work' and it'll get swallowed by urgent stuff. Block it for 'review product roadmap' and you have something to protect."

> Pushes for specificity in the double-down plan

## PHASE 6: Eliminate / Delegate / Automate

### User making cuts

**User:** "The ops review - we're eliminating the meeting and replacing it with an async update. The problem customer - I'm having a conversation about whether this relationship still makes sense. The Friday reports - I'm going to ask finance if they actually need them."

**Advisor:** "Good. Eliminate the meeting format, delegate the customer conversation to a decision, investigate whether the reports are actually required. For the customer, decide what the 'no' sounds like before you have the conversation. And for the reports - if finance says they don't need them, kill them immediately. Don't ask again, just stop."

> Affirms specific actions for each negative item

## PHASE 7: The Implementation Plan

### User with clear first action

**User:** "First thing I'm eliminating: the Friday reports. I'm sending an email to finance today asking if anyone reads them. If the answer is no or silence, I'm stopping this week."

**Advisor:** "Send the email. If you get silence or 'not really,' the reports die this week. That's 3-4 hours back every month. And here's the thing: once you eliminate one thing and the world doesn't end, you'll find it easier to eliminate the next. Start with the easiest kill and build momentum."

> Affirms specific, immediate action with timeline
