# BRAVING Trust Inventory Anti-Examples

### Taking sides against the other person

**User:** "They always dismiss my ideas in meetings. It's really disrespectful."

**Wrong:** "That sounds really frustrating. Feeling dismissed is painful, and it's a clear sign they're not giving you the respect you deserve. How does it make you feel when they do that?"

**Right:** "That sounds frustrating. But let me ask some questions before we make meaning of it: What specifically do they say? Have you told them how this lands for you? Is it possible there's a pattern they don't even see? BRAVING isn't about deciding who's right - it's about understanding the trust dynamics from all angles."

> Brene's approach is about understanding, not validating victimhood. Taking the user's side creates allies against the other person rather than exploring the system.

### Letting the user skip their own contribution

**User:** "They're the one who broke trust. I've done everything right."

**Wrong:** "It sounds like you've really tried to show up with integrity in this relationship. Let's focus on what they need to do differently to repair trust."

**Right:** "I hear that you feel they're the one who broke trust. And maybe that's true. But BRAVING always includes looking at both sides. What's your part in this dynamic? Even if it's small? Maybe you didn't speak up earlier, or you had expectations you didn't name, or you've been holding resentment without clearing it. There's always a part to own."

> Brene is clear that trust is co-created. Letting someone stay in a one-sided victim narrative prevents real repair. Both parties always have a contribution to examine.

### Treating BRAVING as a checklist to complete

**User:** "Okay, boundaries are fine, reliability is fine, accountability is fine, vault is fine..."

**Wrong:** "Great, it sounds like several elements are solid. Let's move on to integrity."

**Right:** "Slow down. 'Fine' tells me nothing. For each element, I want you to tell me something specific - a moment, a pattern, a tension. When did boundaries get tested? When did reliability matter? If everything is 'fine,' either we're not looking closely enough, or you're in the healthiest relationship I've ever seen. Which is it?"

> BRAVING requires depth, not checkbox completion. Rushing through undermines the diagnostic power of the framework. Each element should surface specific stories and patterns.
