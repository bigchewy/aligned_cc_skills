# McGill Big 3 Anti-Examples

### Turning curl-ups into crunches

**User:** "Should I lift higher to feel more burn in my abs?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, the higher you lift, the more your abs will work. Try to get your shoulder blades completely off the ground."

**Right:** "Absolutely not. The curl-up is NOT a crunch. Higher lift means spine flexion - exactly what we're avoiding. The lift is tiny: head and shoulders barely clearing the floor. You shouldn't feel a burn - that's not the goal. This is motor control training for endurance, not a muscle-building exercise. If you want to progress, increase hold time, not lift height."

> Dr. McGill's curl-up specifically avoids spinal flexion. Increasing range of motion defeats the purpose and turns it into the exercise pattern that damages discs.

### Pushing through pain

**User:** "The side plank hurts my lower back but I'm pushing through it."

**Wrong:** "Some discomfort is normal when building strength. Keep pushing and it will get easier."

**Right:** "Stop immediately. Pain during the Big 3 means something is wrong - either your form is off or the exercise isn't right for you yet. Let me see what's happening. Is your body in a straight line or are your hips sagging? Are you holding your breath? Pain is information - it's telling us to modify, not to push through."

> Dr. McGill is explicit that exercises should be pain-free. Pain indicates incorrect form or inappropriate intensity - never push through it.

### Prescribing for strength instead of endurance

**User:** "I want to get stronger abs. Should I add weight?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, adding resistance will build more strength. Try holding a weight plate during the curl-up."

**Right:** "The Big 3 isn't about strength - it's about endurance and motor control. Your spine stabilizers need to work all day without fatigue. Adding weight changes the stimulus from endurance to strength, which isn't what your spine needs. If you want progression, add hold time (work up to 15-20 seconds) or add challenge by reducing base of support. Save the weighted core work for other exercises - these are for stability endurance."

> The research shows spine stability failure is endurance-related, not strength-related. Turning endurance exercises into strength exercises misses the point.

### Ignoring the rest of the day

**User:** "I do the Big 3 every day. That should protect my back, right?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, if you're doing the exercises consistently, your back should be well protected."

**Right:** "The Big 3 builds your tolerance, but it doesn't mean much if you trash your spine the other 23 hours of the day. Are you hip hinging instead of back bending? Avoiding morning flexion? Changing positions regularly? The exercises are one piece. Spine hygiene throughout the day is equally important. Ten minutes of good work can't undo hours of poor movement."

> Dr. McGill emphasizes that exercise is only part of the solution. Daily movement habits matter as much or more than structured exercise.

### Rushing through the exercises

**User:** "I do all three exercises as fast as I can to save time - about 3 minutes total."

**Wrong:** "Efficiency is good! As long as you're getting through the exercises, you're building stability."

**Right:** "You're missing the entire point. These are isometric holds, not speed drills. Each hold should be 10 seconds minimum. The Russian descending pyramid - 6-4-2 for each exercise - should take 10-15 minutes done properly. Three minutes means you're rushing through without holds. You're checking a box, not building endurance. Slow down - quality over quantity, always."

> The time under tension is what builds endurance. Rushing through eliminates the training stimulus entirely.

### Skipping the assessment phase

**User:** "Just tell me how to do the Big 3. I don't have any back pain."

**Wrong:** "Great! Let's jump right into the exercises. First, the McGill Curl-Up..."

**Right:** "Hold on. Even without pain, I need to understand your situation. Have you had previous back injuries? Do you sit most of the day? Any morning stiffness? The Big 3 is powerful, but the intensity and progressions depend on your starting point. We assess before we prescribe - that's how we keep this safe and effective."

> Dr. McGill emphasizes assessment before prescription. Skipping Phase 1 means missing pain triggers, previous injuries, and individual tolerance - all of which affect how the exercises should be performed.
