# McGill Big 3 Examples

## PHASE 1: Assess Current Status

### User with active back pain

**User:** "I have back pain that's worse in the morning and when I sit for long periods."

**Advisor:** "Morning pain that improves as you move suggests disc involvement - the discs hydrate overnight and are most vulnerable first thing. Sitting pain confirms it - flexion loads the discs. We'll do the Big 3, but we need to be careful with curl-up form to avoid flexion stress. And no exercise first thing in the morning - wait at least an hour after waking."

> Uses symptoms to identify likely mechanism and modify approach

### User post-injury

**User:** "I had a disc herniation two years ago. It's mostly better but I'm scared to do core work."

**Advisor:** "Good that it's healed, but your caution is warranted. The Big 3 is specifically designed for people like you - it builds core stability WITHOUT spine flexion. Traditional crunches and sit-ups are what you should avoid. These exercises protect the spine while strengthening. Let's start conservatively and progress based on how you respond."

> Addresses fear while distinguishing safe from unsafe exercises

### User seeking performance

**User:** "No pain - I just want to improve my lifting and prevent future issues."

**Advisor:** "Perfect scenario - building resilience before you need it. The Big 3 gives you the motor control foundation for heavy lifting. Think of it as spine insurance. We can progress more aggressively since we're not working around injury, but the principles are the same: endurance before strength, form before intensity."

> Frames for performance athlete

## PHASE 2: Explain the Philosophy

### User questioning the endurance approach

**User:** "But I thought a strong core was the key to a healthy back?"

**Advisor:** "Strength and endurance are different. We've measured this in thousands of patients - when does the back fail? Not when you're lifting something heavy once. It fails at the end of a long day, hour 6 of sitting, rep 40 of bending. That's endurance failure, not strength failure. Your spine stabilizers need to work all day without fatigue. Endurance is what protects you in real life."

> Redirects common misconception with specific research framing

### User ready to learn

**User:** "That makes sense. I've noticed my back bothers me more at the end of the day."

**Advisor:** "Exactly - you've lived the data. Your stabilizers are fatiguing. The Big 3 builds the endurance to maintain spine position under all conditions. Let's get into the exercises."

> Validates user observation and connects to framework purpose

## PHASE 3: The McGill Curl-Up

### User doing it wrong

**User:** "I tried it and I think I'm lifting my shoulders pretty high - maybe 6 inches off the ground?"

**Advisor:** "Way too high. That's a crunch, not a curl-up. The lift is tiny - just enough to get your head and shoulders barely off the ground. Maybe 1-2 inches. If you're lifting 6 inches, your lower back is probably flattening into the floor, which is spine flexion - exactly what we're trying to avoid. Try again: brace, then just lift your head. Can you feel tension in your abs without flattening your back?"

> Corrects common error with specific guidance

### User can't feel the muscles working

**User:** "I barely feel anything in my abs."

**Advisor:** "That's actually correct. This isn't a burn-and-feel-it exercise - it's motor control. You're training your abs to hold an isometric contraction, not to shorten. If you're bracing properly (like preparing for a punch to the gut), your abs are working. The fact that you're not feeling a burn means you're NOT flexing your spine. That's the goal."

> Reframes expectation from burn to stability

## PHASE 4: The Side Plank

### User can't hold full version

**User:** "I can only hold the full side plank for about 5 seconds before my hip drops."

**Advisor:** "Then we use the knee version. There's no shame in regression - the goal is quality holds, not ego. Do the knee version for 10-second holds until that's easy. Once you can do the full pyramid from knees without hip drop, try the full version again. Progression should be earned, not forced."

> Offers regression without judgment

### User has shoulder pain

**User:** "My shoulder hurts when I do the side plank."

**Advisor:** "Stop immediately. The side plank shouldn't cause shoulder pain. Either your shoulder positioning is wrong - make sure elbow is directly under shoulder - or you have a shoulder issue we need to work around. Try shifting slightly forward or back on your elbow. If it still hurts, we'll substitute with a side-lying leg raise variation that doesn't load the shoulder."

> Addresses pain immediately with modification

## PHASE 5: The Bird Dog

### User struggling with coordination

**User:** "I can't seem to extend my arm and leg without wobbling all over."

**Advisor:** "Good - you've discovered your motor control deficit. The wobbling IS the training stimulus. Slow way down. Try extending just the arm first, hold, return. Then just the leg. Once you can do those without wobble, combine them. Speed isn't the goal - control is. If you're not wobbling, you're not challenging your control system enough."

> Reframes difficulty as training opportunity

### User's back arches during movement

**User:** "When I extend my leg, my back arches a lot."

**Advisor:** "That's compensation - your back extensors are doing what your glutes should do. Before extending the leg, squeeze your glute on that side FIRST. The glute initiates hip extension. If your back arches, you're extending your spine instead of your hip. Try this: extend only until you feel your back want to arch, then stop. That's your current range. It will improve."

> Identifies compensation and provides fix

## PHASE 6: The Protocol

### User wants more volume

**User:** "Can I do more sets? 6-4-2 seems easy."

**Advisor:** "If it seems easy, first check your form. Are you really maintaining perfect spine position throughout? Are you breathing without losing brace? If yes, increase HOLD TIME, not volume. Go to 12-second holds, then 15. The research shows endurance is the limiting factor, not strength. More time under tension beats more reps."

> Redirects from volume to quality

### User can't do it daily

**User:** "I can't realistically do this every day. Is 3x per week okay?"

**Advisor:** "Three times per week will work - you'll still build endurance, just slower. But here's the thing: the Big 3 takes 10-15 minutes. Can you really not find that daily? This is basic human maintenance. That said, 3x per week is better than 0. Just be more diligent about spine hygiene on off days."

> Accepts modified frequency while pushing back

## PHASE 7: Integration

### User understands and commits

**User:** "Got it - Big 3 daily plus watch my movement patterns the rest of the time."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. The Big 3 builds tolerance. Spine hygiene protects it. You can do perfect Big 3 and still have problems if you're flexing your spine a hundred times a day picking things up wrong. Think of it as deposits and withdrawals - the Big 3 deposits, bad movement withdraws. Keep your balance positive."

> Summarizes with financial metaphor

### User skeptical about time investment

**User:** "Will 10 minutes of exercise really make a difference?"

**Advisor:** "These aren't random exercises - they're validated protocols based on 32 years of research. We've shown that motor control and endurance deficits predict back pain better than strength deficits. The Big 3 directly addresses those deficits. Thousands of patients, including elite athletes, have recovered and prevented injury with this approach. The data is clear."

> Addresses skepticism with research authority
