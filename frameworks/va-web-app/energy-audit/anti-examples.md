# Energy Audit Anti-Examples

### Skipping low-scoring dimensions

**User:** "Physical and mental are pretty good. Maybe 7s. Emotional and spiritual... let's not go there."

**Wrong:** "That's great that physical and mental are strong. Let's focus on maintaining those strengths. What are you doing that's working well in those areas?"

**Right:** "You just identified where the real data is. A 7 in two areas is good - but if emotional and spiritual are significantly lower, that's where the problem is. We need to go exactly where you don't want to. What's happening in emotional and spiritual?"

> The audit is about finding the gaps, not confirming the strengths. Avoiding painful dimensions defeats the purpose.

### Treating it as pure diagnosis without action

**User:** "So my scores are 5, 4, 6, 5. Now what?"

**Wrong:** "Those are useful insights to reflect on. Understanding your energy profile is the first step toward greater self-awareness. Notice how these patterns show up in your daily life."

**Right:** "Your emotional energy at 4 is the biggest drain. The key insight: Energy works like a muscle - it needs oscillation between stress and recovery. You're all stress, no recovery in the emotional dimension. What's one specific thing you could do today to start recovering?"

> An energy audit without a recovery plan is just sophisticated complaining. The point is action.

### Making it about productivity optimization

**User:** "I want to maximize my energy so I can get more done."

**Wrong:** "Absolutely - understanding your energy sources is key to peak performance. Let's identify which energy dimension is limiting your productivity so we can optimize your output."

**Right:** "Getting more done is part of it. But the real question is: Are you fully engaged in what matters? High energy in the wrong direction is just efficient burnout. Let's check all four dimensions - including purpose - and see if you're fueling the right fire."

> The Loehr model isn't about doing more - it's about full engagement. Spiritual energy (purpose) is as important as physical. Reducing it to productivity misses the point.
