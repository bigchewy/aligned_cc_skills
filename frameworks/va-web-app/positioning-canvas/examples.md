# Positioning Canvas Examples

## PHASE 1: Check Prerequisites

### User who has done positioning work

**User:** "Yes, I've been through the 5 Components exercise. I have my alternatives, attributes, value, target, and category defined."

**Advisor:** "Good - then we have raw material to capture. The canvas documents what you've figured out. Let's start building it so you have something you can share with your team."

> Confirms prerequisites and moves to canvas creation

### User who hasn't done positioning work

**User:** "I haven't really done formal positioning work. I just have a sense of who we're for."

**Advisor:** "Then we should do the positioning work first. The canvas captures positioning - it doesn't create it. Without clear answers on your competitive alternatives, unique attributes, differentiated value, and target customers, we'd just be documenting guesses. Want to work through the 5 Components instead?"

> Correctly identifies missing prerequisites and redirects

## PHASE 2: Core Positioning Statement

### User with clear statement

**User:** "[Product] helps fast-growing teams manage client relationships without CRM complexity. Unlike traditional CRM tools that need dedicated admins, we automatically capture communications and surface insights with zero setup."

**Advisor:** "That's tight. 'Fast-growing teams' - specific target. 'Without CRM complexity' - clear value. 'Unlike traditional CRM tools' - sets up the alternative. 'Automatically... zero setup' - differentiation. Let me test: could a competitor say this exact thing? Or is this uniquely true of you?"

> Tests for specificity and true differentiation

### User with vague statement

**User:** "We help businesses work smarter with AI-powered automation."

**Advisor:** "'Help businesses work smarter' - that could be literally any company. And 'AI-powered automation' is a technology description, not value. Who specifically? What problem specifically? What can they do that they couldn't before? Rewrite this so a competitor couldn't claim the same thing."

> Rejects vague positioning and demands specificity

## PHASE 3: Competitive Alternatives Box

### User with clear alternatives

**User:** "Our alternatives are: 1) Spreadsheets and email for tracking, 2) Heavyweight CRM like Salesforce, 3) Lightweight CRM tools that don't scale, 4) Just remembering everything and winging it."

**Advisor:** "Four alternatives, each distinct. Spreadsheets, heavyweight, lightweight, and 'wing it.' That covers the real landscape your customers face. Each one has different limitations. Good - that goes on the canvas."

> Validates comprehensive alternative list

## PHASE 4: Unique Attributes Box

### User with provable attributes

**User:** "Our unique attributes: 1) Automatic email capture without forwarding or BCC, 2) AI-generated relationship summaries, 3) One-click setup - works in minutes not weeks, 4) Built for teams under 50 where no one wants to be the CRM admin."

**Advisor:** "Four attributes, all provable. I could demonstrate each of these. 'Without forwarding or BCC' - that's specific and differentiating. 'Works in minutes not weeks' - measurable. 'Built for teams under 50' - clear design philosophy. These go on the canvas."

> Confirms attributes are specific and demonstrable

## PHASE 5: Value Themes Box

### User translating attributes to value

**User:** "Value themes: 1) 'Never lose context' - understand any client in 30 seconds. 2) 'Zero admin burden' - no one has to be the CRM cop. 3) 'Actually gets used' - adoption happens because it's effortless."

**Advisor:** "Three value themes, each connected to your attributes. 'Never lose context' comes from auto-capture and AI summaries. 'Zero admin burden' comes from easy setup and designed-for-small-teams. 'Actually gets used' comes from being effortless. The translation is clear. These go on the canvas."

> Validates value themes as translations of attributes

## PHASE 6: Target Customers Box

### User with characteristic-based targeting

**User:** "Best-fit customers: Fast-growing teams (20-50 people) who have outgrown spreadsheets but don't want to become Salesforce admins. Usually in services businesses with high-touch client relationships."

**Advisor:** "Characteristics, not just demographics. 'Outgrown spreadsheets' is a stage. 'Don't want to become admins' is a mindset. 'High-touch client relationships' is a business model characteristic. These are identifiable and specific. That goes on the canvas."

> Validates characteristic-based over demographic targeting

## PHASE 7: Market Category Box

### User with strategic category choice

**User:** "Market category: Lightweight CRM. Positioning approach: Big fish in a small pond - we're the best lightweight CRM for professional services teams."

**Advisor:** "Lightweight CRM is a real category. 'For professional services teams' narrows the pond. You're not claiming to beat Salesforce - you're claiming to own a niche. Does that category trigger the right expectations when prospects hear it?"

> Tests category for expectation-setting

## SYNTHESIS: The Canvas

### User with complete canvas

**Advisor:** "Here's your positioning canvas:

---

## [Product] Positioning Canvas

### Core Positioning
[Product] helps fast-growing teams manage client relationships without CRM complexity. Unlike traditional CRM tools that need dedicated admins, we automatically capture communications and surface insights with zero setup.

### Competitive Alternatives
- Spreadsheets and email
- Heavyweight CRM (Salesforce)
- Lightweight CRM tools that don't scale
- 'Winging it' / relying on memory

### Unique Attributes
- Automatic email capture (no forwarding/BCC)
- AI-generated relationship summaries
- One-click setup (minutes, not weeks)
- Built for teams under 50

### Value Themes
- Never lose context (30-second understanding)
- Zero admin burden
- Actually gets used (effortless adoption)

### Target Customers
Fast-growing teams (20-50) who've outgrown spreadsheets but don't want CRM admin burden. High-touch client relationships, often professional services.

### Market Category
Lightweight CRM - Big fish, small pond: best lightweight CRM for professional services teams

---

Print this. Share it. When decisions come up about messaging, targeting, or product direction, this is your reference. Does this capture your positioning?"

> Provides formatted canvas for practical use
