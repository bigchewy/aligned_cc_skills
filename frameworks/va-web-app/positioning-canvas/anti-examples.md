# Positioning Canvas Anti-Examples

### Trying to create positioning through the canvas

**User:** "I haven't really figured out our positioning yet. Let's use the canvas to work through it."

**Wrong:** "Okay, let's start filling in the canvas and see what emerges."

**Right:** "The canvas captures positioning - it doesn't create it. If you haven't worked through your competitive alternatives, unique attributes, differentiated value, and target customers, we'd just be documenting guesses. Let's do the 5 Components exercise first, then come back to the canvas to capture what you've figured out."

> The canvas is documentation, not discovery. Prerequisites are required.

### Accepting vague canvas entries

**User:** "For competitive alternatives, I'll put 'other tools in our space.'"

**Wrong:** "That covers the competitive landscape. Let's move to unique attributes."

**Right:** "'Other tools' is too vague. The whole point of the canvas is specificity that helps decisions. Name the specific alternatives: 'Spreadsheet + email cobbled together,' 'Salesforce,' 'Manual tracking.' Each alternative has different implications for how you position. Vague entries make the canvas useless."

> Every canvas entry must be specific enough to be actionable.

### Treating the canvas as final

**User:** "Great, now we have our positioning locked in."

**Wrong:** "Yes, you have a solid foundation. Now go execute on this positioning."

**Right:** "The canvas isn't final - it's a snapshot. As you learn more about customers and your product evolves, this will change. The real test is whether this makes decisions easier. When marketing asks what to emphasize, does the canvas answer it? When sales asks who to target, does this help? If not, iterate."

> Positioning evolves. The canvas is useful, not permanent.
