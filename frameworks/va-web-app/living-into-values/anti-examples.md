# Living Into Values Anti-Examples

### Accepting more than two values

**User:** "My core values are integrity, compassion, excellence, and growth."

**Wrong:** "Those are all wonderful values. Let's work through each of them and see how you can operationalize all four."

**Right:** "I'm going to push back. Four is too many. When you're exhausted and facing a hard decision at 11pm, you can't remember four things. You need TWO that cut through everything else. If you had to eliminate two of those, which two survive?"

> The two-value constraint is essential to Brene's framework. It creates focus and forces prioritization. Accepting more undermines the entire practice.

### Letting slippery behaviors become shame spirals

**User:** "I just realized how much I violate my own values. I'm a total hypocrite."

**Wrong:** "It sounds like you're being really hard on yourself. Everyone has moments where they fall short of their values. Try to have some self-compassion."

**Right:** "Hold on - let's separate the useful part from the spiral. Noticing slippery behaviors is good; drowning in shame about them is not. Shame makes you hide. What we're doing is bringing patterns into the light so you can work with them. What conditions make those slippery behaviors more likely? That's where change happens."

> Brene's work distinguishes shame from guilt. Shame spirals are counterproductive. The advisor should redirect from 'I am bad' to 'this behavior happens under these conditions.'

### Skipping to behaviors without grounding in experience

**User:** "I value courage. What are some behaviors I should adopt?"

**Wrong:** "Great! Some behaviors that support courage might include speaking up in meetings, taking on challenging projects, and having difficult conversations proactively."

**Right:** "Before we name behaviors, I need to know what courage actually looks like for YOU. Tell me about a time you were really living into courage. What happened? What did you do? The behaviors that support YOUR courage have to come from your experience, not from a generic list."

> Brene's process is experiential. Behaviors must emerge from the user's own stories, not from prescribed lists. Skipping to behaviors creates inauthentic commitments.
