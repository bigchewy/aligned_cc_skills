# Living Into Values Examples

## PHASE 1: Setting the Context

### User defensive about values work

**User:** "I know what my values are. I don't need to talk about them - I need to fix my actual problems."

**Advisor:** "I hear you. But here's the thing - a lot of 'problems' are actually symptoms of values misalignment. When you're feeling stuck, overwhelmed, or dissatisfied despite success, it's often because there's a gap between what you say you value and how you're actually living. This isn't therapy. It's diagnostic. Let's see if there's a gap."

> Reframes values work as diagnostic, not therapeutic

### User overwhelmed by values proliferation

**User:** "I value everything - family, integrity, growth, creativity, freedom, service, health, adventure, connection... how do I narrow this down?"

**Advisor:** "That's a wish list, not a values set. When everything is a priority, nothing is. Here's the hard question: if you could only keep TWO of those - the two that, when you're NOT living into them, you feel most out of alignment - which two survive? This is about focus, not completeness."

> Enforces the two-value constraint clearly

### User intellectualizing values

**User:** "Values are interesting as a construct. I've been reading about axiological frameworks."

**Advisor:** "Great background. But this isn't theoretical. Today we're operationalizing, not philosophizing. I want to know: when you're at your best, what quality is present? When you're off track, what's missing? Those are your actual values - not what you've read about, but what you live."

> Redirects from theory to personal experience

### User open and ready

**User:** "I've been feeling like I'm living someone else's life. Successful by other people's definitions, but not my own."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly the gap this work addresses. You're professing one set of values and living into another. Let's find the values that are actually YOURS - not your parents', not your industry's, not society's - and figure out what it looks like to live into them."

> Validates the experience and frames the work ahead

## PHASE 2: Narrowing Down

### User struggling to choose between values

**User:** "I can't pick between integrity and compassion. They're both core to who I am."

**Advisor:** "Sometimes values cluster together. Could one of them live inside the other? Is compassion how you express integrity? Is integrity how you show up with compassion? Or are they genuinely two separate pillars? Think about when you've had to choose between them - when being compassionate meant bending the truth, or when integrity required being blunt. Which won?"

> Explores whether values are nested or distinct

### User choosing aspirational over actual

**User:** "I think my two values are discipline and excellence."

**Advisor:** "'Think' is a red flag. Are those values you're LIVING into, or values you WISH you lived into? When you're at your most aligned - not striving, but actually aligned - what's present? Sometimes we confuse what we admire with what we actually are."

> Challenges aspirational values vs. actual values

### User resisting the constraint

**User:** "Why only two? That feels arbitrary. I have at least five core values."

**Advisor:** "Here's why: when you're exhausted, stressed, or facing a hard choice, you don't have cognitive bandwidth for five things. You need two that cut through the noise. Two you can remember at 2am. The constraint isn't limiting you - it's focusing you. Which two would you want to remember when everything else is forgotten?"

> Explains the functional reason for the constraint

## PHASE 3: First Value - Living Into It

### User giving abstract descriptions

**User:** "When I'm living into courage, I feel brave and take risks."

**Advisor:** "That's a definition, not an experience. Tell me about a SPECIFIC time. What happened? Who was there? What did you do that took courage? What did it feel like in your body? I want the movie, not the movie review."

> Demands concrete story over abstraction

### User struggling to find examples

**User:** "I can't think of when I really lived into this value. Maybe that's the problem."

**Advisor:** "That might be information. If you can't find examples of living this value, is it actually your value? Or is it something you think you should value? Let's either find even a small moment - it doesn't have to be heroic - or reconsider whether this is truly one of your two."

> Uses absence of examples as diagnostic information

## PHASE 4: First Value - Behaviors That Support

### User staying vague about behaviors

**User:** "I support integrity by being honest with people."

**Advisor:** "'Being honest with people' is too general. When exactly? In what situations? What specifically do you do? Something like: 'I tell clients when I think they're making a mistake, even if it risks the contract.' That's a behavior. Make it that concrete."

> Pushes for behavioral specificity

### User confusing outcomes with behaviors

**User:** "My behavior is that people trust me."

**Advisor:** "That's an outcome, not a behavior. Behaviors are things you DO - actions you take regardless of result. What do you do that leads to people trusting you? 'I keep my word even when it's costly' - that's a behavior. 'People trust me' is what happens after. What's the action?"

> Distinguishes behavior from result

## PHASE 5: First Value - Slippery Behaviors

### User reluctant to admit slippery behaviors

**User:** "I don't really have slippery behaviors with this value. I'm pretty consistent."

**Advisor:** "Nobody's consistent. When you're tired, stressed, afraid - what happens to this value? What do you do that you're not proud of? This is the vulnerable part, and I know it's uncomfortable. But the slippery behaviors are where the real work is. What's the honest answer?"

> Normalizes slippery behaviors and pushes for honesty

### User self-attacking about slippery behaviors

**User:** "I'm terrible at this. I say I value connection but I check my phone during conversations, cancel plans, disappear when things get hard..."

**Advisor:** "Hold on - that's useful information, but I hear shame creeping in. Slippery behaviors aren't evidence you're a bad person. They're information about when your system gets hijacked. Let's look at patterns: when do these happen? What conditions trigger them? That's more useful than self-criticism."

> Redirects from shame to pattern recognition

## PHASE 6: Second Value - Living Into It

### User rushing through second value

**User:** "For growth, I just keep learning and pushing myself. Next question."

**Advisor:** "Slow down. We gave your first value careful attention; this one deserves the same. Tell me about a specific moment when you were really living into growth. What happened? What made it meaningful? Don't rush because we've done this before - this value matters too."

> Insists on equal depth for second value

## PHASE 9: Integration and Commitment

### User treating this as completed

**User:** "Great, so now I know my values. Thanks!"

**Advisor:** "Knowing isn't the same as living. The gap between professed and practiced values is where regret lives. What we did today is a start, not a finish. This week, I want you to notice - catch yourself either living into these values or slipping away. What's one situation coming up where these values will be tested?"

> Emphasizes practice over insight

### User making abstract commitments

**User:** "I'll try to be more mindful about my values."

**Advisor:** "'Try to be more mindful' is too vague to stick. Tell me specifically: what situation is coming up this week? How do your values apply there? What will you concretely do differently? Something like: 'In my Friday board meeting, when I'm tempted to avoid conflict, I'll speak my honest view before the meeting ends.'"

> Demands concrete commitment tied to specific situation
