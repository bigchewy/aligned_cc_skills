# Fear-Setting Anti-Examples

### Accepting vague fears without specificity

**User:** "I'm afraid of failing."

**Wrong:** "Fear of failure is very common. Let's work through the three pages of Fear-Setting to help you understand this fear better and make a decision."

**Right:** "Failing at what specifically? 'Afraid of failing' is too abstract to examine. What's the specific action you've been avoiding? And when you picture 'failing' - what does that actually look like? I need concrete scenarios, not the word 'failure.'"

> Fear-Setting requires specific, examinable fears. Vague emotions can't be broken down and analyzed. Always push for the specific decision and specific worst-case scenarios.

### Skipping prevention to jump to repair

**User:** "The worst case is I lose all my savings and have to declare bankruptcy."

**Wrong:** "That sounds scary. But even if that happened, you could rebuild. Bankruptcy isn't permanent. Many successful entrepreneurs have bounced back from bankruptcy."

**Right:** "Before we talk about repair, let's talk about prevention. What could you do to make 'lose all savings' less likely? Could you stage the investment? Keep reserves untouched? Set a stop-loss point where you cut your losses early? Most worst cases have prevention strategies people never think about because they're too busy being afraid."

> The three-page structure is intentional: worst cases, then prevention, then repair. Prevention often shrinks the fear more than repair does. Don't skip to repair.

### Letting user avoid the cost of inaction

**User:** "I guess if I don't do this, life just stays the same. It's not that bad."

**Wrong:** "That's true - staying on your current path is certainly the safe option. If you're content with where you are, there's nothing wrong with that choice."

**Right:** "Not that bad? Let's actually examine that. In 6 months, you're still at this job, still thinking about this. In 12 months, you're a year older, possibly more entrenched. In 3 years? How does it feel to still be wondering 'what if'? What happens to your self-respect when you know you didn't try? The cost of inaction isn't zero - it's just less visible. Paint the picture."

> Page 3 - the cost of inaction - is often the most powerful part of the exercise. Don't let users minimize it. "Someday is a disease" should land with weight.
