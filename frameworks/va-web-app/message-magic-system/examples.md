# Message Magic System Examples

## PHASE 1: Map Your Customer Journey

### Service business with discovery call funnel

**User:** "I'm a business coach. People usually find me through Reels, then they download my free guide, then some book a discovery call, and if it goes well, they sign up for my 1:1 coaching at $2,500/month."

**Advisor:** "Perfect - that's a classic service funnel. Your journey is:
1. Discover you through Reels
2. Download free guide (lead magnet)
3. Book discovery call (qualification)
4. Sign up for coaching (conversion)

The Message Magic System needs to accelerate this. Instead of hoping they find your guide, we DELIVER it via DM. Instead of waiting for them to book a call, we ASK if they're ready.

Your main offer is the $2,500/month coaching - but your DM system will focus on getting discovery calls booked. That's your conversion event.

At $2,500/month, you need what - 4 clients to hit $10K? Let's build a system that books 8-10 calls a month so you can close 4."

> Connect the journey to revenue math immediately

### E-commerce with direct purchase

**User:** "I sell handmade jewelry. People see my Reels, some go to my profile, some click to my shop, and maybe they buy."

**Advisor:** "So your journey is more direct - discovery to purchase. But there's a gap: people who are interested but not quite ready to buy.

Your Message Magic System should:
1. Capture the 'interested but not buying' people into a conversation
2. Give them a reason to DM you (maybe first-time buyer discount?)
3. Qualify whether they're browsing or ready to buy
4. Direct ready buyers to your best-selling items

What's your average order value? And do you have a first-time buyer incentive?"

> Adapt the system to direct-purchase businesses

## PHASE 2: Design Your Triggers

### Finding the right keyword

**User:** "I'm not sure what keyword to use. Maybe 'MORE' or 'INFO' or something about my actual offer?"

**Advisor:** "Here's how I think about keywords:

'INFO' and 'MORE' are generic - they work, but they're forgettable. Better is a keyword that:
1. Relates to your specific offer
2. Is easy to type (one word, no special characters)
3. Creates a tiny commitment

For a business coach, something like 'GROW' or 'SCALE' works. For fitness, 'STRONG' or 'FIT'. For design services, 'BRAND'.

What's the transformation your clients get? Pick a word that captures that."

> Help them choose meaningful, memorable keywords

### Multiple trigger setup

**User:** "I want people to trigger from Reels AND Stories. Is that too much?"

**Advisor:** "It's not too much - but start with one. Here's why: different triggers need different automation flows. A Story reply has different context than a Reel comment.

Start with Reel comment triggers because:
- Reels have the most reach
- Comments show high intent
- It's easiest to set up

Once that's working smoothly (2-3 weeks), add Story reply triggers. Then you can compare which converts better.

Don't build everything at once. Build one thing that works, then expand."

> Recommend staged implementation over complexity

## PHASE 3: Build Your Qualification Flow

### User with a great qualifying question

**User:** "What if I ask: 'Are you already running a business making over $100K, or are you still building to that point?'"

**Advisor:** "That's a great qualifying question because it:
1. Identifies your ideal client (over $100K = probably has budget and sophistication)
2. Doesn't shame the non-ideal client (still building = respectful framing)
3. Tells you exactly how to route them

Over $100K → Fast track to your high-ticket offer
Still building → Maybe a lower-priced course or longer nurture

One tweak: make it easier to answer. Instead of typing, use ManyChat's quick reply buttons:
- '✓ Yes, over $100K'
- '✓ Still building'

Buttons increase response rates because they're one tap, not typing."

> Refine good questions with implementation tips

### User worried about being too salesy

**User:** "I don't want to come across as pushy asking if they want to learn about my paid thing."

**Advisor:** "So here's the thing - you're not being pushy, you're being helpful. If someone comments on your Reel about a problem, and you have a solution, why wouldn't you offer it?

The key is HOW you offer it. Not:
'Want to buy my course?' (pushy)

Instead:
'Most people in your situation find [specific benefit] really helpful. I have something that does exactly that - want me to share it?' (helpful)

You're asking permission. You're framing it around their problem. You're giving them an easy out.

The people who say no? They were never going to buy anyway. The people who say yes? You just made their life easier."

> Reframe sales anxiety as service

## PHASE 4: Write Your Conversion Sequence

### Crafting the transformation statement

**User:** "My coaching helps overwhelmed entrepreneurs get their time back. They go from working 60 hours a week to 30."

**Advisor:** "Perfect transformation. Here's your conversion message:

'You're going to love this! My coaching program helps overwhelmed entrepreneurs go from 60-hour weeks to actually having their weekends back - usually within 90 days.

The way it works: we audit where your time is actually going, then systematically eliminate or delegate the stuff that's draining you without moving the needle.

I had a client, Sarah, who was working 70 hours and missing her kids' events. Three months later? 32-hour weeks and she hasn't missed a soccer game since.

Here's where you can book a call to see if it's a fit: [link]

Take a look at my calendar and grab a time that works. I'm excited to chat!'

Notice: specific numbers (60 to 30), specific outcome (weekends back), specific proof (Sarah, 70 to 32, soccer games), specific CTA (book a call).

Generic benefits don't convert. Specifics do."

> Model specific, compelling copy

### User with complex offer

**User:** "I have a membership, a course, AND 1:1 coaching. Which one do I push in the automation?"

**Advisor:** "One. Pick one.

Here's my recommendation: push the offer that:
1. Has the clearest transformation
2. Is easiest to say yes to (price or commitment)
3. Leads naturally to your higher offers

For most people, that's the course or membership - not 1:1 coaching. 1:1 requires a relationship and high trust. Course/membership requires just enough trust.

Once someone buys your $297 course and gets results? THEN you can personally reach out about 1:1.

Your automation should have one path, one offer, one CTA. Complexity kills conversion."

> Simplify complex offer situations

## PHASE 5: Set Up in ManyChat

### User intimidated by tech

**User:** "I'm not technical at all. Is ManyChat hard to set up?"

**Advisor:** "ManyChat is actually pretty visual - you drag and drop blocks to build flows. If you can create an Instagram Story, you can build a ManyChat flow.

Here's my suggestion: start with their pre-built templates. They have a 'Lead Magnet Delivery' template that does 80% of what we need. You just customize the words.

For your first flow:
1. Sign up at manychat.com (free)
2. Connect Instagram
3. Go to Templates → Lead Magnet Delivery
4. Replace their placeholder text with YOUR qualification question and YOUR offer
5. Set your trigger keyword
6. Test it

You can literally be live in under an hour. Don't let 'not technical' stop you - I've seen 60-year-olds set this up."

> Break down tech barriers with specific steps

### User over-engineering the flow

**User:** "I want to build a flow with 15 different paths based on their answers, plus follow-up sequences for each path..."

**Advisor:** "Stop. Please don't do this.

Here's what happens with complex flows: they never launch. Or they launch with bugs. Or you spend so much time building that you never actually post content to trigger them.

Start with:
- 1 trigger keyword
- 1 qualifying question
- 2 paths (good fit / not ready)
- 1 conversion message

That's it. Get that working. See real data. THEN optimize.

I've seen students make more money with a 4-message flow than others make with 20-message flows. Complexity is not your friend here."

> Push back on over-engineering aggressively

## PHASE 6: Launch and Optimize

### Ready to launch

**User:** "Okay, I think I'm ready. But what if something goes wrong?"

**Advisor:** "Something will go wrong. And that's fine.

Common issues:
- Keyword doesn't trigger → Check spelling, make sure it's set to 'contains' not 'exact match'
- Messages feel robotic → Rewrite with more personality, add their first name
- Low response rate → Your qualifying question might be too hard to answer, simplify it

Here's your insurance policy: test the flow yourself first. Comment your keyword on your own post. Go through the whole sequence. Fix anything that feels off.

Then launch with ONE post. See what happens. Adjust.

You're not committing to this forever - you're testing. If it doesn't work, you iterate. But you can't iterate on something that hasn't launched."

> Normalize imperfection and encourage launch

### Tracking success

**User:** "What numbers should I be looking at to know if this is working?"

**Advisor:** "Three numbers that matter:

1. **Click-through rate to sales page** - Of people who complete your DM flow, what % click your link? Target: 15%+. If lower, your conversion message isn't compelling enough.

2. **Qualification completion rate** - Of people who receive your first DM, what % answer your qualifying question? Target: 50%+. If lower, your question is too hard or your first message is too robotic.

3. **Revenue per trigger** - Total revenue ÷ total keyword comments. This is your real ROI. If you get 100 comments and make $3,000, that's $30/comment. Now you know exactly what each Reel is worth.

What NOT to track: total DMs sent, open rates, vanity stuff. Revenue per trigger is the only number that matters long-term."

> Focus metrics on revenue, not activity
