# ACT Psychological Flexibility Anti-Examples

### Making acceptance mean approval

**User:** "My partner keeps dismissing my feelings. It's really hurtful."

**Wrong:** "Let's practice acceptance. Can you accept that your partner is the way they are? Resisting their behavior only causes you more suffering."

**Right:** "That sounds painful. Let's look at what you're experiencing. What feelings come up when this happens? Not whether they 'should' come up - just what's there. We can accept that you're hurt without accepting that the behavior is okay."

> Acceptance in ACT is about accepting your internal experience, not tolerating harmful external situations. Conflating these is common and dangerous.

### Treating defusion as thought suppression

**User:** "I keep thinking I'm going to fail."

**Wrong:** "Let's work on unhooking from that negative thought. Try replacing 'I'm going to fail' with 'I'm going to succeed.' When the negative thought comes, push it away and think positive."

**Right:** "That thought keeps showing up. Let's try something - can you say 'I notice I'm having the thought that I'm going to fail'? Notice - we're not making the thought go away. We're just changing your relationship to it."

> Defusion is not thought replacement or positive thinking. It's changing your relationship to thoughts, not changing the thoughts themselves.

### Skipping to action without the acceptance work

**User:** "I feel paralyzed. I know what I should do but I can't make myself do it."

**Wrong:** "Well, let's identify a small action you can take. Just commit to doing one thing today - break through that paralysis with action."

**Right:** "Something's blocking you. Before we talk about action, let's look at what you're avoiding. What feeling or thought comes up when you imagine doing what you 'should' do? Let's make some room for that first."

> ACT committed action is about acting WHILE carrying difficult emotions, not despite them. If you haven't done the acceptance work, the action won't hold.
