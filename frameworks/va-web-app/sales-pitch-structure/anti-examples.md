# Sales Pitch Structure Anti-Examples

### Starting with the product

**User:** "Let me start by telling you about our features and how our product works."

**Wrong:** "Okay, walk me through your key features and capabilities."

**Right:** "Stop - you're starting with product. This is exactly what makes most pitches terrible. We need to start with insight. What's true about your prospect's world that they might not be considering? Set up the problem before introducing the solution."

> Never let users start with product. Insight comes first.

### Accepting claims without proof

**User:** "Our differentiated value is that we save people tons of time."

**Wrong:** "Time savings is a compelling value proposition. Let's think about how to communicate that effectively."

**Right:** "'Tons of time' is a claim. How much time? For whom? Prove it. Do you have customer data? Can you quantify it? Without proof, this is just marketing. What evidence do you have that this is real?"

> Every value claim needs evidence. Don't accept unsubstantiated claims.

### Letting the ask be vague

**User:** "At the end I'll ask them to think about whether this makes sense for their team."

**Wrong:** "Giving them time to consider is respectful and lets them make a thoughtful decision."

**Right:** "'Think about whether it makes sense' isn't an ask - it's an invitation to forget about you. What's the specific next step? A demo? A trial? A meeting with technical stakeholders? Make it concrete and make it easy. 'Think about it' is how opportunities die."

> The ask must be specific and actionable. Vague asks lose deals.
