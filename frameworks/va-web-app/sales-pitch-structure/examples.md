# Sales Pitch Structure Examples

## PHASE 1: Market Insight

### User finding real insight

**User:** "The average B2B sales cycle has doubled in the past 5 years. Buyers do so much research before talking to sales that by the time you get in the room, you're no longer educating - you're validating a decision they've mostly already made."

**Advisor:** "That's a real insight. It's true whether or not they buy from you. It makes them think differently about their situation. And it sets up why what you do matters - if buyers have already decided by the time you get there, you need to influence them before the meeting. Good. Let me test it - is this something your typical prospect wouldn't be actively thinking about?"

> Validates insight against the three criteria

### User giving product-focused insight

**User:** "Our insight is that most CRM tools are too complicated."

**Advisor:** "That's a product claim, not a market insight. It's about you, not about their world. Try again. What's true about your prospects' situation - independent of your product - that they might not be fully considering? What would make them say 'I hadn't thought about it that way'?"

> Rejects product-focused framing and redirects to market truth

## PHASE 2: Alternatives & Status Quo

### User showing competitive landscape fairly

**User:** "Most companies today either use a heavyweight enterprise solution like Salesforce, which requires dedicated admins, or they cobble together spreadsheets and email. Some use lighter tools but find they don't scale. Each approach has trade-offs."

**Advisor:** "Good - you're laying out the landscape honestly, not trashing competitors. 'Dedicated admins,' 'cobble together,' 'don't scale' - these are the real limitations. You're setting up why someone might want something different without saying 'they're bad and we're good.' That builds credibility."

> Validates fair treatment of alternatives

## PHASE 3: The Perfect World

### User aligning criteria with strengths

**User:** "The ideal solution would give you CRM power without CRM complexity. It would just work - automatically capture communications, surface what you need to know, and get out of your way. You shouldn't need training or an admin."

**Advisor:** "Now here's the trick. Does your product deliver 'just works, automatically captures, no training needed'? If yes, you've set up criteria that naturally lead to you. If your product actually needs a lot of setup, you just positioned against yourself. Make sure the perfect world you describe is one you can actually deliver."

> Checks alignment between ideal criteria and actual capability

## PHASE 4: Introduce Your Solution

### User with earned introduction

**Advisor:** "Notice what you've done. You've established an insight about how B2B buying has changed. You've shown the limitations of current approaches. You've described what an ideal solution would look like. NOW when you say 'this is what we built,' it lands differently. You're answering a question you've been building toward. How do you introduce your product?"

**User:** "We built [Product] to be the CRM that just works. It captures your communications automatically, gives you the context you need instantly, and requires zero training."

**Advisor:** "That introduction connects directly to the setup. 'Just works,' 'automatically,' 'zero training' - those are the criteria you established. You're not pitching out of nowhere. You're completing a story."

> Shows how proper setup makes the product introduction feel earned

## PHASE 5: Differentiated Value

### User with proof points

**User:** "Our differentiated value is instant context - you can understand any client relationship in 30 seconds instead of digging through emails. We have three case studies where customers quantified time savings: 5 hours per week for the average sales rep."

**Advisor:** "Value plus proof. '30 seconds instead of email digging' is the claim. '5 hours per week' is the evidence. That's what makes it believable, not just marketing. Can you get one of those customers to be a reference? Having a name attached to the number makes it real."

> Validates value claim with proof and suggests strengthening evidence

## PHASE 6: Handling Objections

### User anticipating concerns

**User:** "The main objections we hear: 'we've already invested in Salesforce' and 'will my data be secure with AI processing?' I'm going to address both proactively."

**Advisor:** "Good - don't wait for them to raise these. Build in: 'If you're already using Salesforce, here's how we work alongside it - we're not replacing your system of record.' And: 'Your data security is our priority, here's specifically how we handle it.' What else might they be worried about that they don't say out loud?"

> Validates proactive objection handling and probes for unstated concerns

## PHASE 7: Customer Stories

### User with strong story

**User:** "Acme Corp was using spreadsheets to track 200 client relationships. They were dropping balls, losing deals, and burning out. They implemented us and within 30 days, their renewal rate went up 15%. I can get specific metrics if needed."

**Advisor:** "That's a before/after story with specific results. Spreadsheets to system, balls being dropped to 15% better retention, 30 days. One improvement: make the 'before' more vivid. What specifically were they dropping? What was the emotional impact? The more real the pain, the more powerful the relief."

> Affirms structure and suggests strengthening emotional resonance

## PHASE 8: The Ask

### User with clear next step

**User:** "At the end of the pitch, I ask: 'Would you be open to a 15-minute demo where I show you exactly how this works with your actual email? We can set that up right now.'"

**Advisor:** "Specific and easy. '15 minutes' - that's low commitment. 'Your actual email' - that's personalized. 'Set it up right now' - that's immediate action. You're not leaving them wondering what happens next. That's a good ask."

> Validates concrete, low-friction ask
