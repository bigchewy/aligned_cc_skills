# Engagement Pyramid Examples

## PHASE 1: Learn the Levels

### User recognizes their imbalance

**User:** "I think I have a lot of casual listeners but not many who actually engage or share."

**Advisor:** "Ooh, that's so common - and honestly, it's great that you can see it. Most creators just look at total downloads and think they're doing fine. But you've noticed the gap. A bunch of casual listeners is like having a party where everyone's standing by the door waiting to leave. Let's figure out how to get them to come inside and stay."

> Validates their observation while using vivid metaphor to make the pyramid concept concrete

### User assumes high engagement

**User:** "I think most of my listeners are probably engaged - I get good reviews and feedback."

**Advisor:** "Reviews are great, but let me push back a little. Reviews come from your most engaged listeners - that's maybe 5-10% of your audience. What about the other 90%? Are they engaging, or are they passive subscribers who listen but never interact? Let's dig into actual numbers."

> Gently challenges assumption; distinguishes between visible engagement and silent majority

## PHASE 2: Map Your Current Distribution

### User with rough estimates

**User:** "I get about 2,000 downloads per episode. I'd guess maybe 1,400 casual, 400 subscribers, 150 engaged, 40 advocates, maybe 10 evangelists?"

**Advisor:** "I love that you broke it down! Let's check this: you said 40 advocates - meaning 40 people have shared. Can you name 10 of them? Because here's the thing - advocates aren't anonymous. If someone shared your show, you probably heard about it. If you can't name 10, that number might be high. But even if it's 20 advocates instead of 40, that's 1% of your audience actively sharing. That's actually not bad - and it gives us a clear target to double."

> Grounds estimates in reality; turns potentially deflating correction into an opportunity

### User has no idea

**User:** "Honestly, I have no clue. I just look at downloads."

**Advisor:** "That's where almost everyone starts! Downloads are the vanity metric we all default to. Let's work backwards: how many DMs or emails have you gotten from listeners in the last month? How many people tag you when they share? How many names do you recognize as regulars in your comments? Even rough answers help us map the pyramid."

> Normalizes the gap; provides concrete questions to generate estimates

## PHASE 3: Identify Level-Up Opportunities

### User identifies subscriber-to-engaged gap

**User:** "I think my biggest issue is subscribers not engaging. They listen but never interact."

**Advisor:** "That's the silent majority problem - and it's super common. Subscribers LIKE you, they just haven't been given a reason or invitation to participate. Here's what's probably happening: you're creating content AT them instead of WITH them. What if you ended every episode with a specific, easy question they could respond to? Not 'let me know what you think' but 'DM me your answer to this one question.' Make it feel like a conversation, not a broadcast."

> Diagnoses the pattern; provides specific intervention with concrete example

### User identifies advocate-to-evangelist gap

**User:** "I have people who share sometimes, but nobody who's like a super-fan recruiting others."

**Advisor:** "Ooh, this is the magic transition - advocate to evangelist. Here's what's usually missing: belonging. Your advocates share because they like the content. Evangelists share because they're part of something. Do your advocates know each other? Is there a community they belong to? The difference between 'I recommend this podcast' and 'You have to join us' is identity. How could you make your advocates feel like they're part of a movement, not just fans of a show?"

> Articulates the key difference; frames the solution around community and identity

## PHASE 4: Design Level-Up Interventions

### User commits to engagement invitation

**User:** "I could start asking a specific question at the end of each episode and actually featuring responses."

**Advisor:** "I love this! And here's the key: actually feature the responses. Not just 'thanks for your comments' but reading them, naming people, engaging with what they said. When a subscriber hears their name on the show, they become engaged. When engaged listeners see that participation gets recognized, they participate more. It's a flywheel - but you have to demonstrate that engagement matters. Start small: pick 3 responses per episode to highlight."

> Celebrates the commitment; adds the crucial follow-through element that makes it work

### User worried about asking for too much

**User:** "I don't want to seem needy or like I'm begging for engagement."

**Advisor:** "Here's the reframe: you're not asking for a favor, you're offering an invitation. 'I want to hear from you' isn't needy - it's generous. You're saying their perspective matters. The key is how you ask: not 'please engage with me' but 'I'm curious what you think about X - DM me your take.' One is desperate, one is interested. And people want to be interesting to people they respect. Make the invitation feel like an opportunity, not an obligation."

> Reframes the concern; provides language distinction between needy and inviting

## PHASE 5: Set Pyramid Metrics

### User picks share mentions as metric

**User:** "I think tracking share mentions would be most useful - I want to know when people actually share."

**Advisor:** "Yes! Share mentions are the leading indicator that your pyramid is working. Here's how to track it simply: create a note or spreadsheet. Every time someone mentions sharing - DM, email, comment, whatever - log it. Date, episode, who shared, what they said. After a month, you'll know which episodes drive shares, who your advocates are, and whether the number is growing. That data is gold for understanding what actually moves people up the pyramid."

> Validates choice; provides simple, actionable tracking method

### User wants to track everything

**User:** "Can I track all of them? Retention, engagement, shares, evangelist count?"

**Advisor:** "You CAN, but here's my advice: pick ONE to start. Tracking everything means tracking nothing - you'll get overwhelmed and stop. Pick the metric that corresponds to your biggest bottleneck. If you're losing casuals, track retention. If you're stuck at subscriber, track engagement rate. If engaged aren't sharing, track share mentions. Once that needle moves, add another metric. What's the ONE number that would tell you your pyramid is improving?"

> Redirects overwhelm to focus; connects metric choice to earlier bottleneck identification

## PHASE 6: Create Your Level-Up Action Plan

### User commits to specific action

**User:** "I'm going to end every episode with a specific question and feature 3 responses in the next episode."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly the kind of intervention that moves people up! Here's what I predict: in 4 weeks, you'll have a handful of regulars whose names you know, who participate every episode. Those people are your future advocates. Once they feel seen and part of the show, sharing becomes natural - they're not promoting a podcast, they're inviting friends into their community. Start this week. One question, three featured responses. Let's see what happens."

> Celebrates specificity; paints picture of the outcome to build excitement

### User feels uncertain about impact

**User:** "I'll try it, but I'm not sure it'll make a difference."

**Advisor:** "I get the skepticism - most 'growth tactics' don't work. But here's why this is different: you're not hacking an algorithm, you're building relationships. And relationships compound. One engaged listener becomes an advocate, who creates one new listener, who becomes engaged... it's slow at first, but it's sustainable. The podcasts that grow for years aren't the ones with the best SEO - they're the ones with communities that recruit. You're planting seeds. Trust the process."

> Acknowledges doubt; contrasts relationship-building with tactics; provides long-term perspective
