# Creator Flywheel Examples

## PHASE 1: Map Your Current Flywheel

### Creator with strong content but weak email

**User:** "I post consistently on Instagram - about 5 Reels per week - and I get decent views, usually 5-10K per Reel. But I only have about 800 email subscribers."

**Advisor:** "Interesting. Strong discovery, weak owned audience. You're generating attention but not capturing it. 5-10K views per Reel with only 800 email subscribers means your conversion rate from view to subscriber is essentially zero.

Let's understand the gap: Do you have a clear call-to-action to join your email list? A lead magnet? Or are you just hoping people find their way there?"

> Identify the specific conversion gap between flywheel components

### Creator dependent on one discovery channel

**User:** "My podcast is my main thing. I get about 5,000 downloads per episode, and I have 3,000 email subscribers. Most of my revenue comes from sponsorships."

**Advisor:** "Let me map this:
- **Content**: Podcast, consistent output
- **Discovery**: Podcast apps (somewhat owned - search is evergreen)
- **Audience**: 3,000 email (owned), plus podcast subscribers (harder to measure)
- **Revenue**: Sponsorships

Here's my concern: Sponsorship revenue is dependent on download numbers. If downloads drop, revenue drops. And you don't control podcast discovery - the apps do. What's your plan to convert podcast listeners into email subscribers and eventually direct revenue?"

> Probe platform dependencies and revenue fragility

## PHASE 2: Identify the Weakest Link

### Multiple weak components

**User:** "I think everything is weak. I'm just getting started."

**Advisor:** "Everything can't be the priority. Let's stack rank.

If you had to pick ONE component where improvement would unlock the others, what would it be?

Here's my take: For early creators, the hierarchy is usually:
1. **Content** first - you need something worth discovering
2. **Discovery** second - you need people to find it
3. **Audience capture** third - you need to own the relationship

What's your honest assessment: Is your content strong enough that if people saw it, they'd want more? Or is content the actual gap?"

> Force prioritization - everything can't be the weakest link

### Creator unsure about their weakness

**User:** "I'm not sure. I think my content is good, I get decent engagement, but I'm not making much money."

**Advisor:** "Let me probe deeper.

- Content engagement = ✓ (people are responding)
- Discovery = appears to be working (people finding you)
- Audience = unknown - how many email subscribers?
- Trust = unknown - what's your open rate?
- Revenue = weak (by your admission)

The question is: Is revenue weak because you don't have enough audience? Or is it weak because you have audience but no offers? Or is it weak because you have offers but no trust?

What have you tried to sell, and what happened?"

> Diagnose the root cause of revenue problems - it's usually upstream

## PHASE 3: Assess Platform Dependencies

### Creator fully dependent on Instagram

**User:** "I have 50K followers on Instagram and about 1,000 email subscribers. Most of my revenue comes from brand deals on Instagram."

**Advisor:** "Let's run the sustainability test.

If Instagram shut down your account tomorrow:
- **Discovery**: Gone. 50K followers inaccessible.
- **Revenue**: Gone. Brand deals depend on your follower count.
- **Audience**: You'd have 1,000 email subscribers. That's it.

You've built a business on rented land. 50K followers, but you only OWN 1,000 relationships. That's a 2% ownership rate.

Here's the strategic question: How do you convert 50K rented followers into owned email subscribers before something happens to that account? Algorithm changes, policy changes, account issues - these are when."

> Quantify the ownership gap to make dependency concrete

### Creator with multiple platforms

**User:** "I post on YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn, and TikTok. I have followers on all of them."

**Advisor:** "Four platforms = four algorithms you don't control = four potential points of failure.

Here's the real question: Which of these platforms is actually driving email subscribers? Not followers - subscribers.

If you can't answer that, you're spreading effort across four rented channels without knowing which one leads to ownership. That's not strategy - it's hope.

What percentage of your email list came from each platform?"

> Challenge multi-platform spreading without conversion tracking

## PHASE 4: Design Your Ownership Strategy

### Creator with following but no email capture

**User:** "I post consistently and get good engagement, but I don't really have a lead magnet or anything. I just have a link in bio to my website."

**Advisor:** "That's the gap. You're generating attention but not capturing it.

A 'link in bio to website' is not an audience capture strategy. It's hoping people figure it out.

The audience-to-revenue path requires a clear exchange: You offer something valuable, they give you their email. What could you offer that's valuable enough that someone would trade their email for it?

Think: What's the #1 question your audience asks you? The answer to that question, packaged as a PDF or video, is your lead magnet."

> Identify the specific conversion mechanism missing

### Creator with email list but poor conversion

**User:** "I have 5,000 email subscribers but my open rate is only 12%. And when I try to sell, maybe 10 people buy."

**Advisor:** "Two separate problems here.

12% open rate = low trust. Either your emails aren't valuable, or you're emailing too infrequently (they forgot you), or you attracted the wrong subscribers (lead magnet didn't match your offer).

10 purchases from 5,000 subscribers = 0.2% conversion. That's low, but not surprising if only 12% are even opening.

The path is: Fix opens before you fix sales. If people aren't opening, they can't buy. What does your typical email look like? And how often do you send?"

> Address conversion in order - opens before clicks before purchases

## PHASE 5: Create an Experiment for the Weakest Link

### Designing email capture experiment

**User:** "I think you're right - I need to capture more emails from my Instagram audience."

**Advisor:** "Good. Let's design the experiment.

**Hypothesis**: Your Instagram audience will trade their email for a valuable free resource related to your core content topic.

**Potential experiments**:
1. Create a simple PDF guide and promote it in one week of Stories
2. Host a free live workshop requiring email registration
3. Create a free mini-course delivered via email

Each tests the same thing: Will your audience give you their email?

The PDF is fastest to create. The workshop gives you the most data (you can see who shows up AND have a conversation). The mini-course builds the most trust but takes longest.

Which can you execute in the next 2 weeks?"

> Offer tiered experiment options based on effort and learning value

### Revenue experiment for established creator

**User:** "I have a good email list with solid open rates. I just haven't tried to sell anything yet."

**Advisor:** "That's actually a great position to be in. You have trust but haven't monetized.

**Hypothesis**: Your engaged email list will purchase a low-cost product that solves a specific problem they have.

**Potential experiments**:
1. Survey your list to identify their #1 problem, then presell a $47 workshop addressing it
2. Create a $27 template/guide based on what you know they need
3. Offer limited 1:1 calls at $150 to test service demand

All of these test willingness to pay. The survey + presell approach is riskiest but gives the best data. The template is safest. The calls are highest revenue per unit.

What feels right?"

> Match experiment to creator's current flywheel position

## PHASE 6: Set Measurement Criteria

### Defining success for email capture

**User:** "I'll go with the PDF lead magnet option and promote it in Stories."

**Advisor:** "Good. Let's define success.

**Your experiment**:
- Create a PDF guide on [topic]
- Promote it in Instagram Stories for 7 days
- Measure email sign-ups

**Success metrics**:
- Primary: Number of new email subscribers
- Secondary: Story views to subscriber conversion rate

**What number would make this a success?**

Here's context: A good Story-to-email conversion might be 2-5% of viewers. If you get 5,000 Story views over the week, that's 100-250 new subscribers if it's working.

If you get 10,000 Story views and only 50 subscribers (0.5%), the offer isn't compelling enough.

What's your baseline Story viewership? Let's set realistic targets."

> Ground success metrics in actual reach data
