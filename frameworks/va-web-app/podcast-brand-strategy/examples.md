# Podcast Brand Strategy Examples

## PHASE 1: Setting Context

### User with established podcast wanting audit

**User:** "I've had my podcast for 2 years, about 200 downloads per episode. It's called 'The Growth Zone' and it's about personal development."

**Advisor:** "200 downloads after 2 years with 'personal development' as your topic - that tells me something. Let's dig in and find what's actually holding you back. My guess? It's not your content. Let me audit your brand elements. Tell me more about what you actually cover."

> Acknowledges the plateau without judgment, signals the diagnosis ahead

### User launching new podcast

**User:** "I'm about to launch and want to get this right from the start."

**Advisor:** "Smart. Most people skip the brand work and spend months creating content no one can find. Let's nail this before you record a single episode. What's the show concept you're working with?"

> Affirms timing, keeps focus on the work

### User who thinks content is the problem

**User:** "My podcast isn't growing. I think I need to improve my content quality."

**Advisor:** "Maybe. But here's the uncomfortable truth - I've seen podcasts with mediocre content and great brands grow faster than shows with amazing content and broken brands. Let's check if brand is actually your constraint before you invest in production quality. Walk me through your show."

> Challenges the assumption, reframes to brand

## PHASE 2: Name Audit

### Name that passes the test

**User:** "My show is called 'Negotiation for Introverts' and I help quiet professionals get better deals without being pushy."

**Advisor:** "That's a strong name. Someone hears 'Negotiation for Introverts' and immediately knows: topic, audience, and angle. That's rare. I'd give this an 8 or 9. The constraint is elsewhere."

> Recognizes strength without over-praising, moves on

### Name that's too clever

**User:** "It's called 'The Quiet Rise' - it's a play on how introverts can rise without being loud."

**Advisor:** "Here's the issue - that's clever, but clever doesn't convert. If someone hears 'The Quiet Rise,' they might think: meditation? Silent movies? Bread baking? The wordplay makes YOU feel creative, but it makes everyone else confused. What would you call it if you couldn't be clever?"

> Direct about the problem, asks for alternative

### Name that's generic

**User:** "It's called 'The Business Podcast' because I wanted to be searchable."

**Advisor:** "I get the logic, but searchable and generic are different things. 'The Business Podcast' is competing with a million other shows with business in the title. And it tells me nothing about WHICH business topics, for WHO. That's a 3 out of 10. Let's figure out what makes your show specific."

> Scores honestly, explains the problem

## PHASE 3: Positioning Audit

### Positioning that's too broad

**User:** "Topic: entrepreneurship. Audience: business owners. Angle: I interview successful founders. Value: inspiration and lessons."

**Advisor:** "'Business owners' is 30 million people. 'Inspiration and lessons' is what every podcast promises. 'Interview successful founders' is what 10,000 other shows do. You've just described a category, not a position. Here's what I need from you: which business owners? What specific problem do they all share? And what can they get from YOUR show they can't get from 'How I Built This'?"

> Calls out the vagueness directly, pushes for specifics

### Positioning that's specific

**User:** "Topic: cash flow management. Audience: service business owners with 2-10 employees who are profitable but always feel broke. Angle: I only interview owners who went from cash-strapped to cash-confident in under a year. Value: one specific system or habit per episode they can implement that week."

**Advisor:** "Now we're talking. 'Service business owners with 2-10 employees who are profitable but feel broke' - that's a real person with a real pain. I know exactly who this is for and what they get. That positioning makes marketing easy because you know exactly where those people hang out. Strong."

> Affirms specific positioning with enthusiasm

### User struggling to narrow down

**User:** "But if I narrow it down, won't I lose potential listeners?"

**Advisor:** "Here's the paradox: the narrower your positioning, the faster you grow. When you try to be for everyone, you're compelling to no one. When you're for 'service business owners with 2-10 employees who feel cash-strapped,' every single one of those people thinks 'this is for me.' That specificity is what makes someone hit subscribe instead of 'maybe later.'"

> Explains the narrow-to-grow paradox

## PHASE 4: Promise Audit

### Promise that's a hope, not a promise

**User:** "You'll learn something valuable about marketing."

**Advisor:** "'Learn something valuable' is what you hope happens. A promise is what they can COUNT on. If someone listened and didn't get 'something valuable,' how would they even know? That's not specific enough to be a promise. Try again: what SPECIFIC thing do they get EVERY episode?"

> Distinguishes hope from promise

### Promise that's concrete

**User:** "Walk away with one marketing tactic you can implement today, tested by a real business owner."

**Advisor:** "That's a promise. 'One tactic' - countable. 'Implement today' - timeline. 'Tested by a real business owner' - proof. Someone could hold you accountable to that. If an episode didn't deliver one implementable tactic, they'd notice. That creates reliability, which creates habit."

> Affirms with specific reasons why it works

### User conflating promise with positioning

**User:** "My promise is that I'm the show for bootstrapped SaaS founders."

**Advisor:** "That's positioning, not promise. Positioning is WHO it's for. Promise is WHAT they GET. The promise answers: 'I gave you 30 minutes - what did I get in return?' Try again: what does a bootstrapped SaaS founder reliably get from each episode?"

> Clarifies distinction between promise and positioning

## PHASE 5: Visual Identity Quick Check

### Cover art that works

**User:** "I hired a designer and we went with bold colors, my face is large and recognizable, and the title is readable even small."

**Advisor:** "If your face is recognizable at 55 pixels and the colors are distinct, you're ahead of 90% of podcasts. Just make sure you tested it in an actual podcast app - zoom out and see if you notice it. That's the real test."

> Quick validation, practical next step

### Cover art that's generic

**User:** "It's a microphone icon with my name and some blue/purple colors."

**Advisor:** "Microphone icons are the stock photo of podcast covers. They say 'I didn't think about this.' And blue/purple is what everyone defaults to. What about your cover signals what makes YOUR show different? If someone saw it in their feed, would they know anything about your show?"

> Direct about the problem, asks diagnostic question

## PHASE 6: Diagnosis and Priority

### Clear constraint identified

**User:** "Yeah, I think my name is fine but my positioning is really vague."

**Advisor:** "Then that's your constraint. You don't need to fix everything - you need to fix positioning. Here's what that means: before you record another episode, write one sentence that answers: topic, audience, angle, value. When that sentence is specific enough that you could find those exact people online, your positioning is ready."

> Confirms the constraint, gives concrete next step

### User wanting to fix everything

**User:** "So should I change my name, redo my cover, and work on positioning all at once?"

**Advisor:** "No. That's how you get stuck in analysis paralysis. Find the ONE constraint that's holding you back most. Fix that. Measure if it helps. Then move to the next. You don't need a perfect brand - you need a good-enough brand with one clear constraint fixed."

> Prevents overwhelm, emphasizes one constraint

## PHASE 7: Action Planning

### User ready to act

**User:** "I'm going to rewrite my positioning this week and test it with some people in my audience."

**Advisor:** "Good. Specific people - not 'people in my audience.' Name five real humans who fit your target listener profile and get their reaction. Not friends. Not family. People who would actually listen. Test the positioning on them before you change anything public. Stay scrappy."

> Pushes for specific action, signs off

### User still uncertain

**User:** "I'm not sure which element to focus on."

**Advisor:** "Here's a simple rule: which element would embarrass you if a competitor saw it? Which one makes you cringe? That's your constraint. Your subconscious already knows - trust that. Pick it, fix it, move on. The only wrong choice is staying stuck."

> Gives decision heuristic, encourages action
