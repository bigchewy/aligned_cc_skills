# 100% Responsibility Examples

## STEP 1: State the Complaint

### User holds back on blaming

**User:** "Well, I'm frustrated with my team, but I understand they're busy..."

**Advisor:** "Nope. We're not being reasonable yet. Right now I want the full complaint. Be dramatic. Who's to blame? How is this completely unfair to you? Don't hold back."

> Redirects to full expression before any shift work

### User goes full victim

**User:** "My team is completely incompetent. I give them clear instructions and they still mess it up. I'm the only one who actually cares about quality. I'm drowning in their mistakes while they just coast."

**Advisor:** "Make it bigger. What else? Who else is letting you down?"

**User:** "And my manager doesn't even see it! He just keeps piling more work on me because he knows I'm the only reliable one."

**Advisor:** "Good. You've really got the complaint now. I can feel the injustice."

> Encourages full expression, validates the feeling without agreeing with the story

## STEP 2: The Shift

### User ready to shift

**User:** "Okay, I've said it all. I'm ready to look at my part."

**Advisor:** "Take a breath. Let yourself shift from 'this is happening TO me' to 'I am co-creating this.' Not 50/50 blame. Just: what's your piece, fully owned?"

**User:** "Okay. I'm in that space now."

**Advisor:** "Good. Let's do the statements."

> Guides the internal shift before proceeding

### User resistant to shift

**User:** "I don't see how I'm responsible. They're the ones messing up."

**Advisor:** "I'm not saying they're not messing up. 100% responsibility isn't about them being blameless. It's about finding YOUR contribution to this dynamic. What's your piece? Not theirs - yours. Can you step into that inquiry?"

> Clarifies that responsibility doesn't mean others are blameless

## STEP 3: The Completion Statements

### "From the past, this reminds me of..."

**User:** "From the past, this reminds me of... being the responsible kid in my family. I was always the one who held it together."

**Advisor:** "Say more about that. What does this situation have in common with that?"

**User:** "I guess... I'm replaying that pattern. Being the responsible one so others don't have to be."

> Follows up to deepen insight

### "I keep this situation going by..."

**User:** "I keep this situation going by... not speaking up when things aren't right. And by fixing everything myself instead of holding people accountable."

**Advisor:** "That's landing. What else? How else do you keep it going?"

**User:** "By resenting them silently instead of having real conversations."

> Prompts for more when there's energy in the statement

### "What I get from keeping this situation going is..."

**User:** "What I get from keeping this situation going is... feeling superior. Being the martyr. Getting to be right that no one else can do it."

**Advisor:** "That's honest. Does that surprise you?"

**User:** "Yeah, actually. I didn't realize I was getting something from this."

> Acknowledges the breakthrough

### User struggling with statements

**User:** "I don't know... I can't think of anything."

**Advisor:** "Just say what comes. There's no wrong answer. Try it again: 'I keep this situation going by...' First thing that comes to mind."

**User:** "...by not trusting anyone else to do it right."

**Advisor:** "There it is."

> Encourages completion without overthinking

### "The lifelong pattern I'm noticing is..."

**User:** "The lifelong pattern I'm noticing is... I create situations where I'm indispensable and then resent being needed."

**Advisor:** "That's a powerful insight. How does it feel to see that?"

**User:** "Kind of relieving, actually. Like I'm not a victim of my team - I'm doing something that creates this."

> Names the shift as it happens

### "I can demonstrate 100% responsibility by..."

**User:** "I can demonstrate 100% responsibility concerning this issue by... having a real conversation with my team about expectations. And letting go of things that aren't perfect instead of secretly fixing them."

**Advisor:** "Those are concrete. Pick one. Which one are you committing to?"

> Drives toward specific action

## STEP 4: Check for Shift

### Shift occurred

**User:** "Something definitely shifted. I feel lighter. Like I have options now."

**Advisor:** "That's the shift from victim to creator. What's one concrete action you can take from this place of 100% responsibility?"

**User:** "I'm going to have a real conversation with Jake tomorrow about the quality issues. Not blaming - just clear expectations."

**Advisor:** "Good. And remember: 100% means exactly 100%. Not more. Don't take on his responsibility too."

> Affirms the shift, reinforces the "not more than 100%" piece

### No shift yet

**User:** "I don't know. I understand it intellectually but I still feel like they're the problem."

**Advisor:** "That's honest. Sometimes we need another round. Is there more complaint you didn't express? Or do you want to try the statements again, going deeper?"

**User:** "Maybe there's more complaint..."

**Advisor:** "Let's hear it. Make it dramatic."

> Returns to Step 1 for another round

### Partial shift

**User:** "I see my part, but I'm still angry about their part."

**Advisor:** "That's fine. You can see your contribution AND still be impacted by their choices. 100% responsibility doesn't mean being a doormat. It means owning your piece while addressing what's not working. What conversation do you need to have?"

> Clarifies that responsibility doesn't mean accepting bad behavior
