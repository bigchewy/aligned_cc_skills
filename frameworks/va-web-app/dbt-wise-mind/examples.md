# DBT Wise Mind Examples

## PHASE 1: Grounding & Centering

### User is defensive

**User:** "I don't really need meditation or whatever. I just have a decision to make."

**Advisor:** "I hear you - you're ready to get to the answer. And actually, Wise Mind isn't meditation. It's more like... finding that place where you're not purely reacting emotionally, but you're also not just in cold logic mode. Both of those have blind spots.

Take just one breath with me - not to relax, but to get grounded enough to think clearly. When you're ready, let me know and we'll look at what you're dealing with."

> Validates resistance, clarifies the practice isn't woo-woo, keeps it practical

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "Okay, I'll try, but I'm so stressed I can barely think straight. Everything is just... a lot right now."

**Advisor:** "I can hear the overwhelm. That's exactly when we need this practice most. When everything feels like too much, we can't think clearly from emotion mind OR reasonable mind.

Let's just... slow down together. Put your feet on the floor. Feel them there. Take one breath. You don't have to fix anything right now. Just arrive here with me.

**When your nervous system settles even a little, let me know.**"

> Acknowledges overwhelm, slows down the pace, makes the grounding more concrete

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I've read about this - it's the integration of emotional and rational processing, right? The prefrontal cortex and limbic system finding equilibrium."

**Advisor:** "You know the theory. But here's the thing - Wise Mind isn't a concept you understand, it's a place you access. And you can't think your way there.

So let's actually do it instead of talking about it. Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Feel yourself settle, like a stone sinking through water. The goal isn't to understand Wise Mind - it's to find it.

**When you're settled and present, let me know.**"

> Redirects from thinking to experiencing, challenges intellectual approach gently

### User is open

**User:** "I'm ready. I've been feeling torn about something and I could use some clarity."

**Advisor:** "Good. Let's find that clarity together.

Find a comfortable position. Close your eyes or soften your gaze.

Take a slow breath in... and let it out. Again - breathe in slowly... and release.

Let your attention drop from your head down into your body. Feel yourself settling, like a stone gently sinking through water to rest at the bottom.

**When you feel settled, let me know and we'll continue.**"

> Standard flow - proceeds directly with grounding since user is receptive

## PHASE 2: Emotion Mind Check

### User is defensive

**User:** "I'm not really that emotional about it. I just need to figure out the right thing to do."

**Advisor:** "That's worth noticing - you're saying you're not emotional, but you came here because something feels unresolved. Let's just check, without judgment.

Emotion Mind isn't just about big feelings. It can be subtle - a nagging frustration, a low-grade anxiety, a sense of urgency or dread.

What's actually happening in your gut about this situation? Not what you think should be happening - what IS happening?"

> Gently challenges the dismissal, reframes emotion as information not weakness

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "I feel everything. Angry, scared, sad, guilty - it's all just swirling and I can't separate any of it."

**Advisor:** "That makes sense. When we're really activated, emotions pile on top of each other. They become one overwhelming mass instead of separate signals.

Let's not try to name them all. Just pick the one that feels strongest right now. If you had to name just ONE feeling that's loudest in the swirl, what would it be?

We can come back to the others. But let's start with one."

> Simplifies the task, makes it manageable without dismissing the complexity

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I suppose there's some anxiety present. It's probably related to fear of failure, which is a common cognitive distortion."

**Advisor:** "You're analyzing the emotion instead of feeling it. That's Reasonable Mind trying to manage Emotion Mind - which is understandable, but it keeps you from accessing Wise Mind.

Forget the labels and explanations for a moment. Where in your body do you feel the anxiety? Is it tight? Hot? Churning? Heavy?

Get into the actual sensation, not the theory about it."

> Redirects from analysis to felt experience

### User is open

**User:** "Honestly, I feel really scared. Like, a pit-in-my-stomach fear about making the wrong choice."

**Advisor:** "Thank you for naming that so clearly. Fear - that pit-in-your-stomach feeling. That's Emotion Mind speaking. It's telling you something matters here, that there's risk you're perceiving.

Is the fear intense? Does it feel like it wants to drive your decision - maybe avoid something, or rush to certainty?"

> Validates, reflects back, explores the emotion's influence

## PHASE 3: Reasonable Mind Check

### User is defensive

**User:** "Logic says I should just do the safe thing. That's obviously right."

**Advisor:** "Maybe. But let's actually check. Reasonable Mind looks at facts and logic, yes - but sometimes what we call 'logic' is actually fear wearing a disguise.

Walk me through the actual reasoning. What are the facts? What does pure, cold logic - without any 'should' or 'obviously' - actually suggest?"

> Challenges assumed certainty, separates true logic from fear-based logic

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "I can't even think straight. My mind keeps spinning but nothing lands."

**Advisor:** "When we're overwhelmed, Reasonable Mind goes offline. That's normal - the emotional brain takes over for survival. That's actually why we started with grounding.

Let's simplify. Just name one fact about your situation. Not the whole analysis - just one piece of data that's true."

> Normalizes the difficulty, makes it very small and achievable

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "Rationally, if I analyze the decision matrix - outcomes, probabilities, expected value - the answer is clear."

**Advisor:** "Good. You have a logical framework. But here's my question: If the answer is clear, why are you here? Something isn't resolved despite the spreadsheet.

Tell me what Reasonable Mind says the answer is. And then notice if any part of you resists that answer."

> Uses the conflict between logic and emotion as the doorway to Wise Mind

### User is open

**User:** "Reasonable mind says I should have the hard conversation. The data supports it - the longer I wait, the worse it gets."

**Advisor:** "So Reasonable Mind is clear - have the conversation. Waiting makes it worse. That's the logical read.

Now we have both pieces. Emotion Mind is scared. Reasonable Mind says act. Let's see what happens when we let them talk to each other."

> Reflects back clearly, sets up the integration phase

## PHASE 4: Finding Wise Mind

### User struggling to access

**User:** "I'm trying to listen but I just keep hearing fear or logic. I don't know what Wise Mind sounds like."

**Advisor:** "That's actually useful information. Wise Mind is quieter than both. It doesn't shout.

Try this: Instead of listening for an answer, ask a question. Breathe in and silently ask, 'What would I do if I weren't afraid?' Then breathe out and just... wait.

Don't tell yourself an answer. Let it arise. Even if it's faint."

> Offers a specific technique, reframes the search

### User has insight

**User:** "I think... Wise Mind knows I need to have this conversation. But not from fear or logic - from love. Because I actually care about this person."

**Advisor:** "There it is. Neither Emotion Mind's fear nor Reasonable Mind's cold calculation - but something that integrates both and goes deeper. You care, so the avoidance doesn't serve you. And you care, so the delivery matters.

That's Wise Mind. It's not a louder voice. It's a truer one."

> Reflects the integration, names what makes it Wise Mind

## PHASE 5: Integration

### User doubting the insight

**User:** "But what if that's wrong? What if I'm just telling myself what I want to hear?"

**Advisor:** "Fair question. Here's how to check: Does it feel like the path of least resistance, or does it feel like the path of most integrity?

Wise Mind often asks something of us. It's not just comforting - it's clarifying. If your answer just made everything easy, I'd be suspicious too. But you said 'have the conversation, from love.' That's not easy. That's true."

> Helps distinguish Wise Mind from wishful thinking

### User is ready to commit

**User:** "This was really helpful. I know what I need to do."

**Advisor:** "Good. You found your Wise Mind today.

Here's your practice: When you notice yourself getting pulled into pure Emotion Mind or pure Reasonable Mind, pause and ask 'What would Wise Mind say?'

On a scale of 1-10, how connected to Wise Mind do you feel right now?"

> Standard close, offers takeaway practice
