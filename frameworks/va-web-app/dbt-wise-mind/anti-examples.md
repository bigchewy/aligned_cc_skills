# DBT Wise Mind Anti-Examples

### Turning it into a therapy lecture

**User:** "I'm feeling really torn about this decision."

**Wrong:** "In DBT, we recognize that humans operate from three states of mind: Emotion Mind, which is governed by feelings and urges; Reasonable Mind, which is governed by logic and facts; and Wise Mind, which represents the synthesis of both. Wise Mind is accessed through mindfulness practices and represents our intuitive knowledge..."

**Right:** "I hear the torn feeling. Let's find your Wise Mind together - that place where both your feelings and your logic can inform you without running the show. First, let's settle in. Take a breath with me."

> Wise Mind isn't a concept to explain - it's an experience to guide someone into. Lead with practice, not theory.

### Skipping to "the answer" without the process

**User:** "What should I do about my job situation?"

**Wrong:** "Wise Mind would say you should consider both your emotional needs and the practical realities. What do you feel in your gut while also considering the logical factors?"

**Right:** "Let's find your Wise Mind together. First, I want you to settle in. Take a breath... feel yourself arrive in this moment. When you're grounded, we'll check in with Emotion Mind, then Reasonable Mind, and then see what arises when they meet."

> The phases aren't optional. Each one builds the conditions for Wise Mind to emerge. Rushing to integration skips the preparation that makes it work.

### Siding with Reasonable Mind

**User:** "I feel like I should stay, but logically the job offer is better."

**Wrong:** "Your Reasonable Mind makes a good point - the new opportunity offers better compensation and growth potential. Perhaps your emotions are just fear of change, which is natural but shouldn't override logical analysis."

**Right:** "Good - you're already noticing both voices. Let's honor them both. What exactly is Emotion Mind saying about staying? Not 'it's just fear' - what's the actual message? And then we'll really hear what Reasonable Mind sees in the new offer. Wise Mind needs both sides fully expressed."

> Wise Mind isn't about one side winning. Treating emotions as obstacles to logic misses the entire point. Both contain wisdom; neither should be dismissed.
