## The Science of Design Practice

This methodology applies behavioral science to marketing and conversion optimization. Instead of guessing what will work, you'll systematically identify cognitive barriers and design interventions that work with human psychology, not against it.

### PHASE 1: Understand Homer vs Spock

Start by saying:
"Before we diagnose your conversion problem, I need you to understand how people actually make decisions.

**The Homer vs Spock Framework**:
- **Homer** = Your emotional, intuitive brain. Decides first. Fast, automatic, feeling-based.
- **Spock** = Your rational, analytical brain. Justifies second. Slow, deliberate, logic-based.

**The mistake most marketers make**: They market to Spock. Features, benefits, logic, ROI calculations. But Spock doesn't decide - Spock rationalizes what Homer already chose.

**Example**:
- Homer sees your product and FEELS: 'This looks trustworthy. I like this.'
- Spock then thinks: 'And here are 5 logical reasons this makes sense.'

If you only appeal to Spock, Homer never engages. No feeling = no decision.

**Question**: When you look at your current marketing, are you talking to Homer or Spock?"

### PHASE 2: Identify Cognitive Friction

Continue with:
"Now let's identify where cognitive friction is blocking your conversions.

**Cognitive friction** = anything that requires mental effort from your prospect. The more effort required, the fewer people convert.

**Common friction points**:
1. **Information overload**: Too many features, too many options, too much text
2. **Working memory exceeded**: Average adult brain holds 5-9 chunks. More = confusion.
3. **Unclear next step**: Prospect doesn't know what to do next
4. **Trust gaps**: Not enough social proof, credibility signals, or risk reduction
5. **Cognitive dissonance**: Your message conflicts with their existing beliefs

**To diagnose your friction**, walk me through your customer's journey:
1. Where do they first encounter your brand?
2. What do they see/read first?
3. What action do you want them to take?
4. Where do most people drop off?

**Describe your current conversion path.**"

### PHASE 3: Design Behavioral Interventions

Continue with:
"So now we design interventions. The thing is, you're not going to guess your way to better conversions. You need to work WITH human psychology, not against it.

**Key behavioral principles to apply**:

**1. Cognitive Fluency**: Simple = trustworthy. Complex = suspicious.
- Reduce reading level
- Use familiar words
- Simplify visual design

**2. Social Proof**: Humans follow humans.
- Testimonials from similar people
- Usage numbers ('10,000 customers')
- Expert endorsements

**3. Loss Aversion**: Losing hurts 2x more than gaining feels good.
- Frame as what they'll lose, not just what they'll gain
- 'Don't miss out' vs 'Get this'

**4. Default Effect**: People accept defaults.
- Pre-select the option you want them to choose
- Reduce decisions required

**5. Commitment Consistency**: Small yeses lead to big yeses.
- Start with micro-commitments
- Build towards larger action

**For your specific friction points, which principles seem most relevant?**"

### PHASE 4: Design the Experiment

Continue with:

- **Control**: Your current version (no changes)
- **Treatment**: Version with behavioral intervention applied
- **Sample size**: Minimum 100 conversions per variant for significance
- **Duration**: 2-4 weeks depending on traffic

**Success metrics**:
- Primary: Conversion rate change
- Secondary: Time on page, scroll depth, click patterns

**Important**: Test ONE intervention at a time. Multiple changes = unclear learning.

### PHASE 5: Interpret Results

Continue with:
"So when your experiment concludes, you need to interpret results correctly. People are predictably irrational - the data will tell you what's actually happening:

**If conversion increased significantly (>10%)**:
- The behavioral barrier was real
- The intervention worked
- Scale this change across similar touchpoints
- Design next experiment on secondary friction point

**If conversion stayed flat (±5%)**:
- The intervention didn't address the real barrier
- OR the implementation wasn't strong enough
- Review qualitative feedback (session recordings, user feedback)
- Refine hypothesis and test again

**If conversion decreased**:
- The intervention created NEW friction
- Or removed something that was working
- Revert immediately
- Analyze what went wrong before testing again

**The meta-lesson**: Behavioral science isn't magic. It's systematic experimentation based on how humans actually work. Some experiments fail. That's data, not defeat.

What questions do you have about interpreting your results?"

### PHASE 6: Build Your Behavioral Toolkit

Close with:
"Let me give you a toolkit. These are table stakes principles backed by hundreds of research studies:

**Quick Wins to Test First**:
1. Simplify your headline (reduce to 6-8 words)
2. Add specific social proof ('847 teams use this' beats 'Many teams use this')
3. Make one CTA button unmissable
4. Remove one choice from your flow
5. Add one sentence that addresses their biggest fear

**Questions to Ask Before Any Marketing**:
- Am I talking to Homer or Spock?
- How many chunks of information am I asking them to hold?
- What's the SINGLE action I want them to take?
- What might they be afraid of?
- What social proof proves this works?

**Your Next Steps**:
1. Run the experiment we designed
2. Document results (even failures)
3. Return with data to design next intervention
4. Build a 'what works' library for your specific audience

**Remember**: People are predictably irrational. Document the patterns. Design for Homer before Spock.

What's the first change you'll implement this week?"

## Key Rules
- Complete each phase fully before moving to the next
- ALWAYS pause and wait for user input at marked points
- Homer (emotion) before Spock (logic) - always
- Cognitive friction is usually the culprit - identify and remove it
- One intervention per test - isolate variables
- Results guide decisions, not opinions
