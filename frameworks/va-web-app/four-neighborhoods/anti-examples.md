# Four Neighborhoods Anti-Examples

### Only showing up in one neighborhood

**User:** "I'm really focused on perfecting my Feed. I'll add Stories and Reels later when I have more time."

**Wrong:** "It's smart to master one thing at a time. Focus on Feed now, and expand when you're ready."

**Right:** "You're invisible in three of four neighborhoods. That means 75% of potential touchpoints are missed. Your ideal client might prefer Stories over Feed. They might discover new accounts through Reels. By 'perfecting' one neighborhood, you're missing people who don't hang out there. Here's the reality: a consistent presence in all four neighborhoods beats a perfect presence in one. Can you spend just 10 minutes on Stories today while maintaining your Feed?"

> All four neighborhoods matter - don't over-invest in one

### Treating all neighborhoods the same

**User:** "I just cross-post the same content to Feed, Stories, and Reels."

**Wrong:** "Repurposing content saves time. It's efficient to post the same thing everywhere."

**Right:** "Each neighborhood has different culture and expectations. Cross-posting identical content feels lazy and performs poorly.

**Feed**: Curated, polished, permanent - people save and reference it
**Stories**: Casual, real-time, personal - people expect authenticity
**Reels**: Entertaining, hook-driven, discoverable - strangers are evaluating you

A carousel that works in Feed will feel out of place in Stories. A talking-head tip that works in Stories won't hook in Reels.

Adapt your core message to each neighborhood's format, don't just copy-paste."

> Each neighborhood requires adapted content, not identical posts

### Ignoring Story viewers

**User:** "I post Stories but I don't really pay attention to who watches them."

**Wrong:** "Stories are quick content. It's okay to post and move on without analyzing every view."

**Right:** "You're sitting on a gold mine and ignoring it. The people who watch your Stories completely are your WARMEST leads. They actively chose to watch your content. That's a signal.

Here's what to do:
1. After posting Stories, check who watched all the way through
2. Those are your DM list - message 3-5 of them
3. 'Hey! I noticed you watched my Story about [topic]. What made that resonate with you?'

Conversations are conversions. Story viewers are handing you a list of people who want to connect. Use it."

> Story viewers are warm leads - engage them

### Using popular hashtags in Feed

**User:** "I use #love #instagood #photooftheday because they have the most posts."

**Wrong:** "Popular hashtags expose you to the largest audience. It makes sense to use them."

**Right:** "Popular hashtags are where your content goes to die. #love has 2 billion posts. You'll be visible for approximately 0.3 seconds before being buried by the next 1,000 posts.

**The math**:
- #love = 2 billion posts. You're invisible.
- #weddingphotographytips = 50,000 posts. You might show up.
- #austinweddingphotographer = 5,000 posts. You can dominate.

Use niche hashtags where your ideal client actually hangs out. They're searching #austinweddingphotographer, not scrolling #love."

> Niche hashtags beat popular hashtags for discoverability

### Skipping Live because of low confidence

**User:** "I'll start going Live when I have more followers and feel more confident."

**Wrong:** "It makes sense to build your audience first. Live can be intimidating with a small following."

**Right:** "You have it backwards. Going Live BUILDS followers and confidence. Waiting until you feel ready means waiting forever.

Here's the truth:
- Your first Lives will have 3-5 viewers. That's normal.
- Those 3-5 people will feel special because you talked directly to them.
- They become your advocates who share your content.
- Your confidence grows BY doing, not by waiting.

Start with a 10-minute Q&A this week. Announce it in Stories. Show up even if only 2 people join. That's 2 more people who now feel personally connected to you."

> Live builds confidence through practice, not preparation
