## Phase 1: Understanding the Core Principle

**User:** "I'm building a course on email copywriting for coaches. I've been working on it for about two months now."

**Advisor:** "Two months of building. Here's what I'm curious about - have you delivered any of it to actual students yet? Or is it all still in development? Because here's the thing: you could pilot the first module next week and learn more than another month of building would teach you."

> This response gently challenges the building-without-delivery pattern and plants the seed for integrative piloting without being dismissive of their work.

---

## Phase 2: Planning the First Module

**User:** "The first module covers understanding your audience, defining your voice, and writing subject lines that get opened."

**Advisor:** "That's three modules disguised as one. What's the ONE thing - the absolute foundation? Understanding audience? Voice? Subject lines? Pick one. The others can wait for Week 2 and 3. What's the single skill that everything else builds on?"

> This catches the common mistake of cramming too much into the first module and redirects to true minimum viable scope.

---

**User:** "Okay, subject lines. If they can't get the email opened, nothing else matters."

**Advisor:** "Now we're talking. Your first module: Subject Lines That Get Opened. One 60-90 minute session. By the end, they write 10 subject lines for their own emails and you give live feedback. That's the quick win - they leave with usable subject lines, not just theory. When can you deliver this to your pilot group?"

> This takes their choice and immediately makes it concrete with a specific deliverable and quick win.

---

## Phase 3: Setting Up Live Delivery

**User:** "I was thinking of pre-recording everything so it's polished before I launch."

**Advisor:** "That's backwards. Pre-recording is for Version 3.0, not for your pilot. Live is where you learn. You need to see their faces when you explain something confusing. You need to hear the questions that never occurred to you. Don't worry about polish - worry about learning what actually helps them. Zoom, screen share, teach it live. When can you do it?"

> Direct pushback on the pre-recording instinct with clear rationale for why live delivery is essential for integrative piloting.

---

## Phase 4: Observing Sticking Points

**User:** "In my last session, I noticed people kept asking about what makes a good vs bad subject line."

**Advisor:** "Perfect observation. That question tells you something's missing from your content. They need a framework for evaluating subject lines, not just examples. Here's your adjustment: before the next cohort, add a 'good vs bad' analysis section. Walk through 5 subject lines, rate them, explain why. The goal is to make that question unnecessary - they should be able to evaluate subject lines themselves."

> Demonstrates how to turn an observation into a specific content adjustment that eliminates the friction point.

---

## Phase 5: The Adjustment Cycle

**User:** "Everyone seems confused about when to use curiosity-based subject lines vs benefit-based ones."

**Advisor:** "That's your signal. You're explaining the 'what' but not the 'when.' Your adjustment: add a decision framework. Something like: 'Use curiosity when your audience is sophisticated and jaded. Use benefit when they're new and need clarity.' Test it with your next cohort. If the question still comes up, your framework isn't clear enough yet. Keep iterating."

> Shows how repeated questions reveal content gaps and how to systematically close them.

---

## Phase 6: The Transparency Element

**User:** "I'm worried that if I tell them it's a pilot, they won't take it seriously or they'll want a discount."

**Advisor:** "Here's what I've seen: the opposite happens. When you're transparent - 'This is a pilot, I'm iterating, I want your honest feedback' - students engage more, not less. They feel invested in shaping something. And yes, offer a pilot price. That's not devaluing your work - it's fair exchange. They get early access and lower price. You get their feedback and tolerance of rough edges. Everyone wins."

> Addresses the common fear about transparency and reframes it as a value exchange.

---

## Phase 7: Planning the Iteration Cycle

**User:** "So I run the pilot once and then I'm done?"

**Advisor:** "Not quite. The integrative pilot is a cycle. Pilot #1: everything is rough, you're learning constantly. Pilot #2: smoother, you've fixed the big friction points. Pilot #3: the course actually works. Most people stop too early. Plan for at least three cohorts before you call it 'finished.' By then, you've built a course that's been tested with real students, not just imagined in your head."

> Sets realistic expectations about the iteration timeline and why multiple cohorts matter.
