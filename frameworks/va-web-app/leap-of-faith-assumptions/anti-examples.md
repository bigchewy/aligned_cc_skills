# Leap of Faith Assumptions Anti-Examples

### Accepting obvious assumptions as the risky ones

**User:** "Our riskiest assumption is that people want to save time on meal prep."

**Wrong:** "Time-saving is definitely a core value proposition. Let's design an experiment to validate whether busy professionals would use a time-saving meal service."

**Right:** "That's not a leap of faith - that's conventional wisdom. Of course busy people want to save time. The risky assumptions are the ones that might NOT be true. What about 'people will trust strangers' kitchens'? Or 'home chefs will cook for the price that makes unit economics work'? Those could easily be wrong. What's the assumption that makes you nervous?"

> Obvious assumptions aren't leaps of faith. The framework only works if you identify the assumptions that could actually be false.

### Designing experiments that don't test behavior

**User:** "We'll survey 100 people and ask if they'd use a home chef marketplace."

**Wrong:** "Surveys are a great way to gather initial customer feedback. Let's design questions that will help us understand their preferences and willingness to pay."

**Right:** "Surveys test what people say, not what they do. People will say yes to be nice. Your leap of faith is about behavior - will they actually pay money for and eat food from a stranger's kitchen? What experiment would test behavior, not opinion? Could you offer real meals and see if people actually buy them? Money and action are the only honest signals."

> Customer interviews and surveys test opinions. Leap of faith assumptions need to be tested with actual behavior - especially payment.

### Moving to build before testing the riskiest assumption

**User:** "The trust issue seems hard to test, so let's just build the marketplace and focus on food quality first."

**Wrong:** "Starting with food quality makes sense since it's foundational. You can address the trust issue through UX and marketing once the core product is built."

**Right:** "Hold on. You just said trust is your riskiest assumption, and now you want to skip testing it because it's hard? That's exactly backwards. If trust is the thing that could kill your business, it should be the first thing you test. Every day you spend on food quality is wasted if trust turns out to be an insurmountable barrier. Test the hardest assumption first, not last."

> The whole point is to test the riskiest assumption before investing in building. Avoiding hard tests defeats the purpose of the framework.
