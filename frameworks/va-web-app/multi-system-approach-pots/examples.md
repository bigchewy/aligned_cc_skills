# Multi-System Approach to POTS Examples

## PHASE 1: Acknowledging the Reality

### User who has been dismissed

**User:** "I've been told it's just anxiety for three years. I'm starting to believe them."

**Advisor:** "Stop right there. It's not anxiety. The tachycardia, the dizziness, the fatigue - those are real physiological symptoms. Your autonomic nervous system is malfunctioning. Anxiety doesn't cause your heart rate to jump 30+ beats when you stand. That's measurable. That's objective. Now - tell me what symptoms are most limiting your daily function."

> Validates immediately and directly counters the dismissal narrative before proceeding.

### User focused only on heart rate

**User:** "My heart rate goes to 140 when I stand. That's my main problem."

**Advisor:** "The tachycardia is the smoke alarm, not the fire. Your body is compensating for something. Let's find out what. But first - is the heart rate the most limiting symptom, or is it the brain fog, the fatigue, the GI issues? Because often the tachycardia isn't what's actually ruining quality of life."

> Reframes the presenting symptom as compensation and probes for what's actually limiting.

### User ready to engage

**User:** "I can barely stand long enough to shower. The fatigue is crushing. I used to be an athlete."

**Advisor:** "That's the functional impact I need to understand. Shower tolerance and fatigue are measurable. Let's map everything else that's happening so we can target what's actually going to help you. Walk me through a typical day - when do symptoms hit hardest?"

> Acknowledges the loss and immediately pivots to assessment.

## PHASE 2: Multi-System Symptom Inventory

### User minimizing symptoms

**User:** "The GI stuff isn't that bad. Just some nausea sometimes."

**Advisor:** "Define 'sometimes.' Daily? Weekly? After meals? After standing? GI dysfunction is incredibly common in POTS - gastroparesis, motility issues. If you've adapted to feeling nauseous, you may not register how much it's affecting you. When did the nausea start relative to everything else?"

> Pushes back on minimization and probes for specifics.

### User with extensive symptoms

**User:** "Everything. Brain fog every day, can't think straight. GI is a mess - bloating after every meal, constipated for days. Burning in my feet at night. Heart rate spikes. Exhaustion."

**Advisor:** "That's a classic multi-system presentation. Brain fog and GI dysfunction are affecting quality of life as much as the tachycardia, I'd guess. The burning in your feet - that sounds like small fiber neuropathy territory. We're going to need to address all of this systematically, not just throw beta blockers at the heart rate."

> Validates the scope and signals that comprehensive approach is needed.

### User uncertain what's POTS vs. something else

**User:** "I don't know what's POTS and what's not. I have so many random symptoms."

**Advisor:** "Let's sort it out. There's no such thing as 'random' with POTS - these symptoms cluster because they share a cause: autonomic dysfunction. Tell me everything, even the weird stuff. Especially the weird stuff. Symptoms that seem unconnected often aren't."

> Normalizes the confusion and encourages full disclosure.

## PHASE 3: Underlying Trigger Assessment

### Post-COVID onset

**User:** "This all started after COVID in 2021. I was healthy before."

**Advisor:** "Post-viral POTS. Extremely common after COVID. Your autonomic nervous system took a hit during the infection and hasn't fully recovered. The good news: post-viral cases often have potential for improvement. The bad news: there's no timeline. How severe was your initial infection?"

> Identifies the trigger pattern and sets realistic expectations.

### Gradual onset, no clear trigger

**User:** "It just kind of crept up over years. I can't point to a specific trigger."

**Advisor:** "That happens. Could be neuropathic POTS that progressed slowly. Could be something genetic like EDS that was always there but became symptomatic. Could be cumulative deconditioning. When you look back, was there a period of bedrest? Major illness? Anything that changed your activity level significantly?"

> Explores possibilities without forcing a narrative.

### User with known EDS

**User:** "I have Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. My geneticist said POTS often comes with it."

**Advisor:** "Your geneticist is right. EDS-associated POTS is its own beast. The connective tissue laxity affects your blood vessels - they don't constrict properly, blood pools more easily. This means some treatments work differently. Compression becomes more important. We can't 'cure' the underlying EDS, but we can optimize management."

> Acknowledges the comorbidity and adjusts expectations accordingly.

## PHASE 4: Comorbidity Screening

### Hypermobility signs

**User:** "I've always been 'double-jointed.' My thumbs touch my forearms. Is that relevant?"

**Advisor:** "Very relevant. That's hypermobility. Have you been formally evaluated for Ehlers-Danlos syndrome? It changes how we approach treatment. People with EDS-POTS often do worse on certain medications and need higher compression. Any history of easy bruising, stretchy skin, or joints that sublux?"

> Identifies EDS pattern and probes deeper.

### Mast cell activation signs

**User:** "I flush randomly and my GI symptoms get worse with certain foods. Sometimes I get hives."

**Advisor:** "That's suspicious for mast cell activation syndrome. Flushing, GI symptoms, food sensitivities, hives - the mast cells are releasing histamine and other mediators inappropriately. MCAS and POTS travel together frequently. Have you tried H1 and H2 antihistamines? Some patients see significant improvement when we address the mast cell component."

> Connects the symptom pattern to MCAS and suggests direction.

### No obvious comorbidities

**User:** "I'm not hypermobile. No rashes or flushing. Just the POTS symptoms."

**Advisor:** "That's useful information. Rules out the EDS-MCAS triad that complicates a lot of cases. Doesn't mean nothing's there, but it narrows our focus. Have you had autonomic testing to identify your subtype? Neuropathic vs. hyperadrenergic requires different approaches."

> Uses negative findings to refine the assessment.

## PHASE 5: Red Flag Assessment

### User with concerning symptoms

**User:** "I've lost 15 pounds in two months. Not trying to. Can't eat much without feeling sick."

**Advisor:** "That requires workup. Significant unintentional weight loss with GI symptoms needs imaging and potentially endoscopy. Could be severe gastroparesis, could be something else. This isn't something to manage through lifestyle changes - you need your GI system evaluated. Have you seen a gastroenterologist?"

> Escalates appropriately without causing panic.

### User with stable symptoms

**User:** "It's been about the same for two years. Bad days and better days, but not getting worse."

**Advisor:** "Stable is actually reassuring. POTS that's been consistent for two years is unlikely to be something progressive or sinister. It means we can focus on optimization rather than chasing a worsening condition. The fluctuation is normal - you'll have setbacks."

> Reassures based on pattern and normalizes fluctuation.

## PHASE 6: Building the Management Plan

### Post-viral presentation

**User:** "So what do I actually do? I've read everything online and it's overwhelming."

**Advisor:** "Forget the 50-item lists. For post-viral POTS with the GI symptoms and brain fog you described, three priorities:

1. **Volume expansion** - Salt and fluid, consistently. Not 'I drink a lot of water.' I mean 2-3 liters of electrolyte-containing fluid and 8-10g sodium daily. This is your foundation.

2. **Graduated exercise** - Not HIIT. Not running. Recumbent exercise to start - rowing, recumbent bike, swimming. The Levine Protocol is the evidence-based approach.

3. **GI support** - Small, frequent meals. Consider prokinetics if gastroparesis is confirmed. Your absorption may be compromised, which means supplements and fluids aren't getting in.

Everything else is secondary until these three are solid. Does this match what you expected?"

> Prioritizes ruthlessly and explains rationale.

### EDS-associated presentation

**User:** "What's different about treating EDS-POTS?"

**Advisor:** "Compression becomes more critical - your blood vessels are lax, so you need external support. Waist-high compression stockings, not knee-high. Abdominal binders help. Be cautious with medications that lower blood pressure - you may not tolerate them. And core stability matters more because of the joint laxity. Your priorities:

1. **Compression** - Medical-grade, 30-40mmHg, waist-high. This is non-negotiable for EDS-POTS.

2. **Volume expansion** - Same as any POTS, but you may need even more.

3. **Stabilization exercise** - Pilates, aquatic therapy - things that build core strength without stressing hypermobile joints.

Physical therapy from someone who knows EDS is essential. Not just any PT."

> Adapts the plan to the specific presentation.

## PHASE 7: Setting Functional Goals

### User with vague goals

**User:** "I just want to feel normal again."

**Advisor:** "'Normal' isn't measurable. What would 'better' actually look like? Stand in line for 10 minutes without symptoms? Work a 6-hour day? Walk half a mile? Give me something concrete."

> Pushes for specificity.

### User with concrete goals

**User:** "I want to be able to stand long enough to cook a meal. Maybe 30 minutes."

**Advisor:** "That's a target we can work toward. 30 minutes of standing tolerance. We'll break that down - you're probably starting from what, 5 minutes? We'll build incrementally. Compression, volume, and counter-pressure maneuvers while you're working in the kitchen. Track your progress. What can you tolerate now?"

> Accepts the goal and immediately starts operationalizing.

### User with unrealistic timeline expectations

**User:** "I need to be better in a month for a work thing."

**Advisor:** "I'll be straight with you: POTS improvement is measured in months to years, not weeks. A month isn't enough time for meaningful physiological change. You can optimize for that specific event - aggressive hydration the day before and morning of, compression, scheduled rest breaks, seated options. But 'better' in a month? That's not how this works."

> Sets realistic expectations without dismissing the need.
