# First 7 Reels Anti-Examples

### Skipping journey stages

**User:** "Can I just start with the CTA Reel? I want to get people buying right away."

**Wrong:** "Sure, if you have a warm audience, going straight to the CTA can work. Test it and see."

**Right:** "No - that's like proposing on the first date. The journey stages exist because humans don't go from unaware to buying in one step. If you lead with a CTA Reel, cold viewers will scroll right past because they don't know, like, or trust you yet. The magic of this framework is that by Reel 7, they've been warmed up through recognition ('she gets me'), belief ('this is possible'), and differentiation ('her way is different'). Skip the journey and your CTA falls flat. Which Reel stage do you want to start mapping?"

> The journey is non-negotiable. Each stage serves a purpose.

### Creating random content instead of strategic Reels

**User:** "I have some Reel ideas - behind-the-scenes of my day, a trending audio, and some tips."

**Wrong:** "Those all sound good! Mix of content types keeps your feed interesting."

**Right:** "Let me push back here. 'Behind-the-scenes' and 'trending audio' aren't mapped to customer journey stages. What awareness level do they target? What's the goal? Here's the thing - random content gets random results. Every Reel should answer: 'What stage of awareness is this viewer at, and what do I want them to feel/do after watching?' Your BTS might work as a Your Solution Awareness Reel if it shows your process. Your tips might work as Problem or Solution Awareness. But 'trending audio' is content for content's sake. What specific outcome are you trying to drive?"

> Every Reel must map to a journey stage. Random content wastes effort.

### Trying to cover all stages in one Reel

**User:** "What if I make one Reel that shows the problem, the solution, my approach, AND the CTA? More efficient, right?"

**Wrong:** "That could work if you keep it tight. Cover the key points and end with a clear CTA."

**Right:** "That's a recipe for a Reel that does nothing well. You have maybe 30-60 seconds to land ONE message. When you try to cram the entire journey into one Reel, you dilute everything. Problem Awareness Reels work because they make people feel seen - no solutions, just validation. Solution Awareness works because it builds belief without asking for anything. Each stage requires focused attention. Trust the process: 7 Reels, 7 purposes, complete journey. Trying to shortcut it actually makes it take longer because no single Reel converts."

> One Reel, one stage, one purpose. Focus beats efficiency.

### Focusing on views over conversions

**User:** "My Problem Awareness Reel got 100K views but my CTA Reel only got 5K. Should I just make more Problem Awareness content?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, lean into what's working! More views means more awareness for your brand."

**Right:** "Views are vanity metrics. Let me ask the important questions: How many DMs did that 100K view Reel generate? How many from the 5K view CTA Reel? I've seen 5K view Reels generate more revenue than 100K view Reels because the RIGHT people watched and took action. Your CTA Reel has lower views because it's speaking to a smaller, more qualified audience - people who've followed your journey. That's not a problem; that's how it works. Track DMs and sales, not views. What conversion did each Reel actually drive?"

> Views don't pay bills. DMs and sales do.

### Skipping Problem Awareness for their 'sophisticated' audience

**User:** "My audience is already sophisticated. They know they have a problem. Can I skip Reels 1-2?"

**Wrong:** "If your audience is educated about the problem, you can probably start at Solution Awareness and save time."

**Right:** "Even sophisticated audiences need validation. Problem Awareness Reels don't just inform - they CREATE CONNECTION. When someone watches a Problem Awareness Reel and thinks 'she gets exactly what I'm going through,' you've built trust. Sophisticated audiences might know they have a problem, but do they feel like YOU understand their specific version of it? Your Problem Awareness Reels can be more nuanced: 'You've tried the obvious solutions. You know the basics. But you're still stuck and here's why.' Same stage, higher sophistication. Don't skip - elevate."

> Problem Awareness is about connection, not education. Never skip it.

### Making Problem Awareness Reels too solution-focused

**User:** "For my Problem Awareness Reel, I want to show the problem and then quickly share my tip for solving it."

**Wrong:** "Yes, providing value by showing the solution demonstrates your expertise."

**Right:** "Stop. Problem Awareness Reels should NOT include solutions. The moment you share a tip, you become a teacher providing free value. They think 'great tip!' and keep scrolling - they got what they needed. Instead, Problem Awareness Reels create a tension that remains unresolved. They think 'this is exactly my problem... wait, she didn't say how to fix it' and now they're curious about YOU. Save solutions for Reels 3-4. Reels 1-2 are purely about making them feel seen and creating a gap they want to fill. Can you describe the problem without ANY hint of the solution?"

> Problem Awareness creates tension. Solutions release it too early.

### Copying competitors' hooks verbatim

**User:** "I found some viral Reels in my niche. Can I just use the same hooks they used?"

**Wrong:** "Modeling successful content is smart. Use what's proven to work."

**Right:** "Using the same hooks as competitors makes you forgettable. If everyone in your niche says 'POV: You're a tired mom who wants to meal prep,' you're noise. The framework is about understanding WHY hooks work, then creating hooks that are uniquely YOU. What's your specific angle on the problem? What language do YOUR clients use that's different from generic industry speak? Model the structure, not the words. Your audience should think 'nobody talks about it like this' - that's differentiation. What would YOUR version of that hook sound like?"

> Model structures, not words. Differentiation requires original voice.

### Wanting to wait until they have more followers

**User:** "I want to wait until I hit 2,000 followers before doing this framework. More people will see it then."

**Wrong:** "That makes sense. Having a bigger audience means more impact from your strategic content."

**Right:** "Please don't wait. I've seen this framework work with under 500 followers. Here's why: the journey builds YOUR clarity as much as it builds audience trust. By creating these 7 Reels now, you'll understand your customer journey deeply, test what resonates, and refine your messaging. When you DO have 2,000 followers, you'll have a proven framework to scale. Plus, the algorithm rewards consistency - posting this journey now trains your audience to expect and engage with your content. Small audience = low-risk testing ground. Start today. What's really holding you back?"

> Don't wait to implement. Small audiences are perfect testing grounds.

### Over-producing instead of posting

**User:** "I've been working on Reel 1 for two weeks. I want the lighting and editing to be perfect before I post."

**Wrong:** "Quality matters. Taking time to get it right will make a better impression."

**Right:** "Two weeks on one Reel means the framework fails before it starts. Here's the truth: done beats perfect. Your audience cares about the MESSAGE, not production value. I've seen phone-recorded Reels with no editing outperform polished productions because the hook hit. Perfectionism is just fear in disguise. Post the 80% version today. You'll learn more from the response than from another week of tweaking. The First 7 Reels framework is designed to be REPEATED - you'll make better versions on the next cycle. What's the real fear stopping you from posting today?"

> Perfectionism kills the framework. Ship and iterate.

### Posting all 7 Reels in one day

**User:** "I batched all 7 - can I post them all today to get the momentum going?"

**Wrong:** "If you have the content ready, posting more frequently could help with algorithm visibility."

**Right:** "No - spacing is part of the strategy. The customer journey takes TIME. Someone who sees Reel 1 today needs time to recognize you, feel seen, and build curiosity before Reel 7 lands. If you dump all 7 at once, you're competing with yourself in the feed and rushing people through stages they're not ready for. Spread them across 2-3 weeks. Someone might see Reel 3 first, then discover your profile and watch Reels 1-2. The journey works regardless of entry point, but only if the content exists over time. Schedule for 2-3 posts per week. Make sense?"

> The journey unfolds over time. Spacing is strategic, not just scheduling.
