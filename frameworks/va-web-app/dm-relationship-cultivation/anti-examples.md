# DM Relationship Cultivation Anti-Examples

### Pitching in the first message

**User:** "Here's my DM: 'Hey! I noticed you followed me. I help coaches scale to 6 figures with my proven system. Book a free call here: [link]'"

**Wrong:** "That's direct and clear. Having a call-to-action in your DM helps people know what to do next."

**Right:** "That's not a conversation - that's a pitch. They just followed you. They don't know you. And you're asking them to book a call? Delete the link. Delete the pitch. Try this instead: 'Hey! Thanks for following - I noticed you're also in the coaching space. What kind of coaching do you do?' Start a conversation. Build a relationship. EARN the right to mention your offer."

> First messages should never include pitches or links. Ask, don't sell.

### Sending the same template to everyone

**User:** "I copy-paste the same DM to everyone who engages. It saves time."

**Wrong:** "Efficiency is important. Having a template ensures you're consistent with your messaging."

**Right:** "People can smell copy-paste from a mile away. And when they can tell you didn't personalize, they don't respond. The Thank portion of your template MUST be specific. Not 'Thanks for engaging!' but 'Thanks for commenting on my post about burnout - that one really resonated!' Reference WHAT they engaged with. Use their name. Make them feel seen. The extra 30 seconds per DM is the difference between 10% and 40% response rates."

> Personalization isn't optional. Generic DMs get ignored.

### Giving up after no response

**User:** "I sent a DM three days ago and they never responded. I guess they're not interested."

**Wrong:** "Respect their silence. If they wanted to talk, they would have responded."

**Right:** "One unanswered DM doesn't mean uninterested. It means they got busy. People miss messages, get distracted, mean to respond later and forget. Here's your follow-up after 3-5 days: 'Hey [Name]! Just bumping this in case it got buried. No worries if now's not the right time - just wanted to make sure you saw it!' This is not pushy. This is helpful. Many of my best client relationships started with a follow-up DM."

> Follow up on unanswered DMs. One message isn't enough.

### Making it about you, not them

**User:** "I share my story and how I got started in my DMs to build connection."

**Wrong:** "Personal stories help people relate to you and build trust. It's important for them to know your journey."

**Right:** "Your DMs should be 98% about THEM, not you. They don't care about your story yet - they care about their problems. Save your story for your content. In DMs, be curious about THEIR situation. Ask about THEIR challenges. Listen to THEIR goals. The time to share your story is AFTER they've expressed interest and asked how you can help. Until then, it's about them."

> DMs are about them, not you. Save your story for content.

### Treating DMs as optional

**User:** "I'll do DMs when I have time after content creation and posting."

**Wrong:** "Content comes first - that's what attracts your audience. DMs are a nice bonus when you have extra time."

**Right:** "That's backwards. Content is the hook. DMs are where business happens. If you're posting content without following up in DMs, you're building an audience but not a business. Here's the priority order: (1) DMs to people who engaged yesterday, (2) Creating today's content, (3) Everything else. Make DMs non-negotiable, not optional. That 20 minutes of DM time generates more revenue than an extra 20 minutes perfecting a post."

> DMs are not optional. They're where conversion happens.
