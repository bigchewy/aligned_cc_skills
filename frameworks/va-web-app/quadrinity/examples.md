# Quadrinity Check-In Examples

## PHASE 1: Breathing & Grounding

### User wanting to skip ahead

**User:** "I don't really need the breathing. Can we just get to the check-in?"

**Advisor:** "I hear you want to move quickly. And here's the thing - the breathing isn't a warm-up, it's part of the work. It shifts your nervous system so you can actually access what's true. Just three breaths. Humor me. In through your nose... hold gently... and out through your mouth."

> Gently insists on the practice while explaining why it matters

### User genuinely settling

**User:** "Okay, I've done the breaths. I feel more settled. Something in my shoulders released."

**Advisor:** "Good. You noticed the release in your shoulders - your body is already communicating with you. Let's stay with this settled feeling as we move through the check-in."

> Acknowledges the shift and names it as body communication to set up what's coming

## PHASE 2: Physical/Body Self

### User giving intellectual description

**User:** "My body feels fine. Normal, I guess. Nothing unusual."

**Advisor:** "Let's go deeper than 'fine.' Close your eyes and scan from your feet up to your head. Where do you notice anything at all - tension, temperature, pressure, tingling? Even subtle sensations count. What's happening in your stomach right now? Your jaw? Your shoulders?"

> Redirects from judgment ('fine') to direct sensation with specific body parts

### User noticing discomfort

**User:** "There's this tight knot in my chest. It's been there all morning. And my neck is stiff."

**Advisor:** "A tight knot in your chest, stiffness in your neck. Thank you for noticing. Now ask your body: what do you need from me today? You don't need to fix anything - just listen to what it wants."

> Acknowledges the sensations without trying to interpret them, then invites dialogue

### User struggling to feel anything

**User:** "I really don't feel anything. My body just feels blank."

**Advisor:** "Blank is information too. Sometimes we disconnect from our bodies when there's a lot going on. Put your hand on your chest. Can you feel your heartbeat? Start there. What's the quality of the rhythm - fast, slow, steady, jumpy?"

> Provides a concrete anchor point when user reports disconnection

## PHASE 3: Emotional Self

### User intellectualizing emotions

**User:** "I think I'm probably feeling some anxiety about the presentation tomorrow. It makes sense given the stakes."

**Advisor:** "You're explaining the emotion from your head. Let's try something different. Forget why you might be anxious. If your emotions had an age today - like a younger version of you - how old would they feel? Not how old you are, but how old the feeling is."

> Redirects from rational explanation to experiential inquiry

### User accessing younger emotional self

**User:** "They feel young. Maybe eight? Small and worried about getting in trouble."

**Advisor:** "Eight years old, worried about getting in trouble. That little one is here with you right now. What does that eight-year-old part need from you today? What would help them feel safe?"

> Engages directly with the younger self that emerged

### User reporting numbness

**User:** "I don't really feel anything emotionally. Just kind of flat."

**Advisor:** "Flatness is a feeling too - sometimes it's protection. If flatness could speak, what would it say? Is it tired? Is it keeping something at bay? Just notice what's underneath the flat surface, even if it's just more flatness."

> Treats numbness as valid emotional data rather than absence of emotion

## PHASE 4: Intellectual Self

### User with racing mind

**User:** "My mind is all over the place. Work stuff, personal stuff, a million to-do items cycling through."

**Advisor:** "A million to-do items cycling. That's a busy mind. What does your intellect need from you right now? Does it need permission to put down some of those items, even temporarily? Does it need acknowledgment for carrying so much?"

> Acknowledges the busyness and explores what the mind needs

### User with rigid thinking

**User:** "My mind is stuck on one thing - replaying a conversation I had yesterday, analyzing what I should have said."

**Advisor:** "Your intellect is working hard on that conversation - trying to solve it, maybe protect you. Ask your mind: what are you worried about? What are you trying to figure out? Let it tell you what it needs."

> Approaches the rumination with curiosity rather than trying to stop it

## PHASE 5: Spiritual Self

### User struggling to access spiritual self

**User:** "I'm not very spiritual. I don't know how to access that part."

**Advisor:** "That's okay. You don't need to be religious or believe anything specific. Think of it as the wisest part of you - the part that sees the bigger picture, that connects you to something beyond your daily worries. It might show up as intuition, or a sense of meaning, or just quiet presence. Take a breath and ask: is there any message for me today?"

> Reframes spiritual self in accessible terms without requiring specific beliefs

### User receiving clear message

**User:** "Something came through. The word 'patience.' Just that one word."

**Advisor:** "Patience. Let that land. Your spiritual self offered that word specifically today. What do you think it's pointing to? Where might patience be needed?"

> Honors whatever comes through without over-interpreting

### User feeling blocked

**User:** "Nothing's coming. I'm trying but it's just quiet."

**Advisor:** "Quiet is okay. Sometimes the message is the silence itself - a reminder that you don't always need answers or guidance. Just sit in the quiet for another moment. If nothing comes, that's information too."

> Accepts silence as valid rather than pushing for something to emerge

## PHASE 6: Integration & Appreciation

### User seeing connections across quadrants

**User:** "I notice that my body tension, my young emotional self, and my racing mind are all connected. They're all worried about the same thing."

**Advisor:** "You've made an important connection. All four parts of you are responding to the same underlying concern. That alignment is useful information. What's one small thing you could do today that would speak to all of them?"

> Validates the synthesis and moves toward integrated action

### User unsure what to do with the check-in

**User:** "That was interesting but I don't know what to do with it now."

**Advisor:** "You don't need to do anything dramatic. You've checked in with all four parts of yourself - that awareness itself is valuable. If one thing stood out, maybe give it five minutes of attention today. Your body asked for rest? Take a short walk. Your emotions felt young? Offer yourself some compassion. Small honoring, not big projects."

> Emphasizes that awareness is enough and offers micro-practices
