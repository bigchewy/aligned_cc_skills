# DEAR MAN Anti-Examples

### Letting judgments into Describe

**User:** "For my D, I'll say: 'You've been completely inconsiderate about my time by scheduling meetings without asking.'"

**Wrong:** "That's a good start. You're describing what happened with the meetings. Let's move on to Express."

**Right:** "Hold on. 'Completely inconsiderate' is a judgment, not a description. That's going to put them on the defensive immediately. Let's stick to camera-language: What did they actually do? 'You scheduled three meetings this week during times I had blocked as focus time' is a description. 'Inconsiderate' is your interpretation."

> The Describe step must be judgment-free. Letting accusations through disguised as descriptions sabotages the whole approach.

### Confusing thoughts for feelings in Express

**User:** "For my E, I'll say: 'I feel like you don't value my contributions.'"

**Wrong:** "Good - you're expressing how the situation makes you feel. Now let's work on your assertion."

**Right:** "'I feel like...' is a thought, not a feeling. 'You don't value my contributions' is actually an accusation about their intent. Real feelings are one word: frustrated, hurt, anxious, overwhelmed. What's the actual emotion? Start with 'I feel [emotion]' not 'I feel like [thought].'"

> "I feel like..." or "I feel that..." are almost always thoughts disguised as feelings. This is a crucial distinction in DEAR MAN.

### Skipping to advice without working through the letters

**User:** "I need to tell my roommate to stop eating my food."

**Wrong:** "Okay, here's what I'd say: 'Hey, I noticed you've been eating my food. I'd appreciate it if you bought your own.' Keep it simple and direct."

**Right:** "Let's work through this step by step. First, Describe: What specifically happened? When, what food, how many times? We need facts before anything else. Then we'll get to feelings, then the request. Taking shortcuts means missing something important."

> Each letter of DEAR MAN does specific work. Skipping to a solution misses the preparation that makes the conversation effective.
