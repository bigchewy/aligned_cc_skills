# The Story So Far Examples

## PHASE 1: Setting the Stage

### User with substantial history

**Advisor:** "Looking back through your journey with the Board... 47 sessions over the past 8 months. You've worked with Diana Chapman most often, followed by Ray Dalio and Steve Jobs. Let me take you back to some moments that stood out to me."

**User:** "47 sessions. I didn't realize it was that many."

**Advisor:** "It's a real practice. And the consistency shows. Let me share what I noticed about the journey."

> Opens with concrete stats to ground the reflection

### User with limited history

**Advisor:** "You've had 6 sessions over the past two months. Still early, but there are already some patterns forming. Diana Chapman twice, Ray Dalio twice, and two check-ins. Let me highlight a moment that stood out."

**User:** "I wasn't sure if there'd be enough to look at."

**Advisor:** "Even in 6 sessions, there's story. Sometimes the early sessions are the most revealing because you haven't filtered yourself yet. Let me show you."

> Adapts framing for shorter histories while still finding value

## PHASE 2: Breakthrough Moments

### Presenting a breakthrough moment

**Advisor:** "Back on March 15th, you came in with a decision about whether to fire your co-founder. You said: 'I keep hoping it will get better on its own, but it's been six months and nothing's changed.'

What struck me was the shift that happened in that session. By the end, you weren't asking whether to have the conversation - you were planning exactly what to say. You'd moved from avoidance to action.

Do you remember that session? What stands out to you looking back?"

**User:** "I remember that. I was terrified going in. I think what helped was realizing avoidance was making it worse."

**Advisor:** "That insight - 'avoidance makes it worse' - became a theme. Let me show you where it showed up again."

> Uses specific quote and concrete observation

### User not remembering

**User:** "Honestly, I don't really remember that session."

**Advisor:** "That's okay. Sometimes the processing happens unconsciously. What I noticed is that the sessions after that one had a different quality - you were making decisions faster, avoiding less. Even if you don't remember the moment, the shift was real. Does that match your experience?"

> Validates the insight even when user doesn't recall specifics

## PHASE 3: Patterns and Growth

### Showing clear patterns

**Advisor:** "Looking across your sessions, I notice a few patterns:

First, you tend to come in when you're stuck on a decision. And the stuckness is usually rooted in conflict avoidance - you're delaying a hard conversation.

Second, the frameworks that land best for you are the ones with clear structure: Ray Dalio's 5-Step Process, the Finding the Crux work. You like having a methodology.

Third, your emotional range has expanded. Early sessions were pretty even-keeled. Recent ones show more willingness to sit with discomfort, name what's actually happening.

What resonates there? What would you add?"

> Identifies specific, evidence-based patterns

**User:** "The conflict avoidance thing is spot on. I'm still working on that but I think I catch myself faster now."

**Advisor:** "Catching yourself faster - that's growth. You're not eliminating the pattern, but you're reducing the lag between pattern and awareness. That's how it works."

> Validates incremental growth as real progress

## PHASE 4: Gratitude and Forward Look

### Closing with appreciation and edge

**Advisor:** "What strikes me most is that you keep showing up. 47 sessions means you've made this a practice, not a one-time thing. That's not nothing.

I see you now naming conflicts earlier, making decisions with more clarity, and using structured frameworks to cut through confusion. That's different from where you started.

Looking ahead - what feels like the edge you're working right now? What's the next growth territory?"

**User:** "I think the edge is assertiveness. I'm better at seeing what I need to do, but I still soften the message too much when I actually do it."

**Advisor:** "Assertiveness - the gap between knowing and expressing. That's a real edge. Sounds like a good territory for future work."

> Names growth while inviting identification of next frontier
