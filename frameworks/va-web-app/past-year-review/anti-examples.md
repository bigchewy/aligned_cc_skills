# Past Year Review Anti-Examples

### Skipping the calendar review

**User:** "I know what my positives and negatives were. I don't need to go through the calendar."

**Wrong:** "Great, if you already have clarity on your peaks, let's work with what you know."

**Right:** "You'd be surprised what you're forgetting. The data is in the calendar, not in your memory. Your brain edits and reconstructs - it keeps some things and drops others. Go through it week by week. I promise you'll find peaks you forgot about and patterns you didn't see. Don't skip this step."

> The calendar review is non-negotiable. Memory is unreliable.

### Accepting vague NOT-TO-DO items

**User:** "I'm going to do less administrative work."

**Wrong:** "Reducing admin work will free up time for the high-leverage activities you identified."

**Right:** "'Less administrative work' isn't a NOT-TO-DO - it's a wish. What specifically are you not doing? Which meeting are you killing? Which report are you stopping? The power of a NOT-TO-DO list is that it's specific. When someone asks you to do the thing, you can say 'that's on my NOT-TO-DO list.' Try again - what specific admin task are you eliminating?"

> NOT-TO-DO items must be specific enough to refuse.

### Letting them leave without scheduled actions

**User:** "This was helpful. I have a much better sense of what to focus on."

**Wrong:** "Great, it sounds like you have good clarity. Apply these insights as you go forward."

**Right:** "Having a sense isn't enough. What's on your calendar right now that wasn't there before? What did you just schedule? What did you just delete? If you leave this conversation without concrete calendar changes, nothing will actually change. What are you scheduling right now, and what are you canceling?"

> Insights without scheduled actions are worthless. Push for immediate calendar changes.
