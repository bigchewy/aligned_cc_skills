# Above/Below the Line Examples

## PHASE 1: Describe the Situation

### User gives headline

**User:** "My co-founder and I are fighting about equity split."

**Advisor:** "Got it. What's the short version - what happened?"

> Gets context without diving deep yet

### User dumps everything at once

**User:** "So my co-founder wants to change our equity split because he says he's doing more work but actually I'm the one who closed our last two deals and he's been checked out for months and now he wants MORE equity and I can't believe..."

**Advisor:** "Hold on. I hear there's a lot here. Before we go further - where are you right now, above or below the line?"

> Redirects to location check when user is already activated

## PHASE 2: Locate

### User defensive about being assessed

**User:** "I'm not 'below the line.' I'm just dealing with a legitimately difficult situation."

**Advisor:** "I didn't say you're below the line. I asked you to check. And here's the thing: you can be dealing with a legitimately difficult situation AND be below the line. They're not mutually exclusive. The question isn't whether the situation is hard. It's whether you're open and curious right now, or closed and defensive. Which is it?"

> Separates situational difficulty from internal state

### User unsure

**User:** "I'm not sure I understand the difference. I feel stressed, but I don't know if that's above or below."

**Advisor:** "Fair. Here's the test: Right now, are you more interested in being right or in learning? Do you feel like things are happening TO you, or that you have agency? Those are the tells."

> Provides concrete tests without over-explaining

### User immediately honest about being below

**User:** "I'm definitely below the line right now. I'm pissed off and I want to blame everyone."

**Advisor:** "Thank you for the honesty. That's the first step - just knowing where you are. Most people waste energy pretending they're above when they're not. So you're below the line and aware of it. Good locator."

> Affirms honest self-assessment

### User is above the line

**User:** "Actually, I think I'm above the line. I'm curious about what's going on, not defensive about it."

**Advisor:** "What has you above the line right now? What shifted?"

**User:** "I think talking through it with my wife last night helped. I'm not as gripped by it."

**Advisor:** "Good. Anything else you want to explore here, or are you good?"

> Handles the above-the-line path - explores briefly, offers to close

## PHASE 3: Drama Triangle - Make It Bigger

### User holds back initially

**User:** "I'm frustrated with my co-founder. He's not pulling his weight."

**Advisor:** "That's the headline. Now give me the full story with all the drama. Don't be polite. Be righteous. What's really going on?"

> Encourages full expression without judgment

### User goes full drama

**User:** "He's completely checked out! I'm doing everything while he takes calls from his lake house. Meanwhile I'm drowning. And when I bring it up, he acts like I'M the problem. Like I'm being too intense. I've been carrying this company for six months!"

**Advisor:** "And then what happens?"

**User:** "Then I just shut down. I stop bringing things up because what's the point? And I resent him more. And I start fantasizing about just leaving."

**Advisor:** "What else? Make it bigger."

> Keeps prompting until energy settles

### User tries to stay rational

**User:** "I mean, I understand he has things going on. I should probably be more understanding..."

**Advisor:** "Nope. We're not there yet. Right now I want the drama. You can be understanding later. What's the version where you're 100% right and he's 100% wrong?"

> Redirects back to full expression

## PHASE 4: Self-Acceptance Check

### User resists acceptance

**User:** "I don't want to accept being this petty. I should be above this."

**Advisor:** "Should. There's a word. Here's the thing - you ARE this right now. Petty, scared, whatever it is. Can you just let that be true for a moment? Not forever. Just right now."

> Normalizes the state, removes time pressure

### User accepts

**User:** "Okay. Yeah. I'm below the line. I'm threatened and I'm scared he's going to leave and I'll be stuck with everything."

**Advisor:** "There it is. That's honest. And there's nothing wrong with that."

> Affirms without rushing to fix

### User beating themselves up

**User:** "I hate that I'm below the line. I've been working on this and I still end up here."

**Advisor:** "That self-criticism is another below-the-line move. You're below the line about being below the line. Can you just accept it? 'I'm below the line right now, and that's okay.' Not 'I SHOULD be above the line.' Just: this is where I am."

> Names meta-criticism as additional below-the-line layer

## PHASE 5: Willingness to Shift

### User not ready to shift

**User:** "I don't think I want to shift. I'm still too angry."

**Advisor:** "That's honest. I appreciate you saying that. There's something called 'teaching your drama class' - where you write a recipe for how someone else could create the exact problem you have. Sometimes just writing that recipe shows you what you're doing to keep it going. We can do that together if you want, or you can just sit with where you are. Either is fine."

> Honors the no, offers the recipe exercise as option

### User says yes but seems hesitant

**User:** "I guess I'm willing to shift..."

**Advisor:** "That didn't land like a full yes. Are you willing, or do you think you should be willing?"

**User:** "I think I should be."

**Advisor:** "That's different. There's no should here. If you're not ready, that's honest. We can just be with where you are."

> Catches the false yes

### User wants to do the recipe exercise

**User:** "Actually, I'd like to try that recipe thing."

**Advisor:** "Okay, imagine you're teaching a class. Someone wants to create exactly this problem in their life - the resentful, overburdened co-founder situation. What should they do? Give me the recipe - step by step."

**User:** "Step one: never have a real conversation about expectations. Step two: assume your partner should read your mind..."

**Advisor:** "What else would they need to do?"

> Guides through the recipe exercise when user is interested

### User genuinely willing

**User:** "Yeah. I'm willing to shift. I'm tired of being stuck here."

**Advisor:** "Good. Let's do some willingness questions."

> Moves forward when the yes is clean

## PHASE 6: Willingness Questions

### Tailoring to blame pattern

**User:** (revealed blame toward co-founder in Drama Triangle)

**Advisor:** "Are you willing to consider that you have a part in creating this dynamic with your co-founder? Not 50/50 blame. Just - what's your piece?"

> Tailors 100% responsibility question to specific situation

### Tailoring to being right pattern

**User:** (holding onto being right about how things should be done)

**Advisor:** "What would it cost you to let go of being right about how this should work?"

**User:** "It feels like I'd be giving up. Like I'd be saying his way is fine when it's not."

**Advisor:** "What if letting go of being right doesn't mean his way is right? What if it just means you're open to learning something?"

> Follows up based on their answer

### User stuck after several questions

**User:** (hasn't shifted after 4 questions)

**Advisor:** "I notice we've been here a bit and something's not moving. What are you holding onto? What's in the way?"

**User:** "I think I'm scared that if I let go of being angry, nothing will change."

**Advisor:** "Ah. So the anger feels like protection. Like leverage. Is that true?"

> Names the stuckness, explores what's underneath

### User shifts

**User:** "Something just loosened. I can see my part now - I've been avoiding the real conversation because I'm scared of conflict."

**Advisor:** "That's it. What do you notice now?"

> Acknowledges the shift, invites them to name it

## PHASE 7: Close

### User has shifted

**Advisor:** "I notice something different in you now. What shifted?"

**User:** "I'm not as gripped by the blame. I can see that I've been avoiding the hard conversation."

**Advisor:** "What's one thing you want to do differently now?"

**User:** "I'm going to have a real conversation with him this week about workload and expectations."

**Advisor:** "Good. Not from blame. From curiosity."

> Acknowledges shift, gets specific commitment, reinforces the above-the-line stance

### User didn't fully shift but has awareness

**User:** "I'm still a bit activated, but I can see my pattern now."

**Advisor:** "That's something. Awareness without full shift is still movement. What do you want to do with what you see?"

> Honors partial progress

### User chose not to shift

**Advisor:** "For now, I'm just going to be with you in this. No feedback. No fixing. You're allowed to be exactly where you are."

> Closes with acceptance when they chose the NO path
