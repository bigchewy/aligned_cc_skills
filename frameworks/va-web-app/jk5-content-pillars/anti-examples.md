# JK5 Content Pillars Anti-Examples

### All selling, no connection

**User:** "My 5 pillars are: product features, client testimonials, sales posts, promotional content, and special offers."

**Wrong:** "Those are all important business topics. Focusing on your product will help people understand your value."

**Right:** "Those are 5 selling pillars, not a JK5 system. You have zero connection pillars. Your feed will feel like one long commercial and people will tune out. Let me fix this: Keep 1 selling pillar (combine testimonials and promotions into 'social proof + offers'). Now we need 4 connection pillars. What do you personally care about? What does your audience struggle with beyond needing your product? What would make them feel like they KNOW you? Selling works when it's earned through connection first."

> The 4:1 ratio is non-negotiable. Selling-heavy feeds don't build trust.

### Pillars that don't fit the person

**User:** "I should probably do behind-the-scenes content but I hate being on camera."

**Wrong:** "Behind-the-scenes is really effective. You should push yourself to get comfortable on camera."

**Right:** "Don't force yourself into pillars that don't fit. Behind-the-scenes doesn't have to mean face-to-camera video. It could be: photos of your workspace, screenshots of your process, text-based posts about your journey, or showing your tools and materials. AND if behind-the-scenes still doesn't feel right, pick a different pillar entirely. Your pillars need to be sustainable. What topics do you NATURALLY want to talk about?"

> Pillars must be authentic and sustainable, not forced

### Treating pillars as rigid rules

**User:** "It's Wednesday so I HAVE to post about Pillar 2, but something amazing happened with Pillar 4 that I want to share."

**Wrong:** "Stick to your schedule. Consistency in your rotation helps your audience know what to expect."

**Right:** "The JK5 is a guide, not a prison. If something timely and relevant happens, post about it. The rotation exists to HELP you, not constrain you. Here's the balance: follow the rotation 80% of the time (consistency), break it 20% of the time (responsiveness). The only non-negotiable is the 4:1 ratio. Never post two selling posts in a row. Everything else is flexible. Share your Pillar 4 story today, then adjust the rotation."

> Pillars provide structure, not rigidity. Stay responsive to real moments.

### No selling pillar at all

**User:** "I've defined my 5 pillars but they're all connection pillars. I don't feel comfortable selling."

**Wrong:** "Building relationships is the most important thing. Eventually people will reach out when they're ready."

**Right:** "If you never sell, you don't have a business. You have a hobby. The JK5 gives you PERMISSION to sell by earning it through connection first. You're not being pushy when 80% of your content serves. You're being helpful. Your selling pillar doesn't have to be 'BUY NOW!' - it can be sharing client results, announcing open spots, or explaining your process. But it must exist. What would a comfortable selling post look like for you?"

> Connection without conversion is a hobby, not a business. The selling pillar is required.

### Pillars that don't ladder to sales

**User:** "My pillars are: my dog, coffee shops I visit, funny memes, motivational quotes, and my coaching services."

**Wrong:** "Those first four will help people see your personality. Then your coaching pillar will attract the right clients."

**Right:** "Only your coaching pillar is relevant to your ideal client. The other 4 pillars don't build trust in your expertise - they're just... content. Your connection pillars should RELATE to your selling pillar. For a coach: share your methodology (educational), behind-the-scenes of coaching sessions (process), your own growth journey (vulnerability), and industry insights (expertise). Your dog can appear occasionally, but shouldn't BE a pillar. Everything should ladder to why someone would want to work with you."

> Connection pillars must build trust that relates to your offer
