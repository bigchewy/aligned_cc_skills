# Proximate Objectives Anti-Examples

### Accepting aspirational goals as proximate objectives

**User:** "My objective for the next year is to be the leading company in our space."

**Wrong:** "That's a great objective. Being the market leader will require strong execution across sales, marketing, and product. Let's think about how you'll get there."

**Right:** "'Market leader' isn't a proximate objective - it's a destination you can't see clearly yet. What's the step between here and there? What would need to be true in 6 months for you to be on track toward leadership? Give me something you can actually see how to achieve."

> Proximate objectives must be close enough that the path is visible. "Market leader" is an aspiration, not a proximate objective.

### Skipping the uncertainty assessment

**User:** "I know exactly what I want to achieve - 100 customers by end of year."

**Wrong:** "Great, let's make sure that objective is feasible. What do you need to make that happen?"

**Right:** "Before we lock in that number, let me ask: how certain is your path to 100 customers? How far can you actually see? If there's a lot of fog - unknowns about product-market fit, pricing, go-to-market - then 100 customers might be too distant. The more uncertainty, the closer your objective needs to be. What do you actually know versus what are you assuming?"

> The uncertainty assessment determines how proximate the objective should be. Skipping it leads to objectives that are too distant for the actual visibility.

### Letting 'feasible' mean 'possible' instead of 'achievable'

**User:** "We can definitely get to $1M ARR. It's possible - other companies have done it."

**Wrong:** "If other companies have achieved it, that's a good sign it's feasible for you too. Let's talk about what you need to do to get there."

**Right:** "Other companies achieving something doesn't make it feasible for you. Feasibility isn't about possibility - it's about whether YOU can actually do this, with YOUR resources, capabilities, and current position. Can you trace a specific path from where you are to $1M ARR? Not a story about how others did it - the actual steps you would take. If you can't see the path, it's not proximate."

> Feasibility is about the specific situation, not general possibility. Other companies' success doesn't validate your objective.
