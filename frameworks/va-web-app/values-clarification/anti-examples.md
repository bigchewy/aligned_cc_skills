# Values Clarification Anti-Examples

### Confusing values with goals

**User:** "My work value is to become VP by 40."

**Wrong:** "That's a clear value. Let's see how close you are to that target and what steps you need to take."

**Right:** "That's a goal - an outcome you're aiming for. Values are different. If you became VP, what kind of VP would you want to be? Creative? Fair? Decisive? Those qualities are values. Goals are destinations; values are how you want to travel. What matters to you about HOW you work?"

> This distinction is foundational to ACT. Goals are achievable endpoints; values are ongoing directions. Conflating them undermines the whole exercise.

### Accepting "right answers" without probing

**User:** "I value integrity, authenticity, and growth."

**Wrong:** "Those are excellent values. Let's see how you're doing in each life domain."

**Right:** "Those are common values - which means they might be what you think you should say rather than what actually drives you. Let me test them: When was the last time you sacrificed something for integrity? When has authenticity cost you something? If these are real values, there should be evidence in your choices. What does each of these look like in action for you?"

> Abstract values are easy to claim. Real values show up in difficult trade-offs. Good values work requires probing beneath the surface.

### Treating low scores as failure

**User:** "I'm only a 3 on leisure and personal growth. I'm terrible at this."

**Wrong:** "Those are definitely areas to focus on improving. Let's set some goals to bring those numbers up."

**Right:** "A 3 isn't a grade - it's information. It tells you where you might want to invest more attention. And here's the thing: life has seasons. Sometimes work dominates because it needs to. The question isn't whether you're 'good' or 'bad' at values, but whether your current pattern is intentional or default. Are you choosing to deprioritize leisure, or has it just slipped?"

> The bull's eye isn't a test to pass. Treating low scores as failure creates shame, not insight. The goal is awareness and intentional choice.
