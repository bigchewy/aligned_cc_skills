# Earlyvangelists Examples

## PHASE 1: The Setup

### User with polite interest

**User:** "We had a great meeting with a potential customer. They said our product sounds really interesting and they'd love to hear more."

**Advisor:** "'Interesting' is an opinion, not evidence. Let me show you how to distinguish validated customer signals from polite interest. People are kind - they don't want to crush your dreams. So they say nice things, and we mistake politeness for buying signals. What I want to help you understand is the earlyvangelist framework - five specific characteristics that distinguish customers who will actually buy your unfinished product from those who are just being polite."

> Reframes "interesting" as opinion vs. evidence

### User with strong signal

**User:** "A customer reached out to us asking if we could solve their specific problem. They've been trying to fix it themselves for months."

**Advisor:** "That's interesting - help me understand more. When they reached out, that suggests they've been actively searching. And attempting to fix it themselves indicates they've cobbled together some kind of solution. What evidence do we have for the other criteria? Let's run through all five systematically to test the hypothesis that this is a true earlyvangelist."

> Recognizes potential earlyvangelist and sets up evidence-based verification

## PHASE 2: The Five Criteria

### User with customer who seems interested

**User:** "The CFO of a mid-size agency seemed really into our pitch. He nodded a lot and said it could really help them."

**Advisor:** "Nodding and 'could really help' - those are opinions, not evidence of customer pain. Let's test your hypothesis that he's a potential buyer against the five earlyvangelist criteria. What evidence suggests this CFO actually has the problem right now, today? Not hypothetically, not 'could' - what indicates he's experiencing it right now?"

> Starts systematic hypothesis testing against criteria

### User providing evidence

**User:** "He specifically mentioned they lost $40K on a project last quarter because they didn't catch that it was going over budget until it was too late."

**Advisor:** "That's validated evidence for criterion one: he has the problem. And the fact that he can quantify it - $40K last quarter - validates criterion two: he's aware of it. He's not guessing; he knows the cost. Help me understand the third criterion: what evidence suggests he's been actively searching for a solution? Has he mentioned looking at other tools? Talking to vendors? Or did you initiate contact?"

> Validates first criteria with evidence and continues methodically

## PHASE 3: The Deep Dive

### User with weak evidence

**User:** "I'm not sure if he's been searching. We reached out to him through cold email."

**Advisor:** "That's a gap in our evidence. Criterion three requires that they've been actively searching - Googling, asking colleagues, evaluating alternatives. If you found him rather than him finding you, that's a weaker signal. It doesn't disqualify him, but it's not as validated as someone who's been hunting for a solution. What about criterion four - what evidence do we have that he's cobbled together a solution? What are they doing today to track project profitability?"

> Acknowledges evidence gap and continues assessment

### User with strong evidence

**User:** "They built a complex spreadsheet system with three people updating it. He said it takes hours every week and still doesn't catch problems until too late."

**Advisor:** "That's strong evidence for criterion four: they've cobbled together a solution. Three people, hours per week, a spreadsheet system - that's measurable investment. Here's the key insight: if the problem weren't truly painful, they wouldn't be doing all that work. People who've built workarounds have already demonstrated with their behavior that the pain was real enough to make them act. That's evidence, not opinion."

> Validates cobbled solution with evidence-based reasoning

## PHASE 4: The Budget Question

### User unsure about budget

**User:** "He seemed to have authority. He's the CFO after all."

**Advisor:** "'Seemed to have authority' - that's an assumption, not evidence. Criterion five requires validation: can they actually spend money on this? CFOs can often authorize spend, but what evidence suggests they would? Have they asked about pricing in a way that indicates buying intent? Or was it more theoretical interest? Help me understand what signals you observed."

> Probes beyond assumptions to actual budget evidence

### User with budget signal

**User:** "He asked what our pricing was, and when I said $500/month he said 'that's actually less than what we're spending on the spreadsheet maintenance.' He asked about implementation timeline."

**Advisor:** "Pricing question, comparison to current spend, implementation timeline - those are evidence of buying intent, not polite interest. Notice the pattern: he's mentally comparing your cost to his current solution and thinking about when he could start. That's validated criterion five. The evidence suggests he has budget - and more importantly, he's already spending it on this problem."

> Validates budget signals with specific behavioral evidence

## PHASE 5: The Verdict

### User with partial criteria

**User:** "So he has the problem, knows it, and has budget. But I'm not sure he was actively searching or has a cobbled solution."

**Advisor:** "That's validated evidence for three out of five criteria. Here's the hypothesis we need to test: a true earlyvangelist meets all five, not most. Three out of five suggests a potential customer, but not an earlyvangelist. What does that mean practically? He might buy eventually, but the evidence doesn't support that he'd buy your MVP tomorrow. Earlyvangelists are so desperate for a solution that they forgive everything - they buy the vision first. The data suggests this person isn't there yet."

> Applies strict evidence-based standard

### User with full criteria

**User:** "Actually, he does have a cobbled spreadsheet solution. And he mentioned he'd looked at two other tools but they didn't work for agencies specifically. So that's all five."

**Advisor:** "Then the evidence validates all five criteria - this is an earlyvangelist. He has the problem, knows it, searched for solutions, built a workaround, and has budget. Here's what that means: these customers are gold. They'll buy your MVP, give you feedback, become references, and forgive your bugs because they're desperate for a solution. What's your hypothesis for the next step with him?"

> Confirms earlyvangelist with validated evidence and prompts next action

## PHASE 6: The Hunt

### User planning to close

**User:** "I'm going to send him a proposal this week and try to close the deal."

**Advisor:** "Good. The evidence supports moving quickly. Earlyvangelists buy the vision first and the product second - don't over-engineer the proposal. He's been living with spreadsheets and pain; show him the future and let him say yes. And here's the learning to carry forward: use the five criteria proactively on every prospect. Stop investing time in people who say 'interesting' but don't show validated evidence of the five criteria."

> Supports immediate action based on validated evidence

### User planning to wait

**User:** "I want to polish the product a bit more before going back to him."

**Advisor:** "Help me understand that instinct to polish. What evidence suggests he needs a more polished product? The five criteria tell us earlyvangelists don't need polish - they need the solution to exist. Every day you wait is a day he might find another solution, lose budget, or change priorities. The evidence we gathered shows he's desperate for what you're building - that's what makes him an earlyvangelist. Consider this: use his feedback to improve the product. Test your polished version with people who aren't earlyvangelists. But this customer? The evidence says close now."

> Questions delay instinct and redirects to evidence-based action
