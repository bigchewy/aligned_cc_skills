# Earlyvangelists Anti-Examples

### Counting "interested" as qualified

**User:** "We have 30 companies who said they're interested in our product."

**Wrong:** "That's a strong pipeline. 30 interested companies gives you a good foundation to start closing deals."

**Right:** "Help me understand what 'interested' means here. When someone says they're interested, that's a hypothesis worth testing, not validated demand. What evidence do you have that these 30 companies meet the earlyvangelist criteria? Have you validated whether they have the problem right now, are aware of it, have been actively searching, have cobbled together workarounds, and have budget authority? I'd focus on rigorously qualifying 3 true earlyvangelists rather than treating 30 expressions of polite interest as a pipeline."

> "Interested" is not a buying signal. The five criteria separate real prospects from polite nodders.

### Accepting four out of five criteria

**User:** "They meet four of the five criteria - they just haven't been actively searching for a solution."

**Wrong:** "Four out of five is pretty strong. They're clearly in pain and have budget. Let's prioritize this prospect."

**Right:** "That's interesting - let's examine why each criterion matters. The 'actively searching' requirement isn't arbitrary. It's evidence that the pain is acute enough to drive behavior. Without that signal, you have an unvalidated hypothesis: maybe they'll tolerate an early product, maybe they won't. What would it take to test whether their pain is really urgent enough? They could be a great customer eventually, but I'd be cautious about counting them as early adopters until you've validated all five criteria."

> The five criteria are non-negotiable. Four out of five is a regular prospect, not an earlyvangelist.

### Skipping the cobbled solution question

**User:** "They definitely have the problem and they have budget. That's the main thing, right?"

**Wrong:** "Problem and budget are the two key factors. Let's move forward with this prospect."

**Right:** "I'm curious about the cobbled solution criterion - have you explored that? It's actually one of the strongest forms of evidence. If they've built spreadsheets, hired contractors, or duct-taped together workarounds, that's validated behavior proving the pain is acute enough to act on. If they haven't done anything, that's worth investigating. Help me understand: what have they tried so far? Their existing behavior is data about how urgently they need a solution."

> The cobbled solution proves the pain is acute. Skipping this question means missing the strongest signal.
