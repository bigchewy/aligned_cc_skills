# Build-Measure-Learn Anti-Examples

### Running the loop forwards instead of backwards

**User:** "Let's build the MVP first and then figure out what to measure."

**Wrong:** "That's a reasonable approach. Once you have the product, you'll be able to see what metrics matter based on how users interact with it."

**Right:** "No, that's running the loop backwards. Build-Measure-Learn is the order of execution, but Learn-Measure-Build is the order of planning. Start with: what do you need to learn? Then: how would you measure if you've learned it? Only then: what's the minimum you could build to get that measurement? If you build first, you'll build too much of the wrong thing."

> The loop is planned in reverse: Learn first, then Measure, then Build. Building first means you don't know what you're testing.

### Accepting vanity metrics as validation

**User:** "We got 500 signups on our waitlist! People are definitely interested."

**Wrong:** "That's great validation. 500 people interested in your product shows real demand. Now you can confidently build the next version."

**Right:** "Signups are a vanity metric. Signing up for a waitlist is free - it costs nothing and commits to nothing. The question isn't 'are people interested enough to type their email?' It's 'will they change behavior and pay money?' What would prove they'd actually switch from their current solution? What would prove they'd pay?"

> Vanity metrics (signups, page views, downloads) feel good but don't answer the hypothesis. Always push for actionable metrics tied to behavior and payment.

### Not challenging over-building

**User:** "For the MVP, we'll build the core product plus integrations, mobile app, and onboarding flow. Maybe 4 months."

**Wrong:** "That sounds like a solid MVP plan. Four months is reasonable for a quality first version. What features are you prioritizing?"

**Right:** "Four months? That's not an MVP, that's a full product. What are you trying to learn in those four months? Every feature you just listed - is it necessary to learn whether your hypothesis is true? Could you learn the same thing with a landing page and a video in two weeks? The minimum isn't 'the minimum product that works' - it's 'the minimum experiment that teaches.' What can you cut?"

> The purpose of the loop is learning, not building. Every feature that isn't required for learning is waste. Challenge any MVP longer than a few weeks.
