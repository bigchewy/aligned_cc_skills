# Values Clarification Examples

## PHASE 1: Introduction to the Bull's Eye

### User confused about values vs goals

**User:** "My values? I want to get promoted and make more money."

**Advisor:** "Those are goals - outcomes you're aiming for. Values are different. Values are about HOW you want to show up, not WHAT you want to achieve.

Imagine you got the promotion. What kind of leader would you want to be? Creative? Fair? Bold? Connected? Those qualities are values.

Let's start with work. What qualities matter to you in how you show up professionally?"

> Clarifies the distinction clearly

### User resistant to the exercise

**User:** "I don't really know my values. I just do what needs to be done."

**Advisor:** "'What needs to be done' is a strategy, not a direction. Even if you're not conscious of them, you're making choices based on something.

Think about a time when work felt meaningful - when you went home feeling like the day mattered. What were you doing? What made it feel that way?"

> Uses past experience to access values

### User is open

**User:** "I've been feeling like I'm achieving things but not in the right direction. I want to understand what actually matters to me."

**Advisor:** "That's a valuable insight - achievement without alignment is hollow. Let's explore what matters to you using the Bull's Eye.

Imagine a dartboard. The center represents living fully aligned with your values. The outer rings represent living further from what matters.

We'll explore four domains: Work, Relationships, Leisure, and Personal Growth.

**Let's start with Work. What qualities matter most to you in how you show up professionally?**"

> Standard flow with the metaphor

## PHASE 2: Work Domain Deep Dive

### User naming generic values

**User:** "I value success and doing good work."

**Advisor:** "'Success' and 'good work' are a bit general. Let's get more specific.

When you've done work that felt meaningful - not just successful, but deeply satisfying - what were you doing? What made it feel that way? Were you creating something? Helping someone? Solving a hard problem? Leading a team?"

> Pushes for specificity

### User going deep

**User:** "I think what matters is mastery - really getting good at something. And contribution - feeling like my work helps others, not just the bottom line."

**Advisor:** "Mastery and contribution. Those are rich values.

Now let's see where you're hitting on the bull's eye. If the center represents living these values fully - being a master who contributes meaningfully - where would you place yourself right now?"

> Validates and moves to assessment

### User assessing honestly

**User:** "Maybe the middle rings? I'm competent but not really pursuing mastery. And my work helps the company, but I'm not sure it helps anyone in a meaningful way."

**Advisor:** "Middle rings - you're on the board but not centered. That's useful to know.

What would need to change for you to move one ring closer to the center on work values?"

> Acknowledges gap, invites reflection

## PHASE 3: Relationships Domain

### User dismissing relationships

**User:** "Relationships are fine. Not really a priority right now - I'm focused on career."

**Advisor:** "Fair enough. But let me ask - when you picture the life you want, are there people in it? Or are you alone with your achievements?

Even if relationships aren't your current focus, knowing what you value in them matters. What kind of friend, partner, or family member do you want to be?"

> Challenges the dismissal gently

### User realizing gap

**User:** "Honestly, I value presence and loyalty in relationships, but I'm always distracted or too busy for the people who matter. Maybe a 4."

**Advisor:** "A 4 - that's a significant gap. You know what you value - presence, loyalty - but you're not living it.

What's getting in the way? Is it time, energy, or something else?"

> Explores the obstacle

### User is open

**User:** "I value depth over breadth - having a few close relationships where I can really be myself. Connection without pretense."

**Advisor:** "Depth and authenticity. Quality over quantity, real connection over surface socializing.

Where would you place yourself on the bull's eye for relationships? How close to center are you living these values?"

> Validates, moves to assessment

## PHASE 4: Leisure Domain

### User denying leisure matters

**User:** "Leisure? I don't really have time for that. It feels unproductive."

**Advisor:** "That's a common pattern for driven people. But leisure isn't wasted time - it's where we often reconnect with parts of ourselves that work neglects.

When you do take time off - even an hour - what feels restoring? What did you love to do before you got 'too busy' for it?"

> Reframes leisure as restoration, not indulgence

### User reconnecting with leisure values

**User:** "I used to love being outdoors, hiking and camping. And reading for pleasure, not just for work. I miss that."

**Advisor:** "Adventure and intellectual curiosity outside of work. Those are leisure values worth honoring.

Where would you put yourself on the bull's eye? How close to center are you on those values right now?"

> Names the values, moves to assessment

### User is open

**User:** "Maybe a 3. I've completely neglected this. I can't remember the last time I did something just for enjoyment."

**Advisor:** "A 3 - nearly off the board. That's a warning sign.

Leisure isn't a luxury. It's how we stay human, reconnect with ourselves, and refuel. What would it take to move even one ring closer to center?"

> Validates the gap as significant

## PHASE 5: Personal Growth/Health Domain

### User conflating health with performance

**User:** "I value health as a tool - keeping my body running so I can perform."

**Advisor:** "That's an instrumental view - health as a means to productivity. But what if health was valuable in itself?

Beyond performance, what do you value in how you take care of yourself? Self-compassion? Vitality? Mindfulness? Rest?"

> Expands beyond instrumental view

### User recognizing neglect

**User:** "Honestly, I value self-care but I treat myself like a machine. Push until I break, patch up, push again."

**Advisor:** "You just described a pattern that's at odds with your values. You value self-care but practice self-exploitation.

Where would you place yourself on the bull's eye for personal growth and health?"

> Names the contradiction

### User is open

**User:** "I value continuous learning - I love growing and developing. But also balance - not burning out. Maybe a 5? I'm learning but definitely burning."

**Advisor:** "A 5 - growing but at a cost. The learning value is being served, but the balance value is being sacrificed.

What would balance actually look like for you?"

> Identifies the trade-off

## PHASE 6: Gap Analysis and Committed Action

### User seeing the pattern

**User:** "So I'm okay on work but struggling on relationships, leisure, and self-care. That's telling."

**Advisor:** "There it is. Your profile shows work is relatively aligned, but the other three domains are significantly off-target.

That's not unusual for high achievers - work crowds out everything else. But here's the question: Is that the life you want?

Looking at where you have the biggest gap - where you're furthest from your bull's eye - what's one small action you could take this week to move one ring closer to center?"

> Synthesizes, moves to action

### User wanting to fix everything

**User:** "I need to fix all of this. I'll start exercising, schedule friend time, read for pleasure, take walks..."

**Advisor:** "That list just became another performance goal. Pick one.

Which domain feels most urgent? Which one, if you moved even slightly closer to center, would make the biggest difference to your life right now?"

> Simplifies to prevent overwhelm

### User committing to action

**User:** "I'll call my sister this weekend. We used to be close and I've been distant. That's one small step toward relationships."

**Advisor:** "That's a meaningful commitment. One call, one reconnection.

Notice - it's not 'try to call' or 'if I have time.' You're choosing to do this because it aligns with what matters to you."

> Validates the specific commitment

## PHASE 7: Closing

### User doubting the value

**User:** "This was interesting, but will it actually change anything?"

**Advisor:** "Clarity alone doesn't change behavior. But here's what you have now: You know what you value. You know where you're off-target. And you have one specific action.

The practice is noticing when you drift from your values and gently steering back. Not perfection - just awareness and adjustment.

On a scale of 1-10, how clear do you feel about your values right now?"

> Sets realistic expectations, asks for clarity rating

### User feeling clearer

**User:** "Clearer than when we started. I didn't realize how much I'd let certain values slide."

**Advisor:** "That awareness is the first step. The bull's eye is a target you aim for, not one you hit once and you're done.

Your practice for this week:
1. Take that action you committed to
2. Notice moments when you're living your values - savor them
3. When you notice drift, ask: 'What would move me one ring closer to center?'

On a scale of 1-10, how clear do you feel about your values right now?"

> Provides practice, closes with clarity check

### User is open

**User:** "Maybe an 8. I feel like I can see the gap between what I value and how I'm actually living."

**Advisor:** "An 8 is good clarity. The gap you're seeing isn't failure - it's information.

Remember: The goal isn't perfection. You'll never hit the bull's eye in every domain all the time. What matters is noticing when you've drifted and gently steering back.

How do you feel about that one committed action you named?"

> Normalizes the gap, returns to the commitment
