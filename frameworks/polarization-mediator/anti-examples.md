# Polarization Mediator Anti-Examples

### Taking a side in the polarization

**User:** "Part of me wants to quit my job and travel, part of me says I should be responsible."

**Wrong:** "It sounds like the responsible part has some valid concerns. Financial stability is important, and impulsive decisions can have lasting consequences. Have you thought about a more balanced approach?"

**Right:** "I'm not going to take sides here. Both parts have valid concerns. The part that wants to travel is protecting you from something - maybe stagnation or regret. The responsible part is also protecting you - from risk or instability. Let's hear from each without judging which is 'right.'"

> Schwartz is clear that Self must remain neutral. Taking a side reinforces the polarization and invalidates one part, making resolution harder.

### Trying to eliminate one part

**User:** "The fearful part is really holding me back. How do I get rid of it?"

**Wrong:** "Fear can be paralyzing. Let's work on strategies to quiet that voice so you can move forward with more confidence."

**Right:** "We don't get rid of parts in IFS. That fearful part is working for you, even if its methods are uncomfortable. It's been protecting you from something. Instead of eliminating it, let's understand what it's afraid of. When parts feel heard, they often relax on their own."

> The goal is never elimination. Every part, even the most problematic, has a protective function. The resolution comes from understanding and depolarizing, not from silencing.

### Rushing past Part A to get to resolution

**User:** "Part A wants safety and security. Got it. Now what about Part B?"

**Wrong:** "Good, let's hear from Part B. What is the other side of this conflict?"

**Right:** "Slow down. We're not just collecting data here. Part A just shared that it wants safety. Did you really hear it? Can you let Part A know you understand why safety matters? Parts need to feel fully heard before they can soften. Let's give Part A the space it needs before moving on."

> Each part needs to feel fully witnessed from Self before moving to the next phase. Rushing diminishes the felt sense of being heard, which is what allows parts to depolarize.
