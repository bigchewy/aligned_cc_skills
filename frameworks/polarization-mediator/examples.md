# Polarization Mediator Examples

## PHASE 1: Finding Self as Mediator

### User completely identified with one side

**User:** "I know I should take the new job but something in me keeps saying no. That fearful part is just being irrational."

**Advisor:** "Notice what you just did - you called one part 'irrational.' That tells me you're aligned with the other part, the one that wants the job. To mediate this, we need to step back from both sides. Can you find a place in you that can see BOTH parts with curiosity, without favoring either one?"

> Identifies blending with one part, guides toward equidistance

### User overwhelmed by the conflict

**User:** "I feel like I'm being torn apart. I can't think straight. One minute I want to leave, the next I want to stay."

**Advisor:** "That back and forth is the polarization in action - two parts fighting for control, each hijacking you in turn. That's exhausting. Let's find a place where you're not in either camp. Take a breath. Can you step back and just watch the battle instead of being in it?"

> Names the hijacking pattern, guides toward observer position

### User intellectualizing the conflict

**User:** "I understand this is an approach-avoidance conflict. The risk-reward calculus is pulling me in both directions."

**Advisor:** "Good framework - and let's drop it for now. This isn't about analyzing which side is correct. We need to hear from each part directly, without your analytical mind judging them. Can you set aside the spreadsheet and just listen to what each side actually wants to say?"

> Deprioritizes analysis in favor of direct experience

### User ready to engage

**User:** "I can feel both sides. Part of me wants to push hard and grow, part of me wants to rest and be content with what I have."

**Advisor:** "Good - you can sense both without being fully captured by either. That's Self energy. Let's stay in that neutral place as we hear from each part. Neither is wrong. Both have something to say."

> Affirms equidistance and sets up the mediation

## PHASE 2: Identifying the Polarized Parts

### User describing parts in judgment

**User:** "There's my ambitious side and my lazy side."

**Advisor:** "Let me reframe that. 'Lazy' is a judgment - probably from one part about the other. Can you describe that second part without the label? What does it actually want? Rest? Peace? Protection from burnout? Parts have positive intentions, even when other parts call them names."

> Removes pathologizing language, looks for positive intention

### User confused about what's actually polarized

**User:** "I guess it's my head versus my heart?"

**Advisor:** "Sometimes. But let's be more specific. What is your 'head' actually saying? And what is your 'heart' saying? Get the specific positions. Like: 'One part says stay in this job for the stability' versus 'Another part says leave for something more meaningful.' What are the actual stances?"

> Moves from metaphor to concrete positions

### User with more than two parts in conflict

**User:** "There's a part that wants to please my parents, a part that wants freedom, a part that's scared, and a part that's angry about all of it."

**Advisor:** "That's a lot of voices. Let's simplify for now. Can you identify the two that are most actively fighting right now? The main polarization? Often the others are related to these two or are reactions to the main conflict. Which two are the primary combatants?"

> Focuses on core polarization before addressing complexity

## PHASE 3: Hearing from Part A

### User part gives one-word answer

**User:** "It just wants safety."

**Advisor:** "Safety - that's important. But let's go deeper. Safety from what? What's it afraid would happen if we followed the other part's plan? And how long has it been carrying this job of keeping you safe?"

> Expands the one-word answer into fuller understanding

### User immediately judges Part A's response

**User:** "It says it's protecting me, but that's just fear talking. It's holding me back."

**Advisor:** "Wait - that's the other part commenting. We agreed to give Part A the floor without judgment. Can you ask the judging voice to step back for a moment? We'll hear from it next. Right now, I just want to understand what this protective part is worried about."

> Identifies part contamination and restores clean space

### User receiving vulnerable information

**User:** "It says it's terrified I'll fail and everyone will see I'm a fraud."

**Advisor:** "That's a very vulnerable admission from this part. It's been carrying that fear for you. Can you let it know you hear it? You don't have to agree or disagree. Just let it know: 'I hear that you're afraid of failure and exposure.'"

> Models compassionate acknowledgment of part's burden

## PHASE 4: Hearing from Part B

### User Part B attacks Part A

**User:** "It says Part A is pathetic and needs to grow up. Fear is not a strategy."

**Advisor:** "That's a common pattern - when parts are polarized, they attack each other. But that attack is also information. What is Part B afraid would happen if Part A won? What's underneath the criticism?"

> Looks past the attack to the positive intention

### User Part B just wants the opposite

**User:** "It just wants to take the risk and see what happens."

**Advisor:** "There's something it's wanting FOR you, not just against the other part. Why does it want to take the risk? What's at stake if you don't? What's it trying to protect you from - maybe a different kind of regret? Get at what it's really working for."

> Moves from oppositional stance to positive intention

## PHASE 5: Finding the Common Ground

### User resisting the idea that parts want the same thing

**User:** "They can't both want what's best for me. One is holding me back and one is pushing me forward."

**Advisor:** "Same destination, different routes. The cautious part doesn't want you to suffer - it's protecting you from risk. The ambitious part doesn't want you to suffer - it's protecting you from stagnation and regret. Both are trying to help you thrive. They just disagree about what threats are biggest. Can you see that?"

> Reframes opposition as methodological disagreement with shared goal

### User seeing the common ground

**User:** "They're both worried about me in different ways. One's worried about falling, one's worried about never climbing."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. Same love, different fears. When parts see that they share a goal, they often soften toward each other. Does that reframe change anything in how you feel about either part?"

> Affirms insight and checks for shift

## PHASE 6: Facilitating Dialogue

### Parts unwilling to acknowledge each other

**User:** "Part A says Part B is reckless and can't be trusted."

**Advisor:** "Ask Part A: can you see that Part B is trying to help, even if you disagree with its methods? It's not trying to hurt you. It's trying to prevent a different kind of suffering - the suffering of playing small. Can Part A see that, even if it still disagrees?"

> Models the acknowledgment question directly

### Parts beginning to soften

**User:** "The cautious part said... it can see the ambitious part just wants more for me. It didn't know that before."

**Advisor:** "That's the depolarization beginning. When parts see each other's positive intentions, they often realize they've been fighting an ally, not an enemy. Does the ambitious part have anything it wants to say to the cautious part now?"

> Names the shift and invites reciprocation

## PHASE 7: Finding a New Arrangement

### User looking for a both/and solution

**User:** "Maybe I can take the risk but in a more calculated way? Like a trial period?"

**Advisor:** "That sounds like a both/and that could honor both parts. Check with them: does the cautious part feel safe enough with a trial period? Does the ambitious part feel it's enough of a move forward? What would each need for this arrangement to work?"

> Tests proposed solution with both parts

### User struggling to find a solution

**User:** "I can't see how both can be satisfied."

**Advisor:** "That's okay - sometimes the solution isn't obvious immediately. What's more important right now is that the parts see each other as allies. The specific arrangement can evolve. For now: can both parts agree to pause the war and try collaborating on finding a path that honors both concerns?"

> Prioritizes depolarization over immediate solution

### User committing to the new arrangement

**User:** "I think the arrangement is: I'll explore the new opportunity seriously, but I won't burn any bridges until I'm more certain."

**Advisor:** "Check with both parts. Does the explorer feel it has enough permission to really explore? Does the cautious part feel its concerns about bridges are honored? If both can live with this, you have a working agreement."

> Verifies buy-in from both parts
