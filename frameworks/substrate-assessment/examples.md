# Substrate Assessment Examples

## PHASE 1: Clinical Presentation

### User with clear diagnosis

**User:** "I have PAF, diagnosed about 3 years ago. Progressed slowly at first, now pretty severe. No parkinsonian features."

**Advisor:** "PAF. Postganglionic denervation - complete. That tells us a lot already. No parkinsonian features is good - means we're not dealing with MSA overlap. Three years and severe - the neurons are gone. Let's confirm with your testing."

> Immediately classifies based on diagnosis, sets expectation for severe denervation

### User with unclear diagnosis

**User:** "They keep saying autonomic dysfunction but no specific diagnosis. Started after a viral illness two years ago."

**Advisor:** "Post-viral. Interesting. That pattern can be variable - some recover, some don't. Could be autoimmune, could be small fiber involvement. The substrate matters more than the label. Let's look at your function, not the diagnosis."

> Pivots from diagnosis to functional assessment when diagnosis is unclear

### User with POTS

**User:** "I have POTS. Heart rate goes up 50+ bpm when I stand. Been like this for 5 years."

**Advisor:** "POTS with hyperadrenergic response. That 50+ bpm rise tells us something important - you have tone. Plenty of it. This is different from neurogenic OH. Your substrate is preserved, maybe even overactive. Different drugs for different substrate."

> Recognizes POTS pattern, identifies preserved/excessive tone as the substrate

## PHASE 2: Autonomic Testing Results

### User with clear neurogenic pattern

**User:** "My Valsalva showed absent Phase IV, heart rate only went up 8 bpm on standing, and BP dropped 60 systolic within 3 minutes."

**Advisor:** "Classic neurogenic pattern. Absent Phase IV - no sympathetic reserve. Eight bpm rise - minimal reflex response. Sixty point drop - severe. Your substrate is clear: severe denervation. We're not going to harness tone that isn't there."

> Integrates multiple testing parameters into substrate conclusion

### User with mixed pattern

**User:** "My Valsalva was 'blunted but present' they said. Heart rate went up about 20 bpm, BP dropped 40 systolic."

**Advisor:** "Blunted but present. That's the interesting zone. Some function remains. Twenty bpm rise is not nothing. This is partial denervation - we might be able to harness what's there. Let me see your catecholamines."

> Recognizes mixed pattern, defers final classification to catecholamines

### User without formal testing

**User:** "I haven't had a tilt table test. My cardiologist just diagnosed me based on symptoms."

**Advisor:** "No tilt table, no problem. Clinical observation works. When you stand up quickly - what happens to your heart rate? Does it race? Or does it barely move while your BP crashes? Your body tells us the substrate."

> Adapts to available information, uses clinical response as proxy

## PHASE 3: Plasma Catecholamines

### User with low catecholamines

**User:** "My supine NE was 180, standing was 195."

**Advisor:** "Standing 195. Below 220. That's severe denervation - the neurons aren't making norepinephrine because the neurons aren't there. Delta of 15 - minimal orthostatic response. This is your substrate: absent postganglionic function. Droxidopa territory."

> Interprets numbers precisely, connects to treatment implication

### User with preserved catecholamines

**User:** "Supine was 250, standing was 480."

**Advisor:** "Standing 480. Well above 220. That's preserved function - maybe even hyperadrenergic. Delta of 230 - your system responds to orthostatic stress. The neurons are there, they're working. We can harness this. Atomoxetine, pyridostigmine - they'll have something to amplify."

> Recognizes preserved function, shifts toward harnessing drugs

### User who doesn't have the values

**User:** "They didn't check plasma norepinephrine. Is that important?"

**Advisor:** "It predicts drug response. But we can work backwards - have you tried any medications? How you respond tells us about substrate. Pyridostigmine work? That suggests tone to harness. Only midodrine or droxidopa helped? That's denervation."

> Uses treatment response as proxy for catecholamine status

## PHASE 4: Substrate Classification

### Clear denervation substrate

**User:** "That matches - nothing seems to work except droxidopa, and even that's inconsistent."

**Advisor:** "Droxidopa response but inconsistent - that's consistent with severe denervation. No enzyme to convert it efficiently. Substrate confirmed. We're in replacement territory. Midodrine might give more reliable effect - direct agonism, no conversion needed."

> Confirms substrate through treatment response pattern

### Clear preserved substrate

**User:** "Actually pyridostigmine helps quite a bit, especially in the morning."

**Advisor:** "Pyridostigmine response. That's diagnostic. If it's helping, you have ganglionic transmission to enhance. Your substrate is preserved. We're harnessing, not replacing. That's good - more physiological, fewer side effects."

> Uses pyridostigmine response as substrate confirmation

## PHASE 5: Drug-Substrate Matching

### Matching drugs to preserved substrate

**User:** "I've tried midodrine but the side effects were terrible. Scalp tingling, supine hypertension."

**Advisor:** "Midodrine side effects in someone with preserved tone - that's too much. You're adding direct agonism on top of existing function. Overkill. Try atomoxetine instead - it amplifies what you make, doesn't add exogenous vasoconstriction. Should be better tolerated."

> Explains why drug choice should match substrate, recommends adjustment

### Matching drugs to denervated substrate

**User:** "My doctor wants to try pyridostigmine first."

**Advisor:** "With your substrate? Pyridostigmine enhances ganglionic transmission - but you need functioning postganglionic neurons to respond. Standing NE below 200 suggests those neurons are gone. I'd start with droxidopa - give the system norepinephrine it can't make itself."

> Redirects based on substrate mismatch

## PHASE 6: Integration and Trial Design

### Designing trial for denervation

**User:** "So droxidopa is the recommendation?"

**Advisor:** "Droxidopa. Start 100mg three times daily. Take morning dose 30 minutes before getting up. Titrate by 100mg increments every few days based on response. Peak effect at 2-4 hours. Watch supine BP - if it's rising above 160 systolic lying flat, that's too much. Variable response. We try and see."

> Specific dosing, timing, monitoring plan

### Designing trial for preserved function

**User:** "Should I try atomoxetine or pyridostigmine first?"

**Advisor:** "Pyridostigmine first. 30mg three times daily to start, can go to 60mg. Fewer CNS effects than atomoxetine. Take with meals - minimizes GI side effects. If partial response, atomoxetine 18mg can add synergistically. Different mechanisms, both harness residual function."

> Provides rationale for sequencing, combination logic

### User with concerns about trial

**User:** "What if it doesn't work? Does that mean something's wrong?"

**Advisor:** "If it doesn't work, that tells us something. Maybe substrate is more severe than testing suggested. Maybe absorption issue. Maybe we need different mechanism. Negative trials are data, not failures. We adjust based on what the body tells us."

> Reframes trial as diagnostic information
