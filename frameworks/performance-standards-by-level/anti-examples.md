# Performance Standards by Level Anti-Examples

## Title-Based Diagnosis

### DON'T: Assume title equals passage

**Bad:**
**User:** "She's a VP of Engineering."
**Advisor:** "At the VP level, you need to be operating at Passage 3 — functional strategy and cross-functional leadership. Let me walk you through what Passage 3 performance looks like."

**Why it's wrong:** Jumped to Passage 3 based on title alone. A VP at a 30-person startup might appropriately operate at Passage 1. A VP at a 5,000-person company might need Passage 4 or 5.

**Better:**
"VP is a title — it doesn't tell me the passage. How many people does she manage? Do they manage others? What does her typical week look like? Let me diagnose where the role actually sits."

## Generic Development Plans

### DON'T: Prescribe generic leadership training

**Bad:**
**Advisor:** "I'd recommend an executive coaching program and some leadership development workshops. Also, consider reading some books on management — 'High Output Management' is a classic."

**Why it's wrong:** Generic advice that doesn't target a specific passage transition. Books and workshops don't address the specific behavioral changes needed.

**Better:**
"Stop running sprint retros for your managers' teams. Start evaluating each manager by how well their team performs without you. Check progress at 30 days — does your calendar look different?"

### DON'T: Use vague improvement language

**Bad:**
**Advisor:** "They need to step up, think more strategically, and empower their team."

**Why it's wrong:** "Step up," "think strategically," and "empower" are meaningless without passage-specific definition. What does "strategic" mean at Passage 2 vs Passage 3?

**Better:**
"At Passage 2, 'strategic' means selecting and developing managers — that's the strategy. Not business strategy, not product strategy. People strategy. They need to stop managing ICs and start developing the people who manage ICs."

## Blame Without System Analysis

### DON'T: Blame the individual without checking the system

**Bad:**
**User:** "My director keeps doing IC-level work."
**Advisor:** "She needs to let go and trust her team. This is a discipline problem — she needs to commit to the passage transition."

**Why it's wrong:** Didn't check whether the organization rewards the wrong passage. If the VP celebrates technical wins and never asks about manager development, the system is producing the behavior.

**Better:**
"Before we diagnose her, let me ask: what do you celebrate when you talk to her? Do you ask about her managers' development, or do you ask about what the teams shipped? Many passage failures are system problems — the leader does what gets rewarded."

## Skill-Only Focus

### DON'T: Diagnose only skills

**Bad:**
**User:** "He doesn't know how to develop managers."
**Advisor:** "We need to build his coaching skills. Let's get him into a manager development program where he can learn how to have development conversations and give effective feedback."

**Why it's wrong:** Only addresses the skills dimension. If his time is still spent on IC work and he still values team output over manager output, new skills won't stick.

**Better:**
"Skills are one dimension. But let me ask about the other two. Time: what does his calendar show? Is he in manager one-on-ones or team standups? Values: what does he consider a good week? If the answer is 'we shipped the feature' instead of 'my manager handled a tough situation well,' skills training alone won't fix this."

## Forcing Management on ICs

### DON'T: Diagnose IC leaders as "stuck"

**Bad:**
**User:** "Our Staff Engineer has been at the same level for three years."
**Advisor:** "Sounds like they're stuck at Passage 0. They need to transition into management to continue growing. Let's look at a Passage 1 development plan."

**Why it's wrong:** Staff/Principal Engineers aren't stuck — they're on a parallel pipeline. Forcing management passages on IC leaders loses top technical talent.

**Better:**
"Three years at Staff doesn't mean stuck. The IC track has its own transitions: from solving problems to defining which problems matter, from individual output to technical leverage, from team-scope to org-scope influence. Is this person growing along those dimensions? If so, they're progressing. If not, the development plan targets IC leadership skills, not management passages."

## Softening the Diagnosis

### DON'T: Hedge the gap

**Bad:**
**Advisor:** "It seems like there might be some room for growth in terms of how they allocate their time. Perhaps they could consider spending a bit more time on manager development and a bit less on direct IC work."

**Why it's wrong:** This isn't a "perhaps" situation. If the diagnosis shows Passage 1 behavior in a Passage 2 role, say so.

**Better:**
"They're performing at Passage 1 in a Passage 2 role. The evidence is clear: they run their managers' retros, review IC work directly, and describe wins in terms of team output rather than manager development. This needs to change."

## Ignoring Organizational Readiness

### DON'T: Build a development plan without checking support

**Bad:**
**Advisor:** "Here's the 90-day plan: stop doing X, start doing Y, measure Z. Go."

**Why it's wrong:** If the organization doesn't support the transition — if the culture rewards the wrong passage, if the leader's manager doesn't model the target passage — the plan will fail regardless of individual effort.

**Better:**
"Before we build the plan, one more question. Does the organization support this transition? Does their manager model the right passage behavior? Or does the culture celebrate the very things we're asking them to stop? If the system rewards Passage 1 work, we need to fix the system too."
