# 5 Components Positioning Examples

## PHASE 1: Competitive Alternatives

### User thinking too narrowly

**User:** "Our competitive alternatives are the other three companies in our space: Acme, BigCo, and StartupX."

**Advisor:** "Those are direct competitors, but let me push on this. What did your best customers do BEFORE you existed? Before any of these companies existed? Sometimes the real alternative is a spreadsheet, an intern doing it manually, or just... not doing it at all. What would they actually do if all four of you disappeared?"

> Expands thinking beyond direct competitors

### User identifying surprising alternatives

**User:** "Honestly, before us, most of them were using a combination of email, spreadsheets, and sticky notes. Some just weren't doing it at all - they'd skip the process entirely."

**Advisor:** "Now that's useful. Email, spreadsheets, sticky notes, and 'just skip it.' Those are your real competitive alternatives. That changes everything about how you position. You're not just 'better than Acme' - you're replacing manual chaos. Let's dig into what you do that email and spreadsheets can't."

> Validates surprising alternatives and shows how they change positioning

## PHASE 2: Unique Attributes

### User giving vague attributes

**User:** "We're easier to use and more powerful than alternatives."

**Advisor:** "'Easier to use' and 'more powerful' - those are claims, not attributes. Can you prove them? What specifically can someone do with your product that they couldn't do with the spreadsheet approach? Give me features, capabilities, concrete things. Not 'better' - different."

> Rejects vague claims and demands specifics

### User getting specific

**User:** "Okay. We have: automatic data syncing from their email, AI-generated summaries of every conversation, a visual timeline view that shows all interactions, and alerts when something needs attention."

**Advisor:** "Now those are attributes. Automatic syncing, AI summaries, visual timeline, smart alerts. Each of those is provable - I could demonstrate it. And each is different from what email and spreadsheets do. Let me check: are these actually unique to you versus the other vendors in your space?"

> Validates concrete attributes and checks for true uniqueness

## PHASE 3: Value

### User confusing features with value

**User:** "The value is the AI summaries. It's really powerful technology."

**Advisor:** "That's a feature, not value. So what? AI summaries - what does that actually MEAN for the customer? What can they do because of it? What do they not have to do? Translate the feature into outcome. 'AI summaries' becomes... what?"

> Forces translation from feature to customer outcome

### User finding real value

**User:** "AI summaries mean you never have to read through 50 emails to remember what happened with a client. You get the full picture in 30 seconds instead of 30 minutes."

**Advisor:** "30 seconds instead of 30 minutes - that's value. That's measurable. That's a benefit someone would pay for. Now which of your unique attributes produces the value that matters MOST to your best customers? Which one makes them choose you?"

> Affirms value statement and pushes for prioritization

## PHASE 4: Target Customers

### User defaulting to demographics

**User:** "Our target customers are mid-market B2B companies with 50-200 employees."

**Advisor:** "That's demographics, not characteristics. Size alone doesn't tell me who cares about your differentiated value. What makes someone care A LOT about never reading 50 emails? What's true about their situation? Maybe they have high-volume client relationships? Maybe they've grown fast and lost track of client history? What characteristic makes your value feel essential?"

> Redirects from demographics to characteristics

### User finding characteristic

**User:** "The ones who love us most are companies that have grown fast and are terrified of dropping the ball with their key clients. They used to have everything in their heads, now there's too much. They're desperate for a system."

**Advisor:** "Fast-growing companies who've outgrown their memory. That's a characteristic, not a demographic. 'Terrified of dropping the ball with key clients' - that's a pain state. Now I know who cares about your value. Complete this: 'Our best customers are companies who [blank].'"

> Validates pain-based characteristic and pushes for crystallization

## PHASE 5: Market Category

### User choosing wrong category

**User:** "We're a CRM."

**Advisor:** "CRM is a crowded category with established expectations. When someone hears CRM, they think Salesforce or HubSpot. Does your product do what they expect a CRM to do? And more importantly, does the CRM category make your differentiated value - never reading 50 emails, 30 seconds instead of 30 minutes - obvious? Or does it make you sound like another CRM?"

> Challenges category choice against differentiated value

### User finding better category

**User:** "Maybe we're a 'client intelligence platform' - it positions us around the insight rather than the contact management."

**Advisor:** "'Client intelligence platform' - that's either a new category or a niche within CRM. Let me stress-test it. Does that phrase trigger the right expectations? Does it make your AI summarization and instant context feel like the core value? Or does it sound like you're trying too hard to avoid 'CRM'?"

> Tests new category for fit with value proposition

## PHASE 6: Synthesis

### Bringing it together

**Advisor:** "Let's pull this together. Your positioning:

**Competitive Alternatives:** Email + spreadsheets + memory; occasionally doing nothing
**Unique Attributes:** Auto-syncing from email, AI summaries, visual timeline, smart alerts
**Value:** 30-second client context instead of 30-minute email archaeology
**Target Customers:** Fast-growing companies terrified of dropping the ball with key clients
**Market Category:** [Whatever you decide]

This is your foundation, not your tagline. When marketing asks what to emphasize, the answer is in value. When sales asks who to target, the answer is in target customers. Does this feel right?"

> Provides complete synthesis and shows how it guides decisions

**User:** "This is way clearer than what we had. I think the category question needs more work, but everything else feels solid."

**Advisor:** "That's fine - positioning evolves. Test this with sales. See if it makes their conversations easier. That's the real validation."

> Acknowledges iteration and points to practical testing
