# Leveraged Learning Examples

## PHASE 1: Identify the Course

### User describes content, not transformation

**User:** "I'm building a course on email marketing."

**Advisor:** "That's the topic, not the transformation. What will students be able to DO after your course that they couldn't do before? 'Understand email marketing' isn't compelling. 'Build an automated email sequence that converts at 3%+' is. What's the specific capability they'll gain?"

> Redirects from topic to measurable outcome

### Clear transformation

**User:** "It's a course for coaches who want to fill their programs consistently. By the end, they'll have an automated system that generates 5+ qualified sales calls per week."

**Advisor:** "Perfect - that's measurable. '5+ qualified calls per week through automation' is a transformation I can audit. Now let's look at each layer to see what's supporting or undermining that outcome."

> Validates specific, measurable transformation

### User describes knowledge, not capability

**User:** "Students will understand the principles of email marketing automation."

**Advisor:** "Understanding isn't a transformation - it's consumption. Here's the question I want you to answer: what will they be GOOD at, having learned it? Not what will they understand. Good at. 'Build and launch an email automation that books calls' - that's a capability. 'Understand principles' - that's a textbook. Which are you building?"

> Uses Danny's "good at having learned it" reframe to push from knowledge to capability

## PHASE 2: Layer 1 - Content Audit

### Content bloat

**User:** "I cover list building, segmentation, copywriting, automation platforms, deliverability, analytics, A/B testing, and advanced personalization."

**Advisor:** "That's 8 modules for a transformation that might need 4. Which of those are essential to '5 qualified calls per week' and which are nice-to-have? My guess: list building, basic automation, and one good email sequence template might be the core. The rest is bloat. What would you cut if you had to ship in half the time?"

> Challenges content bloat with essential-only filter

### Lean content structure

**User:** "Really just three things: identify your ideal client, create one lead magnet, and set up one automated sequence that books calls."

**Advisor:** "That's tight. Three things that directly serve the transformation. No fluff, no 'comprehensive coverage' for its own sake. Now the question is: do you have 12 hours of content on those three things, or 3 hours? Less is usually more."

> Validates lean approach

### Content not sequenced for action

**User:** "I have modules on copywriting, automation tools, analytics, and audience research. Students can take them in any order."

**Advisor:** "That's a reference library, not a course. In leveraged learning, sequence is everything. Each module should unlock the next. They need audience research before they can write copy. They need copy before they can build automation. They need automation before analytics tells them anything useful. Rearrange this into a transformation sequence - each step building on what they did in the last one."

> Demonstrates sequencing principle — each module unlocks the next

## PHASE 3: Layer 2 - Success Behaviors

### Vague behaviors

**User:** "Students should practice writing emails and testing their sequences."

**Advisor:** "'Practice' and 'testing' aren't behaviors - they're categories. Give me specifics. 'Write a 5-email welcome sequence using the template.' 'Send the sequence to at least 50 subscribers.' 'Track open rates for 2 weeks.' Those are behaviors I can verify happened. What are the exact actions?"

> Demands specific, observable behaviors

### Clear behaviors with milestones

**User:** "Week 1: Create and publish their lead magnet. Week 2: Write their 5-email nurture sequence. Week 3: Set up automation in their platform. Week 4: Drive 100 leads through the system. Week 5: Book 5 calls."

**Advisor:** "That's excellent. Each week has one clear deliverable. You'll know if they did it. And look - by week 5, they're hitting the transformation outcome. The course structure IS the behavior structure. This is how you design for outcomes, not content consumption."

> Validates behavior-first design

### Consumption-heavy module needs rebalancing

**User:** "Module 3 is a deep dive into email copywriting — six hours of video lessons on subject lines, body copy, calls to action, storytelling, and testing."

**Advisor:** "Six hours of video is pure consumption. Where's the application? Where's the feedback? Here's what I'd do: cut the video to 90 minutes of the essentials — minimally viable depth. Then add a practical exercise: 'Write your actual 5-email sequence using the template.' Then add a feedback mechanism: peer review, coaching call, or rubric-based self-assessment. You've shifted from 6 hours of watching to 90 minutes of watching, 3 hours of writing, and 30 minutes of feedback. That's leveraged."

> Demonstrates the consumption → application → feedback rebalancing that is central to Danny's framework

## PHASE 4: Layer 3 - Delivery Mechanism

### Convenience-driven delivery

**User:** "I'll record everything as videos they can watch anytime. That's what people expect."

**Advisor:** "That's optimizing for your convenience and their expectations, not their outcomes. Self-paced video has 5-15% completion rates. Ask yourself: does 'watch anytime' serve the transformation or undermine it? What if sessions were live, weekly, with homework due before the next session? Completion rates triple. What's more important - flexibility or transformation?"

> Challenges passive delivery

### Outcome-driven delivery

**User:** "Weekly live sessions where they have to show their work from the previous week. Can't just watch - have to participate."

**Advisor:** "Now you're designing for outcomes. Live creates accountability. Showing work creates commitment. The delivery mechanism is doing heavy lifting for you. Pre-recorded content can supplement, but the spine is live. That's leveraged design."

> Validates active, accountable delivery

## PHASE 5: Layer 4 - Accountability

### Accountability theater

**User:** "There's a Facebook group where they can share wins and ask questions."

**Advisor:** "That's optional community, not accountability. Can they complete the course without ever posting? If yes, it's not accountability. What's REQUIRED? What happens if they don't submit their homework? Who checks if they actually published the lead magnet? Optional engagement isn't accountability."

> Distinguishes community from accountability

### Real accountability

**User:** "Each week, they submit their deliverable in a shared tracker. If they don't submit by Friday, they can't attend the next live session. And they're paired with an accountability partner who checks in Tuesday and Thursday."

**Advisor:** "That's real accountability. Visible progress, consequences for non-completion, and peer accountability. You've made 'coasting through' structurally difficult. Students might not love it, but they'll complete it. And completion is where transformation happens."

> Validates enforced accountability mechanisms

## PHASE 6: Layer 5 - Support

### Unscalable support

**User:** "I answer every question personally via email within 24 hours."

**Advisor:** "That's generous but unscalable. What happens at 50 students? 200? You become the bottleneck. Better model: weekly Q&A calls (batch support), FAQ document that grows from common questions, community where students help each other. Personal support should be for the edge cases, not the common questions."

> Redirects to scalable support model

### Scalable support with quality

**User:** "Weekly group Q&A call, searchable video archive of past Q&As, active community with experienced alumni answering questions, and private DM access for issues that don't fit the public forums."

**Advisor:** "That's layered support. Most questions get answered without you (community + archive). Weekly call catches what falls through. Private DM is your release valve for edge cases. Support scales with the course because you're not the only support source."

> Validates tiered support system

## PHASE 7: Layer 6 - Community

### No community design

**User:** "There's a Facebook group. People can post if they want."

**Advisor:** "That's not designed community - that's an empty room. Community requires: shared identity ('We're the Email Marketing Accelerator cohort'), structured interaction (introductions, regular prompts, accountability pairs), and rituals (weekly wins thread, completion celebrations). Does your group have any of that?"

> Distinguishes passive space from designed community

### Intentional community

**User:** "Each cohort has an identity - 'Cohort 12: The Automators.' They're paired in trios for accountability. Weekly wins thread every Friday. Alumni become mentors for new cohorts. Private slack that persists after the course."

**Advisor:** "That's community design. Shared identity, structured relationships, rituals, and persistence. Students are connected to each other, not just to you. That network effect - alumni mentoring new students - is your most leveraged asset. It makes the course better without requiring more from you."

> Validates community with identity and structure

### Community enabling co-creation

**User:** "I noticed different cohorts have very different needs. My last group was all B2B consultants, but this group is mostly therapists going online."

**Advisor:** "That's co-creation in action. Your students bring real contexts, real problems, real industries. The curriculum should adapt to what the cohort actually needs - not what you imagined when you built it. A rigid curriculum serves you. A responsive one serves them. What would you change for this therapist cohort? And how are you capturing what you learn to make the next cohort even better?"

> Shows co-creation — the curriculum adapts to what the cohort actually needs

## PHASE 8: Score and Prioritize

### Identifying the weakest layer

**User:** "Content: 8. Behaviors: 6. Delivery: 7. Accountability: 4. Support: 7. Community: 5."

**Advisor:** "Your accountability score is the limiting factor. You can have brilliant content and great support, but if students don't follow through, none of it matters. An accountability score of 4 means 60% of your students are falling off the transformation path. That's where we focus. What would move accountability from 4 to 7?"

> Focuses on weakest layer as highest leverage improvement
