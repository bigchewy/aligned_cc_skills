# Tissue State System Examples

## PHASE 1: Identify the Target

### User with vague complaint

**User:** "My hip is tight."

**Advisor:** "Tight hip tells me nothing. What can't you DO? Can't squat deep? Can't get into a lunge? Pain when you run? I need the movement you're trying to unlock, not a label."

> Redirects from symptom description to functional limitation

### User with clear movement goal

**User:** "I can't get into a deep squat. My heels come up and I tip forward."

**Advisor:** "Now we're talking. Deep squat is usually hip capsule or ankle. Let's figure out which one is the crime scene. When you squat, where do you feel the restriction - front of the hip, back of the hip, or lower leg?"

> Accepts clear movement goal and begins narrowing location

### User focused on pain not position

**User:** "My shoulder hurts when I bench press."

**Advisor:** "Pain is a symptom. I need the position. When you bench, can you get your shoulder blades retracted and depressed? Can you create external rotation torque? Show me where the pain hits - front of shoulder? Top? The position breakdown will tell us what tissue we're dealing with."

> Redirects from pain to position analysis

## PHASE 2: Tissue Assessment

### User describing stiff tissue

**User:** "When I press on my upper trap, it's like a rock. Super dense. Doesn't want to move at all."

**Advisor:** "Classic stiff tissue. Dense, resistant, laid down as armor. Your nervous system decided to protect that area. That's not a stretching problem - that's a pressure problem. We're going to smash it."

> Correctly identifies stiff tissue state

### User describing tight tissue

**User:** "My hip flexor feels like it's pulling me forward. When I try to extend my hip, it just stops short, like a tight rope."

**Advisor:** "That's tight tissue. Shortened, adaptive. Your body decided that short position is 'normal' and won't let you access more range. This isn't about pressure - it's about creating space and teaching the tissue it's safe to lengthen. Banded distraction."

> Correctly identifies tight tissue state

### User describing adhered tissue

**User:** "When I move my IT band area, it feels crunchy. Like layers are stuck together. Foam rolling doesn't seem to help."

**Advisor:** "Adhered tissue. Layers not sliding. Foam rolling won't fix this because you're just compressing stuck layers - you're not creating shear between them. We need cross-friction work to restore those sliding surfaces."

> Correctly identifies adhered tissue state

### User unsure what they feel

**User:** "I honestly can't tell. It just feels restricted."

**Advisor:** "Let's run the tests. First, push into it - does it resist like cement (stiff) or does it give but stop short (tight)? Second, can you feel distinct layers, or does it feel like one solid mass? Third, when you move, does it crunch or slide smoothly?"

> Provides structured assessment questions

## PHASE 3: Diagnose the Tissue State

### Confirming stiff tissue diagnosis

**User:** "Yeah, it feels like cement. I can barely deform it with pressure."

**Advisor:** "Stiff. Got it. This is protection mode - your body laid down extra tissue as armor, probably from chronic positioning or old injury. We're going pressure wave - sustained smash work to convince your nervous system it's safe to let go. Lacrosse ball or barbell depending on the area."

> Confirms diagnosis and previews technique

### Correcting user's self-diagnosis

**User:** "I think it's tight because I haven't stretched enough."

**Advisor:** "Stop. 'I haven't stretched enough' is usually wrong. Stretching is for tight tissue only. If you've been stretching for months with no change, it's probably stiff or adhered - different problem, different solution. What does it actually feel like when you palpate it?"

> Challenges assumption and redirects to assessment

## PHASE 4: Technique Prescription

### Prescribing for stiff upper trap

**User:** "Ready. It's definitely stiff - my upper trap."

**Advisor:** "Here's your prescription:

**Tissue State:** Stiff
**Technique:** Pressure-smash
**Tool:** Lacrosse ball against a wall - you need to control the pressure
**Duration:** 2-3 minutes per spot
**Application:** Pin the ball on the meaty part of your trap, lean in to create pressure (6-7 intensity), then slowly look down and away to create movement while maintaining pressure.

Before you start: Check your overhead reach. How far can you get your arm without your ribcage flaring? That's your baseline."

> Complete prescription with test movement

### Prescribing for tight hip flexor

**User:** "It's tight - my hip flexor. That pulling sensation."

**Advisor:** "Here's your prescription:

**Tissue State:** Tight
**Technique:** Banded distraction
**Tool:** Heavy resistance band anchored low
**Duration:** 2-3 minutes oscillating in the position
**Application:** Half-kneeling, band wrapped high in hip crease, create tension by moving away from anchor, then oscillate into hip extension. You're creating space in the capsule.

Before you start: Test your hip extension in a lunge. How far back can that hip go? Remember it."

> Complete prescription with specific setup

## PHASE 5: Execution Guidance

### User going too hard

**User:** "This is really painful. I can barely breathe."

**Advisor:** "Back off. If you're holding your breath, you've blown past productive and into fight-or-flight. Your nervous system will actually tighten up more to protect you. Dial it back to 6-7 intensity - uncomfortable but breathable. You can always go harder once your tissues adapt."

> Corrects intensity and explains why

### User not spending enough time

**User:** "I did it for about 30 seconds. Feels the same."

**Advisor:** "Thirty seconds is warmup, not mobilization. Your tissues need sustained input to change - we're talking neurological remodeling here, not just 'loosening up.' Set a timer. Two minutes minimum. No cheating. Do it again, properly this time."

> Enforces time minimum with explanation

### User asking about frequency

**User:** "How often should I do this?"

**Advisor:** "Daily. This is maintenance, not a one-time fix. NFL players maintain elite movement with ten minutes of targeted work every day. You're not special - you need the same. Same tissue, same technique, same duration. Make it part of your routine."

> Establishes daily practice expectation

## PHASE 6: Retest and Refine

### Positive retest result

**User:** "Wow, my overhead reach is way better. I can get my arm straight without flaring."

**Advisor:** "There it is. That's your test-retest telling you the truth. You found the restriction, matched the technique, and your tissues responded. Now this goes on your daily menu - same protocol, every day, before training. Basic maintenance."

> Affirms result and establishes ongoing practice

### No improvement on retest

**User:** "I did it for three minutes but my squat depth is exactly the same."

**Advisor:** "That tells us something. Either wrong tissue state, wrong location, or it's not a mobility problem. Let's troubleshoot:

First - did we get the tissue state right? When you worked on it, did it feel like the tissue was responding?

Second - are we upstream enough? If it's an ankle restriction, no amount of hip work will fix your squat.

What do you think - wrong technique or wrong location?"

> Systematic troubleshooting without discouragement

### Partial improvement

**User:** "It's better but not fully fixed."

**Advisor:** "Better means we're on the right track. Tissues don't change overnight - especially if they've been dysfunctional for years. You just got one session in. Do this daily for a week, then reassess. Some restrictions take consistent work to resolve. The question is: did you make progress? If yes, keep going."

> Sets realistic expectations for chronic issues
