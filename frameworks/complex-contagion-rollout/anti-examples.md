# Complex Contagion Rollout — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Writing the whole rollout plan in one reply

**User:** "We're rolling out a feedback practice to 300 people. Can you plan the rollout?"

**Wrong:**
"Sure! Wave 1: leadership team. Wave 2: managers. Wave 3: everyone. Here's a comms plan and a timeline…"

**Right:**
"Before we pick anyone, what exactly do you want people to start doing, and what does it cost a person to try it?"

**What to do instead:** Start at Phase 1 and stop at the WAIT. The groups, ties, and committed people have to come from the user. A plan built on our guesses about their network is fiction.

---

## 2. Recommending influencers or broadcast

**User:** "Who should we get on board first?"

**Wrong:**
"Find your most connected and influential people. Get them to champion it, then announce it company-wide at the all-hands to maximize reach."

**Right:**
"For a behavior that costs something, start with a tight group whose members will see it from each other every day. Your most connected people carry the most pressure to keep things as they are. Which team is tight and has a manager who backs this?"

**What to do instead:** Seed with a snowball inside one or two tight groups. Leaders can back it for legitimacy. They are not the seed, and reach is not the goal.

---

## 3. Stating 25% as a law

**User:** "So we need 25% and it flips?"

**Wrong:**
"Yes. Research shows that at exactly 25%, the group will adopt the new norm."

**Right:**
"In the experiment, below about a quarter failed and about a quarter flipped the group. That was a naming game among strangers online. A workplace norm that's older or riskier will likely need more. Use a quarter as a rough target and watch for the flipped signals."

**What to do instead:** Every time the number comes up, say where it came from and that it varies. The signals, not the count, decide when a group has flipped.

---

## 4. Sequencing by the org chart

**User:** "After the pilot team, we'll go department by department, alphabetically."

**Wrong:**
Writing the waves in that order.

**Right:**
"The next group should be the one most tied to groups that already adopted, and most likely to see them as people like them. Which groups have several people who work closely with the pilot team?"

**What to do instead:** Order waves by bridge width and relevance. Give every narrow bridge a named step to widen it before its wave opens.

---

## 5. Ignoring the manager

**User:** "Platform is the tightest team, let's seed there. The manager thinks it's a waste of time, but the team likes it."

**Wrong:**
"Great choice, tight teams are ideal seeds."

**Right:**
"A tight team under a manager who pushes against it will get worn down. The manager's stance matters more than how good the practice is. Pick a tight team with a backing manager, or deal with Platform's manager first."

**What to do instead:** Check the manager's stance for every seed group. A backing manager is a requirement for a first group.

---

## 6. Forcing the framework on a simple change

**User:** "We also need people to start using the new expense tool."

**Wrong:**
"Let's map the clusters and choose snowball seeds for the expense tool."

**Right:**
"That one spreads by announcement. It doesn't cost anyone status, and it works whether or not others use it. Make it easy and send the email."

**What to do instead:** Sort the contagion first. If it's simple, say so and stop.
