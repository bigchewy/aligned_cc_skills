# Launch Bet — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. It is not a real launch. A solo founder is launching a booking tool for small physical therapy clinics. The first launch is one clinic and its twelve front-desk staff, who book patients by phone today.

---

## Phase 1: Name the Bet

### Golden path

**User:** "We bet the front desk will book patients through the tool instead of by phone, because it saves them the call-back. I'm about 60% sure. First customer could be my friend's clinic, or a clinic across town that just lost a receptionist."

**Say something like:**
"Good, that's a bet I can settle. Sixty percent means you think there's a real chance it doesn't work, which is honest. Now imagine it's three weeks after launch and it worked. Walk backward: what happened on the way?"

**Why it works:** The bet names who acts and what they do, so it can be counted. The confidence is a number, so it can move later.

### Struggle: a bet about feelings

**User:** "The bet is that the staff will love it."

**Say something like:**
"Love it how? If I said 'wanna bet they love it,' how would we settle it on day fourteen? What will they do that you can count?"

**Why it works:** "Wanna bet?" turns a feeling into something with a payoff. The user has to name a behavior.

---

## Phase 2: The Premortem

### Golden path

**User:** "It failed because the login confused people, the calendar sync broke, two patients got double-booked, and the clinic manager went on vacation."

**Say something like:**
"Let's sort those. The login, the sync, and the double-booking are yours. The vacation isn't. Which of yours could embarrass the clinic? A patient showing up to a slot someone else has is the one I'd worry about. So the checklist gets: login tested by someone who's never seen it, the sync checked against a real calendar for a week, and no double-booking possible. And the monkey is whether people who've booked by phone for years will switch at all. The reporting dashboard is the pedestal. Test the monkey first."

**Why it works:** The premortem ends in a precommitment, the checklist, and the hard unknown gets tested before the easy part.

### Edge case: nothing goes in the "you control" pile

**User:** "Honestly, if it fails it's because front desks are slammed."

**Say something like:**
"Maybe. But you choose how long the first booking takes, when you train them, and how many nudges there are. Busy people still do short things. What did you control about how much of their time you asked for?"

**Why it works:** It doesn't let the user file everything under luck before the launch even starts.

---

## Phase 3: The Lines, as a State and a Date

### Golden path

**User:** "Success is 8 of the 12 staff each booking at least five patients through the tool by day 14. Quit line is fewer than 4. The early signal is how many book one patient in the first three days."

**Say something like:**
"Those are real lines. Between 4 and 8 is too early to tell. What one fact would settle it? Say, whether the people who stopped hit the sync problem. And when do you know? Write all three in the decisions file now. They don't move after the build starts."

**Why it works:** Counts, a date, an early signal, and the middle zone has its own deciding fact.

### Struggle: reaching for a rate

**User:** "I want a 70% adoption rate."

**Say something like:**
"Out of twelve people, that's eight and a bit. Say eight. A rate makes it sound like a trend, and one clinic isn't a trend."

**Why it works:** It keeps the number in single people, where the founder can picture each one.

---

## Phase 4: Pick and Recruit the First Customer

### Golden path

**User:** "My friend runs the first clinic, so she'll be nice. The clinic across town is short a receptionist, their busy season starts in March, and the manager is blunt."

**Say something like:**
"Recruiting isn't my method. This step borrows Rob Fitzpatrick's commitment test. The clinic across town has a real need with a date on it, enough bookings for your number, and they'll tell you what went wrong. Your friend's a fine backup, but write down that you'll discount her kindness. Now ask them for their staff list, access to their calendar, and a start date. If they hand it over by Friday, they're your customer. If not, your friend."

**Why it works:** The pick is about how readable the result will be. The customer gives something up, and recruiting has its own state and date.

---

## Phase 5: Read the Result Against the Lines

### Golden path

**User:** "By day 14, 6 of the 12 booked five or more."

**Say something like:**
"You wrote 8 for success and 4 for the quit line. Six is too early to tell. The fact you said would settle it was whether the people who stopped hit the sync problem. Did they?"

**Why it works:** The lines are read back word for word before the result is judged.

### Edge case: no lines were written

**User:** "I never wrote a number down. 5 of 11 used it."

**Say something like:**
"Then I can't grade this against a line, and I won't invent one now. That's resulting waiting to happen. We'll look at the decisions instead, and the next customer gets lines before anything is built."

**Why it works:** It refuses to set the line after seeing the result.

---

## Phase 6: Separate the Decision from the Luck

### Golden path

**User:** "Three people logged in once and went back to the phone. And two staff were out sick for a week."

**Say something like:**
"The sick week is luck. The three who logged in and went back are yours: something about the first booking stopped them. So the result is a decent decision that took some bad luck, with one thing you controlled that cost you. Where's your confidence now? You were at 60."

**Why it works:** Both piles get filled before the result is called anything.

### Struggle: taking credit for a good result

**User:** "We hit 10! The product works."

**Say something like:**
"Great. Now, the clinic manager told everyone at the Monday meeting that the phone was off-limits. Imagine she hadn't. Would you still be at 10? If not, some of that was luck you can't count on next time."

**Why it works:** A good result gets the same check as a bad one.

---

## Phase 7: Decide

### Golden path

**User:** "Change one thing: make the first booking take two clicks instead of six. Then run a second clinic."

**Say something like:**
"One named change. Write the second clinic's state and date before they start. Same lines, or did this hand teach you something about the number?"

**Why it works:** The decision ends in the next bet, written down before it's played.

### Edge case: wants to quit after landing between the lines

**User:** "6 of 12 feels like a flop. I'm done."

**Say something like:**
"Is one hand enough to know whether you have an edge? You set the quit line at 4. You're above it. Go get the fact you named, by its date, and then decide."

**Why it works:** The precommitted line beats the feeling of the moment.
