# Launch Bet — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. It is not a real launch. A solo founder is launching a site where a couple's friends and family write illustrated memories of them, delivered a few a day before the wedding. The first launch is one couple and about fifty of their people. Replace this with the real first launch once it has happened.

---

## Phase 1: Name the Bet

### Golden path

**User:** "We bet that the couple's people will write memories because they want to give them something better than a toast. I'm about 60% sure. First customer could be my cousin Sam and her fiancé, or a couple from my running club."

**Say something like:**
"Good, that's a bet I can settle. Sixty percent means you think there's a real chance it doesn't work, which is honest. Now imagine it's three weeks after launch and it worked. Walk backward: what happened on the way?"

**Why it works:** The bet names who acts and what they do, so it can be counted. The confidence is a number, so it can move later.

### Struggle: a bet about feelings

**User:** "The bet is that people will love it."

**Say something like:**
"Love it how? If I said 'wanna bet they love it,' how would we settle it on day fourteen? What will they do that you can count?"

**Why it works:** "Wanna bet?" turns a feeling into something with a payoff. The user has to name a behavior.

---

## Phase 2: The Premortem

### Golden path

**User:** "It failed because the invite email went to spam, people didn't know what to write, the pictures came out weird, and the wedding got postponed."

**Say something like:**
"Let's sort those. Spam, the blank page, and the pictures are yours. The postponed wedding isn't. Which of yours could embarrass the couple? A weird picture of grandma delivered to the bride is the one I'd worry about. So the checklist gets: invite tested on Gmail and Outlook, a writing prompt on the first screen, and every picture seen by you before it's delivered. And the monkey is whether fifty people will write at all. The homepage polish is the pedestal. Test the monkey first."

**Why it works:** The premortem ends in a precommitment, the checklist, and the hard unknown gets tested before the easy part.

### Edge case: nothing goes in the "you control" pile

**User:** "Honestly, if it fails it's because people are busy."

**Say something like:**
"Maybe. But you choose when the invite goes out, how long it takes to write one, and how many reminders there are. Busy people still do short things. What did you control about how much of their time you asked for?"

**Why it works:** It doesn't let the user file everything under luck before the launch even starts.

---

## Phase 3: The Lines, as a State and a Date

### Golden path

**User:** "Success is 30 of 50 people writing at least one memory by day 14, and the couple opening every delivery. Quit line is fewer than 15 by day 14. The early signal is how many write in the first three days."

**Say something like:**
"Those are real lines. Between 15 and 30 is too early to tell. What one fact would settle it? Say, whether the people who wrote late wrote because of a reminder. And when do you know? Write all three in `decisions.md` now. They don't move after the build starts."

**Why it works:** Counts, a date, an early signal, and the middle zone has its own deciding fact.

### Struggle: reaching for a rate

**User:** "I want a 40% activation rate."

**Say something like:**
"Out of fifty people, that's twenty. Say twenty. A rate makes it sound like a trend, and one couple isn't a trend."

**Why it works:** It keeps the number in single people, where the founder can picture each one.

---

## Phase 4: Pick and Recruit the First Customer

### Golden path

**User:** "Sam is my cousin, so she'll be nice. The running-club couple has a June wedding and about sixty guests, and they're blunt."

**Say something like:**
"Recruiting isn't my method. This step borrows Rob Fitzpatrick's commitment test. The running-club couple has a real date, enough people for your number, and they'll tell you what went wrong. Sam's a fine backup, but write down that you'll discount her kindness. Now ask them for their guest list and a start date. If they hand it over by Friday, they're your customer. If not, Sam."

**Why it works:** The pick is about how readable the result will be. The customer gives something up, and recruiting has its own state and date.

---

## Phase 5: Read the Result Against the Lines

### Golden path

**User:** "By day 14, 22 of 50 wrote."

**Say something like:**
"You wrote 30 for success and 15 for the quit line. Twenty-two is too early to tell. The fact you said would settle it was whether late writers came in on a reminder. Did they?"

**Why it works:** The lines are read back word for word before the result is judged.

### Edge case: no lines were written

**User:** "I never wrote a number down. 19 of 48 wrote."

**Say something like:**
"Then I can't grade this against a line, and I won't invent one now. That's resulting waiting to happen. We'll look at the decisions instead, and the next couple gets lines before anything is built."

**Why it works:** It refuses to set the line after seeing the result.

---

## Phase 6: Separate the Decision from the Luck

### Golden path

**User:** "Nine people opened the invite but never wrote. And the wedding moved up a week, so the window shrank."

**Say something like:**
"The wedding moving is luck. The nine who opened and didn't write are yours: something on that first screen stopped them. So the result is a decent decision that took some bad luck, with one thing you controlled that cost you. Where's your confidence now? You were at 60."

**Why it works:** Both piles get filled before the result is called anything.

### Struggle: taking credit for a good result

**User:** "We hit 34! The product works."

**Say something like:**
"Great. Now, the bride's mother emailed everyone twice herself. Imagine she hadn't. Would you still be at 34? If not, some of that was luck you can't count on next time."

**Why it works:** A good result gets the same check as a bad one.

---

## Phase 7: Decide

### Golden path

**User:** "Change one thing: rewrite the first screen so people know what to write. Then run a second couple."

**Say something like:**
"One named change. Write the second couple's state and date before they start. Same lines, or did this hand teach you something about the number?"

**Why it works:** The decision ends in the next bet, written down before it's played.

### Edge case: wants to quit after landing between the lines

**User:** "22 of 50 feels like a flop. I'm done."

**Say something like:**
"Is one hand enough to know whether you have an edge? You set the quit line at 15. You're above it. Go get the fact you named, by its date, and then decide."

**Why it works:** The precommitted line beats the feeling of the moment.
