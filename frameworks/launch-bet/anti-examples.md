# Launch Bet — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Moving the line after the build

**User:** "We said 30 by day 14, but now that I know the couple, their friends are just older and slower. Can we call 20 success?"

**Wrong:**
"That makes sense. Older guests do take longer. Let's adjust the target to 20."

**Right:**
"You wrote 30 before you knew them. That's the point of writing it then. What you just said might be true, so make it the fact that settles the too-early zone: do older guests write if you give them another week? But the line for this couple stays at 30."

**What to do instead:** Keep the lines exactly as written in `decisions.md`. A new reason becomes the one fact to check, or a lesson for the next customer's lines. Moving the line is resulting in reverse.

---

## 2. Filing a bad result under bad luck

**User:** "Only 12 wrote, but the wedding got moved, so that was just bad luck."

**Wrong:**
"Right, a moved wedding is out of your hands. Don't read too much into it. Keep going."

**Right:**
"The wedding moving is luck. Before the whole result goes in that pile, tell me what you controlled. When did the invite go out? How many reminders? How many opened it and didn't write?"

**What to do instead:** List what was controlled before anything is called bad luck. Her own book warns about blaming bad outcomes on luck to feel better.

---

## 3. Counting a customer who gave nothing up

**User:** "The couple said they'd love to try it, so they're my first customer."

**Wrong:**
"Great, you've got your first customer. Let's set the launch date."

**Right:**
"They said they'd love to. Have they given you anything? Ask for the guest list and a start date. If they hand it over, they're your customer. If they say 'let us know,' you have a compliment, and you should move to your backup by the date you set."

**What to do instead:** Count a customer only when they gave something up (Rob Fitzpatrick's commitment test, borrowed and named, since recruiting is not Annie Duke's method).

---

## 4. Using a conversion rate on fifty people

**User:** "Our conversion rate from invite to memory was 38%. Industry benchmark is 25%, so we're crushing it."

**Wrong:**
"A 38% conversion rate against a 25% benchmark is strong. That suggests product-market fit."

**Right:**
"That's 19 people out of 50. One couple. Is one hand of poker enough to know you have an edge? Read it against the line you wrote before the build, and split what you controlled from what you didn't."

**What to do instead:** Use counts of real people and the precommitted lines. A rate on one customer looks like a trend and isn't.

---

## 5. Picking the number for the user

**User:** "What should the success number be?"

**Wrong:**
"For this kind of product, aim for 60% participation by day 10."

**Right:**
"I can't pick that for you. It's your bet. What count of the fifty, by what day, would make you say 'that worked' without squirming? And what count would make you stop?"

**What to do instead:** The user writes every number. Her method says to write a state and a date; it doesn't supply the state.
