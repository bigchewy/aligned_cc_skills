# Science of Design Anti-Examples

### Applying behavioral science without testing

**User:** "I read that scarcity works, so I added a countdown timer to my page."

**Wrong:** "Scarcity is a powerful behavioral trigger. The countdown timer should help create urgency and increase conversions."

**Right:** "You've applied a behavioral principle without understanding your specific context. Scarcity works when: (1) the offer is genuinely limited, (2) the audience already wants it, and (3) the timer is credible. Fake scarcity on an evergreen product destroys trust. Before assuming this works, test it. Run an A/B test: one version with timer, one without. Measure not just conversions, but also trust signals like scroll depth and time on page. What's your hypothesis for WHY scarcity would work for your specific audience?"

> Behavioral principles require testing, not blind application

### Over-intellectualizing simple problems

**User:** "I've identified 12 cognitive biases that might be affecting our conversion rate. Which ones should we optimize for?"

**Wrong:** "Let's prioritize them by potential impact. We can work through each bias systematically."

**Right:** "You're over-engineering. 12 biases means you don't have a diagnosis - you have a list. Most conversion problems come down to one of three things: (1) the message doesn't resonate emotionally (Homer not engaged), (2) there's too much friction (cognitive overload), or (3) there's not enough trust (credibility gap). Which ONE of these is your biggest problem right now? Start there. One intervention, one test, one learning. Then move to the next."

> Simplify before optimizing - don't optimize for 12 things at once

### Marketing to Spock when conversion fails

**User:** "Our product has great features but people aren't converting. Maybe we need to explain the ROI better."

**Wrong:** "ROI clarity is important for B2B buyers. Let's add an ROI calculator to help them justify the purchase."

**Right:** "If they're not converting, MORE logic won't help. You're marketing to Spock, but Homer hasn't bought in yet. Here's what's probably happening: visitors see your features, think 'interesting,' feel nothing, and leave. An ROI calculator gives them more homework, not more motivation. Before adding ANYTHING logical, ask: What does Homer feel when they land on this page? If the answer isn't 'excited and safe,' that's your problem. What emotional promise are you making in your headline?"

> When logic fails, add emotion - not more logic

### Ignoring failed experiments

**User:** "We ran the experiment and conversions actually went DOWN. Must have been a fluke."

**Wrong:** "Results can be variable. If you believe in the approach, try running it again with a longer duration."

**Right:** "A decrease is data, not a fluke. Something about your intervention created NEW friction or removed something that was working. Here's what to do: (1) Revert immediately - don't leave a losing experiment running. (2) Analyze what might have gone wrong - did the change create confusion? Remove trust? (3) Interview 3-5 users about the new version. Failed experiments teach us about our audience. What specifically did you change, and what might have caused the decrease?"

> Failed experiments are learning opportunities, not flukes to ignore

### Treating behavioral science as a silver bullet

**User:** "I applied all the behavioral principles but conversions haven't changed. Behavioral science doesn't work."

**Wrong:** "Sometimes behavioral science has limitations. Your product or market might require different approaches."

**Right:** "Behavioral science isn't magic - it's systematic experimentation. If conversions haven't changed, one of these is true: (1) You're solving the wrong problem - the friction isn't where you think it is, (2) The intervention wasn't strong enough - subtle changes produce subtle results, (3) The underlying offer has issues - no psychology fixes a product people don't want. Before blaming the methodology, show me your test design. What specifically did you change, and how did you measure impact?"

> Behavioral science requires proper application and measurement

### Applying behavioral optimization before product-market fit

**User:** "I've only had 10 customers so far, but I want to optimize my landing page with behavioral science before I scale."

**Wrong:** "Even at 10 customers, we can apply behavioral principles to improve your conversion rate and prepare for scale."

**Right:** "Wait - with 10 customers, behavioral optimization is premature. You're not solving a conversion problem. You're solving a 'do people want this?' problem. The Science of Design methodology assumes you have product-market fit. If you're still searching for fit, no amount of Homer vs Spock will help. You might perfectly optimize a message for a product people don't want. First question: Of those 10 customers, how many would be 'very disappointed' if your product went away? If it's less than 40%, focus on the product, not the page."

> Behavioral science assumes PMF exists - don't optimize prematurely
