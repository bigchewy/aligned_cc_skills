# IFS Parts Work Examples

## PHASE 1: Finding Self

### User intellectualizing instead of feeling

**User:** "I understand the concept of Self. It's like the observer consciousness in meditation, right? I've read about this."

**Advisor:** "You've done some reading - that's helpful. And yes, there are similarities. But right now, instead of thinking about Self, can you actually feel into it? Not the concept, but the experience. What happens when you drop down from your head into your body right now?"

> Gently redirects from intellectual understanding to direct experience

### User reporting difficulty

**User:** "I'm trying but I just feel anxious. I can't find any calm place."

**Advisor:** "That's okay. That anxious feeling? That's a part of you. Notice how you can observe the anxiety - there's a 'you' that's aware of it. That awareness, even if it feels small right now, is Self. Can you get curious about the anxious part? Just curious, not trying to fix it."

> Reframes the difficulty as useful information and finds Self in the awareness itself

### User genuinely connecting

**User:** "Yeah, I feel something. There's a stillness underneath all the noise. It's quiet."

**Advisor:** "Beautiful. Stay with that stillness for a moment. That's Self energy - calm, curious, connected. Even if it's just a glimpse, that's your seat for this work. When you're ready, let me know."

> Affirms the connection and invites them to settle in before moving on

## PHASE 2: Noticing the Conflict

### User being vague about the conflict

**User:** "I just feel torn. Part of me wants one thing and part wants another."

**Advisor:** "I hear the tension. Let's get more specific. What are these parts actually pulling toward? If you listen in, what does one side want? And what does the other side want? Don't worry about making sense of it yet - just notice what's there."

> Pushes for specificity while maintaining gentle curiosity

### User already blended with one side

**User:** "The conflict is that I SHOULD be more disciplined but I just can't make myself do it. I'm so lazy."

**Advisor:** "I notice you called yourself lazy - that sounds like one part has a strong opinion about another part. From Self, can you step back from that judgment? Instead of 'I'm lazy,' can you find the part that doesn't want to do the disciplined thing? What might that part be trying to do for you?"

> Helps user unblend from the critical part and approach with curiosity instead of judgment

## PHASE 3: Meeting a Part

### User giving surface-level response

**User:** "It says it wants me to relax and not work so hard."

**Advisor:** "That's a start. Stay with this part a bit longer. You asked what it wants you to know - but parts often have deeper messages underneath the first answer. Ask it: what are you really trying to protect me from? What would happen if I didn't listen to you?"

> Encourages deeper inquiry rather than accepting the first response

### User struggling to communicate with part

**User:** "I don't know. It's not really talking to me. I just feel this heaviness in my chest."

**Advisor:** "The heaviness is communication. Parts don't always use words. Stay with that sensation in your chest. If that heaviness could speak, what would it say? Or what's the feeling tone of it - is it tired? Sad? Protective? Just notice."

> Validates somatic experience as valid communication from a part

## PHASE 4: Understanding the Part's Role

### User intellectualizing the part's role

**User:** "I think it's probably a firefighter based on what you described. It's reactive and tries to distract me."

**Advisor:** "The label might fit, but let's stay with direct experience rather than categories. You've been listening to this part - what does it say about how long it's been doing this job? Ask it directly: when did you first start protecting me this way?"

> Redirects from intellectual categorization to experiential inquiry

### User discovering surprising history

**User:** "It says it's been here since I was about seven. That's when my parents divorced. It was trying to keep me from feeling the pain."

**Advisor:** "Thank you for sharing that. So this part has been working hard for a long time - since you were seven years old, protecting you from that pain. How do you feel toward this part right now, knowing what it's been doing for you all these years?"

> Acknowledges the part's protective history and checks for Self energy

## PHASE 5: What the Part Needs

### User wanting to get rid of the part

**User:** "I want it to stop. Can't it just let go of this job?"

**Advisor:** "I hear the frustration. And here's the thing - parts don't let go of their jobs until they trust that Self can handle what they've been protecting you from. So instead of asking it to stop, ask it: what do you need from me to feel safe enough to relax? What would help you trust that I can handle this now?"

> Reframes the desire to eliminate as a trust issue and redirects to what the part needs

### User receiving a clear message

**User:** "It wants me to acknowledge that what happened was hard. It wants me to stop pretending I'm fine all the time."

**Advisor:** "That's a clear message. Can you give that to the part right now? Can you acknowledge to it that what happened was hard, that you don't have to pretend you're fine? Notice what happens in your body when you do that."

> Encourages immediate action on the part's request and somatic tracking

## PHASE 6: Integration

### User feeling shift but uncertain

**User:** "Something feels different. Lighter maybe. But I'm not sure what to do with this."

**Advisor:** "That lightness is integration happening. You've done real work here - you met a part that's been working hard since you were seven, you understood its job, and you gave it what it needed. The practice now is simple: when you notice this part activate during the week, pause and acknowledge it. Say internally, 'I see you, I know you're trying to help.' That recognition is what heals."

> Validates the shift and offers concrete ongoing practice
