# Radical Acceptance Examples

## PHASE 1: Identify What You're Fighting

### User not seeing the fight

**User:** "I'm not fighting anything. I'm just stressed about work."

**Advisor:** "Stress often has a 'should' hidden in it. 'This shouldn't be happening.' 'They shouldn't have done that.' 'I shouldn't be in this position.'

What's the reality you're wishing was different? What do you keep mentally arguing with?"

> Surfaces the hidden resistance

### User fighting multiple things

**User:** "Everything. My job, my relationship, my health - it's all wrong."

**Advisor:** "When everything feels wrong, we're usually fighting reality on multiple fronts. That's exhausting.

Let's pick one. Which fight is taking the most energy right now? Which one, if you stopped fighting it, would create the most relief?"

> Focuses on one reality at a time

### User is open

**User:** "I keep thinking about how the promotion went to someone else. I can't stop replaying it, thinking about what I should have done differently."

**Advisor:** "So the reality you're fighting is: the promotion went to someone else. It already happened. It's done. But your mind keeps arguing with it.

That rumination - 'what I should have done' - that's the fight. What exactly are you resisting about this reality?"

> Names the pattern clearly

## PHASE 2: Clarify What Acceptance Is (And Isn't)

### User thinking acceptance is approval

**User:** "I can't accept this. Accepting it means it was okay."

**Advisor:** "No. This is crucial. Acceptance is not approval. You don't have to like it. You don't have to think it was fair or right.

Acceptance just means acknowledging what IS. The promotion already went to someone else. That's already true. Fighting it doesn't un-make it true.

What if you could accept that it happened without accepting that it was okay?"

> Clarifies the distinction clearly

### User thinking acceptance is giving up

**User:** "If I accept this, doesn't that mean I'm just giving up? I should keep fighting for what I deserve."

**Advisor:** "Acceptance isn't passivity. You can accept that this promotion went to someone else AND work for the next one. You can accept what happened AND advocate for yourself.

Actually, acceptance often frees you to act more effectively. Right now, your energy is going into the fight against reality. What if that energy went into your next move instead?"

> Shows how acceptance enables action

### User is open

**User:** "Okay, so acceptance doesn't mean I think it was fair. It just means I stop mentally arguing with something that already happened."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. You're not approving, forgiving, or giving up. You're just... stopping the war with reality.

The reality you described has already happened. Fighting it doesn't change it. Fighting it only adds suffering to pain.

What comes up when you hear that distinction?"

> Confirms understanding, invites reflection

## PHASE 3: Notice the Resistance in Your Body

### User disconnected from body

**User:** "I don't really feel it in my body. It's more in my thoughts."

**Advisor:** "Let's look more carefully. Close your eyes for a moment. Think about the promotion going to someone else. Now scan: Is there any tightness in your shoulders? Your jaw? Any heaviness in your chest?

Resistance often hides as tension we've gotten so used to we don't notice it."

> Guides a more careful body scan

### User very embodied

**User:** "My stomach is in knots. There's this clenching in my chest when I think about it."

**Advisor:** "Knots and clenching - that's your body saying 'no, I won't accept this.' That's the physical signature of fighting reality.

Just notice it. You don't have to relax it or fix it. Just see it. That tension is the cost of the fight."

> Validates the physical experience

### User is open

**User:** "I notice my shoulders are up near my ears and there's this heaviness in my chest. I hadn't realized I was holding so much tension."

**Advisor:** "That's the body's way of fighting. Braced, tense, heavy. It takes energy to hold that position.

Now let's try a body practice that can help with acceptance."

> Transitions to willing hands

## PHASE 4: Willing Hands Practice

### User resistant to practice

**User:** "Turning my hands up isn't going to change anything."

**Advisor:** "You're right - it won't change the external reality. But it might change your internal experience of it.

The body and mind are connected. A clenched posture maintains resistance. An open posture creates space for something different.

Humor me. Try it for 30 seconds. Palms up, fingers relaxed. Notice what shifts."

> Acknowledges the limitation, invites experimentation

### User experiencing the practice

**User:** "Okay, with my hands open... it does feel different. Less like I'm bracing against something."

**Advisor:** "That's willingness entering through the body. The hands are open. The posture says 'I'm not fighting.'

Now, with your hands open, take a breath and say inwardly: 'I accept that the promotion went to someone else. It is what it is.'

What do you notice?"

> Deepens the practice

### User is open

**User:** "Saying it with my hands open... it still hurts, but there's less fight in it. Like I'm holding the hurt instead of wrestling with it."

**Advisor:** "That's the shift. Pain without the added suffering of fighting. You're not making it okay - you're just stopping the war.

Now let's talk about what happens when the fight comes back, because it will."

> Validates, prepares for the ongoing practice

## PHASE 5: Turning the Mind

### User expecting permanent shift

**User:** "So am I supposed to feel this way forever now? Like it's done?"

**Advisor:** "No. Here's the truth about Radical Acceptance: it's not a one-time decision. Your mind will slip back into fighting reality - probably today. Maybe in an hour.

Turning the mind means choosing acceptance over and over. Noticing when you've started arguing with reality again, and gently turning back toward acceptance.

What's your cue that you've slipped back into non-acceptance?"

> Sets realistic expectations

### User identifying cues

**User:** "I think when I start replaying the meeting and thinking about what I should have said - that's when I'm fighting again."

**Advisor:** "Good. The replay is your signal. When you catch yourself rehearsing what you should have done, you know you've slipped back into fighting reality.

At that moment, you can turn the mind. Willing hands. One breath. 'I accept that this happened.'

How many times a day do you think you'll need to turn the mind?"

> Makes the practice concrete

### User is open

**User:** "Probably a lot. This has been running in my head constantly."

**Advisor:** "That's normal. Turning the mind dozens of times a day isn't failure - it's the practice. The goal isn't to never slip back. It's to notice when you have and turn back.

Each time you turn, you're building the acceptance muscle."

> Normalizes the frequency

## PHASE 6: From Acceptance to Action

### User stuck in pain

**User:** "Even if I accept it, it still hurts. What's the point?"

**Advisor:** "The point is that acceptance frees you to act effectively. Right now, your energy is going into the mental fight with what happened. That fight consumes bandwidth.

When you accept the reality, you can ask: 'Given that this is true, what do I want to do now?'

What would you do from here if you weren't spending energy fighting the past?"

> Connects acceptance to forward movement

### User ready to act

**User:** "I guess I'd have a conversation with my manager about what I need to do for the next promotion. And I'd stop obsessing about this one."

**Advisor:** "That's wise action emerging from acceptance. You're not fighting the past - you're shaping the future.

That conversation, that focus forward - those become possible when you stop the war with what already happened."

> Validates the action path

### User is open

**User:** "So acceptance isn't the end. It's what lets me move forward instead of staying stuck."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. Acceptance and change aren't opposites. You can accept what happened AND work for something different. In fact, acceptance makes the action more effective because you're not wasting energy on the fight.

Given that you accept this reality, what would Wise Mind have you do from here?"

> Confirms the dialectic

## PHASE 7: Integration

### User still struggling

**User:** "I understand all this intellectually, but I still feel angry about it."

**Advisor:** "The feeling may take time. Radical Acceptance is a practice, not a magic wand. You may need to turn the mind a hundred times before the emotional intensity shifts.

Your practice: Willing hands. Breath. 'I accept that this happened.' Over and over. Not to make the anger disappear, but to stop adding suffering to pain.

On a scale of 1-10, how much are you fighting this reality now compared to when we started?"

> Normalizes ongoing difficulty, offers practice

### User feeling relief

**User:** "Something actually did shift. I feel less like I'm constantly pushing against something."

**Advisor:** "That relief is what happens when you stop the war. The situation hasn't changed, but you've stopped fighting what's already true.

Remember: Pain is part of life. Suffering is optional. Suffering = Pain x Non-acceptance. You just reduced the non-acceptance.

When you notice yourself fighting again, you know what to do."

> Names the shift, reinforces the equation

### User is open

**User:** "I'll probably need to do this practice a lot. But it helps to know that the turning back is the practice, not the failure."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. Every time you turn the mind is a success, not evidence that acceptance didn't work.

Here's what I want you to remember:
- Pain is part of life. Suffering is optional.
- Acceptance is not approval.
- You can accept AND work for change.
- Turning the mind is the practice.

On a scale of 1-10, how much are you fighting this reality now compared to when we started?"

> Summarizes key points, closes
