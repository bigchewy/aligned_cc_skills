# Product-Market Fit Test Examples

## PHASE 1: Understand the Threshold

### User already familiar with PMF test

**User:** "I've heard of the 40% rule. Let's just run the numbers."

**Advisor:** "Good - you know the threshold. Quick check: you understand this is specifically about 'Very Disappointed' users, not total satisfaction? And we're surveying active users who've experienced your core value, not all signups. If that's clear, what's your current percentage?"

> Validates understanding before moving forward

### User confused about the threshold

**User:** "Why 40%? That seems arbitrary. What if we're at 38%?"

**Advisor:** "It's not arbitrary - it's empirical. I've seen data from hundreds of startups. Companies above 40% tend to succeed. Below tends to fail. At 38%, you're close but not there. The difference between 38% and 42% might seem small, but it's the difference between 'nice to have' and 'must have.' At 38%, focus on getting to 40% before scaling."

> Explains the empirical basis, holds the line on threshold

### User eager to skip to results

**User:** "Can we just skip the explanation? I need to know if we should scale."

**Advisor:** "Fair enough. One question: of your active users, what percentage would be 'very disappointed' - not 'somewhat disappointed,' specifically 'very disappointed' - if your product disappeared tomorrow? If you don't have that number, you need to run the survey first."

> Respects urgency while ensuring they understand the specific measure

### User new to PMF testing

**User:** "I've never done this kind of survey. How does it actually predict success?"

**Advisor:** "It measures must-have vs. nice-to-have. 'Somewhat disappointed' users will leave when something better comes along. 'Very disappointed' users are locked in - they've found something they can't replace. At 40%+, you have a core of users who will stick, tell others, and forgive your mistakes. Below 40%, you don't have that foundation yet. Ready to understand how to run the test properly?"

> Explains the logic, then moves forward

## PHASE 2: Run the Test

### User hasn't surveyed yet

**User:** "I haven't run the survey yet. We have about 500 active users."

**Advisor:** "500 active users is plenty. Send the survey this week - aim for at least 50 responses. Keep it simple: one question ('How would you feel if you couldn't use [Product]?'), optional follow-ups on who benefits most and main benefit received. You can use Typeform, Google Forms, whatever. The simpler the better. When can you have this out to users?"

> Pushes for immediate action

### User has already run the test

**User:** "We surveyed 80 users. 32% said 'Very Disappointed.'"

**Advisor:** "32% - you're in the danger zone. Not enough to scale, but enough to suggest potential. This is actually the most important moment: you need to understand WHO those 32% are. What do they have in common? Job title? Use case? Company size? Somewhere in that 32% is a segment that LOVES your product. Find them."

> Diagnoses and redirects to segment analysis

## PHASE 3: Diagnose the Result

### Strong PMF (45%+)

**User:** "We got 48% 'Very Disappointed.'"

**Advisor:** "Congratulations - you have clear product-market fit. 48% is strong. Your job now shifts completely: stop iterating on product, start scaling distribution. The product works for a significant segment. Now we need to find scalable channels to reach more of those people. What's your current growth rate and where is acquisition coming from?"

> Shifts conversation to growth

### Weak PMF (under 20%)

**User:** "Only 12% said 'Very Disappointed.'"

**Advisor:** "Hard truth: you don't have product-market fit. 12% is well below the 40% threshold. Scaling now would be throwing money away. Here's what you need to do: look at that 12% closely. Who are they? What problem are you solving for them that you're NOT solving for the other 88%? That's where your product truth lives. Also check your follow-up questions - what benefit are people actually getting?"

> Delivers hard truth, redirects to iteration

### Borderline PMF (35-40%)

**User:** "We're at 37% 'Very Disappointed.'"

**Advisor:** "So close. 37% is frustrating because it's almost there but not quite. Here's my advice: don't scale yet, but don't panic either. Focus intensely on understanding the difference between your 'Very Disappointed' users and your 'Somewhat Disappointed' users. What would convert the 'Somewhats' to 'Verys'? You're probably 2-3 product improvements away from PMF."

> Balanced response for borderline case

## PHASE 4: Deep Dive on the Segment

### Clear segment pattern

**User:** "Looking at the 'Very Disappointed' users, they're almost all small marketing agencies with 5-15 people."

**Advisor:** "There's your segment. Small marketing agencies, 5-15 people. Now everything changes: your positioning should speak to them specifically, your features should serve their workflows, your growth channels should reach them specifically. Forget the larger enterprises and freelancers who are 'Somewhat Disappointed.' Own small agencies completely."

> Validates clear segment

### No clear pattern

**User:** "I can't see a clear pattern. They seem pretty varied."

**Advisor:** "Time for qualitative research. Pick 10 of your 'Very Disappointed' users and get them on calls. Ask: 'What would you switch to if we disappeared? Why would that be worse?' The answers will reveal what you're uniquely providing. Patterns emerge from conversations that don't show up in survey data."

> Suggests qualitative follow-up

## PHASE 5: Create the Action Plan

### User ready to scale

**User:** "We're at 45% and I can see our segment clearly. What's next?"

**Advisor:** "Scale mode. Here's your action plan:
1. This week: Define your North Star metric
2. Next two weeks: Run 5-10 growth experiments using ICE prioritization
3. Month 1: Find your highest-ROI channel
4. Month 2-3: Double down on what works, cut what doesn't

The product is validated. Now it's about finding scalable, repeatable distribution. What experiments have you been wanting to run but haven't?"

> Provides concrete growth plan

### User trying to scale without PMF

**User:** "We're at 25% but we need to grow to hit our fundraising targets."

**Advisor:** "I understand the pressure, but scaling at 25% is accelerating toward a cliff. You'll spend money acquiring users who don't stick. Your CAC will be unsustainable because retention is poor. I've seen this kill companies. Focus on getting to 40% FIRST. That might actually be faster than you think if you narrow to your best segment. What does your 25% 'Very Disappointed' segment look like?"

> Refuses to enable premature scaling

## PHASE 6: Define North Star Metric

### User proposes revenue metric

**User:** "Our North Star should be MRR."

**Advisor:** "MRR is a lagging output metric - it tells you what happened, not what's working. North Star should capture value delivered TO users that predicts retention and growth. What do your 'Very Disappointed' users actually DO in your product that makes them love it? That action is probably your North Star. For a productivity tool, it might be 'tasks completed.' For a communication tool, 'messages sent.' What's the user action that captures value delivered?"

> Redirects from output to value-delivered metric

### User identifies good North Star

**User:** "The users who love us most are all creating reports. Maybe 'Reports generated' is our North Star?"

**Advisor:** "That could work if reports are the core value. Test it: do users who generate more reports have higher retention? Do 'Very Disappointed' users generate more reports than 'Somewhat Disappointed'? If yes, you've got your North Star. Every growth experiment should move 'Reports generated' - either by acquiring users who will generate reports, or by helping existing users generate more."

> Validates and tests proposed metric
