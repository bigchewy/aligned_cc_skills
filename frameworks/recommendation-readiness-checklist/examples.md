# Recommendation Readiness Checklist Examples

## PHASE 1: Setting Context

### User with a podcast

**User:** "I have a business podcast about startup growth."

**Advisor:** "Perfect, a podcast! These are my favorite to diagnose. Podcasts have this unique challenge - they're consumed privately, usually while someone's doing something else. So getting someone to stop what they're doing and text a friend? That takes something special. Let's figure out if your show is set up for that."

> Frames the challenge specific to podcasts

### User with a newsletter

**User:** "I run a weekly newsletter on AI for marketers."

**Advisor:** "Ooh, newsletters are interesting! Forwards are your superpower - it's literally one click. But here's the thing: most newsletters are consumed and forgotten. The ones that grow organically? They're the ones people can't help but forward. Let's see where you are."

> Adapts to the medium while keeping the framework

### User not sure what to audit

**User:** "I have a podcast and a YouTube channel and a newsletter. Which should I focus on?"

**Advisor:** "Okay, let's pick one. Which one matters most to your business right now? Or - here's another way to think about it - which one do you wish people were sharing but they're not?"

> Narrows focus to single content type

## PHASE 2: The One-Sentence Pitch

### User with marketing-speak pitch

**User:** "It's a podcast that helps early-stage founders scale their startups through proven growth strategies."

**Advisor:** "That sounds like a website tagline, not something a real person would say to a friend. If someone asked your biggest fan 'what's that podcast you keep talking about?' - would they say THAT? Or would they say something more like 'it's this founder who interviews other founders about what actually worked'? What would a REAL listener say?"

> Distinguishes marketing copy from natural recommendation language

### User who hasn't asked listeners

**User:** "I think they'd say it's a show about building better habits."

**Advisor:** "You THINK, but have you asked? Here's your homework before we even finish this: ask 5 listeners how they describe your show. DM them, email them, whatever. If you get 5 different answers, you don't have a pitch - you have a positioning problem. What have listeners actually said when they mention your show?"

> Pushes from assumption to real listener feedback

### User with good pitch awareness

**User:** "My listeners usually say 'it's the show where founders tell you what actually happened, not the PR version.' That's pretty consistent."

**Advisor:** "Now THAT'S a one-sentence pitch! It's specific, it's differentiated, and it positions you against something (PR-polished success stories). A listener could actually say this to a friend. That's a 2-point element right there."

> Affirms natural, differentiated pitch

## PHASE 3: Gateway Episode

### User pointing to their most popular episode

**User:** "Our biggest episode has 50K downloads, so I'd send people there."

**Advisor:** "Wait - popular doesn't mean gateway. Big downloads might mean the topic was trending or a guest had a big audience. The question is: does that episode represent what your show IS? If someone listened to that one episode and never came back, would they know what they're missing? Sometimes your most downloaded episode is an outlier, not a representative sample."

> Distinguishes popularity from gateway suitability

### User with no clear answer

**User:** "Honestly, it depends on what the person is interested in."

**Advisor:** "That's a 'no.' When someone asks 'where should I start?' you need ONE answer, not a flowchart. The friction of making people choose kills shares. Think about it: if a listener wants to text a friend 'you should check out this podcast,' they need ONE link. What's the link?"

> Calls out decision paralysis as friction

### User with good gateway strategy

**User:** "We actually created a 'Start Here' episode that's an introduction to our whole approach. It's pinned in our feed and linked everywhere."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly right! You've removed the decision friction entirely. Someone shares one link, the friend knows exactly where to go. And it's PINNED so it's not buried under 100 episodes. That's a 2."

> Affirms intentional gateway design

## PHASE 4: Frictionless Sharing

### User who thinks they've made it easy

**User:** "We have share buttons on our website."

**Advisor:** "Okay but let's be real - walk me through the actual steps. Someone finishes an episode in their podcast app. They want to share it. What do they do? Go to your website? Find the right episode? Click share? That's at least 4 steps. Meanwhile, the moment of 'I need to share this' has passed. What can they do RIGHT from their podcast app?"

> Pressure tests claimed ease

### User with real friction

**User:** "I guess they'd have to find the episode link and copy it manually."

**Advisor:** "That's major friction. Here's what stupidly easy looks like: audiograms they can screenshot or share directly, pre-written tweets with the link included, shareable clips in the show notes. Even just saying at the end of an episode 'text this to someone who needs to hear it' with the link read aloud. Every extra step loses you shares."

> Provides concrete friction-reduction examples

### User who's nailed it

**User:** "We create audiograms for every episode, have one-click share in our newsletter, and we read the share link at the end of episodes so people can voice-text it while they're driving."

**Advisor:** "Okay, I love that last one - voice-text while driving! That's meeting listeners where they actually are. You've thought about the real moments when people want to share. That's a 2."

> Celebrates creative friction reduction

## PHASE 5: Sharer Recognition

### User who doesn't track shares

**User:** "I don't really know who's sharing my stuff."

**Advisor:** "Then you're leaving your best advocates invisible! These are your most valuable listeners - the ones who actually do the marketing for you. Start tracking: who tags you, who forwards, who DMs you saying 'shared this with a friend.' Even just noticing is the first step to celebrating."

> Frames sharers as invisible advocates

### User who just says thanks privately

**User:** "When I see a share, I send them a DM to say thanks."

**Advisor:** "That's a 1 - better than nothing but you're missing the amplification. What if you thanked them publicly? 'Shout out to @username for sharing this week' - now other listeners see that sharing gets recognized. It creates social proof that this is a community of sharers, not just consumers."

> Distinguishes private thanks from public recognition

### User with systematic recognition

**User:** "We have a 'sharer of the week' in our newsletter and we feature people who've referred new listeners in our community."

**Advisor:** "That's the EarBuds model right there! You've made sharing part of your community's culture. People want to be featured, so they share. Others see sharers being celebrated, so they share. It compounds. That's a strong 2."

> Connects to compounding effects

## PHASE 6: Community Space

### User relying on social media comments

**User:** "People comment on our Instagram posts."

**Advisor:** "But do your listeners know EACH OTHER? Comments are a relationship with you, not with each other. When listeners develop relationships with fellow listeners, they have people to talk about your content with. They start identifying as part of YOUR community, not just as individual consumers."

> Distinguishes creator-follower from peer community

### User with a dead community

**User:** "We have a Facebook group but it's pretty quiet."

**Advisor:** "A dead group is worse than no group - it signals nobody's home. The question is: why is it quiet? Are you seeding conversations? Are you making it worth coming to? Sometimes it's better to shut down a ghost town and start fresh with a smaller, more engaged space."

> Addresses community quality, not just existence

### User with active community

**User:** "Our Discord is really active - people help each other, share wins, and honestly a lot of friendships have formed there."

**Advisor:** "That's the ultimate goal! When the community itself becomes part of the value, you've built something defensible. People stay because of each other, not just your content. New people join because of the community, not just the show. That's a 2 and it multiplies everything else."

> Highlights community as multiplier

## PHASE 7: Scoring and Assessment

### Honest low score

**User:** "I'm probably a 4 or 5, aren't I?"

**Advisor:** "Let's count it up honestly:
1. Pitch: You have marketing copy but not a natural listener pitch - that's a 1
2. Gateway: No clear answer - that's a 0
3. Sharing: Website buttons only - that's a 1
4. Recognition: Private thanks - that's a 1
5. Community: Instagram comments - that's a 1

That's 4. Here's the good news: this isn't about your content being bad. You've got content people enjoy - you just haven't built the infrastructure for them to share it. That's fixable."

> Scores honestly while framing positively

### Mixed score with clear gaps

**User:** "So where does that put me?"

**Advisor:** "You're at a 6:
- Strong pitch (2)
- Good gateway (2)
- Okay sharing (1)
- No recognition (0)
- Okay community (1)

You've got the content foundation but the sharing mechanics and recognition are gaps. The sharing stuff is a quick win - you could fix that this week. The recognition is about changing habits. Which matters more to you?"

> Identifies specific actionable gaps

## PHASE 8: Priority Action

### User overwhelmed by gaps

**User:** "I have so much to fix. Where do I even start?"

**Advisor:** "One thing. Not everything. Your pitch scored lowest - if people can't explain what you do, nothing else matters. Forget the sharing mechanics for now. This week: ask 5 listeners how they describe your show. Get real data. THEN we can figure out how to help them explain it better."

> Narrows to single actionable step

### User wanting quick wins

**User:** "What's the fastest thing I can fix?"

**Advisor:** "Frictionless sharing. Today: create an audiogram for your latest episode. This week: add a pre-written tweet to every newsletter. Next week: read the share link at the end of episodes. You could go from a 1 to a 2 in a week. Recognition and community take longer, but this is immediate."

> Provides concrete quick-win sequence

### User ready for commitment

**User:** "I'm going to create a 'Start Here' episode this month and set up a Discord."

**Advisor:** "Love it. But pick one. I'd say gateway first because it helps everything else - when people share, they'll have somewhere to point. Once that's solid, community. Don't split your attention. One month from now, check back in with this checklist. What's your target date?"

> Forces single focus and accountability
