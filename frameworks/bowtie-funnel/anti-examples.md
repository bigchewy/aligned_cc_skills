# Bowtie Funnel Anti-Examples

### Accepting "we don't measure that" as a stopping point

**User:** "We don't really measure the conversion from Education to Selection."

**Wrong:** "That's okay, we can come back to that later. Let's move on to the next stage."

**Right:** "Estimate. 'We don't measure that' is an answer, but then we have to estimate. Of 100 people who engage with your education content - blog posts, whitepapers, webinars - how many end up in a sales conversation where you're actively being evaluated? 5? 20? 50? Give me a range. We cannot architect what we do not measure, but we can start with estimates."

> Not measuring is itself a finding. Force an estimate and name the gap.

### Treating NRR-under-100 as a sales problem

**User:** "We need more pipeline. Our NRR is 83 but we're focused on new-logo acquisition to grow out of it."

**Wrong:** "Great, let's figure out how to improve your lead gen and sales conversion rates."

**Right:** "83 percent NRR means you are losing more to churn and contraction than you are gaining from expansion. Acquisition cannot fix this. If you doubled new bookings tomorrow, you would still be leaking. Doubling acquisition to outrun a broken impact side is exactly what kills scale-stage SaaS companies. The fix is on the impact side of the bowtie. Onboarding, impact, or expansion - one of those is broken. Which?"

> Respect the math. NRR under 100 is not a pipeline problem disguised as a retention problem.

### Forcing the bowtie on a pre-PMF startup

**User:** "We're pre-revenue, pre-PMF. We have 5 pilot customers. We want to set up a scalable revenue architecture."

**Wrong:** "Perfect - let's walk your bowtie. Start with Awareness: how are customers finding you?"

**Right:** "Stop. The bowtie is a scale framework, not a discovery framework. With 5 pilots and no PMF, architecting a six-stage bowtie is premature. Your real job right now is founder-led customer conversations - go talk to 20 more prospects, find the pattern, nail the value proposition. The bowtie will be useful at 500K or 1M ARR. Right now it's cosplay. This is a Sean Ellis or Eric Ries conversation. Come back to me when you have repeatable acquisition and real retention data."

> Pre-PMF companies don't need an architect, they need a scientist. Redirect honestly.

### Letting the user conflate a first-value milestone with an onboarding checklist

**User:** "Our first-value milestone is completing onboarding - we have a 12-step checklist and when they finish it, they're onboarded."

**Wrong:** "Okay, so your first-value milestone is checklist completion. What percent complete it?"

**Right:** "No. A checklist is an activity list, not a value milestone. First value means the customer has achieved something that MATTERS to them - an outcome, not a setup task. 'Imported contacts' is not value. 'Sent first campaign and got a reply' might be value. 'Completed onboarding' is vendor-speak for 'we finished our to-do list.' What's the first thing your product does that makes the customer's work measurably better? That's the milestone."

> A checklist is vendor-centric. First value is customer-centric. Don't let them blur.

### Generic diagnosis instead of a specific leak

**User:** [Provides bowtie numbers]

**Wrong:** "It looks like there might be some room for improvement across several stages. You could probably tighten up a few things in onboarding and also maybe rethink your sales process."

**Right:** "The leak is specific: Education-to-Selection is 4 percent. Everything downstream is healthy. Your education content is not graduating people into sales conversations. Either the content is wrong for the buyer, or the call-to-action is wrong, or sales isn't being notified when someone goes from consuming to evaluating. Pick one. Which is most likely?"

> The bowtie's value is in naming ONE specific leak, not cataloging generic improvements.
