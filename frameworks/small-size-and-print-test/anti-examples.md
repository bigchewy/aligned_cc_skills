# The Small-Size and Print Test — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Judging on taste

**User:** "Is this a good logo?"

**Wrong:**
"I really like it. The colors are lovely and the shape feels friendly."

**Right:**
"Let's not ask whether we like it. Let's ask whether it works. Sagi Haviv's three tests: is it appropriate, is it distinctive and memorable, is it simple? Then we shrink it to 16 pixels and print it in one color."

**What to do instead:** Run Haviv's three tests and the hard places, and say what passes and what fails.

---

## 2. Judging a color alone

**User:** "Is this mustard accent okay?"

**Wrong:**
"Mustard is a warm, sophisticated choice. It should work well."

**Right:**
"Josef Albers: color deceives continually. Let's put it on the real background, next to the real text color, and check the numbers. On cream it's about 2.3 to 1. A mark needs 3:1 to be seen and text needs 4.5:1. Darken it, then look again."

**What to do instead:** Judge every color next to its neighbors, with contrast numbers.

---

## 3. Treating small size as a later fix

**User:** "We'll worry about the favicon after launch."

**Wrong:**
"Sure, the main logo matters most. The favicon can be cropped later."

**Right:**
"The favicon is the place most people will see it most often. If it fails at 16 pixels, it fails there every day. Let's check it now and make a small version if we need one."

**What to do instead:** Test the smallest size before shipping. A small version is fine if it comes from the same mark.

---

## 4. A list with no call

**User:** "So what's the verdict?"

**Wrong:**
"There are strengths and weaknesses. The shape is strong, the color could be better, and small sizes might be a challenge. It's up to you."

**Right:**
"Ship it with two fixes: the small version for the favicon, and the darker mustard on cream. The small version matters most. Then check both at real size again."

**What to do instead:** End with one call, ship, ship with fixes, or redraw, and name the fix that matters most.

---

## 5. Fixing a distinctiveness failure with polish

**User:** "It's a lightbulb, but we made it really clean. Does it pass?"

**Wrong:**
"The clean lines help a lot. A few tweaks to the weight and it'll pass."

**Right:**
"Clean doesn't make it yours. A lightbulb could belong to any company with ideas. That's Haviv's distinctive test, and polish doesn't fix it. That's a redraw. Start from what your product already has, with Boil It Down."

**What to do instead:** Call a distinctiveness failure a redraw, and send it back to Boil It Down.
