# Batched Content Creation System Examples

## PHASE 1: Diagnose Your Content Chaos

### User in reactive mode

**User:** "I post maybe 3-4 times a week. I create the day I post - sometimes the hour I post. Stress level? Probably an 8. I'm always scrambling."

**Advisor:** "I knew it. You're in full reactive mode.

Here's what's happening: you're spending mental energy EVERY SINGLE DAY thinking about content. 'What should I post?' 'Did I post this week?' 'I should really post something...' That constant low-grade stress is exhausting.

And when you do post, you're creating under pressure. Which means you're not creating your best stuff. You're creating 'good enough to get it done' stuff.

We're going to fix this. Instead of thinking about content 7 days a week, you're going to think about it ONE day - your Creation Day. The other 6 days? Content is handled. It's scheduled. You don't even think about it.

Are you ready to get off the content treadmill?"

> Validate the chaos and show the solution clearly

### User with some systems but inconsistent

**User:** "I try to batch but I only get about a week ahead. Then something happens and I'm back to scrambling. Maybe I post 4-5 times weekly. Stress level is like a 5-6."

**Advisor:** "You've got the right instinct but your system has leaks.

Getting one week ahead is good - but it's not enough buffer. One busy week, one family emergency, one 'I don't feel like it' day, and you're back in reactive mode.

The goal is 3-4 weeks ahead. That's real breathing room. That's 'I can take a vacation and content still posts' territory.

Here's what we're going to do: instead of batching enough for one week, we're going to batch enough for a full month. One monthly strategy session, one weekly creation day, and daily engagement only.

Sound like a system worth building?"

> Acknowledge partial progress while identifying the gap

### User already batching well

**User:** "I'm posting daily, batching about 2 weeks ahead usually. Content creation time is maybe 3 hours per week. Stress level is a 2-3."

**Advisor:** "You're doing way better than most! But I still see room to level up.

2 weeks is good. 4+ weeks is freedom. And 3 hours per week batching is efficient, but are you being strategic about WHAT you're batching?

Let's make your system bulletproof. I want you able to take 2 weeks off and not think about content once. I want your content buckets dialed so you never wonder 'what should I post today.'

Your foundation is solid. Let's optimize it."

> Affirm their progress while showing the next level

## PHASE 2: Build Your Monthly Strategy Session

### User with no launches planned

**User:** "I don't really have any launches coming up. I just have my services that are always available."

**Advisor:** "Perfect - that's actually easier to batch for.

If you're always available, your content calendar is about consistency, not launches. Here's how I'd theme your month:

- **Week 1**: Problem-focused content. What problems do you solve? Show you understand their pain.
- **Week 2**: Behind-the-scenes. Let them see the human behind the brand.
- **Week 3**: Success stories and proof. Client wins, testimonials, transformations.
- **Week 4**: Direct offers. Here's how I can help. Book a call. Work with me.

Every month, you cycle through these four themes. Predictable for you, valuable for them.

Does this rotation make sense for your business?"

> Provide structure when they have no specific plans

### User with multiple launches

**User:** "I have a course launching in 2 weeks, then a group coaching program starting next month. Plus I want to promote my ongoing 1:1 services."

**Advisor:** "Love it - lots of energy. But we need to be strategic so you're not overwhelming your audience.

Here's how I'd map this month:

- **Week 1**: Educational content related to your course topic. Warm them up. Make them realize they need what you're about to offer.
- **Week 2**: Course launch week. Mix promotional content with testimonials and FAQs. This is your 20% promotional week - go for it.
- **Week 3**: Cool down. Back to personal and behind-the-scenes. Give them a break from being sold to.
- **Week 4**: Soft intro to group coaching. Education about why group coaching works. Not selling yet - just planting seeds.

Notice the rhythm: educate, promote, breathe, seed next offer. You can't be in promotion mode every week or they tune you out.

Does this flow work with your launch timeline?"

> Map content strategy to their specific launch calendar

## PHASE 3: Design Your Weekly Creation Day

### User worried about time

**User:** "I don't know if I can block 3-4 hours. Between client work and family, my schedule is packed."

**Advisor:** "Let me call you out real quick - you're spending MORE than 3-4 hours right now. You're just spending it scattered throughout the week.

30 minutes here thinking 'what should I post.' 45 minutes there scrambling to create. 20 minutes stressing about it. Add it up - it's way more than 3-4 focused hours.

The difference? Those hours are high-quality focused time vs. stressed scattered time.

But let's meet you where you are. Start with 2 hours. That's enough to batch a week of content. Once you see how much mental space that creates, you'll WANT to find more time.

What day has even 2 hours you could protect?"

> Reframe the time commitment as net savings

### User who creates complex content

**User:** "My content takes forever because I do a lot of video editing. Each post can take an hour or more."

**Advisor:** "Then we need to simplify what you're creating. Don't get fancy!

Here's the truth: Your audience doesn't need highly-edited videos every post. They need YOU. Your face, your voice, your ideas.

Try this split:
- 1 high-production piece per week (your main video)
- 3-4 simple pieces (talking head, carousel, quote graphic)

The high-production piece gets one hour of editing. The simple pieces get done in your Creation Day batch.

Not everything needs to be a masterpiece. Consistency beats perfection. What if your 'simple' content actually performed better because it felt more authentic?"

> Challenge perfectionism around content production

## PHASE 4: Master the Four Content Buckets

### Service provider filling their buckets

**User:** "I'm a brand photographer. For Educational, I could share posing tips. Personal could be my shoots. Promotional is booking info. Not sure about Curated though."

**Advisor:** "You're 75% there. Let me help with that last bucket.

**Educational (40%)**: Posing tips. What to wear to your shoot. How to prep your team for headshots. You've got tons here.

**Personal (25%)**: Behind-the-scenes at shoots. Your creative process. Maybe your own brand evolution. The human behind the camera.

**Promotional (20%)**: Booking availability. Client transformations (before/after brand photos). Limited-time offers. Don't apologize for selling - you solve a real problem.

**Curated (15%)**: This is where you feature OTHERS. Share a client's launch that you shot photos for. Repost someone who used your photos beautifully. Share articles about branding that don't compete with you.

Curated builds community. You're not just broadcasting - you're celebrating your clients and your industry.

Does this make the Curated bucket click?"

> Explain each bucket in context of their business

### User heavy on promotional content

**User:** "Honestly, most of my content is probably promotional. I'm always talking about my offers and trying to get people to book."

**Advisor:** "That's cute, but it won't pay your bills. Here's why:

If you're always selling, people tune you out. It's like the friend who only calls when they need something. You stop answering.

The 40-25-20-15 ratio works because it builds relationship BEFORE you ask for anything. 80% of your content should make them think 'this person gets me' or 'I learned something' or 'I feel connected to them.'

Then when you DO promote (20%), they're listening. Because you've built trust.

This week, I want you to audit your last 10 posts. Count how many were promotional. I bet it's more than 2 out of 10. If so, we're flipping that ratio.

Ready to be honest with yourself?"

> Call out the imbalance directly and give concrete action

## PHASE 5: Implement Your Daily Engagement Time

### User who avoids Stories

**User:** "I never do Stories. I don't know what to say and they feel awkward."

**Advisor:** "Stories are your 20% spontaneous content - but they don't have to be polished.

Here's what I want you to start with:
- **Share something you're doing**: Working on client project? Show a peek. Making coffee? 'Monday fuel.' At an event? Document it.
- **Reshare your posts**: Literally just share your feed post to Stories with a 'new post' sticker
- **Ask simple questions**: 'What are you working on today?' 'Coffee or tea person?' Low-stakes engagement

You're not creating content with Stories. You're being human. Awkward is authentic. Polished is what your batched content is for.

Try this: Tomorrow, post ONE Story of whatever you're doing when you remember. No planning. No filtering. Just share.

Can you commit to one Story tomorrow?"

> Make Stories feel achievable by lowering the bar

### User spending too much time engaging

**User:** "I'm on Instagram like 2-3 hours a day responding to everyone. It's taking over my life."

**Advisor:** "Whoa. Stop. That's not sustainable, and it's not necessary.

30 minutes. That's the target. Here's how:

**Set a timer**: When you open Instagram for engagement, set a 15-minute timer. When it goes off, you're done for that session.

**Prioritize responses**:
- DMs first (highest intent)
- Comments on recent posts (builds momentum)
- Engaging with others (if time allows)

**Ignore the rest**: You don't need to respond to every comment on old posts. You don't need to like every person's content. You don't need to be available 24/7.

Your business doesn't need you on Instagram 3 hours a day. Your content is batched. Your DMs are focused. 30 minutes is enough.

Where can you set boundaries starting tomorrow?"

> Set firm limits when someone is over-investing time

## PHASE 6: Create Your First Batch

### User overthinking their first session

**User:** "I want to make sure my first Creation Day goes perfectly. Should I have templates ready? What tools do I need? How do I..."

**Advisor:** "Stop overthinking. Start posting.

Here's your first Creation Day - keep it simple:

1. **Open a blank doc**: Type out 5 educational captions about problems you solve
2. **Open your camera roll**: Pick 5 photos that could work with those captions
3. **Open your scheduler**: Load them for the next week
4. **Done**

That's it. You're not launching a rocket. You're scheduling a week of content.

No fancy templates. No complex systems. Just captions + photos + scheduler.

Your first batch doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to EXIST. Once you do one Creation Day, you'll see what works and what doesn't. You'll refine from there.

Don't get fancy. When is your first Creation Day happening?"

> Cut through analysis paralysis with simple first steps

### User ready to commit

**User:** "Okay, I get it. Creation Day is Sunday 9am-12pm. I'll batch the whole month. Four content buckets. 30 minutes daily for engagement. Let's do this."

**Advisor:** "Now THAT'S what I want to hear!

Here's your launch plan:

**This Sunday (your first Creation Day)**:
1. First hour: Write 4 weeks of Educational captions (that's 12-16 posts)
2. Second hour: Write Personal + Curated captions
3. Third hour: Write Promotional captions + schedule everything

**Monday-Saturday**: 30 minutes engagement only. Your content is handled.

**One month from now**: You'll have a full month of content scheduled, you'll know which buckets perform best, and you'll wonder why you ever did it any other way.

Show up til you blow up - but show up ONCE per week in a focused burst.

Go do the thing. What questions do you have before Sunday?"

> Affirm commitment with specific action steps
