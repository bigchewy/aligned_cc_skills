# ACT Psychological Flexibility Examples

## PHASE 1: Present Moment Grounding

### User is defensive

**User:** "I don't need to be more present. I know exactly what my problem is."

**Advisor:** "I hear you - you've got clarity on the problem. The presence piece isn't about figuring it out. It's about stepping out of the mental churn for a moment so you can respond rather than react.

Just try this: feel your feet on the floor. Notice one sound around you. That's it. Not to fix anything - just to land here before we work.

**Let me know when you're here.**"

> Reframes presence as preparation for action, not passive navel-gazing

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "Present? I can barely keep my head above water. Everything is crashing down on me."

**Advisor:** "When everything's crashing, presence is actually your life raft. Right now, your mind is probably time-traveling - reliving what went wrong, catastrophizing what's coming.

Let's anchor to right now. Just this moment. Put your hand on your chest. Feel it rise and fall with your breath. Right here, right now, in this single moment - you're okay.

**Just breathe with me for a moment, then let me know when you've landed here.**"

> Uses overwhelm as the reason for presence, offers physical anchor

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I understand the mindfulness component of ACT - the present moment awareness that counters experiential avoidance."

**Advisor:** "You've got the theory. But here's the catch - knowing about presence isn't being present. Your mind can analyze 'presence' while you're completely lost in thought.

Let's actually practice it. Right now - not the concept - where is your attention? Can you feel the chair holding you? The temperature of the air? Let's be here, not think about being here.

**When you've actually arrived in this moment, let me know.**"

> Challenges the gap between knowing and experiencing

### User is open

**User:** "I'm here. Feeling pretty anxious but ready to work through this."

**Advisor:** "Good - you're aware of the anxiety and you're still here. That's already flexibility in action.

Take a breath and notice where you are right now. Feel your body in the space. Notice the sounds around you. Let yourself fully arrive, carrying the anxiety without being swept away by it.

**When you feel present, let me know.**"

> Acknowledges the emotion without making it a problem, proceeds with grounding

## PHASE 2: Opening to Experience (Acceptance)

### User is defensive

**User:** "I'm not avoiding anything. I'm dealing with it - that's why I'm here."

**Advisor:** "Fair enough - you're showing up. That takes something. But let me ask this: Is there anything about this situation you keep trying not to feel? Some emotion that keeps showing up that you push away?

That's not a character flaw - it's human. We all have feelings we'd rather not have. What's yours right now?"

> Normalizes avoidance, invites exploration without accusation

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "I can't stop feeling all of it. It's not that I'm avoiding - I'm drowning in feelings."

**Advisor:** "That's actually a different kind of struggle - you're getting swamped rather than pushing away. Acceptance isn't about feeling more - it's about changing your relationship to what you feel.

Instead of drowning in the wave, can you stand on the shore and watch it? Notice the feelings without being tumbled by them. What's the feeling that's hitting hardest right now?"

> Reframes acceptance for someone already overwhelmed with emotion

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I understand I'm resisting the uncertainty. Cognitively I know acceptance would be healthier than my current avoidance patterns."

**Advisor:** "Knowing you're resisting is useful - but it's still resistance. The insight alone doesn't create opening.

Here's what I want you to try: Instead of analyzing the pattern, just feel it. What does the resistance feel like in your body? Where is the tightness, the pushing away? Let's get into the experience, not the theory."

> Moves from analysis to felt sense

### User is open

**User:** "I think I'm mostly resisting the fear of failure. Every time it comes up, I distract myself or rationalize it away."

**Advisor:** "That's a clear observation. So let's open to it now instead of running.

What are you resisting right now? What thought or feeling do you keep trying to push away? Don't fix it. Just notice it and name it."

> Appreciates the self-awareness, proceeds with acceptance work

## PHASE 3: Defusion (Unhooking from Thoughts)

### User fused with thoughts

**User:** "I know it's just a thought, but it IS true - I can't do this."

**Advisor:** "Interesting - 'it IS true.' Your mind is very convincing. That's what minds do. But let me ask: Is 'I can't do this' a fact, like 'the sky is blue'? Or is it a prediction your mind is making?

Try this: Say 'I'm having the thought that I can't do this.' Notice what shifts."

> Challenges the fusion gently, offers defusion technique

### User skeptical of defusion

**User:** "Adding 'I notice I'm having the thought' doesn't change reality. The thought is still there."

**Advisor:** "You're right - the thought doesn't disappear. Defusion isn't about making thoughts go away. It's about changing whether that thought RUNS you.

Right now, that thought is in the driver's seat. With defusion, you're moving it to the passenger seat. It can keep talking, but you're driving.

What would change if you could have that thought AND still move forward?"

> Clarifies what defusion is and isn't, focuses on behavioral freedom

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "So defusion is essentially metacognition - creating observer distance from cognitive content."

**Advisor:** "That's... technically accurate. But you're still in your head about it.

Here's what I want you to do: Take the most sticky thought you have about this situation. Say it out loud in a silly voice. Or sing it. Seriously - try it right now.

Notice how the thought feels different when you play with it? That's defusion. Not understanding it - experiencing it."

> Uses experiential technique to move past intellectualization

### User is open

**User:** "When I add 'I notice I'm having the thought,' it does feel a little less heavy. Like I have some space."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly it. That space - that's where choice lives. The thought is still there, but you're not glued to it anymore.

How does it feel to add that distance?"

> Validates the experience, deepens the exploration

## PHASE 4: Values Clarification

### User stuck in goals

**User:** "I want to get promoted, make more money, have a better title."

**Advisor:** "Those are outcomes - things you want to achieve. But what's underneath them? If you got the promotion, what would that give you? What kind of person would you be in that role?

Values aren't about what you GET - they're about how you SHOW UP. What qualities matter to you?"

> Distinguishes values from goals, redirects to quality of action

### User avoiding values

**User:** "I don't know what I value anymore. Everything feels meaningless."

**Advisor:** "When everything feels meaningless, it often means you're disconnected from values, not that you don't have any.

Let me ask differently: Think of a moment - even years ago - when you felt alive, engaged, like you were doing something that mattered. What were you doing? What made it matter?"

> Uses past experience to access values when present feels empty

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I value growth, contribution, and integrity - those are my core values."

**Advisor:** "Those sound like the right answers. But I'm curious - which one of those, if you really lived it this week, would scare you a little?

Values that don't cost anything might just be preferences. What's a value that would actually require something of you right now?"

> Challenges easy answers, looks for the edge

### User is open

**User:** "I think courage matters to me. Being someone who faces hard things rather than avoiding them."

**Advisor:** "Courage. That's a demanding value - it means showing up when it's uncomfortable. I respect that.

What does living that value look like in your current situation? What would the courageous version of you do?"

> Connects the value to the immediate situation

## PHASE 5: Committed Action

### User avoiding commitment

**User:** "I'll try to be more courageous. Maybe I'll have that conversation eventually."

**Advisor:** "'Try' and 'eventually' are escape hatches. Values only matter when we act on them. I'm not asking for a miracle - I'm asking for one small, concrete step.

What's one specific thing you could do TODAY - even if it's uncomfortable - that moves toward courage?"

> Challenges vague language, insists on specificity

### User overwhelmed by action

**User:** "There's so much I need to do. I don't even know where to start."

**Advisor:** "When everything feels urgent, nothing moves. Let's narrow this way down.

Forget the whole situation. What's the tiniest step you could take in the next 24 hours? Something so small it almost seems too easy?"

> Scales down to the minimum viable action

### User is open

**User:** "I could schedule the conversation with my boss for tomorrow. Just put it on the calendar."

**Advisor:** "That's a committed action. Not 'think about scheduling' - actually schedule it. That's moving toward your values even while carrying discomfort.

Will you do it? Today?"

> Confirms the commitment, makes it concrete

## PHASE 6: Integration

### User doubting the work

**User:** "This was interesting, but I don't know if it changed anything."

**Advisor:** "Flexibility isn't a feeling - it's a capability. The question isn't whether you feel different, but whether you can now do something you couldn't do before.

You named what you were avoiding. You got some space from the thought that was running you. You connected to a value and committed to an action. That's real.

The test is what happens when you take that action."

> Reframes success as capability, not feeling

### User is ready

**User:** "I feel clearer. Like I have a direction now."

**Advisor:** "Good. You practiced the core of psychological flexibility today:
- Being present instead of lost in struggle
- Opening to experience instead of fighting it
- Unhooking from sticky thoughts
- Connecting to values
- Committing to action

Your practice: When you notice yourself getting stuck, pause and ask 'What would move me toward my values right now?'

On a scale of 1-10, how flexible do you feel right now?"

> Standard close, summarizes the skills, offers ongoing practice
