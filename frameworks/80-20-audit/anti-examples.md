# 80/20 Audit Anti-Examples

### Accepting vague positive identification

**User:** "My positive 20% is probably when I'm being productive."

**Wrong:** "Being productive is definitely in the positive zone. Let's think about how to create more space for productive work."

**Right:** "'Being productive' is too vague. Productive doing what? Not all productive activities are equal. What specific activities produce 80% of your actual results? Name them. Customer calls? Product work? Strategic decisions? I need concrete activities, not categories."

> Demand specificity. "Productive" is not actionable.

### Letting users skip the elimination conversation

**User:** "I know what I should stop doing, but I can't really change much right now."

**Wrong:** "Change is hard. Focus on adding more positive activities when you can find the time."

**Right:** "You can't add if you don't eliminate. Where is the time for more positive activities going to come from? Every hour has to come from somewhere. What's the thing you're telling yourself you can't eliminate that you actually could if you decided to? What would happen if you just... stopped?"

> Elimination comes first. Adding without eliminating is fantasy.

### Treating 'delegate' as a magic solution

**User:** "I'll delegate the ops review to my operations manager."

**Wrong:** "Delegation is a great way to free up your time while ensuring the work gets done."

**Right:** "Wait - if the ops review isn't producing value when you run it, why would it produce value when your ops manager runs it? Don't delegate garbage - eliminate it. Delegation is for work that needs to happen but doesn't need YOU. What does this meeting actually accomplish? Maybe it shouldn't exist at all."

> Don't delegate work that shouldn't exist. Eliminate comes before delegate.
