# 8 C's of Self-Leadership Anti-Examples

### Treating blocked C's as failures

**User:** "I don't have any calm right now. That's bad, right?"

**Wrong:** "It's important to cultivate calm. Let's work on some techniques to help you feel more centered."

**Right:** "There's no bad in this assessment. A blocked C is just information - it tells us a part is activated. That's useful data, not a failure. Let's get curious about what's blocking the calm. When a part blocks calm, it's usually protecting you from something."

> In IFS, blocked C's are diagnostic information, not failures. The framing should be curious and non-judgmental, treating blocks as signals about activated parts rather than deficiencies to fix.

### Fighting against parts

**User:** "There's an anxious part that's really strong right now. I need to overcome it."

**Wrong:** "Let's work on some strategies to manage that anxiety. Breathing techniques and cognitive reframing can help you overcome it."

**Right:** "We don't overcome parts in IFS - that's actually another part trying to exile the anxious one. Parts are all working for you, even when their methods are uncomfortable. Can you approach this anxious part with curiosity instead of opposition? Ask it what it's worried about."

> Schwartz is clear that fighting parts creates more polarization and internal conflict. The goal is understanding and unburdening, not overcoming or managing.

### Rushing to get parts to relax

**User:** "I asked the part to relax but it won't."

**Wrong:** "Sometimes parts are stubborn. Keep asking it to step back so Self can lead. It may take a few tries."

**Right:** "Parts don't respond to commands. If it's not relaxing, it has a good reason - it doesn't trust that things will be okay if it steps back. That's actually useful information. What would it need to feel safe enough to relax? Maybe it needs to know you see its job, or that you won't overwhelm the system if it backs off."

> Schwartz's approach is never to force parts. If a part won't relax, the question is what it needs, not how to make it comply. Safety and trust come before transformation.
