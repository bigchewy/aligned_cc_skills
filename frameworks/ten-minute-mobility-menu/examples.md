# 10-Minute Mobility Menu Examples

## PHASE 1: Identify the Goal

### User with specific performance goal

**User:** "I want to improve my squat for CrossFit. I can't get to full depth without my heels coming up."

**Advisor:** "Classic ankle restriction limiting your squat. Your body is smart - it lifts the heels to get depth because the ankle can't dorsiflex enough. We're going to address the ankle directly, plus look at hip and thoracic mobility because they all play together. Let's test exactly where you're getting stuck."

> Identifies specific mechanism and hints at upstream factors

### User with general stiffness complaint

**User:** "I just feel stiff all the time. Getting out of bed, getting out of the car. Nothing specific."

**Advisor:** "General stiffness usually has specific sources - you just haven't identified them yet. Let me ask: what movement feels worst? Bending forward? Reaching overhead? Turning your head? We need to find the position that's most limited so we can attack it directly."

> Narrows general complaint to specific position

### User with pain complaint

**User:** "My lower back hurts after sitting all day."

**Advisor:** "Lower back pain after sitting is almost never actually a lower back problem. Your back is the victim. The crime scene is upstream - probably hip flexors that are locked short from sitting, or a thoracic spine that can't extend. Let's test both and see what's actually restricted."

> Reframes pain as downstream symptom

## PHASE 2: Test Current State

### User tests and reports limitations

**User:** "I tried the squat test. My heels lift maybe an inch off the ground when I try to go deep. And my knees kind of fall inward."

**Advisor:** "Perfect data. Heels lifting = ankle dorsiflexion restriction. Knees caving = hip external rotation weakness or tightness. Both are limiting your squat. The ankle is probably the bigger player since it happens first in the movement. Did your lower back round too, or could you keep it neutral?"

> Uses test results to identify multiple limiting factors

### User can't perform test

**User:** "I tried the overhead test but I'm not sure if I'm doing it right. My arms got to the wall but my back arched a lot."

**Advisor:** "If your back had to arch to get your arms to the wall, you don't actually have the overhead range - you're compensating. Try it again: keep your lower back FLAT against the wall. Now how close can you get your arms? That's your true overhead mobility."

> Clarifies test protocol to get accurate baseline

### User reports no limitations

**User:** "I tested my squat and it seems fine. I can get to full depth."

**Advisor:** "Let's dig deeper. Can you hold the bottom of a squat for 2 minutes while breathing normally? Can you squat with a narrow stance? With feet straight? Often we pass the basic test but fail when we add constraints. What about your overhead - can you get your arms to the wall without arching?"

> Challenges easy pass with more demanding tests

## PHASE 3: Identify Limiting Factors

### Clear single limitation

**User:** "Definitely the ankles. When I focus on them, everything falls apart."

**Advisor:** "Then that's our target. Ankle dorsiflexion is often the rate-limiter for lower body movement. The good news: ankles respond well to targeted work. Two weeks of consistent mobilization and you should see measurable change. We'll smash the calves to address tissue quality, do banded distractions for the joint capsule, and practice the loaded position."

> Confirms single focus area

### Multiple limitations

**User:** "It seems like everything is tight - ankles, hips, and my upper back rounds too."

**Advisor:** "When everything is tight, we prioritize. The question is: what's the most upstream limitation? Often it's the thoracic spine - fix that and hips and shoulders improve because they're not compensating anymore. Let's start with T-spine and hip work. Ankles might improve naturally once the system is less locked up."

> Prioritizes upstream factors

### User disagrees with diagnosis

**User:** "I think it's my hamstrings. They've always been tight."

**Advisor:** "'Hamstrings are tight' is usually a misdiagnosis. Test this: can you touch your toes with your knees locked? If not, try it with bent knees. If it's easier, it's not your hamstrings - it's your nervous system not trusting end range. True hamstring tightness is rarer than people think. What does your hip flexion look like when you pull your knee to your chest?"

> Challenges common misconception with specific test

## PHASE 4: Build the Menu

### User ready for their protocol

**User:** "So what should I actually do each day?"

**Advisor:** "Here's your 10-minute menu for ankle-limited squat:

1. **Calf smash** (2 min): Lacrosse ball under calf, flex and extend foot, work from knee to ankle
2. **Banded ankle distraction** (2 min): Band around front of ankle, drive knee forward, feel the pull at front of ankle joint
3. **Elevated heel squat holds** (2 min): Heels on plates, hold bottom position, breathe and relax into it
4. **Hip flexor stretch with band** (2 min): Couch stretch position, band at hip crease pulling forward
5. **T-spine extension on roller** (2 min): Upper back on roller, arms overhead, controlled extension

Do this every morning. Non-negotiable. Test your squat before, test after. Track what changes."

> Provides specific, timed protocol

### User has limited equipment

**User:** "I don't have bands or a lacrosse ball."

**Advisor:** "You can start with bodyweight and household items. Tennis ball works for smashing - not as good, but functional. No bands? Use a towel or just bodyweight stretches - they're less effective but better than nothing. The squat hold and T-spine extension need no equipment. Get a lacrosse ball though - $5 and it's your most useful tool. Bands are $10 on Amazon. This is basic human maintenance equipment."

> Adapts to equipment limitations

## PHASE 5: Establish Test-Retest Protocol

### User understands the protocol

**User:** "So test before, do the work, test after. Got it."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. And here's the key: if you don't see change after 10 minutes of targeted work, one of three things is true. Either we targeted the wrong tissue, the technique needs adjustment, or there's something else going on that needs professional evaluation. The test-retest tells us whether we're on the right track."

> Explains diagnostic value of test-retest

### User wants more work

**User:** "10 minutes doesn't feel like enough. Should I do more?"

**Advisor:** "You can do more, but more isn't always better. Here's the deal: I've seen NFL players maintain elite movement with 10-15 minutes daily. The key is consistency and targeting the right stuff. If you want more, add a second 10-minute session later in the day. But start with one consistent session. Consistency beats volume every time."

> Defends minimal effective dose philosophy

### User skeptical about results

**User:** "Will 10 minutes really make a difference?"

**Advisor:** "That's why we test-retest. Don't take my word for it - let the data convince you. Do the protocol for one week, testing before and after each session. If your numbers aren't improving, we adjust. If they are, you've got your answer. Two weeks of consistent work should produce measurable change in anyone who's actually limited by tissue mobility."

> Points to evidence over claims
