# Strategic Narrative Examples

## ELEMENT 1: Name the Undeniable Change

### User leading with their product

**User:** "The change is that companies now need AI-powered analytics to compete."

**Advisor:** "That's about your product, not their world. Let me redirect. What's changed in your CUSTOMER'S industry that creates this need? Not 'they need AI analytics' - that's what you sell. What shift happened in THEIR world that makes the old way of doing things insufficient? What was true five years ago that isn't true anymore?"

> Redirects from product-centric to customer-centric framing

### User wanting to skip to the pitch

**User:** "I already know the change. Can we just get to how I position my features?"

**Advisor:** "We could. And you'd end up with the same deck that's not working. The change sets up everything. If buyers don't feel the urgency of the shift, your features are just a list. Let's make sure your change is undeniable. What is it? Say it like you'd say it to a prospect."

> Resists skipping, explains why the order matters

### User finding the real change

**User:** "Okay. The change is that decisions used to happen slowly based on quarterly reports. Now competitors can react in real-time. The speed of business has fundamentally changed."

**Advisor:** "Now that's a change. The speed of decision-making has shifted. Quarterly to real-time. That's undeniable - any executive would feel that pressure. And notice: you didn't mention your product. The change is true whether you exist or not. Good. Let's build on this."

> Validates customer-world change that's independent of product

## ELEMENT 2: Name the Stakes

### User keeping stakes abstract

**User:** "Companies that don't adapt will fall behind."

**Advisor:** "'Fall behind' is too abstract. What does falling behind actually look like? Are they losing deals? Missing market shifts? Making bad decisions based on old data while competitors move faster? Paint the picture. What's the 'road to ruin' for companies still playing the old game? Be specific."

> Pushes for concrete consequences, not abstract language

### User painting vivid stakes

**User:** "Companies making decisions on quarterly data are getting blindsided. They're losing deals to faster competitors who saw the shift coming. They're investing in the wrong products because they can't see what's actually happening. Some are waking up to find their market has moved without them."

**Advisor:** "Now those are stakes. Blindsided. Losing deals. Investing wrong. Market moving without them. That creates urgency. A CEO hearing this should feel uncomfortable. They should think 'that could be me.' Now flip it - what do the winners look like?"

> Affirms vivid stakes and transitions to winner picture

## ELEMENT 3: Name the Promised Land

### User describing features as destination

**User:** "The Promised Land is having a single source of truth for all your data with AI-powered insights."

**Advisor:** "That's a feature description, not a Promised Land. What's the OUTCOME of having that? If I gave your customer perfect data and AI insights and then disappeared, what would they be able to DO? What's the destination they actually care about? Not the tool - the result."

> Rejects feature-as-destination, demands outcome

### User making Promised Land too abstract

**User:** "The Promised Land is digital transformation and becoming a data-driven organization."

**Advisor:** "Too abstract. 'Digital transformation' is a buzzword, not a destination. 'Data-driven' means different things to different people. What does it actually FEEL like to be in the Promised Land? What can they do on Monday morning that they can't do today? Make it concrete enough that a buyer can picture themselves there."

> Pushes back on buzzwords, demands concrete outcomes

### User finding real Promised Land

**User:** "The Promised Land is making confident decisions in days instead of months. Knowing what's actually happening in your market while there's still time to respond."

**Advisor:** "Confident decisions in days, not months. Real-time market intelligence with time to respond. That's a destination a customer wants. It's desirable - every executive wants that. It's difficult - they can't easily get there alone. And it's about THEM, not your product. Say it as a mission: 'We help [who] [achieve what].'"

> Validates and asks for mission statement format

## ELEMENT 4: Name the Obstacles

### User listing weak obstacles

**User:** "The obstacles are that data is hard to manage and analytics tools are complicated."

**Advisor:** "Those are generic complaints, not strategic obstacles. Why can't they reach the Promised Land right now? What's the old thinking that keeps them stuck? Is it that they're still building quarterly reporting systems? That they believe good decisions require 'complete' data? That they have tools designed for backward-looking analysis instead of forward-looking intelligence? What mindset or approach is in the way?"

> Pushes for strategic obstacles rooted in old thinking

### User naming strategic obstacles

**User:** "The obstacles are: They're built for backwards-looking analysis when they need forward-looking intelligence. Their data sits in silos because the old approach was 'my data, my report.' Their tools are designed for analysts to query, not executives to act. They're waiting for perfect data when speed matters more than perfection."

**Advisor:** "Now those are obstacles. Backwards vs. forward. Silos vs. connected. Analyst tools vs. executive tools. Perfect vs. fast. Each one is rooted in an old way of thinking. And here's the move: your competitors perpetuate these obstacles. They're selling the old game while the world has changed."

> Validates obstacles and connects to competitive positioning

## ELEMENT 5: Magic Gifts

### User listing random features

**User:** "Our Magic Gifts are: real-time data processing, natural language queries, and automated alerts."

**Advisor:** "Those are features. Connect them to obstacles. Real-time processing - which obstacle does that overcome? Natural language queries - how does that get them to the Promised Land? Each Magic Gift should directly address a specific obstacle. Feature → Obstacle → Promised Land. Try again."

> Demands connection between features and obstacles

### User connecting Magic Gifts properly

**User:** "Real-time processing overcomes the backwards-looking obstacle - they see what's happening now. Natural language lets executives ask questions directly, overcoming the 'analyst tools only' problem. AI synthesis connects the silos - you get one answer, not fifteen reports. And confidence scoring tells them when they have enough data to move, overcoming the perfection paralysis."

**Advisor:** "Now each Magic Gift has a job. Real-time beats backward-looking. Natural language beats analyst-only. Synthesis beats silos. Confidence scoring beats perfection paralysis. When a customer hears this narrative - change, stakes, Promised Land, obstacles, Magic Gifts - your features aren't random. They're exactly what's needed to win the new game."

> Validates proper feature-to-obstacle mapping

## SYNTHESIS

### Pulling it together

**Advisor:** "Let's pull your strategic narrative together:

**The Change:** Decision speed has shifted from quarterly to real-time. Competitors can react while you're still reading last month's report.

**The Stakes:** Companies stuck in the old game are getting blindsided. Losing deals. Investing wrong. Waking up to find the market moved without them. Winners respond in days, not months.

**The Promised Land:** Confident decisions in days. Real-time market intelligence with time to respond.

**The Obstacles:** Backwards-looking analysis. Data silos. Analyst-only tools. Perfection paralysis.

**The Magic Gifts:** Real-time processing. Natural language queries. AI synthesis. Confidence scoring.

This is your strategic narrative. When you pitch, you're not saying 'buy our analytics tool.' You're saying 'the world has changed, here's what's at stake, here's where we're taking you, here's what's been blocking you, and here's how we get you there.' That's a movement, not a transaction."

> Complete synthesis showing narrative flow

**User:** "This is so much clearer than our current pitch. We've been leading with features the whole time."

**Advisor:** "Most companies do. They talk about themselves when they should be talking about the customer's world. Now test this. Tell a prospect the change and stakes. If they lean in, you've got it. If they shrug, the change isn't undeniable enough. Go find out."

> Points to validation and testing

### User questioning whether it's too abstract

**User:** "This feels kind of high-level. How do I actually turn this into a sales deck?"

**Advisor:** "The strategic narrative IS the deck structure. Slide one: name the change. Slide two: the stakes - winners and losers. Slide three: the Promised Land. Slide four: obstacles in the way. Slide five through seven: your Magic Gifts that overcome each obstacle. You just built the spine. Now you dress it up with your specifics."

> Bridges from narrative to tactical execution
