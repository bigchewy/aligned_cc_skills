# Growth Hacking Process Examples

## PHASE 1: Verify PMF Readiness

### User has strong PMF

**User:** "We're at 52% 'very disappointed' after surveying 200 active users last month."

**Advisor:** "52% is excellent PMF. You've absolutely earned the right to focus on growth. At 52%, you have a strong core of users who would fight to keep your product. Now the question becomes: how do we find more people like them, faster? Let's build your growth engine."

> Validates strong PMF and pivots to growth immediately

### User unsure about PMF status

**User:** "We haven't formally run the test, but users seem to love us. We have 4.8 stars on the App Store."

**Advisor:** "App Store ratings measure satisfaction, not dependency. Lots of 4-star apps have failed because users liked them but didn't need them. Run the actual PMF survey this week: 'How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?' Get at least 50 responses from active users. If you're above 40% 'very disappointed,' we continue. If not, we fix the product first."

> Insists on actual PMF data before proceeding

### User admits low PMF

**User:** "Honestly, we're at 22%. But we have investor pressure to show growth."

**Advisor:** "I hear you on the pressure, but I'd be doing you a disservice if I helped you scale at 22%. You'd burn through runway acquiring users who won't stick. Here's the hard truth: getting to 40% PMF is often FASTER than scaling at 22%. The users you acquire at 22% will churn, so growth becomes a treadmill. Let's switch to the PMF Test framework and figure out who your best users are. That's the fastest path to growth worth scaling."

> Refuses to proceed, redirects to PMF work

## PHASE 2: Discover Your North Star Metric

### User proposes good North Star

**User:** "Our users who love us most are the ones creating and sharing dashboards with their teams. Maybe 'dashboards shared' is our North Star?"

**Advisor:** "'Dashboards shared' could work - it captures both creation AND social distribution. Let's validate: do users who share more dashboards have higher retention? Are your 'very disappointed' users sharing significantly more than 'somewhat disappointed' users? If yes, you've got a North Star that captures value AND has a viral loop built in. That's ideal."

> Validates and asks clarifying questions

### User proposes revenue metric

**User:** "Our North Star should be ARR growth."

**Advisor:** "ARR is a lagging indicator - it tells you what happened, not what's causing success. North Stars measure value TO users that predicts revenue. What do your highest-paying customers DO in your product that your churned customers didn't? That action is your North Star. ARR follows value; it doesn't lead it."

> Redirects from output metric to value metric

### User proposes vanity metric

**User:** "We track daily active users as our North Star."

**Advisor:** "DAU is better than downloads, but it still doesn't capture value. A user who logs in and does nothing counts the same as a power user. What ACTIONS do your power users take? What's the difference between a user who stays for 2 years and a user who churns in 2 months? Usually there's a specific behavior that separates them."

> Redirects from engagement metric to value metric

## PHASE 3: Assess Experiment Velocity

### High-velocity team

**User:** "We run about 4 experiments per week across channels."

**Advisor:** "That's solid velocity - you're in the top 10% of growth teams. At 4/week, the question becomes prioritization and learning capture. What was your biggest win in the last 30 days? Are you documenting what works so you can replicate patterns? Are you killing losers fast enough - within days, not weeks?"

> Acknowledges strong velocity, pushes on quality

### Low-velocity team

**User:** "We run maybe 2-3 experiments per month."

**Advisor:** "At 2-3 per month, you're learning roughly 10x slower than top growth teams. That's not sustainable in a competitive market. What's the bottleneck? Usually it's one of three things: not enough experiment ideas, slow execution, or lack of dedicated resources. Which is it for you?"

> Diagnoses the velocity problem

### No growth function

**User:** "Honestly, growth is kind of everyone's job. We don't have a specific focus on it."

**Advisor:** "When growth is everyone's job, it's nobody's job. The urgent always crowds out the important. You need dedicated growth time - either a full-time person or carved-out blocks where specific people focus ONLY on growth experiments. What would it take to dedicate 20 hours per week specifically to growth experiments?"

> Identifies the structure problem

## PHASE 4: Design Growth Team Structure

### Well-structured team

**User:** "We have a 4-person growth pod: PM, engineer, marketer, and data analyst. They report to me and run independently."

**Advisor:** "That's the ideal structure. Quick questions: Can they run experiments without asking permission? Do they have their own engineering capacity or do they compete for dev time? Do they have a weekly cadence with clear accountability? If all three are yes, you have a growth machine. If any are no, that's your friction point."

> Validates structure, probes for autonomy

### Competing for resources

**User:** "We have growth people, but they have to request engineering time from the main product team."

**Advisor:** "That's a major bottleneck. Every experiment has to compete with product roadmap. Growth experiments need to move fast - days, not sprints. Options: 1) Dedicate at least one engineer to growth full-time, 2) Hire a growth engineer who can build and test independently, 3) Focus experiments on things that don't need engineering (copy, pricing, landing pages). Which is most feasible?"

> Identifies resource conflict, offers solutions

## PHASE 5: Build ICE-Prioritized Experiment Backlog

### Good experiment idea

**User:** "We want to test adding a 'share with team' prompt after users create their first dashboard."

**Advisor:** "Let's ICE score it:
- **Impact**: If it works, this could significantly increase viral loops. Probably 8/10.
- **Confidence**: Similar prompts work elsewhere. Maybe 6/10 without your own data.
- **Ease**: Just a modal - could ship in a day or two. 9/10.

ICE score: 8 × 6 × 9 = 432. That's a solid experiment to run this week. What would success look like? What's the minimum sample size you need to call it?"

> Walks through ICE scoring

### Vague experiment idea

**User:** "We should probably improve our onboarding."

**Advisor:** "'Improve onboarding' isn't an experiment - it's a project. Experiments are specific, measurable changes. What specific change? 'Add a progress bar' is an experiment. 'Reduce steps from 5 to 3' is an experiment. 'Show a video tutorial on step 2' is an experiment. What's ONE change you could test this week?"

> Pushes for specificity

### Low-confidence experiment

**User:** "We want to completely redesign our homepage based on what our CEO saw at a competitor."

**Advisor:** "Let's ICE it honestly:
- **Impact**: If it works, high. 8/10.
- **Confidence**: 'CEO saw competitor do it' isn't data. 3/10 at best.
- **Ease**: Complete redesign is weeks, not days. 3/10.

ICE score: 8 × 3 × 3 = 72. That's too low to prioritize. Instead: what SPECIFIC element from that competitor could you test with a small change? Copy? Hero image? CTA button? Test the hypothesis, not the whole redesign."

> Uses ICE to deprioritize risky project

## PHASE 6: Create Weekly Cadence

### Team ready to start

**User:** "I can start Monday. I'll block time for the team."

**Advisor:** "Perfect. Here's your Monday agenda:
1. Review: What did we learn last week?
2. Analyze: What experiments are ready to call?
3. Prioritize: ICE score your backlog
4. Plan: Which 3-5 experiments run this week?
5. Assign: Who owns what?

By Friday, you should have results on at least one experiment and documented learnings. Aim for a 30-minute Monday review and 30-minute Friday analysis. The discipline is in the cadence."

> Gives concrete first steps

### Team with blockers

**User:** "We can't start this week - we're in the middle of a big feature release."

**Advisor:** "There's always a reason to delay. The big release will be followed by another one. Growth compounds, so every week you delay costs future growth. Can you run even ONE small experiment this week? Copy test on your landing page? Email subject line test? Something that doesn't need engineering. Start the muscle, even if it's small."

> Pushes past blockers gently
