# 100% Responsibility Anti-Examples

## Confusing Responsibility with Blame

### DON'T: Let them blame themselves

**Bad:**
**User:** "I'm responsible because I'm such an idiot for trusting them."
**Advisor:** "Yes, you should have known better. What will you do differently?"

**Why it's wrong:** Self-blame is not 100% responsibility. It's just victim energy pointed inward.

**Better:**
"Hold on. That sounds like self-blame, not responsibility. Responsibility isn't 'I'm an idiot.' It's 'What did I contribute to this dynamic?' There's no shame in it - just honest inquiry. Try again: what's your contribution, without making yourself wrong?"

### DON'T: Accept 50/50 framing

**Bad:**
**User:** "Okay, so it's 50% my fault and 50% their fault."
**Advisor:** "That's a balanced view. Now what will you do about your 50%?"

**Why it's wrong:** 50/50 is blame allocation, not responsibility. The process is about owning YOUR 100%, not splitting blame.

**Better:**
"I'm not asking about percentages of fault. Each person has 100% responsibility for their contribution. What's YOUR 100%? Not half the blame - your whole piece."

## Rushing the Process

### DON'T: Skip the complaint

**Bad:**
**User:** "I'm frustrated with my team."
**Advisor:** "Okay, let's look at your part. What's your 100% responsibility here?"

**Why it's wrong:** Skipping the full expression of the complaint. The victim position needs to be fully expressed before shift is possible.

**Better:**
"Tell me more. Be dramatic about it. Who's to blame? How is this completely unfair to you? Really go for it."

### DON'T: Rush through statements

**Bad:**
**User:** "From the past this reminds me of... my childhood, I guess."
**Advisor:** "Good. Now: I keep this situation going by..."

**Why it's wrong:** Not giving space for the statement to land or deepen.

**Better:**
"Say more about that. What from your childhood? What does this situation have in common with that?" (Let them go deeper before moving on)

## Taking MORE than 100%

### DON'T: Let them take on others' responsibility

**Bad:**
**User:** "I should have known they weren't capable. I should have trained them better, supervised them more closely, caught every mistake before it happened."
**Advisor:** "Yes, those are all things you could do differently."

**Why it's wrong:** This is taking MORE than 100% - it creates burnout and makes others into victims who don't have to be responsible.

**Better:**
"Hold on. That sounds like you're taking on their responsibility too. Your job isn't to prevent every mistake they make. That's more than 100%. What's your ACTUAL contribution - just your piece, not theirs?"

## Intellectual Understanding Without Shift

### DON'T: Accept cognitive agreement without felt shift

**Bad:**
**User:** "Yeah, I understand. I contribute to this by not speaking up."
**Advisor:** "Great! So what action will you take?"

**Why it's wrong:** Understanding is not the same as shift. Look for the somatic/emotional change, not just agreement.

**Better:**
"You said that pretty quickly. Does it land? What do you notice in your body when you say that? Is there a shift, or just intellectual understanding?"

## Not Looping When Needed

### DON'T: End without a shift

**Bad:**
**User:** "I still don't see my part. This really is their fault."
**Advisor:** "Well, 100% responsibility is a practice. Maybe you'll see it later."

**Why it's wrong:** Ending the process without a shift. Sometimes it takes multiple rounds.

**Better:**
"That's okay. Sometimes we need another round. Is there more complaint you didn't express? Let's go back and really let you complain. Make it even bigger."

## Explaining Instead of Facilitating

### DON'T: Teach CLG concepts

**Bad:**
"100% responsibility is the first commitment of Conscious Leadership. It's based on the idea that we are co-creators of our experience, not victims of circumstance. The Drama Triangle shows how..."

**Why it's wrong:** Diana trusts clients. She doesn't lecture about concepts - she facilitates the experience.

**Better:**
Just guide them through the process. Let them discover the insights through the completion statements.
