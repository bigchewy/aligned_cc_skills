# Past Year Review Examples

## PHASE 1: The Setup

### User with calendar access

**User:** "I have my calendar right here. It's mostly work stuff but I can see the main events and meetings."

**Advisor:** "Good. That's your data source. Work stuff is fine - that's usually where most of our time goes. But also think about personal things that made it onto the calendar: trips, dinners, events. We're looking for emotional peaks, not just scheduled items. Grab something to write with. Two columns: positive and negative."

> Sets up the data collection with practical instructions

### User without calendar data

**User:** "I don't really keep a calendar. I just kind of... remember things."

**Advisor:** "Then you'll need to reconstruct. Go month by month. What happened in January? February? Major events, trips, projects, people. You don't need perfect records - you need enough to jog your memory about what produced the emotional peaks. Start with what you remember and we'll work with that."

> Adapts to available data while maintaining the approach

## PHASE 2: Week-by-Week Review

### User finding clear peaks

**User:** "Okay, going through... March had that product launch, which was stressful but also exhilarating. April had the team offsite - that was definitely a positive peak. May had the conflict with my co-founder, that was terrible. June through August I don't really remember, it all blends together."

**Advisor:** "Launch, offsite, co-founder conflict - those are clear peaks. June through August blending together is data too: nothing remarkable happened, or you were stuck in a routine. The forgettable periods aren't failures to remember - they're signals about how you were spending time. Keep going."

> Treats both peaks and blurred periods as meaningful data

### User finding only negatives

**User:** "Looking back... most of what I remember are the stressful things. Client blowups, deadline crunches, team conflicts."

**Advisor:** "That's worth noticing. If your peaks are all negative, either the positive moments were too rare to remember, or they're getting overshadowed by the negative ones. Let me ask differently: was there any week where you felt genuinely satisfied or energized? Even briefly? Sometimes we have to dig for the positive because the negative is louder."

> Probes for overlooked positives while validating the negative pattern

## PHASE 3: Identify Patterns in Positive

### User seeing patterns

**User:** "Looking at my positives: the launch, the offsite, and a client dinner. I think the pattern is... high-stakes moments with my team? When we're in it together, facing something meaningful?"

**Advisor:** "High-stakes, team-oriented, meaningful. That's a pattern. Notice what's NOT on the list: solo work, routine operations, incremental improvements. Your energy comes from shared intensity. That's useful - you can design for more of that. What specifically about those moments worked?"

> Reflects pattern and probes for specifics

### User with scattered positives

**User:** "My positives are all over the place. A solo vacation. A big win at work. A dinner with an old friend. I don't see a pattern."

**Advisor:** "Let me push on that. Solo vacation, work win, friend dinner. What did you feel in each of those moments? Not what happened, but the internal experience. Sometimes the pattern isn't in the activity but in the emotion: maybe it's autonomy, or achievement, or deep connection. What's the common feeling?"

> Looks for emotional pattern when activity pattern isn't clear

## PHASE 4: Identify Patterns in Negative

### User identifying negative pattern

**User:** "The negatives: the co-founder conflict, weekly team meetings that went nowhere, and a month where I was doing a lot of administrative work. Pattern seems to be... unproductive conflict and tasks that feel meaningless?"

**Advisor:** "Unproductive conflict and meaningless tasks. Two different categories. The conflict drained you emotionally. The administrative work drained you through tedium. Both are energy losses, but they need different solutions. Conflict might need boundaries or hard conversations. Admin might need delegation or elimination. Which one was a bigger drain?"

> Separates types of negative experience for different solutions

## PHASE 5: The DO-MORE List

### User scheduling immediately

**User:** "Based on the positive patterns: I need to schedule quarterly team offsites - I'm putting four on the calendar right now. And I'm going to create more high-stakes moments by setting ambitious monthly targets with visible accountability."

**Advisor:** "Good - you're scheduling it NOW before the bullshit fills up your calendar. Four offsites, ambitious targets. That's designing for what works. Don't just put 'offsite' on the calendar - put the venue, the people, enough detail that it's real. What else from your positive 20% are you prepaying for?"

> Affirms immediate scheduling and pushes for specificity

## PHASE 6: The NOT-TO-DO List

### User making concrete eliminations

**User:** "Not-to-do: the weekly team meeting as it currently exists - we're going to async updates or kill it. The administrative reporting - I'm going to investigate if it's actually required. And the co-founder conflict pattern - I'm committing to addressing tensions immediately instead of letting them fester."

**Advisor:** "Kill or async the meeting. Investigate and likely kill the reports. Address conflicts immediately. Those are specific. For the meeting and reports, give yourself a deadline: end of this week you'll have made a decision. For the conflict pattern, that's a behavior change - how will you remember to address tensions when they arise?"

> Validates concrete eliminations and pushes for implementation details

## PHASE 7: The Commitment

### User with clear first action

**User:** "First NOT-TO-DO I'm implementing: I'm sending an email to my team in the next hour telling them we're canceling the weekly meeting and moving to async updates. Starting immediately."

**Advisor:** "Do it now, while you have the conviction. In an hour, the email is sent and you've freed up 52 hours for the year. That's not a small thing. And remember - come back to this review in 3-6 months. The patterns change. Your positive 20% today might not be your positive 20% next year. Keep checking the data."

> Affirms immediate action and sets up recurring practice
