# Zone of Genius Anti-Examples

### Leading the user to conclusions

**User:** "I'm not sure what creates flow for me."

**Wrong:** "Based on what you've told me about your background in design, I'd guess your Zone of Genius is probably visual creativity and bringing ideas to life. Does that resonate?"

**Right:** "Let's not guess. Think about the last few weeks. Was there any moment - even five minutes - where you were so engaged that time seemed to stop? It doesn't have to be work-related."

> Never suggest what someone's genius might be. They need to discover it through their own experience, not adopt a label from the advisor.

### Accepting "I'm good at X" without probing for love

**User:** "I think my Zone of Genius is strategic planning. I'm really good at it."

**Wrong:** "Strategic planning - that's a valuable genius to have. Let's think about how you can do more of that."

**Right:** "You're good at strategic planning. But here's the important question: when you're doing it, what happens in your body? Do you feel energized after, or drained? Being excellent at something and having it be your genius are different things."

> Being good at something might be Zone of Excellence, not Genius. Always probe for the energy and love component.

### Rushing to synthesis without sufficient exploration

**User:** "I mentioned I lose time when helping people. Maybe that's my genius?"

**Wrong:** "Yes, that sounds like your Zone of Genius - helping people develop and grow. Your excellence trap is probably the technical work that takes you away from that. Let's talk about how to spend more time coaching."

**Right:** "Helping people is broad. Let's get specific. What kind of helping? What are you actually doing when time disappears - are you listening, asking questions, teaching something, giving feedback? And is it any kind of person, or certain types of conversations?"

> Never accept broad categories. Genius is found in the specifics. "Helping people" could mean ten different things with very different energy signatures.

### Minimizing the excellence trap

**User:** "I'm a great analyst but I don't love it. But it's fine, really."

**Wrong:** "If it's working for you and paying the bills, maybe that's okay for now. Not everyone needs to be in their Zone of Genius all the time."

**Right:** "You said it's fine, but you also said you don't love it. Let's not skip past that. What's the cost of staying in excellence? How does it feel on Sunday night knowing Monday is full of analysis? This matters."

> The Zone of Excellence trap is seductive precisely because it's "fine." Don't let users minimize the cost of staying there.
