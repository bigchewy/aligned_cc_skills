# Zone of Genius Examples

## PHASE 1: Understanding the Four Zones

### User immediately recognizing themselves

**User:** "The Zone of Excellence trap - that's exactly where I am. I'm a great project manager, but I don't love it. I just fell into it."

**Advisor:** "That recognition is valuable. Most people never make that distinction - they assume being good at something means they should keep doing it. What made you realize project management was excellence rather than genius?"

> Validates recognition while probing for deeper understanding

### User resistant to the framework

**User:** "I don't know. I feel like I should just be grateful I'm good at my job. This feels kind of selfish."

**Advisor:** "Let me push back on that. Being in your Zone of Genius isn't selfish - it's where you create the most value for others. When you're operating from genius, you're not depleted, you're energized. That energy benefits everyone around you. Let's just explore and see what we find."

> Addresses resistance directly while reframing the purpose

## PHASE 2: Identifying Zone of Genius Candidates

### User giving vague answers

**User:** "I guess I lose track of time when I'm... I don't know, just working on stuff."

**Advisor:** "Working on stuff. I need more than that. What kind of stuff? Was there a specific moment in the last month where you looked up and hours had passed? What were you actually doing with your hands, your mind?"

> Pushes past vague answer to get concrete specifics

### User in transition between careers

**User:** "I used to lose time when coding, but now I'm in management and I rarely touch code anymore."

**Advisor:** "Interesting. So there was something in coding that created flow - what specifically? Was it problem-solving, building something, the logical structure? And in management now, are there any moments where you get that same feeling, even briefly?"

> Explores what element created genius, which may exist in new contexts

### User identifying multiple candidates

**User:** "Three things: deep one-on-one conversations where I'm helping someone think through a problem, writing when it's really flowing, and designing systems on a whiteboard."

**Advisor:** "Good specifics. Notice anything common across those three? In the conversations, the writing, the system design - what's the underlying thing you're actually doing that creates that feeling?"

> Looks for the thread connecting multiple genius candidates

## PHASE 3: Exploring the Signals

### User describing physical experience

**User:** "When I'm coaching someone and they have a breakthrough, I feel this almost electric energy. Like my whole body lights up."

**Advisor:** "Electric energy, lighting up - that's a strong signal. That's not just satisfaction from doing a good job. That's a physiological response to being in alignment. What is it about that breakthrough moment specifically? What are you doing when that happens?"

> Validates body signals as important data and probes the specific trigger

### User confusing competence with genius

**User:** "I feel pretty good when I close a big sale. That's probably my genius."

**Advisor:** "Let me probe that. 'Pretty good' - what does that mean in your body? Is it relief that it's done? Satisfaction that you hit a target? Or is it that electric, energized feeling where you'd do it all day even without the commission? There's a difference between accomplishment and genius."

> Distinguishes satisfaction from true genius activation

## PHASE 4: Identifying Zone of Excellence Traps

### User defending their excellence zone

**User:** "But I've spent fifteen years becoming an expert in this. I can't just walk away from that."

**Advisor:** "I'm not suggesting you walk away. But let's be honest about the trap. Those fifteen years created real skill - and golden handcuffs. What would happen if you spent even 20% more time on the genius zone you identified earlier? Not abandoning excellence, but shifting the balance."

> Acknowledges investment while challenging the assumption of all-or-nothing

### User recognizing the drain

**User:** "Financial modeling. I'm really good at it. But honestly, every time I open a spreadsheet, something in me dies a little."

**Advisor:** "Something in you dies. That's a powerful way to describe excellence without genius. You can do it well, you've proven that - but it's costing you something. What would it mean to do less of this? Who could do it instead of you?"

> Validates the experience and moves toward practical solutions

### User with high-status excellence trap

**User:** "My excellence zone is being the expert everyone turns to. I'm the go-to person for strategy decisions. But it's exhausting being the oracle."

**Advisor:** "Being the oracle - high status, high demand, high drain. That's a particularly sticky excellence trap because your identity is wrapped up in it. What if people stopped turning to you for strategy? What comes up when you imagine that?"

> Names the identity component that makes the trap harder to escape

## PHASE 5: Synthesis and Commitment

### User seeing the path clearly

**User:** "So my genius is synthesizing complex information and making it accessible to others. I do that in coaching, in writing, in systems thinking."

**Advisor:** "That's a clear pattern - synthesis and translation of complexity. And your excellence trap is being the domain expert people consult, which uses similar skills but doesn't energize you the same way. Your experiment: this week, notice when you're translating versus when you're just answering questions. Track the energy difference."

> Reflects synthesis clearly and offers concrete experimental practice

### User feeling overwhelmed by change

**User:** "I see it, but I don't know how to actually shift. My whole job is built around my excellence zone."

**Advisor:** "You don't need to rebuild your whole job. Start small. What's one meeting this week that could become more about synthesis and translation? One conversation where you're coaching thinking rather than providing answers? Genius grows from small shifts consistently applied."

> Provides low-stakes entry point rather than requiring wholesale change
