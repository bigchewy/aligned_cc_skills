# Origin Story Interview Examples

## PHASE 1: The Trigger

### User with clear trigger moment

**User:** "The moment was when I spent an entire weekend digging through my email trying to remember what we'd discussed with a key client. My wife asked why I was so stressed and I realized: this is insane. There has to be a better way."

**Advisor:** "An entire weekend in email, wife asking what's wrong, the realization of 'this is insane.' That's a specific moment. The frustration had built up, but that weekend crystallized it. What happened next? What did you do with that realization?"

> Validates specific trigger and probes for next step

### User with vague trigger

**User:** "I'd been thinking about this problem for a while and eventually decided to do something about it."

**Advisor:** "That's too vague. I need the specific moment. Not 'I'd been thinking' - when did it tip from thinking to doing? Was there a conversation? A failure? A moment of frustration that finally boiled over? What happened right before you actually started building?"

> Pushes past vagueness to find the concrete moment

## PHASE 2: What Existed Before

### User describing real attempts

**User:** "I tried everything. Spreadsheets - I had a massive one that I tried to keep updated, but it was always out of date. I tried three different CRMs - each one required too much manual entry and I gave up after a few weeks. I even hired a virtual assistant to help track things, but they couldn't read my mind about what mattered."

**Advisor:** "Spreadsheets (always outdated), three CRMs (too much work, gave up), VA (couldn't read your mind). Those are real attempts with real failures. Each failure taught you something about what the solution needed to be. What specifically was missing that would have made any of those work?"

> Catalogs specific attempts and transitions to gap analysis

### User claiming nothing existed

**User:** "There wasn't really anything out there."

**Advisor:** "That's rarely true. People don't live with unsolved problems - they find workarounds. What was the workaround before you built this? How did people, including you, manage before? Even if it was 'just remembering stuff' or 'using sticky notes,' that's an existing solution. What were you doing?"

> Challenges the 'nothing existed' claim

## PHASE 3: The First Version

### User describing scrappy v1

**User:** "The first version was embarrassingly simple. It was basically a Chrome extension that auto-saved emails to a searchable database. No AI, no fancy features. Just 'I can find that email from 6 months ago.'"

**Advisor:** "Embarrassingly simple - that's often where the insight lives. What surprised you when you started using it? Did it solve the problem you expected, or did you discover something else?"

> Validates simplicity and probes for unexpected learnings

### User describing the surprise

**User:** "The surprise was that the search wasn't the main value. It was that I could see ALL the emails with a client in one view. The timeline, the context. I didn't just find the email I was looking for - I saw the whole relationship."

**Advisor:** "'Not search, but seeing the whole relationship.' That's a discovery you couldn't have made without building. You thought you were solving for 'find old emails' and discovered you were actually solving for 'understand relationships.' That pivot is the story."

> Names the insight that emerged from building

## PHASE 4: The Evolution

### User tracing product evolution

**User:** "V1 was search. Then I added timeline view. Then I realized I was spending too much time reading, so I added AI summaries. Each evolution came from using it myself and noticing what was still painful."

**Advisor:** "Search → timeline → summaries. Each transition solved a new pain that only became visible after the previous pain was solved. You couldn't have designed the summary feature without first living with the timeline. What did you learn at each stage that you couldn't have known at the start?"

> Maps evolution and connects to sequential learning

## PHASE 5: The Key Insight

### User articulating non-obvious truth

**User:** "The insight is that CRM adoption fails because it asks humans to do robot work - entering data. The only sustainable approach is to automate the capture and let humans do human things - making decisions with context."

**Advisor:** "'CRM fails because it asks humans to do robot work.' That's counterintuitive. Most people think CRM is a discipline problem. You're saying it's a design problem. Why don't other people see this? What's blocking them?"

> Validates insight and probes for its non-obviousness

### User struggling to find insight

**User:** "I'm not sure I have a non-obvious insight. It just seemed like a good idea."

**Advisor:** "There's always an insight - you just might not have articulated it yet. What do you believe about this problem that most people who've looked at it don't believe? What did you figure out that your failed competitors missed? Something made you right when others were wrong. What is it?"

> Guides toward uncovering the latent insight

## PHASE 6: Use Cases

### User with vivid example

**User:** "A client called me panicking about a deadline. Normally I'd have spent 20 minutes reconstructing what we'd discussed. Instead, I opened the app, saw the full timeline, and in 30 seconds could remind them exactly what we'd agreed. They said 'how do you remember everything?' I didn't. The app did."

**Advisor:** "'How do you remember everything?' - that's the reaction you're designing for. Panic call, 30 seconds to full context, client amazed. That's your use case story. What's another example, ideally a different type of situation?"

> Captures the 'magic moment' and asks for variation

## PHASE 7: The Impossible Thing

### User naming the transformation

**User:** "Before, I could only manage about 20 real relationships. Beyond that, I'd start dropping balls. Now I can maintain context on 200+ relationships. It's not just faster - it's a different scale of possibility."

**Advisor:** "20 relationships → 200 relationships. That's not optimization - that's a 10x expansion of capacity. Before this product, managing 200 relationships with real context was literally impossible for one person. Now it's not. That's your 'impossible thing.'"

> Validates the transformation from impossible to possible

## SYNTHESIS

### Reflecting the complete story

**Advisor:** "Let me tell you back your origin story:

**The Trigger:** Weekend lost to email archaeology, wife asking what's wrong, the realization this is insane
**What Existed:** Spreadsheets (always stale), CRMs (too much work), VA (can't read your mind)
**The First Version:** Chrome extension for searchable email archive
**The Surprise:** Not search but seeing the whole relationship timeline
**The Evolution:** Search → Timeline → AI Summaries - each solving the pain that became visible after solving the previous one
**The Key Insight:** CRM fails because it asks humans to do robot work. Automate capture, let humans make decisions.
**Use Cases:** Panic call resolved in 30 seconds; 'How do you remember everything?'
**The Impossible Thing:** 20 relationships → 200 relationships with full context

This is your raw material. From here you can shape a homepage, a pitch, a one-liner. Does this capture your story? What's missing?"

> Provides complete narrative summary for user validation
