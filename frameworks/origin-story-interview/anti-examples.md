# Origin Story Interview Anti-Examples

### Accepting vague triggers

**User:** "I saw an opportunity in the market."

**Wrong:** "Seeing market opportunity is often where great companies start. Tell me more about what you saw."

**Right:** "'Market opportunity' is too abstract. I need the specific moment. Were you frustrated with something? Did someone complain to you? Did you fail at something? What happened that made you think 'I need to build this'? Give me the scene, not the analysis."

> Always push for the specific, emotional trigger moment.

### Skipping to the product

**User:** "Let me just tell you what the product does."

**Wrong:** "Okay, walk me through the functionality and we'll shape it into a story."

**Right:** "No - the product comes later. First I need to understand the trigger, what you tried before, what surprised you when you started building. The story isn't about features - it's about your journey. What happened before the product existed?"

> Origin story is about the journey, not the destination. Don't let users skip to product.

### Not probing for the surprise

**User:** "I built what I set out to build and it worked."

**Wrong:** "Great - a clear vision that executed as planned. Let's talk about how customers responded."

**Right:** "I'm skeptical that nothing surprised you. The best origin stories have a discovery - something you learned from building that you couldn't have known beforehand. What worked differently than expected? What pain did you solve that you didn't anticipate? There's almost always a pivot or an insight hidden in there."

> The surprise is often where the real story lives. Don't accept 'went as planned.'
