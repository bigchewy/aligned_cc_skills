# Why My Page Doesn't Rank — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. A solo founder launched a small site eight weeks ago about first camping trips with young kids. The page is `/first-camping-trip-checklist`, a printable checklist. The query is "camping checklist for kids." The domain is new, with almost no links.

---

## Phase 1: The Page, the Query, and Who Ranks

### Golden path

**User:** "URL is /first-camping-trip-checklist. Query: camping checklist for kids. Top three are an REI guide, a printable PDF from a parenting blog, and a Reddit thread."

**Say something like:**
"Good. That tells us a lot already. Two of the three are printable or practical, one is real parents talking. That's what this search wants. Now let's check Google can even see yours."

**Why it works:** One URL, one query, and the real competition named before any diagnosis. Every later comparison has something concrete to compare against.

### Struggle: no target query

**User:** "I just want it to rank. For anything about camping."

**Say something like:**
"Ranking is always for a query. Which search would a parent type the night before they pack? Pick one. We'll run the checks against it."

**Why it works:** The checklist is comparative. Without a query there are no top ranked pages to compare with, so the diagnosis can't start.

---

## Phase 2: Crawlable and Indexable

### Golden path: it isn't indexed

**User:** "URL Inspection says 'URL is not on Google.' Reason: 'Discovered, currently not indexed.'"

**Say something like:**
"Then this is the answer, and we stop here. Google knows the URL exists and hasn't crawled it yet. A page that isn't indexed can't rank. Is it in your sitemap? Which pages on your site link to it? Let's fix that first, then Request Indexing."

**Why it works:** It stops at question 2. The founder came in worried about content and links; the real problem was earlier, and fixing it first saves a week of the wrong work.

### Edge case: noindex left on from staging

**User:** "It says 'Excluded by noindex tag.' I think the theme had 'discourage search engines' on when I launched."

**Say something like:**
"That's it. Turn that setting off, check the page's HTML no longer has the noindex, then run URL Inspection again and Request Indexing. Check your other pages too. If one has it, they probably all do."

**Why it works:** The fix is named exactly, and the check widens to the rest of the site, because a site-wide setting causes it.

---

## Phase 3: More Relevant than the Top Ranked Pages

### Golden path

**User:** "Theirs all say 'printable' in the title. Mine says 'Before You Go: Our Family Camping Story.' The checklist is halfway down after a long intro."

**Say something like:**
"There's the relevance gap. The search wants a checklist, and your title and first screen say story. Put 'camping checklist for kids' in the title and main heading, and put the checklist at the top. Keep the story below it."

**Why it works:** It compares the page with the top three in plain terms (title, heading, first paragraph) and turns the gap into one specific change.

### Struggle: the top results are a different kind of page

**User:** "The top results for 'best tent for families' are all big review sites with 20 tents tested. I have one page about the tent we use."

**Say something like:**
"Then Google thinks that search wants a comparison. Your page may be good and still be the wrong type for this query. Either build a real comparison, or pick a query that wants the page you have, like 'camping with a toddler in a tent.'"

**Why it works:** It names a mismatch between the page type and the search, which no amount of tuning fixes.

---

## Phase 4: More Helpful than the Top Ranked Pages

### Golden path

**User:** "Mine has the things we forgot on our first trip, with photos of our actual gear. Theirs are generic lists."

**Say something like:**
"That's what makes it worth ranking above them. First-hand experience and your own photos. Make sure that's visible near the top, and say who you are."

**Why it works:** It finds the one thing only this founder has, and moves it to where both people and search systems see it.

### Struggle: "mine is longer"

**User:** "Mine is 3,000 words. Theirs are 800."

**Say something like:**
"Longer isn't more helpful. What does a parent know after reading yours that they wouldn't after reading theirs?"

**Why it works:** It turns a length claim into the actual question the checklist asks: helpful for the search need.

---

## Phase 7: Links, Disavows and Manual Actions

### Golden path: new domain, few links

**User:** "Search Console shows two sites linking to me. No manual actions. I've never disavowed anything."

**Say something like:**
"Then question 8 doesn't apply, and question 9 is clear. Question 7 is your gap: the top pages have far more links than you. That won't close this week, and buying links is not the answer. Put this week into what you control. Over time, publish something people want to reference, like the forgotten-items list with your photos."

**Why it works:** It says plainly which questions rarely apply to a new domain, names the one it loses, and moves the effort to what can change now.

### Edge case: a bought domain with a past

**User:** "I bought the domain at auction. It was a coupon site before."

**Say something like:**
"Then question 9 matters. Check Manual actions in Search Console, and look at the Links report for old spam pointing at you. A past life can follow a domain."

**Why it works:** It doesn't apply the new-domain shortcut blindly. A domain with history needs the checks that a clean one can skip.

---

## Phase 8: The Search Result Itself

### Golden path

**User:** "Performance shows 1,200 impressions for the query, position about 14, 6 clicks."

**Say something like:**
"It ranks, and it loses the click. Your title says 'Our Family Camping Story.' Theirs say 'Printable Camping Checklist for Kids.' Rewrite the title and description for this query. Position will matter more once it's on page one, but the click starts with the result."

**Why it works:** It reads the Performance data for one query and connects it to question 10 directly.

---

## Phase 9: One Fix First

### Golden path

**User:** "So what do I do?"

**Say something like:**
"One fix first: put the checklist at the top and 'camping checklist for kids' in the title and heading, because question 3 is where it loses. Next: link it from your home page and your packing post, then rewrite the description. Not this week: links. Check again in URL Inspection and Performance in three weeks."

**Why it works:** One action that answers the question where the page fails, a short ordered list after it, the slow gap named honestly, and a date to look at the data again.

### Edge case: the problem isn't on the list

**User:** "Every page on my site dropped on the same day last month, not just this one."

**Say something like:**
"Then it's not this page. A whole site dropping on one day points to a site-wide issue or a Google update, and the ten questions are for one page. Check whether the date matches a Google update, and check URL Inspection on a few pages for the same error."

**Why it works:** It says when the checklist doesn't fit the problem instead of forcing it.
