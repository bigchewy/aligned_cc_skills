# Why My Page Doesn't Rank — Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Jumping to links before checking the page is indexed

**User:** "My page doesn't rank. I think I need backlinks. Should I buy links from a guest post service?"

**Wrong:**
"Backlinks are a key ranking factor. A few quality guest posts on relevant sites will help. Look for sites with good domain authority and natural anchor text."

**Right:**
"Before we talk about links, let's check Google can even see it. Question 1: Is the page Crawlable? Question 2: Is the page Indexable? Run URL Inspection in Search Console and tell me what it says. And don't buy links. I'm not a link builder, and paid links are how sites earn a manual action."

**What to do instead:** Run the questions in her order. Crawlable and indexable come first, because a page that isn't indexed can't rank whatever its links. Never recommend buying links.

---

## 2. Recommending a disavow on a new domain

**User:** "Someone told me to disavow my bad links. My site launched two months ago."

**Wrong:**
"Good idea. Export your links, flag anything with low authority, and upload a disavow file to Search Console."

**Right:**
"Question 8 asks whether good links are being disavowed. On a new domain it rarely applies. You have very few links and you haven't disavowed anything. Leave the disavow file alone and check whether the page is indexed."

**What to do instead:** Say plainly which questions rarely apply to a new domain (8 and usually 9), and spend the time on the ones the page can win.

---

## 3. Judging the page without a query or the top ranked pages

**User:** "Can you look at my page and tell me why it doesn't rank?"

**Wrong:**
"Here are some improvements: add more keywords to your headings, increase word count to 2,000, add an FAQ section, and improve your meta description."

**Right:**
"For which query? Ranking is always for a query. Tell me the one you want, then search it and paste me the top three. Most of the ten questions compare your page with them."

**What to do instead:** Lock one target query and the top three results in Phase 1. Questions 3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 all compare against the top ranked pages for target queries, not against a generic checklist of keyword tips.

---

## 4. Handing over everything that's wrong

**User:** "OK, we've been through all ten. What should I do?"

**Wrong:**
"Here are 23 recommendations: fix your LCP, add schema to every page, rewrite all titles, build 50 backlinks, add alt text, compress images, create a pillar page, add an author bio, start a newsletter..."

**Right:**
"One fix first: the checklist goes to the top and the title says 'camping checklist for kids,' because question 3 is where it loses. Then two more, in order. Links aren't this week. Check again in three weeks."

**What to do instead:** "Your recommendations shouldn't include all that matters, is too complex & won't be done anyway." End with one fix first, a short ordered list, and a date to check the data.

---

## 5. Promising a ranking or a timeline

**User:** "If I fix all this, how long until I'm on page one?"

**Wrong:**
"Most pages see results in 4 to 6 weeks. With these fixes you should be in the top 5 by next quarter."

**Right:**
"I can't promise a position or a date, and nobody honest can. What I can say: once it's indexed and more relevant than the top three, it has a chance. Links are the gap that takes longest. Check URL Inspection and Performance on the date we set, and go look at your data."

**What to do instead:** Never invent a number of weeks or a ranking. She never published one. Set a date to recheck the data instead.
