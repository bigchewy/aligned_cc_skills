# Pilot Program Methodology Examples

## PHASE 1: Clarify the Transformation

### Vague transformation

**User:** "I want to teach people about marketing."

**Advisor:** "That's not a transformation - that's a topic. What will they be able to DO after your course that they can't do now? 'Know more about marketing' isn't compelling. 'Launch their first profitable Facebook ad campaign' is. What's the specific ability they'll gain?"

> Pushes from topic to outcome

### Clear transformation

**User:** "By the end, they'll be able to create and launch their first online course and have at least 5 paying students."

**Advisor:** "Perfect - that's measurable. 'First online course with 5 paying students' is a transformation I can design a pilot around. Now we know exactly what we're validating: can you help someone go from 'I have expertise but no course' to 'I have a course with paying students'? Let's build the minimum viable pilot for that."

> Validates clear outcome

## PHASE 2: Define the Minimum Viable Pilot

### User wants to include everything

**User:** "I want to cover content strategy, video production, platform selection, pricing, marketing, launch strategy, and student success systems."

**Advisor:** "That's a comprehensive course, not a pilot. You're building for months before validating demand. For a 4-6 week pilot, pick ONE core transformation. Which of those is the highest value? If I had to guess: getting to launch with paying students. Content strategy, video production - those can be 'good enough' for a pilot. Focus on the critical path to the transformation."

> Ruthlessly scopes down

### User identifies core outcome

**User:** "The most important thing is probably getting them to actually launch and enroll their first students. Everything else supports that."

**Advisor:** "Exactly. Your pilot is 'From Idea to Enrolled Students in 6 Weeks.' Everything else you mentioned - content, video, platform - those become just-in-time modules to serve the launch goal. You're not teaching comprehensively; you're guiding them to one specific outcome. That's pilotable."

> Confirms appropriate scope

## PHASE 3: Set the Price

### User wants to charge too little

**User:** "I was thinking $97 to make it accessible."

**Advisor:** "$97 doesn't validate B2B demand for a professional transformation. At that price, people buy and never show up. You need skin in the game from students. If your transformation is 'launch a course with paying students,' that's worth $500-1,500 to the right audience. Start at $500 minimum for the pilot. You can always offer a guarantee."

> Pushes for meaningful price

### User nervous about charging

**User:** "What if no one pays? Then I'll have wasted time building the curriculum."

**Advisor:** "You haven't built curriculum yet - that's the point. You're validating BEFORE building. If nobody pays $500 for your pilot, you just learned something valuable: either the transformation isn't compelling, the audience is wrong, or your positioning needs work. Better to learn that in 3 weeks than after 6 months of course building. That's the whole point of validation."

> Reframes pilot as validation

### User pricing too high

**User:** "I was thinking $5,000 since that's what established courses charge."

**Advisor:** "That's backwards. You're pricing like a proven course, but you're running an unproven pilot. $5,000 requires established credibility, testimonials, and proven results. For a pilot, $500-1,500 is the sweet spot - enough to validate willingness to pay, low enough that the pilot discount makes sense. Once you have results from this cohort, THEN you can price higher."

> Prevents overpricing that kills pilot enrollment

## PHASE 4: Create the Transparent Offer

### Good draft with too much hedge

**User:** "I'm thinking of maybe starting a pilot program if anyone's interested in learning about course creation."

**Advisor:** "Way too hedgy. 'Maybe,' 'if anyone's interested' - that's not an offer, that's an apology. Be direct. 'I'm launching a 6-week pilot program for [specific audience] who want to [specific outcome]. I need 10 founding members willing to give me honest feedback. Pilot price is $500 (will be $1,500 later). Full refund if it doesn't deliver. Apply here.' Confident, clear, bounded."

> Strengthens weak offer

### Strong transparent offer

**User:** "'Calling 10 experienced coaches who want to launch their first online course and enroll paying students in the next 8 weeks. I'm piloting a new program and need founding members who'll give me real feedback along the way. In exchange, you get the program for $750 instead of the eventual $2,000. If you don't have at least 5 paying students by the end, full refund. DM me if this is you.'"

**Advisor:** "That's excellent. Specific audience (experienced coaches), specific outcome (course + 5 students), specific timeline (8 weeks), transparent pilot status, clear value exchange (discount for feedback), and risk reversal (refund). That's an offer people can say yes or no to. Ship it."

> Validates strong offer

## PHASE 5: Plan the Pilot Delivery

### Overbuilt curriculum

**User:** "I want to have detailed slide decks, worksheets, templates, and pre-recorded videos for each module."

**Advisor:** "Stop. That's not a pilot - that's a full course build. For the pilot, you show up live, teach what's needed that week, answer questions in real-time, and adjust based on what students struggle with. Slides can be rough. Worksheets can be created between sessions. The pilot is about validating the transformation path, not producing polished assets."

> Keeps pilot lightweight

### Appropriate pilot structure

**User:** "Week 1: Choose their course topic and ideal student. Week 2: Outline the transformation path. Week 3: Create the pilot offer. Week 4: Reach out to potential students. Week 5: Deliver their own pilot. Week 6: Debrief and iterate."

**Advisor:** "That's a tight, focused pilot. Each week has one clear deliverable that moves toward the outcome. You're not teaching theory - you're guiding action. And look: weeks 3-5, they're doing the same pilot methodology YOU'RE doing. That's elegant. This is pilotable."

> Validates focused structure

## PHASE 6: Launch Strategy

### User has no audience

**User:** "I don't have an email list or big following. How do I find 10 people?"

**Advisor:** "You don't need an audience - you need 10 specific people. Start with: Who do you already know who fits the profile? Former colleagues, LinkedIn connections, people in communities you're part of. You're not launching to thousands; you're reaching out personally to 30-50 people to find 10 who are ready. Personal outreach beats broadcast marketing for pilots."

> Reframes from audience to network

### User has warm network

**User:** "I have about 500 people on an email list from my coaching business."

**Advisor:** "Perfect. Email your list with the pilot offer. Be personal: 'I'm building something new and want 10 people to help me shape it.' Follow up individually with people who click or reply. 500 warm contacts is more than enough to fill a 10-person pilot. You might fill it in a week."

> Uses existing assets

### User wants to avoid personal outreach

**User:** "Can I just run some Facebook ads to fill the pilot?"

**Advisor:** "That's backwards for a pilot. Paid ads require optimized copy, landing pages, and usually multiple iterations to convert. You're adding complexity when you should be validating. For 10 people, personal outreach wins. Who do you already know who fits the profile? LinkedIn connections, past clients, people in communities you're part of. Start there. Ads are for scaling after validation, not for testing."

> Redirects from ads to personal outreach for pilot stage
