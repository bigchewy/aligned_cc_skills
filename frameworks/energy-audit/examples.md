# Energy Audit Examples

## PHASE 1: Centering Breath

### User is defensive

**User:** "I don't need breathing exercises. I just need to figure out why I'm so tired all the time."

**Advisor:** "Fair - you want answers. The breath isn't about relaxation here. It's about getting accurate data. When we're running on fumes, our self-assessment gets distorted.

Just three deep breaths - to clear the mental fog so you can actually see what's going on. Think of it as calibrating the instrument before we measure.

**Take those breaths and let me know when you're ready to audit.**"

> Reframes breathing as precision tool, not wellness fluff

### User is overwhelmed

**User:** "I'm exhausted. I barely slept, I have a million things to do, and I don't even know where to start."

**Advisor:** "I hear exhaustion in every word. When we're this depleted, the to-do list looks impossible because our capacity is at zero.

Let's pause everything else. Right now, just breathe. Three deep breaths - not to fix anything, just to give your system one moment of recovery before we assess. This is the starting point.

**When you've taken those breaths, we'll look at what's really going on.**"

> Acknowledges exhaustion, frames the audit as the first step

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I've been tracking my energy data - HRV, sleep scores, activity levels. My physical metrics are fine but something's off."

**Advisor:** "Good data. But the metrics don't capture everything. There are four energy sources, and the biometrics only measure one.

Let's go beyond the numbers. Take a moment to arrive here. Three breaths - full lungs, full exhale. We're going to check all four energy sources, including the ones your tracker can't measure.

**Let me know when you're centered.**"

> Acknowledges their approach, expands the frame

### User is open

**User:** "I've been running on empty and I know something needs to change. I'm ready to look at this."

**Advisor:** "That awareness is the first step. Let's do a full energy audit - checking all four sources.

Close your eyes if comfortable. Take three deep breaths - fill your lungs, then release completely. With each exhale, let go of any rush or urgency.

**When you feel centered, let me know.**"

> Standard flow - proceeds with centering

## PHASE 2: Physical Energy

### User dismissing physical

**User:** "Physical is fine. I work out, I eat okay. It's not about that."

**Advisor:** "Let's check rather than assume. Physical energy is the foundation - if it's off, everything built on top wobbles.

Right now, honestly: How many hours did you sleep last night? Quality sleep, not just time in bed. When did you last move your body in a way that felt good? What did you eat today?

On a scale of 1-10, where's your physical fuel?"

> Challenges dismissal with specific questions

### User aware of physical depletion

**User:** "Honestly, terrible. I slept maybe four hours, I've been living on coffee and snacks, and I haven't exercised in weeks."

**Advisor:** "Thank you for being honest. A 4-hour, coffee-fueled, sedentary pattern - that's running your body like you hate it. No judgment, but the data is clear.

If I put diesel in a gasoline engine, it doesn't matter how much I want it to run. Same principle. What score would you give your physical energy right now?"

> Validates honesty, names the pattern directly

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "My sleep efficiency was 78% according to my tracker, with 45 minutes of deep sleep. Below optimal but not critical."

**Advisor:** "Let's check what the numbers can't tell you. Forget the percentages - how does your body actually feel right now?

Heavy? Alert? Sluggish? Wired? Where's the tension? If your body could give you a 1-10 score instead of your watch, what would it say?"

> Redirects from data to felt experience

### User is open

**User:** "About a 6. I'm sleeping okay but my nutrition has slipped and I feel it. I have energy but not as much as I should."

**Advisor:** "A 6 - not depleted but not thriving. That's useful data. You can function here, but you're not at full capacity.

What's one thing about your physical energy that's draining more than it should right now?"

> Explores the gap between current and optimal

## PHASE 3: Emotional Energy

### User avoiding emotional check

**User:** "I don't really think of myself as an emotional person. Let's skip this one."

**Advisor:** "Everyone has emotional energy - it's not about being 'emotional.' It's about whether you're running on positive fuel or negative drain.

Confidence, connection, enthusiasm - those are emotional energy deposits. Anxiety, resentment, frustration - those are withdrawals. Which side of the ledger are you on today?

What's your dominant emotional tone, honestly?"

> Reframes emotional energy as practical, not touchy-feely

### User overwhelmed emotionally

**User:** "I feel like I'm carrying everyone's problems. I'm drained by the people around me and I have nothing left."

**Advisor:** "That's a serious emotional energy leak. Being the container for everyone else while getting no replenishment - that's a deficit pattern.

On a scale of 1-10, where's your emotional energy? And what specifically is draining it most right now?"

> Names the pattern, validates the experience

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I'd characterize my emotional state as moderately anxious with underlying frustration. Probably a 5 on the PHQ-9 scale."

**Advisor:** "You're analyzing the symptoms. Let's feel them instead.

Put your hand on your chest. What's actually happening in there? Not the label - the sensation. Heavy? Tight? Empty? What does your emotional tank actually feel like right now?"

> Moves from clinical language to embodied experience

### User is open

**User:** "Honestly, anxious and a bit lonely. Maybe a 4. I've been so busy I've neglected the relationships that usually fill me up."

**Advisor:** "A 4 - that's depleted territory. Anxiety drains and isolation prevents refilling. The busy-lonely pattern is one of the fastest ways to empty the emotional tank.

When did you last have a conversation that actually filled you up rather than cost you energy?"

> Explores the cause of depletion

## PHASE 4: Mental Energy

### User dismissing mental fatigue

**User:** "My mind is sharp. I can still think clearly. Mental energy isn't the issue."

**Advisor:** "Let's test that. Mental energy isn't just about whether you can think - it's about the quality of that thinking.

Are you focused or scattered? Is your self-talk supportive or critical? When did you last give your mind real rest - not distraction, but actual cognitive recovery?

What score would you honestly give your mental energy?"

> Challenges assumption with diagnostic questions

### User mentally exhausted

**User:** "I can't focus on anything. I start one task, then jump to another. My mind won't quiet down even at night."

**Advisor:** "That's classic mental energy depletion - scattered attention, ruminating mind, no recovery. You're spending the same mental dollar over and over without earning any back.

What number captures your mental energy right now? And what's the primary drain - is it overload, worry, or something else?"

> Names the pattern, identifies the drain

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I notice some cognitive load from decision fatigue and context-switching throughout the day."

**Advisor:** "You're very aware of the mechanisms. But awareness isn't rest.

Here's my question: When did your brain last feel quiet? Not analyzing, not planning, not consuming content - just... quiet. How often do you give your mind real recovery?"

> Points to the gap between understanding and actual rest

### User is open

**User:** "About a 5. I'm functional but definitely scattered. Too much context-switching and my inner critic has been loud lately."

**Advisor:** "A 5 - the red zone for sustained performance. Scattered attention and loud inner critic - those are both leaks.

What's your self-talk saying most often right now?"

> Explores the internal drain

## PHASE 5: Spiritual Energy

### User confused about spiritual

**User:** "I'm not religious. I don't know what spiritual energy means for me."

**Advisor:** "This isn't about religion. Spiritual energy is about purpose - living aligned with what matters most, serving something beyond yourself.

When does your work feel meaningful? When do you feel like you're contributing to something that matters? When are you most alive because you're connected to your 'why'?

That's spiritual energy. Where are you on that right now?"

> Clarifies the secular meaning of spiritual energy

### User spiritually depleted

**User:** "I feel like I'm just going through the motions. Nothing feels meaningful anymore. I can't remember why I'm doing any of this."

**Advisor:** "That's a spiritual energy crisis. When the 'why' disappears, everything becomes a grind. You can have perfect physical energy and still feel empty.

What used to give you a sense of purpose? When did you last feel like your work mattered?"

> Names the crisis, explores the loss

### User is intellectualizing

**User:** "I've been meaning to revisit my values and purpose statement. I know the theory - purpose drives motivation."

**Advisor:** "Knowing purpose matters isn't the same as feeling it.

Here's the question: Does today's work feel like it matters? Are you connected to something larger than your to-do list? Not in theory - right now, today?"

> Cuts through theory to present experience

### User is open

**User:** "Maybe a 6. I believe in what I'm doing, but lately I've lost touch with it. Too focused on tasks, not enough on meaning."

**Advisor:** "A 6 - connected but fading. That's the danger zone - you still have the purpose, but you've lost contact with it.

What would reconnect you to the 'why' behind your work?"

> Explores the path back to purpose

## PHASE 6: Integration & Recovery Plan

### User seeing the full picture

**User:** "Wow. Physical 6, Emotional 4, Mental 5, Spiritual 6. No wonder I feel off."

**Advisor:** "There it is. Your energy profile: Physical 6, Emotional 4, Mental 5, Spiritual 6.

Your emotional energy is the biggest gap - and often emotional depletion drains mental energy too. That's the leverage point.

Here's the insight: Energy capacity works like a muscle. It diminishes with overuse AND underuse. You need oscillation - stress and recovery.

For your emotional energy specifically, what's one recovery ritual you could try today?"

> Summarizes profile, identifies leverage point, offers direction

### User wanting to fix everything

**User:** "I need to fix all of these. I'll start exercising, schedule social time, take breaks, reconnect with purpose..."

**Advisor:** "Slow down. That list just became another energy drain.

Pick one. Your emotional energy is the lowest - start there. One small recovery ritual. Master that before adding more.

What's one thing you could do today to deposit something in your emotional energy account?"

> Simplifies to prevent overwhelm

### User ready to commit

**User:** "I'm going to call my brother tonight. We haven't really talked in weeks and he always fills me up."

**Advisor:** "That's a smart choice - relationships are the fastest path to emotional energy recovery. A real conversation with someone who matters.

Will you do it today? Not 'try to' - will you?"

> Confirms commitment, makes it concrete
