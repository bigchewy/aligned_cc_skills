# Declutter and Focus — Sources

All pages opened on 2026-11-01. Research file: `docs/advisors/new_advisors/v_cole-nussbaumer-knaflic.md`.

## Why declutter and focus are one framework

- The 2021 study "Declutter and Focus" by Kiran Ajani, Elsie Lee, Cindy Xiong, Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, William Kemper and Steven Franconeri, *IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics*. Decluttering alone looked more professional but didn't improve getting the main point; declutter plus focus made people about 2.5 times as likely to get it. Some found the focused versions heavy-handed. Summary: https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/do-people-understand-your-data-visualizations
- Book chapters 3 ("clutter is your enemy!") and 4 ("focus your audience's attention") of *Storytelling with Data* (Wiley, 2015). Table of contents: https://media.wiley.com/product_data/excerpt/57/11190022/1119002257-6.pdf

## Phases

- Phase 1, The Chart and Its Point: Cole, book chapter 1 (who and what before the data). https://media.wiley.com/product_data/excerpt/57/11190022/1119002257-4.pdf. Hand-offs to `choose-an-effective-visual` and `big-idea-and-takeaway-title` are the sibling frameworks. The "wrong chart" check: Simon Rowe, when decluttering isn't enough. https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/when-decluttering-isnt-enough
- Phase 2, Push Everything to Gray: Cole, the biggest bang for your buck. https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2015/03/the-biggest-bang-for-your-buck. Start with nothing emphasized: Elizabeth Ricks, the most important dataviz decision you make. https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2021/1/11/the-most-important-dataviz-decision-you-make
- Phase 3, Cut the Clutter: Elizabeth Ricks, declutter! (and question default settings). https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2019/5/13/declutter-and-question-defaults. Data labels: Cole, drawing attention with data labels. https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2012/06/drawing-attention-with-data-labels. Show fewer series: Simon Rowe (above).
- Phase 4, Add Focus: accent color, Cole, the biggest bang for your buck (above). Takeaway title, Cole, so what? https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/3/22/so-what. The 2.5 figure: the 2021 study (above).
- Phase 5, Where Are Your Eyes Drawn?: Cole, where are your eyes drawn? https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/whereareyoureyesdrawn
- Phase 6, On a Phone: Mike Cisneros, you can take it with you (recap of a mobile challenge created by Andy Cotgreave). https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2019/8/20/you-can-take-it-with-you
- Phase 7, The Changes, In Order: Added: the ordered list of changes is the deliverable the user asked for; it collects the decisions from Phases 2 to 6 so someone can make them in the chart tool.
- Added: Phase 1's "pick one tile" branch for dashboards. Amy Esselman's from dashboard to story supports pulling one story out of a dashboard (https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/from-dashboard-to-story), but the one-tile rule is ours.
- Added: in Phase 4, "make the second series a darker gray" instead of a second color. It follows from Cole's "use color sparingly," but the darker-gray move is not quoted from a page.

## Lines quoted as Cole's own words

- "I often start by making every single component of my visual light grey, pushing it all to the background" — https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2015/03/the-biggest-bang-for-your-buck
- "Rethink how you use color - don't use it to make your graph colorful." — same page
- "When used sparingly, color is your single biggest tool for drawing your audience's attention" — same page
- "Labeling every data point creates a cluttered visual" (shortened from "...visual, one of the issues with the original graph.") — https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2012/06/drawing-attention-with-data-labels
- "Too often we underutilize this space with a descriptive title." / "Put your key takeaway—the *so what?*—there." — https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/3/22/so-what
- "you create your visual, then look away from it (or close your eyes) and look back at it," "taking note of where your eyes go first." / "This is likely where your audience's eyes will land as well." — https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/whereareyoureyesdrawn
- Phase 4's "it's easy for people to say 'Oh, that's interesting' and move on" paraphrases book chapter 1: "If you simply present data, it's easy for your audience to say, "Oh, that's interesting," and move on to the next thing." — https://media.wiley.com/product_data/excerpt/57/11190022/1119002257-4.pdf

## Credit to others

- Elizabeth Ricks: the decluttering checklist (border, gridlines, data labels, legend next to the data, question defaults) and starting with nothing emphasized.
- Simon Rowe: decluttering isn't enough; a subset or total can be clearer than all the data.
- Mike Cisneros: the phone lessons (first screen, vertical scrolling, limited interactivity).
- Andy Cotgreave: created the mobile challenge Cisneros wrote up.
- Kiran Ajani, Elsie Lee, Cindy Xiong, William Kemper, Steven Franconeri: co-authors of the 2021 study.
