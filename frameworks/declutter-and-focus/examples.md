# Declutter and Focus — Examples

One running example, made up to show the moves. A growth lead at a small software company has a line chart of weekly sign-ups by channel for the last six months: five lines (paid ads, referral, organic search, social, partners), each in the tool's default color. It has a box border, horizontal and vertical gridlines, a data label on every point, a legend box at the bottom, and the title "Weekly Sign-ups by Channel." It goes on a slide in the monthly leadership meeting.

The coaching lines are written for this framework. The lines quoted as Cole's own words are listed in `sources.md` with their URLs.

---

## Phase 1: The Chart and Its Point

### Golden path

**User:** "It's a line chart, weeks on the x-axis, sign-ups on the y-axis, five channels. Default colors, gridlines, labels on everything, legend at the bottom. Leadership sees it on a slide. The point: referral passed paid ads in August, and we spend almost nothing on referral."

**Say something like:**
"Good, that's a real point, and it has a so what in it. Time across the x-axis with a line per channel is the right kind of chart for change over time, so we keep the chart type and clean it up. Five lines, one of which matters most. Let's start by making all five gray."

**Why it works:** The chart's parts, the audience and the point are on the table before anything changes. The chart type is checked once and kept, so the session doesn't drift into picking a new chart.

### Struggle: no point yet

**User:** "Honestly, I just want it to look better. It's our standard channel chart."

**Say something like:**
"I can make it look better in ten minutes. But looking better on its own doesn't help people get anything from it. That's what our study found. If you had to tell the room one thing from this chart, what would it be? If nothing comes to mind, let's run `big-idea-and-takeaway-title` first and come back."

**User:** "I guess... referral is growing and nobody's noticed."

**Why it works:** The person is asked for the point, not told off for not having one. A rough sentence is enough to go on, and the hand-off is offered if they really have none.

### Edge case: the chart type is wrong

**User:** "It's a line chart where the x-axis is the five channels and the line goes up and down between them."

**Say something like:**
"Then let's pause the cleanup. A line across channels says there's something in between paid ads and referral, and there isn't. Decluttering a wrong chart gives you a cleaner wrong chart. Let's run `choose-an-effective-visual`. It'll probably come back as a bar chart, and then we'll do this on that."

**Why it works:** The wrong chart type is caught before effort goes into polishing it, and the hand-off names what to do next.

---

## Phase 2: Push Everything to Gray

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Everything goes gray: all five lines, the axis labels, the axis titles, the data labels, the legend text. I often start by making every single component of my visual light grey, pushing it all to the background. Anything here that has to keep a color for a reason outside this slide?"

**User:** "Paid ads is always red in our other reports."

**Say something like:**
"Then if paid ads ever needs color, it gets red. On this chart it stays gray, because the point is about referral."

**Why it works:** Every part is named, so nothing keeps its color by accident. The user's rule is respected and still doesn't take over the chart.

### Struggle: worried it looks dull

**User:** "All gray looks like a draft."

**Say something like:**
"For a minute it does. We're halfway there. One color is about to come back, and it'll go exactly where you want them to look."

**Why it works:** The worry is real and short-lived, so the response is short and points to the next step.

---

## Phase 3: Cut the Clutter

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Here's Elizabeth Ricks's checklist, applied to yours:
- Border: gone.
- Gridlines: the vertical ones go. The horizontal ones go too, since nobody reads exact weekly counts off a slide.
- Data labels: labeling every data point creates a cluttered visual. Keep two: referral and paid ads in the August week where they cross.
- Legend: delete the box. Write each channel's name at the right end of its line.
- Defaults: drop the tick marks and the decimal places on the y-axis.
Anything here someone in the meeting actually uses?"

**User:** "The CFO likes to see the last week's number for paid ads."

**Say something like:**
"Then keep that one label too, at the end of the paid ads line."

**Why it works:** The checklist is credited to the person who wrote it and applied to this chart, item by item. The one label someone really uses stays.

### Edge case: decluttered, still hard to read

**User:** "Even clean, five lines crossing each other is a mess in the spring."

**Say something like:**
"Simon Rowe on our team makes this point: decluttering isn't enough on its own. Sometimes fewer series say it better. Social and partners are small and flat. Could we combine them as 'other' in gray, so the eye has three lines to sort out instead of five?"

**Why it works:** Decluttering hits its limit, and the next move is about showing less data, not adding decoration. The idea is credited to Simon Rowe.

---

## Phase 4: Add Focus

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Now focus. In our study, declutter plus focus made people about 2.5 times as likely to get the point. Two moves:
- One accent color: referral, in your brand blue. Everything else stays gray, paid ads included.
- A title that states the takeaway. Not 'Weekly Sign-ups by Channel.' Try: 'Referral passed paid ads in August, on almost no spend.'
Does that title say what you want leadership to walk away with?"

**User:** "Yes, but I want paid ads in red so they see what referral passed."

**Say something like:**
"Two bright colors will fight for the eye. Make paid ads a darker gray than the rest. It stands out from the background without competing with referral."

**Why it works:** Both halves of focus are done: one accent color and a title with a verb. The study is cited as the reason, and the request for a second color is met with a darker gray.

### Struggle: title is still a label

**User:** "How about 'Sign-ups by Channel, H1'?"

**Say something like:**
"That tells me what the chart is. I want it to tell me what to think. What happened, and to which channel? Say it as a sentence."

**Why it works:** The difference between a label and a takeaway is shown in one line, and the user writes the new title.

---

## Phase 5: Where Are Your Eyes Drawn?

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Make the change, then look away and look back, and note where your eyes go first. I'd expect the title first, then the blue referral line where it crosses paid ads in August."

**User:** "My eyes went to the company logo in the slide corner first. It's bright orange."

**Say something like:**
"Then the logo is competing. Shrink it or make it gray on this slide, and test again. Will people see this on a phone?"

**User:** "No, slides only."

**Why it works:** The test finds something outside the chart that steals attention, and the fix is to push it back, not to make the accent louder. The phone question is asked and answered before closing.

---

## Phase 6: On a Phone

### Edge case: the slide gets sent around

**User:** "Actually, the deck gets forwarded and people open it on their phones."

**Say something like:**
"Then the first screen matters most. Mike Cisneros wrote up a mobile challenge started by Andy Cotgreave, and the lessons fit here. On the first screen: the title and the blue line. Six months of five lines is wide for a phone, so show the last twelve weeks, or stack referral and paid ads as two small charts one above the other. No hover tooltips; they don't work on a phone."

**Why it works:** The phone step runs only because the user said yes. The fix is fewer points and vertical stacking, not smaller text, and the lessons are credited to Mike Cisneros and Andy Cotgreave.

---

## Phase 7: The Changes, In Order

### Golden path

**Say something like:**
"Here are the changes, in order.
1. Gray: all five lines, axis labels, axis titles, all text.
2. Remove: the border, all gridlines, the tick marks.
3. Labels: keep referral and paid ads at the August crossing, and paid ads' last week. Delete the rest.
4. Legend: delete it. Write each channel name at the right end of its line.
5. Defaults: whole numbers on the y-axis; combine social and partners into 'other.'
6. Accent color: brand blue on referral. Paid ads in a darker gray.
7. Title: 'Referral passed paid ads in August, on almost no spend.'
8. Logo: gray on this slide.
9. Test: look away, look back. Your eyes should land on the title, then the blue line in August."

**Why it works:** Each line is one action someone can make in the chart tool, in the order to make them. It ends with the test, so the person can check their own work.
