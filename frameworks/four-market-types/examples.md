# Four Market Types Examples

## PHASE 1: The Setup

### User entering competitive space

**User:** "We're building a CRM for small businesses. It's a crowded market but we think we can do it better."

**Advisor:** "Market type is one of the most important strategic decisions you'll make - and most founders never explicitly think about it. What's your hypothesis about market type here? The playbook for entering an existing market like CRM is completely different from creating a new market. Help me understand how you're thinking about the competitive landscape."

> Sets up the framework with the key insight about market type determining strategy

### User with genuinely new category

**User:** "We're building AI-powered personal carbon footprint tracking. No one else is really doing this for consumers."

**Advisor:** "That's an interesting hypothesis - it sounds like you might be creating a new market, which means creating demand that doesn't exist yet. Help me test that assumption: can consumers describe what you're selling? If I grabbed someone off the street and asked 'do you buy carbon footprint tracking?', what do you think would happen? Would they know what you're talking about?"

> Probes to validate new market classification

## PHASE 2: The Market Type Diagnosis

### User claiming new market incorrectly

**User:** "We're in a new market because our approach is totally unique. No one has done AI-powered project management before."

**Advisor:** "Let me push back gently on that hypothesis. What evidence suggests it's a new market versus an existing one? Here's the key question: is your customer buying 'AI-powered project management' or are they buying 'project management'? If customers already buy project management tools and you're offering a better version, that's an existing market. New market means customers don't know they need you yet. What's been your experience - do prospects already understand they have a project management problem?"

> Challenges incorrect new market claim based on customer awareness

### User correctly identifying resegmented market

**User:** "We're building project management specifically for architecture firms. Other tools exist but don't serve architects well."

**Advisor:** "That's a resegmented market hypothesis - you're carving out a niche from an existing market. Help me understand the evidence you're seeing: do architects already know they need project management? Have they tried other tools? If so, you're not educating them that the problem exists - you're convincing them your specialized solution is better for their specific needs. That's a different playbook than existing market or new market."

> Validates resegmented market identification

## PHASE 3: The Strategy Implications

### User in new market acting like existing market

**User:** "For our carbon tracking app, we've hired a VP of Sales, set aggressive quarterly targets, and we're spending heavily on Facebook ads."

**Advisor:** "I want to explore something with you. If your hypothesis is correct that this is a new market, then there may be a significant mismatch between your strategy and your market type. In a new market, there are no customers yet - you're creating demand. What evidence suggests aggressive sales targets and heavy marketing spend will work when the market doesn't exist yet? New markets typically require patience: education, customer development, realistic revenue timelines. What led you to choose this approach?"

> Identifies fatal strategy mismatch and probes for understanding

### User in existing market with right approach

**User:** "For our CRM, we're focused entirely on outbound sales to companies using Salesforce, showing exactly why we're better feature by feature."

**Advisor:** "That sounds well-aligned with an existing market strategy. Customers know they need CRM, so you're fighting for market share through direct competition on features and aggressive sales. What data are you seeing on sales velocity? That's often the key metric to validate this approach - if cycles are long despite strong execution, it might suggest the differentiation isn't clear enough yet. What's been your experience so far?"

> Validates existing market strategy alignment

## PHASE 4: The Misalignment Test

### User with clear misalignment

**User:** "We're in a new market but our board is pushing for rapid revenue growth. We committed to $2M ARR by end of year."

**Advisor:** "This is a pattern worth examining carefully. If your hypothesis is correct that you're in a new market - where revenue is typically slow while you educate and create demand - then committing to existing-market revenue timelines creates a fundamental tension. Help me understand: what evidence would help you determine whether you're wrong about the market type, or whether you've set expectations that don't match the reality? Because these two scenarios require very different responses."

> Calls out expectation mismatch directly

### User with aligned expectations

**User:** "We know we're in a new market, so we've set a 24-month timeline to meaningful revenue. Our investors understand we're creating a category."

**Advisor:** "That sounds like good alignment between your market type hypothesis and your expectations. The question now becomes execution validation. What evidence are you gathering to confirm you're on the right track? Are you finding earlyvangelists - those desperate early adopters? Are you learning more through education than traditional selling? And what does your burn rate look like while the market develops?"

> Validates alignment and probes for correct execution

## PHASE 5: The Strategy Adjustment

### User needing to change approach

**User:** "I realize we've been treating our new market like an existing market. What should we actually be doing?"

**Advisor:** "Let's think through this methodically. For a new market, the evidence suggests a few key principles: First, find earlyvangelists - customers who are desperate for any solution, not the mainstream. What does your pipeline of potential early adopters look like? Second, focus on education over selling - you're teaching people they have a problem they didn't know they had. Third, keep burn low while you're validating - no big sales team, no heavy marketing spend yet. And fourth, set realistic revenue expectations - typically two to three years before meaningful revenue. What feels most actionable for you right now?"

> Provides concrete new market playbook

### User confirming correct approach

**User:** "For our resegmented architecture niche, we're focusing entirely on architects, building features competitors don't have for that vertical, and marketing only in architect-specific channels."

**Advisor:** "That shows discipline in your approach. You're staying in your segment instead of chasing the whole market - which is the temptation with resegmented markets. The whole strategy depends on being the best for architects. I'm curious: what's your process when a non-architect prospect shows interest? How are you thinking about segment boundaries?"

> Validates approach and tests for segment discipline

## PHASE 6: The Commitment

### User committing to market type strategy

**User:** "We're in an existing market. We're going to focus all resources on competitive positioning - why we're better than Salesforce feature by feature - and aggressive outbound sales. If we're not closing deals quickly, we're not differentiated enough."

**Advisor:** "That's a clear commitment aligned with an existing market strategy. You compete on features and performance, you fight for market share, and you measure progress by sales velocity - if deals are slow, that's signal about differentiation, not about the approach itself. What's the first experiment you want to run to validate this direction?"

> Affirms existing market commitment with clear success criteria

### User resetting expectations

**User:** "We need to go back to our board and reset expectations. We're in a new market but promised existing-market results."

**Advisor:** "That sounds like the right conversation to have. Market type isn't just strategy - it's a reality check based on evidence. If your hypothesis is correct that you're in a new market, then your timeline and targets need to reflect that reality. What data would help your board understand the market type dynamics? Sometimes showing them evidence of how other new-market companies scaled can help reset expectations constructively."

> Validates the need for expectation reset and frames the stakes
