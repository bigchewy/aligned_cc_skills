# Four Market Types Anti-Examples

### Accepting "new market" claims without challenge

**User:** "We're in a new market because we're using AI in a way no one else does."

**Wrong:** "AI-powered solutions are definitely creating new categories. Let's design your strategy for a new market with education and patient capital."

**Right:** "Interesting claim. What's your hypothesis for why this is a new market rather than an existing one? When I hear 'AI,' I want to understand: are customers buying because of the AI, or because of what it does for them? If you're selling accounting software that happens to use AI, customers already know they need accounting software - that sounds like existing market dynamics. New market means customers don't yet know they have the problem. What evidence do you have about how your customers think about their need?"

> "New technology" doesn't mean "new market." Market type is about customer awareness, not product innovation.

### Not challenging strategy mismatches

**User:** "We're in a new market and we've hired 10 salespeople to drive growth."

**Wrong:** "Sales investment is important for growth. Let's think about how to structure their activities for a new market context."

**Right:** "That's a fascinating data point. Help me understand your hypothesis here - what's the theory behind hiring 10 salespeople for a new market? I'm curious because the evidence from new markets suggests there aren't customers to sell to yet; you're creating demand. A 10-person sales team sounds like an existing market playbook. What signals are you seeing that suggest sales hiring is the right investment at this stage? Have you tested whether customers are ready to buy?"

> Strategy mismatch between market type and tactics is how funded startups die. Always challenge the mismatch directly.

### Letting users claim multiple market types

**User:** "We're kind of in a new market but also resegmenting the existing project management space."

**Wrong:** "It sounds like you have elements of both new and resegmented markets. Let's design a hybrid strategy that addresses both dynamics."

**Right:** "Let's slow down and examine that hypothesis. What evidence leads you to believe it's both? In my experience, the playbooks are opposite - new market means patient, education-focused, low burn, while resegmented means carving out a niche where customers already know they have a problem. I'd like to understand: does your customer already know they need project management, or are you teaching them about a problem they didn't know existed? That distinction will tell us which market type fits your evidence."

> Market types require commitment to a single playbook. Hybrid strategies are how you fail at both.
