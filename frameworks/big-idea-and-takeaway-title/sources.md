## Phases

- Phase 1, Exploring or Explaining: Cole, "exploratory vs. explanatory analysis" (2014), https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/04/exploratory-vs-explanatory-analysis ; *Storytelling with Data* ch. 1, https://media.wiley.com/product_data/excerpt/57/11190022/1119002257-4.pdf ; dashboard-to-story move from Amy Esselman, "from dashboard to story," https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/from-dashboard-to-story
- Phase 2, Who and What: *Storytelling with Data* ch. 1 (who, what, and how), Wiley PDF above.
- Phase 3, The 3-Minute Story: *Storytelling with Data* ch. 1, Wiley PDF above. (The 2014 blog post did not load.)
- Phase 4, The Big Idea in One Sentence: Cole, "what's the Big Idea?" (2014), https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/02/whats-big-idea ; book ch. 1. Cole credits the Big Idea to Nancy Duarte's *Resonate*.
- Phase 5, Every Title Says the So What: Cole, "so what?" (2017), https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/3/22/so-what ; executive summary from Alex Velez, "how to create executive summary slides" (2022), https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2022/1/14/executive-summary-slides
- Phase 6, The Big Idea and the Titles: Added: the closing table and the hand-offs to `choose-an-effective-visual` and `declutter-and-focus`, so the session ends with something the user can paste in and a next step.

## Added rules

- Added: question-and-answer titles for pages people check every day ("Is North on time? Not yet: 81% this week"). Cole's so-what post is about explanatory charts; this keeps the takeaway in the title when the number changes daily.
- Added: "read just the titles top to bottom" check in Phase 6. It follows from the 3-minute story and the takeaway titles but is not a step Cole names.

## Quotes attributed to Cole

- "If you simply present data, it's easy for your audience to say, "Oh, that's interesting," and move on to the next thing." — book ch. 1.
- "If you know exactly what it is you want to communicate, you can make it fit the time slot you're given, even if it isn't the one for which you are prepared." — book ch. 1.
- "Exploratory analysis is the process of turning over 100 rocks to find perhaps 1 or 2 precious gemstones." — https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2014/04/exploratory-vs-explanatory-analysis
