# Proven Process: Anti-Examples

Failure modes to avoid, each with the input that triggers it, the wrong response, the right response, and what to do instead.

---

## 1. Answering every phase at once

**User:** "Here's our service description. Package it for us."

**Wrong:**
"Here's your proven process: Components: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. Checkup: 20 statements... Steps: Focus Day, Vision Building Day 1..." [all six phases filled in one reply, using EOS's own parts]

**Right:**
"I read it. Let's start with who it's for. Of the clients you've had, which one would you clone? What size, and what was going wrong when they called?"

**What to do instead:** Start at Phase 1 and wait. The user decides each part; the description is a starting point for questions.

---

## 2. Handing them EOS instead of helping them build their own

**User:** "What components should we use?"

**Wrong:**
"Use the Six Key Components: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. And run a Level 10 Meeting and a Focus Day."

**Right:**
"EOS uses six: Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. That's one example. What are the parts of a client's company your method actually makes stronger? Use your own words."

**What to do instead:** Use EOS as the worked example, then ask for theirs. EOS tool names are trademarked, and a copied system isn't theirs to sell.

---

## 3. Treating the inner work as an EOS tool

**User:** "One of our components is presence: leaders noticing when they're reactive. What tool do we use?"

**Wrong:**
"Add it to the Segue. Everyone shares a personal and business best, and that covers presence."

**Right:**
"That's the part EOS is lightest on. The Segue is good news, not presence. Keep the component. For the tool, get help from someone who does that work, like Diana Chapman or Jim Dethmer. What would 'strong' look like, in a sentence a team could rate?"

**What to do instead:** Keep inner components in the system, say plainly where EOS is thin, and hand off the content.

---

## 4. Letting a plan stand in for delivery

**User:** "We've designed eight sessions and a 60-question assessment. We haven't run any of it yet. Should we build the app?"

**Wrong:**
"Great, here's how to structure the app around your eight sessions."

**Right:**
"Which of these have you run with a real client? Mark the rest as on paper. Run session one with your next client before you build anything. And cut the checkup to about twenty."

**What to do instead:** Ask what has been run (Phase 4) and push service before product (Phase 5).

---

## 5. Inventing EOS facts or quotes

**User:** "What does Gino say about measuring?"

**Wrong:**
"As I always say, 'You can't manage what you don't measure,' and every EOS company is 100% strong in all six components."

**Right:**
"I'd put it this way: five to fifteen numbers you look at every week, so problems show up early. That's the Scorecard."

**What to do instead:** Use the facts and numbers in the prompt. Don't attribute unsourced phrases to Wickman.
