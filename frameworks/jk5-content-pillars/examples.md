# JK5 Content Pillars Examples

## PHASE 1: Understand the Philosophy

### User skeptical of the 4:1 ratio

**User:** "Isn't selling just 20% of the time going to hurt my revenue? I need to promote my offers more."

**Advisor:** "I hear you, and I know it feels counterintuitive. But here's what happens when you sell too much: people tune out. They scroll past. They mute you. And then when you DO have something amazing to offer, nobody's listening.

When you serve 80% of the time, you're building trust. You're showing up as a real person, not a walking billboard. And when that 20% selling moment comes? People are READY to hear it because they already know, like, and trust you.

Think about who you buy from. Is it the person who pitches you constantly, or the one who's been helping you, showing up, being real - and then occasionally says 'hey, I have something that might help you'?

The 4:1 isn't about selling less. It's about making your selling work better."

> Addresses revenue concern by reframing selling effectiveness

### User already familiar with the concept

**User:** "I've heard of content pillars before but I never stick with them."

**Advisor:** "Okay, so you've tried this. Let's figure out what went wrong last time.

Usually it's one of three things:
1. The pillars didn't feel like YOU - they were what you thought you 'should' post
2. There were too many pillars and it got overwhelming
3. There was no system - just categories with no rotation

The JK5 is different because it's only 5 topics, the ratio is fixed (4:1), and we're going to make sure these pillars are ones you WANT to talk about, not what some expert told you to talk about.

What happened last time you tried content pillars?"

> Acknowledges past failure and differentiates this approach

## PHASE 2: Define Your 4 Connection Pillars

### Wedding photographer defining pillars

**User:** "I'm a wedding photographer. I mostly post photos from weddings but I'm not sure what else to share."

**Advisor:** "Photos from weddings is actually your SELLING pillar - it showcases your work. Let's define 4 connection pillars that make couples feel like they KNOW you.

**Pillar 1 - Behind-the-Scenes**: Show what wedding day looks like from your perspective. The 4am wake-up. The moment before the couple sees their photos. The gear bag you bring. People love seeing the 'making of.'

**Pillar 2 - Wedding Planning Tips**: Educational content that helps couples even if they don't hire you. 'Timeline tips for golden hour photos.' 'How to choose locations.' You're serving engaged couples, not just advertising.

**Pillar 3 - Personal Life**: Your coffee order. Your dog. Your relationship. What you do on non-wedding weekends. This makes you relatable and human, not just a vendor.

**Pillar 4 - Industry Insights**: Trends you're seeing. Vendor recommendations. Things you wish couples knew. This positions you as an expert who cares about weddings, not just photos.

Now your wedding photos (Pillar 5 - Selling) are surrounded by content that builds trust and connection. Does this feel like a feed you could sustain?"

> Define pillars that differentiate from portfolio posting

### Service provider struggling with personal content

**User:** "I'm a business consultant. I'm not comfortable sharing personal stuff - it feels unprofessional."

**Advisor:** "I hear you. But here's the truth: people buy from people they like. Personality differentiates you from every other consultant.

Let's find personal content that feels comfortable:

**Option 1 - Professional behind-the-scenes**: Not your kids or coffee. Instead, your workspace setup, how you prepare for client calls, books on your desk. Professional but personal.

**Option 2 - Values-based content**: What you believe about business. Stands you take. Things you refuse to do. This is personal without being intimate.

**Option 3 - Learning moments**: Conferences you attend. Podcasts that shaped your thinking. Professional development journey.

You don't have to share your marriage or your morning routine. But you DO need to be more than a talking head with business tips. What feels like a safe 'personal' pillar for you?"

> Adapt personal content to comfort level while maintaining humanity

## PHASE 3: Define Your 1 Selling Pillar

### User worried about being too salesy

**User:** "I don't want my selling pillar to feel pushy. I hate when brands are constantly asking me to buy."

**Advisor:** "That's exactly why we have 4 connection pillars first. When you only sell 20% of the time, it doesn't feel pushy - it feels natural.

Also, your selling pillar should still provide value. Here's how:

**Instead of**: 'Buy my course now!'
**Try**: 'I just opened enrollment for [course]. Here's what a past student achieved: [result]. If you're struggling with [problem], this might help. Link in bio.'

**Instead of**: 'DM me to work together!'
**Try**: 'Here's what my clients say about working with me: [testimonial]. If this resonates, I have 2 spots open for [month].'

You're selling through stories and results, not begging for sales. The 4 connection pillars EARN you the right to sell. When people trust you, your selling pillar feels like a helpful recommendation, not a pitch.

What would make YOUR selling content feel valuable, not pushy?"

> Reframe selling as service, not pitch

### User confused about which offer to feature

**User:** "I have a course, a membership, and 1:1 coaching. Which one is my selling pillar?"

**Advisor:** "All three can live in your selling pillar - but you need a focus.

Here's how I'd think about it: What's your PRIMARY offer right now? The one you most want to fill, that serves the most people, or that's the clearest entry point to working with you?

**Option A - Lead with one**: If your course is your main thing, that's the hero of your selling pillar. Mention the others occasionally, but the course gets 80% of your selling content.

**Option B - Launch rhythm**: If you have launch windows, your selling pillar features whatever's currently open. Course launch month? All course. Membership enrollment? All membership.

**Option C - Value ladder**: Your selling pillar walks people up. Start with course content, mention that graduates often join the membership, hint that serious clients upgrade to 1:1.

What's the offer you want people to know you for FIRST?"

> Helps user focus selling pillar when they have multiple offers

## PHASE 4: Create Your Rotation System

### User posts inconsistently

**User:** "I post maybe 2-3 times per week, but it's random. Some weeks I post 5 times, some weeks nothing."

**Advisor:** "Random posting means random results. Let's create a sustainable rhythm.

For 3 posts per week, here's your JK5 rotation:

**Week 1**:
- Monday: Pillar 1 (Connection)
- Wednesday: Pillar 2 (Connection)
- Friday: Pillar 5 (Selling)

**Week 2**:
- Monday: Pillar 3 (Connection)
- Wednesday: Pillar 4 (Connection)
- Friday: Pillar 1 (Connection)

**Week 3**:
- Monday: Pillar 2 (Connection)
- Wednesday: Pillar 3 (Connection)
- Friday: Pillar 5 (Selling)

Notice: You're selling every other week, not every week. And you're rotating through all 4 connection pillars over 2-3 weeks.

The key is CONSISTENCY in timing. Post on the same days at the same times. Your audience starts expecting you. Random posting means they never know when to look for you.

What three days work best for your schedule?"

> Create specific rotation for their posting frequency

## PHASE 5: Align Bio to Pillars

### User's bio doesn't match content

**User:** "My bio says 'Helping entrepreneurs grow' but my pillars are about wedding planning."

**Advisor:** "That's a major disconnect. Your bio makes a promise your content doesn't keep.

If someone reads 'Helping entrepreneurs grow,' they expect business tips. Then they scroll and see wedding content - confusion. They leave.

**Two options**:

**Option 1 - Change your bio to match your content**:
'Wedding planning made simple | Tips for engaged couples | Showcasing love stories'

**Option 2 - Change your content to match your bio**:
If you actually want to serve entrepreneurs, we need to redesign your pillars.

Which direction is TRUE to what you want to build?

Your bio is a promise. Your content keeps that promise. They MUST align."

> Bio-content alignment is non-negotiable

### User's bio is too vague

**User:** "My bio just says 'Creative. Dreamer. Mom.'"

**Advisor:** "That tells me nothing about how you can help me or why I should follow you. It describes you but doesn't invite me in.

Let's rewrite it using the JK5 structure:

**Line 1**: Who you help + transformation
'Helping creative entrepreneurs build businesses they love'

**Line 2**: Credibility/proof
'7-figure business built from $300 camera'

**Line 3**: Personal hook (connection pillar nod)
'Midwest mom obsessed with coffee & my golden retriever'

**Line 4**: Clear CTA
'Get my free content guide ↓'

See how this covers ALL your pillars? Business (Pillar 1), personal (Pillar 3-4), and selling (Pillar 5)?

What transformation do you help people achieve?"

> Transform vague bios into pillar-aligned promises

## PHASE 6: Create Your Action Plan

### User feeling overwhelmed

**User:** "This is a lot. I don't know if I can actually do all of this."

**Advisor:** "Okay, pause. Let's make this smaller.

You do NOT need to do everything this week. Here's the absolute minimum to start:

**This week**: Just identify ONE pillar and batch 5 pieces of content for it. That's it. One pillar. Five ideas.

**Next week**: Do the same for pillar two.

In five weeks, you'll have 25 pieces of content ready. That's over a month of posts at 5x/week, and you built it slowly.

The JK5 is meant to REDUCE overwhelm, not create it. If it feels like too much, we've gone too fast. What's the ONE pillar you're most excited to create content for? Let's start there."

> Reduces overwhelm by simplifying first steps

### User ready to take action

**User:** "I'm excited! I know exactly what I want to post about now."

**Advisor:** "I love that energy! Let's capture it before it fades.

Your homework:
1. Set a timer for 15 minutes
2. Open your notes app
3. Brainstorm AT LEAST 5 ideas for your first connection pillar
4. Don't edit yourself - just dump ideas

Do this while you're still feeling it. Motivation is a terrible long-term strategy, but it's a great short-term fuel. Use it now.

Then tomorrow, do the same for pillar two.

You've got this. Go create."

> Captures momentum with immediate actionable next step
